# Domain-Expert Supplement: Advantageous Photosensitizer Scaffolds

## Reading rule

“Advantageous” means advantageous for a stated design problem and condition, not universally superior. The scaffold entries below combine direct evidence from the uploaded papers with authoritative design context. A reported numerical value is not a transferable threshold unless the assay and conditions match.

## Scaffold families

| Family / examples | Structural characteristics | Evidence-supported advantages | Conditions and limitations |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Phthalocyanines** — Makhseed 2013; Machacek 2015 | Large tetrapyrrolic macrocycle; Zn, Mg, or metal-free centers; peripheral ionic/steric groups; cationic substituents; far-red absorption | Makhseed's Zn compound combines sixteen quaternized imidazolyl groups with steric bulk and ionic repulsion, reporting water solubility, non-aggregation, `ΦΔ > 0.33` in water, `EC50 36.7 nM`, and dark `TC50 395 µM`. Machacek reports `ΦΔ` up to `0.91`, `λmax` up to `750 nm`, submicromolar Zn-complex phototoxicity, and dark `TC50 ≈1500 µM`; Mg analogues were inactive in the tested assay (`IC50 >100 µM`). | Strong scaffold for far-red Type-II PDT and aqueous aggregation-control studies. Metal identity, substitution position, charge, aggregation, and assay conditions are confounded; values must not be pooled. |
| **Porphyrins and N-alkylpyridylporphyrins** — Odeh 2014; Chen 2021 | Tetrapyrrole macrocycle with tunable metal center and cationic pyridyl/alkyl substituents; can be formulated as polymer nanotheranostics | Cationic alkyl architecture is associated with mitochondrial targeting in Odeh; porphyrin-polymer systems support imaging-guided PDT in Chen. These designs connect macrocycle photophysics to delivery and organelle exposure. | Localization and formulation can dominate apparent efficacy; DFT on an isolated chromophore cannot reproduce polymer, protein, membrane, or aggregation behavior without an explicit model. |
| **Chlorins / bacteriochlorins / phthalocyanines as red/NIR tetrapyrroles** — corpus reviews plus authoritative PDT reviews | More reduced macrocycles or fused aza-macrocycles with strong red/far-red absorption; substitution controls amphiphilicity and clearance | Authoritative reviews identify tetrapyrroles as a clinically important family; chlorins, bacteriochlorins, and phthalocyanines can provide absorption in approximately the `600–800 nm` region. Bacteriochlorins can move toward NIR but may have stability or modification limitations. | The `600–800 nm` range is a design window, not a hard reward threshold. Tissue optical properties, oxygen, extinction coefficient, quantum yield, pharmacokinetics, and photobleaching all matter. |
| **BODIPY and donor–acceptor conjugated oligomers** — Peng 2021 | BODIPY acceptor coupled to cyclopenta-dithiophene donors in a D–A–D conjugated oligomer; ICT and low bandgap | Peng reports dual fluorescence at `463` and `730 nm`, ROS production, an ICT-associated ISC interpretation, and photothermal conversion efficiency `33.1%`. This family offers tunable absorption/emission, imaging, PDT, and PTT integration. | Photothermal output competes with productive photochemistry; the reported antimicrobial activity does not establish anticancer or pyroptosis performance. ICT descriptors require method- and environment-aware calculation. |
| **AIE photosensitizers** — Wang 2021; Wu 2021 | Conformationally restricted or aggregation-emission-enabled chromophores; can be activated in situ, mitochondria-targeted, or membrane-anchored through cationic/recognition groups | AIE designs can retain useful emission in condensed environments and enable imaging. Wang uses cancer-cell-activated in situ synthesis for mitochondria targeting; Wu links stronger membrane anchoring to ROS-mediated membrane damage and increasing pyroptosis dominance. | “AIE” is not equivalent to high ROS yield. Aggregation state, local packing, membrane composition, activation chemistry, and target-cell expression must be measured. |
| **Metal complexes / heavy-atom chromophores** — Su 2022 Re(I); Rossi 2021 Pd porphyrins; Zhang 2023 Cu bacteriochlorin | Heavy atoms or metal centers can alter spin–orbit coupling, triplet formation, redox chemistry, and localization; ligands can add CAIX targeting or membrane affinity | CA-Re combines CAIX anchoring with reported Type-I/Type-II PDT, hypoxia activity, and GSDMD pyroptosis. Rossi demonstrates cationic Pd porphyrin photodamage in mycobacteria. The uploaded papers support metal/ligand architecture as a route to tune spin and targeting. | Heavy atoms can increase intersystem crossing but may also change excited-state quenching, toxicity, stability, and synthesis. DFT requires appropriate relativistic treatment and basis sets; simple HOMO–LUMO heuristics are insufficient. |
| **Type-I / radical-generating photodrugs** — Yu 2022 NI-TA; Yi 2024 M-TOP; Ma 2024 review; Liu 2024 | Electron-transfer-capable donor/acceptor or redox-tuned chromophores; may include mitochondria-targeting motifs, activatable groups, or oxygen-recycling pathways | NI-TA generates superoxide and remains active at `≤2% O2`; M-TOP is described as mitochondria-targeting, NIR-activatable, Type-I, and less oxygen-dependent, with dose-dependent apoptosis/pyroptosis switching. These scaffolds are attractive for hypoxia-aware design. | Type-I labels require species-specific ROS and oxygen-controlled evidence. Redox potentials and excited-state energies are useful computational inputs but are not direct cellular outcomes. |
| **Targeting and activatable conjugates** — Odeh, Wang, Wu, Su, Yi | A photosensitizer core is covalently linked to mitochondrial, membrane, CAIX, cancer-cell-activated, or other recognition/activation groups | Localization can increase effective local damage while reducing required bulk dose. This is a structure–distribution–performance route complementary to increasing intrinsic ROS yield. | Target engagement, uptake, cleavage/activation, and expression heterogeneity are biological variables. Keep localization and photochemistry as separate objectives. |

## Cross-family design principles

1. **A macrocycle is a platform, not a guarantee.** Zn phthalocyanines, porphyrins, chlorins, and bacteriochlorins are useful because their conjugation, metal center, charge, and substitution can be tuned, but aggregation and localization can reverse apparent rankings.
2. **Charge and steric design jointly control aqueous behavior.** Makhseed's combination is evidence for a specific Zn-phthalocyanine design, not proof that any cationic or bulky substituent prevents aggregation.
3. **Electronic tuning must be paired with site control.** ICT, heavy atoms, donor–acceptor separation, and redox tuning influence excited states; targeting groups determine where the resulting ROS is generated.
4. **Type-I and Type-II should be treated as a conditional continuum.** Su, Yu, Yi, and Ma motivate oxygen-aware design, but the operative route depends on substrate, oxygen, solvent, aggregation, and illumination.
5. **Phototoxicity is a joint property.** A useful scaffold should be evaluated as `structure → electronic states → ROS → localization → cell endpoint`, with each arrow measured or validated separately.

## Scaffold-selection checklist for candidate generation

- Choose the family based on intended excitation window and oxygen regime.
- Encode metal identity, charge, donor/acceptor topology, conjugation length, steric shielding, targeting group, and activation mechanism as explicit design variables.
- Screen aggregation and protonation/charge states before interpreting isolated-molecule photophysics.
- Preserve a low-dark-toxicity constraint and report light dose, oxygen, cell type, and incubation with every potency label.
- Do not assign a pyroptosis objective from a scaffold name; require pathway markers such as gasdermin cleavage, pore formation, caspase route, LDH, and cytokine data.
