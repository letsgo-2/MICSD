# Mitochondria-targeting type-I photodrug for pyroptotic oncotherapy

A mitochondria-targeting type-I photodrug for pyroptotic oncotherapy refers, in this study, to a photodynamic strategy designed to localize a type-I photodrug to [[mitochondria]] and use a [[caspase-3]]-linked signaling switch to drive inflammatory cancer cell death for tumor treatment. The supplementary material identifies this approach as the focus of the work titled *Mitochondria-Targeting Type-I Photodrug: Harnessing Caspase-3 Activity for Pyroptotic Oncotherapy*. ^[microsoft-word-supporting-information-reversion.md:9-13]

## Core design

The supplementary material describes four related photodrugs, `C-TOP`, `N-TOP`, `E-TOP`, and `M-TOP`, and includes synthetic methods, spectroscopic characterization, and theoretical calculations for them. `M-TOP` is the mitochondria-targeting variant emphasized in the in vitro and in vivo treatment methods, including cell-death assays, dendritic-cell activation assays, and mouse tumor studies. ^[microsoft-word-supporting-information-reversion.md:68-85, microsoft-word-supporting-information-reversion.md:85-88, microsoft-word-supporting-information-reversion.md:161-187, microsoft-word-supporting-information-reversion.md:248-285, microsoft-word-supporting-information-reversion.md:306-366]

The study compares this platform with conventional type-I photodrugs, type-II photodrugs, and nanoparticle-based photodrugs. In that comparison, the mitochondria-targeting type-I photodrug is characterized as having good biocompatibility, mitochondria targeting, low oxygen dependency, a signaling-pathway switch "via caspase-3," a controllable inflammatory response, and controllable immunotherapy against primary, distant, and recurrent tumors. ^[microsoft-word-supporting-information-reversion.md:523-599]

## Mitochondrial targeting

Intracellular distribution was assessed in `U251-MG` cells by confocal laser scanning microscopy after incubation with photodrugs at `0.5 uM` for `30 min`, using [[MitoTracker]] Green FM to confirm mitochondrial distribution and LysoTracker Green DND 26 to assess endo-/lysosomal colocalization. Figure S18 is described as showing colocalization of the photodrugs with mitochondria or lysosomes together with corresponding Pearson correlation coefficients. ^[microsoft-word-supporting-information-reversion.md:217-232, microsoft-word-supporting-information-reversion.md:443-445]

Cellular uptake studies in `U251-MG` cells were performed by [[flow cytometry]] at a fixed concentration of `0.5 uM` over `0-60 min`, and Figure S20 is described as summarizing confocal imaging and statistical analysis for different incubation durations and concentrations of `M-TOP`. Figure S21 is described as a flow-cytometry analysis of different photodrugs showing their cellular uptake features. ^[microsoft-word-supporting-information-reversion.md:210-216, microsoft-word-supporting-information-reversion.md:458-465]

## Type-I photodynamic behavior

Reactive oxygen species were evaluated using `DCFH-DA` for general ROS, `SOSG` for singlet oxygen, and `DHE` for superoxide anion radical detection. Cells were treated with `0.5 uM` photodrug for `30 min`, stained with the respective probes, and imaged immediately after `1 min` of `640 nm` LED irradiation at `15 mW/cm2`; Figure S19 is described as reporting the corresponding probe emissions in PBS solutions containing `1 uM` photodrug after `10 s` of `640 nm` irradiation. ^[microsoft-word-supporting-information-reversion.md:233-247, microsoft-word-supporting-information-reversion.md:450-453]

The comparison table states that the mitochondria-targeting type-I photodrug has low oxygen dependency, unlike the listed type-II and nanoparticle-based photodrugs, which are marked as highly oxygen-dependent. The same table also reports low in vitro anticancer `IC50` values of about `0.3 uM` under both normal oxygen and hypoxic conditions for this work. ^[microsoft-word-supporting-information-reversion.md:531-545, microsoft-word-supporting-information-reversion.md:566-580]

## Pyroptotic cell-death program

Apoptosis and pyroptosis were assessed in `U251-MG` cells by Annexin V-FITC and `PI` staining, microscopy of morphological changes, and flow cytometry. Dose-dependent cell death was measured with `M-TOP` from `0-1 uM` at a fixed irradiation condition of `640 nm`, `15 mW/cm2`, `5 min`, while irradiation-dependent cell death was tested over `0-10 min` at a fixed `M-TOP` dose of `0.25 uM`. ^[microsoft-word-supporting-information-reversion.md:248-262]

The supplementary methods further state that western blotting was used to measure `caspase-3`, cleaved `PARP`, `GSDME`, and `GAPDH` in `U251-MG` cells after photodrug treatment, and that cell supernatants were collected for `IL-1beta` and `LDH` analysis. These readouts were used in assays associated with Figure 1i after treatment with `1 uM` `C-TOP`, `N-TOP`, `E-TOP`, or `M-TOP`, followed by `640 nm` irradiation at `15 mW/cm2` for `5 min` and `3 h` of additional culture. ^[microsoft-word-supporting-information-reversion.md:263-290]

In the study-wide comparison table, this work is the only listed strategy marked as having a signaling pathway switch and controllable inflammatory response, with the signaling switch specified as occurring via [[caspase-3]]. ^[microsoft-word-supporting-information-reversion.md:545-565]

## Immune activation and oncotherapy

To test whether photo-treated tumor cells could stimulate immune activation, immature `DC2.4` dendritic cells were co-cultured with supernatants from photodrug-treated tumor cells for `24 h`. The dendritic cells were then analyzed for `CD40` and `CD86` expression by flow cytometry, and `TNF-alpha` and `IL-1beta` release in dendritic-cell supernatants was measured by ELISA; Figure S23 is described as presenting these maturation and cytokine-release results. ^[microsoft-word-supporting-information-reversion.md:306-311, microsoft-word-supporting-information-reversion.md:476-480]

In a subcutaneous `Hepa1-6` xenograft model in female `C57BL6` mice, tumors were established by injecting `1 x 10^6` cells into the right flank. Mice were assigned to `PBS`, `Light`, `M-TOP`, and `M-TOP+Light` groups, and after tumors reached about `100 mm3`, `M-TOP` was injected intratumorally at `50 uL` of `1 uM` in PBS. Thirty minutes later, the light-treated groups received `640 nm` laser irradiation at `150 mW/cm2` for `10 min`; administration and irradiation were repeated three times every two days, and tumor volume was monitored during therapy. ^[microsoft-word-supporting-information-reversion.md:317-334]

Collected tumors from the subcutaneous model were used for immune analyses. Single-cell suspensions were stained with markers including `CD3`, `CD4`, `CD8`, `CD11b`, `CD11c`, `CD69`, `CD86`, `CD25`, `FOXP3`, and `CD206` for flow-cytometric analysis, while other tumors were fixed for immunohistochemical analysis of proteins such as `HMGB1` and `CRT`. ^[microsoft-word-supporting-information-reversion.md:335-343]

## Distant and recurrent tumor models

For a bilateral distal-tumor model, `Hepa1-6` cells were injected into both flanks of `C57BL6` mice. After both tumors reached about `100 mm3`, intratumoral photodrug injection and laser irradiation were applied only to the left-flank tumor, while the right-flank tumor remained untreated; tumor volumes on both flanks were monitored throughout therapy, and distal-tumor suspensions were stained with `CD11b` and `CD11c` for flow-cytometric analysis. ^[microsoft-word-supporting-information-reversion.md:344-358]

For a recurrent-tumor model, `Hepa1-6` tumors grown in the left flank were surgically resected by `90%` once they reached `400 mm3`, and photodynamic therapy was then performed three times every six days. Tumor growth and survival were monitored until day `54`, recurrent tumors were evaluated for immunological memory by flow cytometry, and serum `IL-6`, `TNF-alpha`, and `IFN-gamma` were measured by ELISA. ^[microsoft-word-supporting-information-reversion.md:359-366]

The study comparison table summarizes the in vivo outcome of this platform as controllable immunotherapy against primary, distant, and recurrent tumors, distinguishing it from the other listed photodynamic strategies. ^[microsoft-word-supporting-information-reversion.md:581-599]
