# Isomer-dependent photoinactivation of rapidly growing mycobacteria by Pd(II) porphyrins

This study examined two isomeric tetra-cationic palladium(II) porphyrins, `4-PdTPyP` and `3-PdTPyP`, as [[photosensitizer|photosensitizers]] for [[antimicrobial photodynamic therapy]] against rapidly growing mycobacteria (RGM). The tested strains were *Mycobacteroides abscessus* subsp. *abscessus* (ATCC 19977), *Mycolicibacterium fortuitum* (ATCC 6841), *Mycolicibacterium smegmatis* (ATCC 700084), and *Mycobacteroides abscessus* subsp. *massiliense* (ATCC 48898). The central finding was isomer-dependent activity: the meta-substituted porphyrin `3-PdTPyP` was markedly more effective under irradiation than the para-substituted `4-PdTPyP`. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:27-38, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:143-146, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:223-231]

## Study design

The porphyrins were tested over concentrations from `93.75` to `0.04 ug/mL` (`39` to `0.017 uM`). Photodynamic assays used white LED light at `400-800 nm`, `50 mW/cm^2`, with a total dose of `270 J/cm^2` over `90 min`, alongside light-only and dark controls. Minimum inhibitory concentration ([[MIC]]) testing followed broth microdilution conditions for the four standard RGM strains. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:123-135, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:137-141, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:149-168]

## Isomer-dependent antimycobacterial activity

Under dark conditions, both `4-PdTPyP` and `3-PdTPyP` showed the same MIC against all tested strains: `93.75 ug/mL` (`39 uM`). Under white-light irradiation, `4-PdTPyP` improved only modestly to `46.87 ug/mL` (`19.5 uM`) for all strains, whereas `3-PdTPyP` showed much stronger activity, with MICs of `2.92 ug/mL` (`1.21 uM`) for *M. fortuitum* and *M. abscessus*, and `5.85 ug/mL` (`2.43 uM`) for *M. massiliense* and *M. smegmatis*. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:223-231, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:267-276]

The authors emphasize that the tetra-substituted cationic Pd(II) porphyrins had significant antimycobacterial photoactivity, with `3-PdTPyP` as the standout compound. They further associate the weaker performance of `4-PdTPyP` with lower aqueous solubility, greater aggregation tendency, and higher hydrophobicity relative to `3-PdTPyP`, which would reduce interaction with bacteria and diminish reactive oxygen species generation. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:420-428, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:443-457]

## Bactericidal effect across irradiation sessions

Because `3-PdTPyP` gave the best MIC results, it was selected for time-kill analysis. At `MIC` and `2xMIC`, it inhibited the growth of most tested RGM strains after two white-light irradiation sessions applied at `0` and `24 h`. For *M. smegmatis*, bactericidal action at `MIC` required `48 h` and therefore three irradiation sessions at `0`, `24`, and `48 h`. The `1/2 MIC` condition still reduced mycobacterial growth to some extent relative to the control. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:169-178, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:232-242]

The discussion states that `3-PdTPyP` could eradicate mycobacterial growth with only two sessions of visible white-light irradiation, which the authors highlight as relevant because RGM infections often require prolonged multidrug treatment. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:549-570]

## Reactive oxygen species mechanism

Reactive oxygen species ([[ROS]]) involvement was examined with scavenger assays using ascorbic acid for [[singlet oxygen]], DMSO for superoxide radical, tert-butanol for hydroxyl radical, and N-acetylcysteine for hydroperoxyl radical. For `3-PdTPyP`, susceptibility was reduced mainly in the presence of ascorbic acid, indicating that singlet oxygen was the main species involved in photoinactivation. In contrast, `4-PdTPyP` did not show evidence of ROS-species involvement in this assay. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:179-191, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:243-253]

For `3-PdTPyP`, the MIC increased in the presence of the singlet-oxygen quencher to `23.43 ug/mL` (`9.74 uM`) for *M. fortuitum* and *M. massiliense*, and to `11.71 ug/mL` (`4.87 uM`) for *M. abscessus* and *M. smegmatis*. The discussion interprets this pattern as evidence that `^1O2` is the principal ROS mediating antimycobacterial activity for this isomer. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:247-253, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:458-467]

## Cell-wall and surface effects observed by AFM

[[Atomic force microscopy|AFM]] was used to examine *M. smegmatis* after incubation with `3-PdTPyP` under dark and light conditions. The topographic images indicated that porphyrin plus white light played a key role in the cell-death pathway: untreated bacteria preserved their morphology in the dark, while porphyrin under light significantly altered morphology, causing wrinkling and thinning of the bacilli. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:192-214, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:255-287]

AFM nanomechanical measurements showed increased adhesion forces after `3-PdTPyP` treatment, reaching `0.25 +/- 0.05 uN` in the dark and `0.4 +/- 0.16 uN` under light, compared with `0.14 +/- 0.03 uN` for untreated dark controls and `0.17 +/- 0.03 uN` for light-only controls. Treated bacilli also became narrower, with widths decreasing from `0.75 +/- 0.03 um` in dark controls to `0.46 +/- 0.05 um` in dark plus porphyrin and `0.43 +/- 0.05 um` in light plus porphyrin, while length increased to `3.60 +/- 0.33 um` in the light-plus-porphyrin condition. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:288-298, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:475-492]

Electrostatic force microscopy maps showed decreased electrostatic components after porphyrin treatment, and after light exposure many spots correlated with membrane cavities seen in topographic images. The discussion states that `3-PdTPyP` affected both nanomechanical and electrostatic properties of the bacteria, with significant electrochemical degradation of the cell wall under the applied conditions. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:299-314, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:516-548]

## Proposed basis for isomer dependence

The authors propose that the cationic character of these porphyrins favors interaction with negatively charged microbial structures and, in mycobacteria, likely with anionic groups in membrane-associated components. They suggest that this interaction alters membrane electrostatic potential and increases susceptibility to ROS attack. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:429-442]

They further argue that `3-PdTPyP` outperforms `4-PdTPyP` because the meta isomer is more soluble and less aggregation-prone in aqueous media. According to the paper, aggregation reduces ROS generation and therefore lowers photo-oxidative efficiency, providing a mechanistic explanation for the isomer-dependent difference in antimycobacterial activity. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:443-457]

## Conclusion

The paper concludes that this was the first demonstration of antimycobacterial effects from tetra-cationic porphyrins bearing peripheral `[Pd(bpy)Cl]+` complexes. Among the two isomers, `3-PdTPyP` was the more promising photosensitizer because its photochemical and photobiological properties supported local ROS production, especially singlet oxygen, leading to mycobacterial cell-wall damage and altered nanomechanical and electrostatic adhesion properties. The authors also suggest that such photosensitizers may be of interest for localized RGM infections and could be combined with conventional antimycobacterial therapy. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:571-593]
