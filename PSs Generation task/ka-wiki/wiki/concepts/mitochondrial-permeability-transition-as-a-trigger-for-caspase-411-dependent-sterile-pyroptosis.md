# Mitochondrial permeability transition as a trigger for caspase-4/11-dependent sterile pyroptosis

[[Mitochondrial permeability transition]] (MPT) is presented in the source as a cell-intrinsic trigger of sterile [[pyroptosis]] mediated by [[caspase-4]]/[[caspase-11]]. The study identifies caspase-4/11 as a general sensor of MPT and describes an [[Apaf-1 pyroptosome]] that links mitochondrial stress to pyroptotic execution under sterile conditions. ^[apaf-1-pyroptosome-senses-mitochondrial-permeability-transition.md:58-66, apaf-1-pyroptosome-senses-mitochondrial-permeability-transition.md:133-143]

## Overview

The paper contrasts this pathway with the established role of caspase-4/11 as intracellular sensors of cytosolic LPS in infection. It asks how caspase-4/11 might detect host-derived signals in noninfectious disease and concludes that MPT, induced by bile acids, calcium overload, or ANT activators, can activate caspase-4/11 and drive pyroptosis under sterile conditions. ^[apaf-1-pyroptosome-senses-mitochondrial-permeability-transition.md:56-66, apaf-1-pyroptosome-senses-mitochondrial-permeability-transition.md:102-109, apaf-1-pyroptosome-senses-mitochondrial-permeability-transition.md:133-143]

MPT is described as a state caused by persistent opening of the [[mitochondrial permeability transition pore]] (MPTP), producing loss of inner mitochondrial membrane integrity and osmotic imbalance. In the study, MPT had previously been associated with regulated necrosis, but the molecular mechanism connecting MPT to cell death had remained unclear. ^[apaf-1-pyroptosome-senses-mitochondrial-permeability-transition.md:117-132, apaf-1-pyroptosome-senses-mitochondrial-permeability-transition.md:303-310]

## Upstream triggers of MPT-driven pyroptosis

The study reports that bile acids such as deoxycholic acid and chenodeoxycholic acid trigger pyroptotic death in macrophages and THP-1 cells, with activation of caspase-11 or caspase-4, release of HMGB1 and IL-1alpha, and pyroptotic morphology. This cell death was reduced in Caspase-11-deficient macrophages and was independent of canonical [[NLRP3 inflammasome]] signaling and largely independent of caspase-1. ^[apaf-1-pyroptosome-senses-mitochondrial-permeability-transition.md:145-190]

Bile acids did not directly bind recombinant caspase-4 or caspase-11 and did not directly activate these caspases in a cell-free system, unlike LPS. Instead, bile acid treatment induced MPT, shown by loss of mitochondrial cristae, persistent dissipation of mitochondrial membrane potential, and loss of inner membrane integrity. ^[apaf-1-pyroptosome-senses-mitochondrial-permeability-transition.md:223-240, apaf-1-pyroptosome-senses-mitochondrial-permeability-transition.md:268-275]

Pharmacologic experiments implicated ANT-facilitated MPT in this response. The ANT inhibitor bongkrekic acid blocked bile acid-induced MPT, reduced cell death and LDH release, and abolished activation of caspase-11/4, caspase-3, and GSDME, whereas inhibitors of cyclophilin D and phosphate carrier did not. LPS-induced pyroptosis was not blocked by bongkrekic acid, indicating that the MPT-dependent route is mechanistically distinct from LPS-triggered noncanonical pyroptosis. ^[apaf-1-pyroptosome-senses-mitochondrial-permeability-transition.md:276-302]

The source extends this mechanism beyond bile acids. Thapsigargin-induced mitochondrial calcium overload provoked MPT and pyroptotic cell death that was blocked by bongkrekic acid and required caspase-11 in macrophages. Lonidamine, an ANT1-targeting agent, also induced persistent MPT and pyroptotic signaling, and bile acid- or lonidamine-induced pyroptosis in HepG2 cells was largely prevented by bongkrekic acid pretreatment or ANT1 silencing. ^[apaf-1-pyroptosome-senses-mitochondrial-permeability-transition.md:303-346]

## [[apaf-1-pyroptosome|Apaf-1 pyroptosome]] assembly

To explain how MPT activates caspase-4/11, the authors identified a protein complex containing [[Apaf-1]] and caspase-4/11. Immunoprecipitation and pull-down experiments showed that Apaf-1 specifically interacts with caspase-4 at its CARD domain, while caspase-9 was not required for this interaction and other death adaptors such as RIPK2 and FADD were not detected in the complex. Confocal analysis also showed increased colocalization of Apaf-1 and caspase-4 after bile acid or lonidamine treatment. ^[apaf-1-pyroptosome-senses-mitochondrial-permeability-transition.md:347-367, apaf-1-pyroptosome-senses-mitochondrial-permeability-transition.md:392-410]

Functional experiments showed that Apaf-1 is required for this pathway. In APAF-1 knockout HepG2 cells, bile acids and lonidamine failed to activate the caspase-4/caspase-3/GSDME axis and failed to induce pyroptotic death and LDH release. In a reconstituted cell-free system, Apaf-1 activated caspase-4 only when cytochrome c and ATP were present, supporting the idea that MPT-driven mitochondrial factor release enables assembly of this complex. ^[apaf-1-pyroptosome-senses-mitochondrial-permeability-transition.md:411-423, apaf-1-pyroptosome-senses-mitochondrial-permeability-transition.md:478-489]

The source terms this complex the [[apaf-1-pyroptosome|Apaf-1 pyroptosome]]. It reports that the complex activates caspase-4/11 upstream of caspase-3 and that the pyroptosome has an Apaf-1:caspase-4 stoichiometry of approximately 7:2, distinct from the 7:4 stoichiometry described for the apoptosome with caspase-9. ^[apaf-1-pyroptosome-senses-mitochondrial-permeability-transition.md:61-64, apaf-1-pyroptosome-senses-mitochondrial-permeability-transition.md:463-467, apaf-1-pyroptosome-senses-mitochondrial-permeability-transition.md:587-610]

## Downstream execution pathway

In this sterile MPT-triggered pathway, activated caspase-4/11 does not primarily cleave [[Gasdermin D|GSDMD]] into its pore-forming fragment. Instead, it activates [[caspase-3]], which then cleaves [[Gasdermin E|GSDME]] to execute pyroptosis. Macrophages from GSDME-deficient mice, but not GSDMD-deficient mice, were resistant to bile acid-induced pyroptosis, and CASPASE-3- or GSDME-silenced HepG2 cells were similarly protected. ^[apaf-1-pyroptosome-senses-mitochondrial-permeability-transition.md:191-222]

Cell-free experiments further supported this signaling order. Apaf-1/caspase-4 complexes activated pro-caspase-3, whereas caspase-8 was not activated in the reconstituted system; active recombinant caspase-4, but not caspase-1, directly cleaved caspase-3. The study therefore places caspase-4/11 upstream of caspase-3 in MPT-triggered pyroptosis. ^[apaf-1-pyroptosome-senses-mitochondrial-permeability-transition.md:446-467]

The paper also distinguishes this pathway from LPS-driven noncanonical pyroptosis. Upon LPS ligation, caspase-4/11 directly associates with GSDMD and promotes its productive cleavage, whereas in the MPT setting GSDMD is not properly recruited and is instead cleaved by caspase-3 into an inactive fragment. This difference helps explain why sterile MPT-triggered pyroptosis is executed mainly through GSDME rather than GSDMD. ^[apaf-1-pyroptosome-senses-mitochondrial-permeability-transition.md:62-64, apaf-1-pyroptosome-senses-mitochondrial-permeability-transition.md:197-222, apaf-1-pyroptosome-senses-mitochondrial-permeability-transition.md:734-745]

## Relationship to apoptosis and mitochondrial stress intensity

The study distinguishes MPT-triggered pyroptosis from [[mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization]] (MOMP)-driven apoptosis. Low-dose bile acids and doxorubicin, which induced MOMP-associated apoptosis, activated caspase-9 and caspase-3 but not caspase-11 or caspase-1. By contrast, high-dose bile acids that caused MPT rapidly activated caspase-11 and caspase-3 without activating caspase-9 or caspase-1 in that setting. ^[apaf-1-pyroptosome-senses-mitochondrial-permeability-transition.md:522-542]

This difference was reflected in Apaf-1 complex assembly and mitochondrial metabolite release. Low-dose bile acids promoted Apaf-1/caspase-9 apoptosome assembly, whereas high-dose bile acids promoted Apaf-1/caspase-11 pyroptosome assembly. MPT caused earlier cytochrome c release and an immediate rise in cytoplasmic ATP, and the source reports that caspase-4 recruitment to Apaf-1 required a higher ATP level than caspase-9. ^[apaf-1-pyroptosome-senses-mitochondrial-permeability-transition.md:540-542, apaf-1-pyroptosome-senses-mitochondrial-permeability-transition.md:574-586]

Genetic perturbation further separated these pathways. Knockout of CASPASE-4, but not CASPASE-9, reduced MPT-elicited pyroptotic death, while BAX/BAK loss impaired MOMP-associated apoptotic responses but had negligible effects on bile acid- and lonidamine-induced MPT signaling and pyroptosis. The study therefore concludes that MOMP mainly facilitates apoptosome assembly, whereas MPT mainly facilitates Apaf-1/caspase-4 pyroptosome assembly. ^[apaf-1-pyroptosome-senses-mitochondrial-permeability-transition.md:546-573]

## Pathophysiologic relevance

The paper tests this mechanism in bile duct ligation-induced cholestatic liver injury. In this model, mice showed bile acid accumulation, increased ANT1, persistent MPT, formation of an Apaf-1/caspase-11 complex, caspase-11 activation, and cleavage of caspase-3 and GSDME in liver tissue. Bile acids also induced MPT, caspase-11 activation, and GSDME cleavage in primary mouse hepatocytes, and these effects were blocked by bongkrekic acid. ^[apaf-1-pyroptosome-senses-mitochondrial-permeability-transition.md:611-646]

Loss-of-function results supported a major contribution of this pathway to cholestatic injury. Caspase-11-deficient mice were resistant to bile duct ligation-induced caspase-3 activation, GSDME cleavage, and release of IL-1alpha, IL-1beta, and HMGB1, while GSDME deficiency completely rescued mortality in this model. The authors note that caspase-1/GSDMD signaling may also contribute, but conclude that the caspase-11-caspase-3-GSDME axis is dominant in determining cholestatic liver failure. ^[apaf-1-pyroptosome-senses-mitochondrial-permeability-transition.md:647-660]

## Interpretation

In the source, MPT-driven cell death is reframed from poorly defined regulated necrosis to a defined sterile pyroptotic program centered on Apaf-1-dependent activation of caspase-4/11. The proposed sequence is MPT, release of cytochrome c and ATP, assembly of the [[apaf-1-pyroptosome|Apaf-1 pyroptosome]], activation of caspase-4/11, cleavage of caspase-3, and GSDME-dependent pyroptosis. ^[apaf-1-pyroptosome-senses-mitochondrial-permeability-transition.md:664-689]

The discussion also places this mechanism in a broader disease context. Because MPT can be induced by calcium overload, oxidative stress, and toxic endobiotics such as bile acids, the findings are presented as relevant to sterile inflammatory conditions including cholestatic liver failure and ischemic tissue damage. ^[apaf-1-pyroptosome-senses-mitochondrial-permeability-transition.md:670-680, apaf-1-pyroptosome-senses-mitochondrial-permeability-transition.md:749-760]
