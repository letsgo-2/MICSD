# Pyroptosis dominance increases with membrane-anchoring strength during PDT

This concept describes a trend reported for a series of membrane-anchoring aggregation-induced emission ([[AIE]]) [[photosensitizers]] used in [[photodynamic therapy|PDT]]. In the study, three related compounds, `TBD-1C`, `TBD-2C`, and `TBD-3C`, were designed with increasing numbers of cationic chains to strengthen membrane anchoring. The authors reported that, as membrane-anchoring capability increased across this series, pyroptosis progressively became the dominant cell-death pathway during PDT. ^[activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:17-29, activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:125-150]

## Design basis

The photosensitizer series was built on the same `TBD` core and differed only in the number of cationic chains: one in `TBD-1C`, two in `TBD-2C`, and three in `TBD-3C`. The cationic chains were introduced to provide membrane anchoring, and the study states that membrane-anchoring capability increased significantly with increasing numbers of these chains. `TBD-3C` was identified as the strongest membrane anchor in the series. ^[activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:129-145, activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:443-455]

This design rationale is tied to the spatial constraints of PDT. The source notes that reactive oxygen species ([[ROS]]) have a short lifetime and limited diffusion range, so photodamage is usually confined to regions where photosensitizers accumulate. It therefore frames membrane localization as a way to direct ROS production to the plasma membrane, a site where damage can promote [[pyroptosis]]. ^[activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:64-92, activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:116-124]

## Evidence for increasing membrane anchoring

Confocal imaging in HeLa cells showed increasing colocalization of the photosensitizers with a membrane tracker, with Pearson correlation coefficients of `0.75` for `TBD-1C`, `0.88` for `TBD-2C`, and `0.95` for `TBD-3C`. The study also notes that `TBD-1C` showed clearly observable cytoplasmic fluorescence, whereas `TBD-3C` overlapped well with the membrane signal, supporting the conclusion that added cationic chains improved membrane anchoring. ^[activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:189-219]

Additional imaging and flow-cytometry analysis across 4T1, HeLa, and C6 cells showed that all three compounds labeled cancer-cell membranes, but `TBD-3C` labeled them more strongly than `TBD-1C` and `TBD-2C`. Its membrane labeling also increased over time, with successful membrane staining achieved within 30 minutes. ^[activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:220-238]

## Relationship to PDT killing efficiency

The three compounds had similar ROS-generation capacity in an ABDA assay, and the paper states that differences in charge number did not produce a meaningful distinction in ROS-generating ability. Even so, under irradiation, `TBD-3C` showed the strongest cancer-cell killing in 4T1, HeLa, and C6 cells, with lower `IC50` values than `TBD-1C` and `TBD-2C`. The authors attribute this stronger ablation effect to superior membrane anchoring rather than to substantially higher intrinsic ROS output. ^[activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:174-188, activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:239-258]

## Evidence that pyroptosis increases with anchoring strength

During live-cell and scanning electron microscopy observations after PDT, treated cells showed swelling and bubble-like protrusions on the plasma membrane. The paper identifies these protrusions as pyroptotic bodies and states that pyroptosis was more obvious in cells treated with `TBD-3C` than in cells treated with `TBD-1C` or `TBD-2C`. The source explicitly interprets this pattern as a positive correlation between pyroptosis level and membrane-anchoring ability. ^[activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:259-330]

Biochemical measurements followed the same order. Light-irradiated 4T1 cells showed increasing `LDH` release from `TBD-1C` to `TBD-2C` to `TBD-3C` (`22.1%`, `59.7%`, and `78.7%`, respectively), while dark controls showed negligible release. Similar trends were also reported in HeLa and C6 cells, which the authors use as support for pyroptotic cell death induced by membrane-anchoring PDT. ^[activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:331-344]

Molecular assays also increased in the same direction. Under PDT, the levels of cleaved [[Gasdermin D|GSDMD]] and cleaved [[Caspase-1]] rose, and secretion of the pro-inflammatory cytokines `IL-1beta` and `IL-18` increased. Among the three compounds, `TBD-3C` produced the highest increase in pyroptosis-associated proteins and inflammatory factors, further linking stronger membrane anchoring with stronger pyroptotic signaling. ^[activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:345-398]

## Shift in dominant cell-death mode

The study did not present pyroptosis as the only death pathway. It reports that pyroptosis and [[apoptosis]] coexisted during membrane-targeting PDT with these AIE photosensitizers. However, apoptosis markers moved in the opposite direction: the apoptosis response was highest for `TBD-1C` and lower for `TBD-2C` and `TBD-3C`, whereas pyroptosis markers increased from `TBD-1C` to `TBD-3C`. ^[activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:399-424]

Flow-cytometry results reinforce this shift. After irradiation, the fraction of annexin V/PI double-positive cells, interpreted in the paper as pyroptotic cells, increased from `4.00%` for `TBD-1C` to `34.3%` for `TBD-2C` and `52.5%` for `TBD-3C`. By contrast, annexin V single-positive cells, interpreted as apoptotic cells, decreased from `54.4%` for `TBD-1C` to `8.95%` for `TBD-2C` and `7.71%` for `TBD-3C`. The authors conclude from these combined findings that pyroptosis gradually played a more dominant role as the number of cationic chains increased. ^[activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:425-440]

## Mechanistic interpretation in the source

The proposed mechanism is that stronger membrane anchoring concentrates the photosensitizer at the plasma membrane, enabling more in situ ROS-mediated membrane damage during irradiation. The source states that this membrane damage promotes caspase-1 activation, GSDMD cleavage, pore formation in the membrane, and release of inflammatory cytokines, thereby favoring pyroptosis. In this framework, increasing anchoring strength shifts PDT damage toward a membrane-centered, pyroptosis-associated death program. ^[activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:323-330, activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:380-398, activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:450-463]

## Significance

The source positions this finding as evidence that pyroptosis can be an important and previously underemphasized contributor to photodynamic cancer-cell ablation. It also suggests that photo-activated pyroptosis may offer an alternative therapeutic strategy in settings where cancer cells resist apoptosis, while potentially adding immunostimulatory consequences through release of cell contents and inflammatory cytokines. ^[activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:50-63, activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:146-153, activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:436-463]
