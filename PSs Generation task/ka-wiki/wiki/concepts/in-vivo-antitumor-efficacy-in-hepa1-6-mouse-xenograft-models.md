# In vivo antitumor efficacy in Hepa1-6 mouse xenograft models

In this study, in vivo antitumor efficacy was evaluated using [[Hepa1-6]] mouse hepatoma xenograft models in female 6-week-old [[C57BL6]] mice. The supporting information describes three related subcutaneous models: a primary unilateral tumor model, a bilateral distal tumor model, and a recurrent tumor model, all used to assess the effects of the photodrug [[M-TOP]] with and without light irradiation. ^[microsoft-word-supporting-information-reversion.md:312-317, microsoft-word-supporting-information-reversion.md:317-366]

## Primary subcutaneous tumor model

For the primary subcutaneous carcinoma model, `1 x 10^6` Hepa1-6 cells in `50 uL` PBS were injected into the right flank of each mouse. After 7 days of growth, when tumors reached about `100 mm^3`, mice were randomized into four groups: `PBS`, `Light`, `M-TOP`, and `M-TOP+Light`, with `n = 8` per group. ^[microsoft-word-supporting-information-reversion.md:317-326]

In this model, M-TOP was administered by intratumoral injection at `50 uL` of `1 uM` in PBS to the `M-TOP` and `M-TOP+Light` groups, while control groups received the same volume of PBS. Thirty minutes after injection, only the `Light` and `M-TOP+Light` groups were irradiated with a `640 nm` laser at `150 mW/cm^2` for `10 min`. Intratumoral administration and laser treatment were repeated three times at 2-day intervals. ^[microsoft-word-supporting-information-reversion.md:326-330]

Tumor volume was recorded every 6 days using the formula `Length x Width^2 / 2`. Mice were euthanized when tumor volume reached `2 cm^3` in accordance with animal welfare requirements, or at the end of the 60-day treatment period. At endpoint, tumor weight and photographs were recorded after tumor collection. ^[microsoft-word-supporting-information-reversion.md:331-334]

After treatment, some tumors were processed into single-cell suspensions for immune activation assays, while others were fixed for immunohistochemical analysis. Flow cytometry staining included markers for T cells, myeloid cells, activation state, and regulatory T cells, including `CD3`, `CD4`, `CD8`, `CD11b`, `CD11c`, `CD69`, `CD86`, `CD206`, `CD25`, and `FOXP3`. Separate tumor samples were analyzed by fluorescence microscopy for [[HMGB1]] and [[calreticulin|CRT]] expression. ^[microsoft-word-supporting-information-reversion.md:335-343]

## Bilateral distal tumor model

To examine effects on distant untreated tumors, the study also established a bilateral subcutaneous xenograft model by injecting `1 x 10^6` Hepa1-6 cells in `50 uL` PBS into both the left and right flanks of each mouse. Mice were randomized into four groups with `n = 8` per group, and treatment began after 7 days, when tumors on both flanks had reached approximately `100 mm^3`. ^[microsoft-word-supporting-information-reversion.md:344-348]

In the bilateral model, intratumoral M-TOP injection and laser irradiation used the same dose and exposure conditions as in the unilateral model, but were applied only to the tumor in the left flank. The right-flank tumor remained untreated. Mice were euthanized at the end of the 60-day treatment period, and tumor volumes on both sides were monitored every 6 days during therapy. Tumor weights and photographs from both sides were collected after each treatment course. ^[microsoft-word-supporting-information-reversion.md:348-353]

For immune analysis in the distal tumor model, tumor cell suspensions were stained with `CD11b` and `CD11c` antibodies for flow cytometric analysis. The supporting information also includes immunofluorescence staining of distal tumor tissues for HMGB1 and CRT, indicating that distal-tumor immune or damage-associated readouts were assessed in this model. ^[microsoft-word-supporting-information-reversion.md:357-358, microsoft-word-supporting-information-reversion.md:485-486]

## Recurrent tumor model

A recurrent subcutaneous xenograft model was generated by implanting `1 x 10^6` Hepa1-6 cells into the left flank of C57BL6 mice. After 15 days of growth, when tumors reached `400 mm^3`, about 90% of each tumor was surgically removed. Mice were assigned with `n = 6` per group, and photodynamic therapy was then performed three times every 6 days. ^[microsoft-word-supporting-information-reversion.md:359-363]

In the recurrent model, tumor growth and mouse survival were monitored through the end of therapy at day 54. The tumors were further evaluated for an immunological memory effect by [[flow cytometry]], and serum levels of `IL-6`, `TNF-alpha`, and `IFN-gamma` were measured by [[ELISA]]. ^[microsoft-word-supporting-information-reversion.md:363-366]

## Experimental context

The in vivo efficacy experiments were part of a broader evaluation of a mitochondria-targeting type-I photodrug designed for pyroptotic oncotherapy. The supporting information summarizes the in vivo scope of this work as controllable immunotherapy against primary, distant, and recurrent tumors, in contrast to conventional photodynamic strategies that the table describes as having either no in vivo data or normal PDT responses. ^[microsoft-word-supporting-information-reversion.md:523-599]
