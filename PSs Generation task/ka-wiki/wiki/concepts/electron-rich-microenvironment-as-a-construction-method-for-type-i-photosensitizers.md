# Electron-rich microenvironment as a construction method for type-I photosensitizers

An **electron-rich microenvironment** is presented in the review as one of the three main direct-construction methods for [[type-I photosensitizer|type-I photosensitizers]]. In the review’s framework, direct strategy aims to enhance [[electron transfer]] rather than suppress [[excitation energy transfer]]. More specifically, the authors state that type-I photosensitizer construction can be realized by forming an electron-rich microenvironment, providing an electron-deficient intermediate transmitter, or introducing an enhanced electron-transfer-capacity primitive. ^[the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:20-30, the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:388-416]

## Role in type-I photodynamic therapy

In the type-I mechanism described by the review, the excited triplet photosensitizer can first abstract an electron from the surrounding environment and then transfer that electron to molecular oxygen, producing superoxide radical (`O2·−`). On that basis, the review states that constructing an electron-rich environment is conducive to this electron-transfer pathway. The authors further summarize that an electron-rich environment helps promote electron transfer between the photosensitizer and surrounding molecules, thereby promoting [[reactive oxygen species]] generation and improving type-I [[photodynamic therapy]]. ^[the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:390-405, the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:424-430]

## How the review says it can be built

The review states that an electron-rich microenvironment can be created either by providing an “electron library” or by incorporating heteroatoms or heterocycles. In its summary of direct-strategy methods, it specifically lists bovine serum albumin ([[bovine serum albumin|BSA]]), bispillar[5]arene (`BP5A`), heteroatoms, and heterocyclic rings as ways to form such an environment. ^[the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:416-430, the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:882-888]

## Supramolecular electron-rich substrates

One example in the review uses iodide BODIPY (`G`), described as a traditional type-II photosensitizer, together with the electron-rich substrate `BP5A` to form a supramolecular photosensitizer system (`HG`). The review states that `BP5A` provided an electron-rich environment that enhanced electron transfer, and reports increased `DHE` fluorescence and matching `ESR` evidence for `O2·−` generation. It further explains the proposed mechanism in terms of energy levels: electron-rich `BP5A` acted as an electron donor, transferring electrons to excited `G` to form `G·−`, which then transferred electrons to `O2` to produce `O2·−`. Emission quenching of `G` upon addition of `BP5A` was used as supporting evidence for photoinduced electron transfer. ^[the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:431-448, the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:653-657]

A second example uses [[bovine serum albumin|BSA]] as an “electron reservoir” to construct an electron-rich environment around heat-activated delayed-fluorescence materials. The review says this strategy converted those materials into type-I photosensitizers and that `O2·−` was the predominant ROS in the PS/BSA system. Electrochemical experiments are described as support for BSA’s reservoir role: after BSA was added to the photosensitizer solution, the reduction current gradually decreased over scanning cycles, which the authors interpret as evidence that an electrochemically inactive PS-BSA complex may form. ^[the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:448-459, the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:664-668]

## Heteroatoms and heterocyclic rings

The review presents covalent introduction of heteroatoms or heterocycles as another way to create an electron-rich microenvironment without relying on separate assembled substrates. It states that lone-pair electrons from heteroatoms can enhance [[intersystem crossing]], facilitate electron transfer, and ultimately increase type-I ROS production. ^[the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:460-479]

As one example, the review describes `COi6-4Cl` as a highly efficient type-I ROS reagent produced by introducing electron-rich oxygen and sulfur atoms. According to the review, these atoms significantly improved electron-donating capability, while the molecule’s strong acceptor-donor-acceptor structure promoted HOMO-LUMO separation and gave a `ΔES1-T2` value of `0.27 eV`, supporting efficient intersystem crossing and photodynamic performance. `EPR` data are cited as evidence for free-radical ROS generation. ^[the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:480-495]

The review also describes a heterocyclic-ring strategy in a series of photosensitizers `TPP`, `TPS`, and `TPO`. It states that the presence of electron-rich heterocyclic rings increased the proportion of type-I photosensitizers, with ROS yield and `O2·−` proportion following the order `TPO > TPS > TPP`. Cyclic voltammetry was used to support the claim that the heterocyclic rings increased electron-transfer ability, and the review notes that `TPO` showed a greater tendency to accept electrons. ^[the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:496-512]

In another series, `TPA-OS`, `TPA-T-OS`, and `TPA-2T-OS`, the review attributes stronger type-I behavior to an electron-rich environment created by heterocyclic rings. All three photosensitizers generated ROS under irradiation, and `DMPO` detection showed free-radical signals, with `TPA-2T-OS` displaying more pronounced hydroxyl-radical signals. The review interprets these findings as evidence that enhanced electron-transfer ability in the heterocycle-derived electron-rich environment benefits the type-I proportion. ^[the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:513-541]

## Design considerations and limitations

The review notes that when electron-rich substrates such as `BP5A` and `BSA` are used, successful implementation depends on careful assembly between the photosensitizer and the electron-rich component, including energy-level matching, distance, and interaction. It also states more broadly that direct strategies face a balance problem: although strong electron donors can create an electron-rich microenvironment that promotes photosensitizer electron transfer, they can also lower the oxidation potential of the photosensitizer and weaken its photooxidation ability toward biomolecules in type-I photodynamic therapy. ^[the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:460-476, the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:842-869]

In the review’s concluding outlook, electron-rich microenvironment design is retained as a core part of the direct-strategy toolbox, but not as a complete solution by itself. The authors summarize this method alongside electron-deficient intermediate transmitters and quinone-based electron-transfer primitives, while also noting that type-I and type-II ROS often coexist and that further development of broader material systems and clearer design principles is still needed. ^[the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:878-888, the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:909-918, the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:944-950]
