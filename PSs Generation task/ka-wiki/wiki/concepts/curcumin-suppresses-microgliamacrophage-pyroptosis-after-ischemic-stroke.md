# Curcumin suppresses microglia/macrophage pyroptosis after ischemic stroke

Curcumin was studied as a post-stroke treatment in a mouse model of transient [[middle cerebral artery occlusion]] and in primary microglia exposed to `LPS+ATP` to test whether it can inhibit [[pyroptosis]] in microglia/macrophages after ischemic stroke. The study reports that curcumin reduced microglia/macrophage pyroptosis, improved white matter integrity, and improved sensorimotor outcomes after stroke. ^[omcl1552127-125.md:27-44, omcl1552127-125.md:147-155]

## Study overview

In vivo, ischemic stroke was induced by 60 minutes of MCAO, and mice received curcumin at `150 mg/kg` intraperitoneally immediately after reperfusion and then daily for 7 days. In vitro, primary microglia were treated with curcumin at `12.5 uM` and stimulated with `LPS` followed by `ATP` to induce pyroptosis. ^[omcl1552127-125.md:30-31, omcl1552127-125.md:215-228]

The authors examined pyroptosis-related proteins, including [[NLRP3 inflammasome|NLRP3]], cleaved [[caspase-1]], `GSDMD-N`, `IL-1beta`, and `IL-18`, and also assessed the [[NF-kappaB]] pathway as a potential upstream mechanism. They further tested the role of NLRP3 directly using AAV-based `NLRP3` shRNA in vivo and `NLRP3` siRNA in vitro. ^[omcl1552127-125.md:36-40, omcl1552127-125.md:151-155, omcl1552127-125.md:194-214, omcl1552127-125.md:587-602]

## Effects on microglia/macrophage pyroptosis

After MCAO, curcumin reduced pyroptosis-associated changes in peri-infarct brain tissue. Western blot analysis showed that stroke increased `NLRP3`, cleaved caspase-1, `GSDMD-N`, pro-`IL-1beta`, and `IL-1beta`, while curcumin treatment suppressed these increases 21 days after stroke. ^[omcl1552127-125.md:785-815, omcl1552127-125.md:669-673]

Immunofluorescence provided cell-level evidence in microglia/macrophages. MCAO increased the proportion of `GSDMD+ Iba-1+` and `caspase-1+ Iba-1+` cells in the ipsilateral cortex, corpus callosum, and striatum, and curcumin markedly reduced these populations, supporting suppression of microglia/macrophage pyroptosis in vivo. ^[omcl1552127-125.md:916-934, omcl1552127-125.md:833-841, omcl1552127-125.md:999-1007]

In primary microglia, `LPS+ATP` increased protein expression of `NLRP3`, cleaved caspase-1, `GSDMD-N`, pro-`IL-1beta`, `IL-1beta`, pro-`IL-18`, and `IL-18`, as well as mRNA levels of `NLRP3`, `caspase-1`, `IL-1beta`, and `IL-18`. Curcumin significantly reduced these pyroptosis- and inflammasome-related readouts in vitro. ^[omcl1552127-125.md:935-956, omcl1552127-125.md:1345-1353]

## Mechanistic interpretation

The study links curcumin’s anti-pyroptotic effect to inhibition of the [[NLRP3 inflammasome]]. In both the MCAO model and `LPS+ATP`-stimulated microglia, direct `NLRP3` knockdown reduced cleaved caspase-1, `GSDMD-N`, pro-`IL-1beta`, `IL-1beta`, pro-`IL-18`, and `IL-18`, and it enhanced the inhibitory effects of curcumin on these pyroptosis-related markers. ^[omcl1552127-125.md:957-978, omcl1552127-125.md:1197-1203, omcl1552127-125.md:1627-1634, omcl1552127-125.md:1721-1728]

The paper further identifies suppression of [[NF-kappaB]] signaling as an upstream mechanism. Stroke or `LPS+ATP` increased phosphorylation of `IKKalpha/beta`, `IkappaBalpha`, and `p65`, whereas curcumin reversed these changes in vivo and in vitro. The authors therefore conclude that curcumin suppresses microglial pyroptosis at least partly through `NF-kappaB` suppression and downstream inhibition of `NLRP3` inflammasome activation. ^[omcl1552127-125.md:37-43, omcl1552127-125.md:1655-1666, omcl1552127-125.md:1808-1824, omcl1552127-125.md:2393-2402]

## Relationship to stroke outcomes

Beyond its effects on pyroptosis markers, curcumin reduced infarct volume at 3 days, decreased brain tissue loss at 21 days, improved adhesive-removal test performance at 3, 10, and 21 days, and lessened white matter demyelination in the corpus callosum and striatum. These findings place suppression of microglia/macrophage pyroptosis within a broader neuroprotective response after ischemic stroke. ^[omcl1552127-125.md:31-33, omcl1552127-125.md:469-480, omcl1552127-125.md:747-784]

NLRP3 knockdown alone improved sensorimotor recovery and reduced white matter injury after MCAO, and it further enhanced the protective effects of curcumin. This supports the study’s interpretation that inhibition of `NLRP3`-dependent microglial pyroptosis contributes to better structural and functional recovery after stroke. ^[omcl1552127-125.md:39-41, omcl1552127-125.md:1635-1654, omcl1552127-125.md:1883-1893]

## Conclusion

According to the study, curcumin suppresses microglia/macrophage pyroptosis after ischemic stroke by reducing `NF-kappaB` activation and inhibiting the `NLRP3` inflammasome pathway. In the reported models, this effect was associated with lower proinflammatory signaling, reduced white matter damage, and improved functional recovery. ^[omcl1552127-125.md:41-44, omcl1552127-125.md:2364-2372, omcl1552127-125.md:2475-2481]
