# Cationic-chain-dependent membrane anchoring in TBD-R photosensitizers

Cationic-chain-dependent membrane anchoring in [[TBD-R photosensitizers]] refers to the reported relationship between the number of cationic chains attached to a shared TBD-based photosensitizer scaffold and the resulting ability of the molecule to associate with cancer-cell membranes. In the source study, the authors designed three aggregation-induced emission ([[AIE]]) photosensitizers, `TBD-1C`, `TBD-2C`, and `TBD-3C`, by conjugating the TBD core and a phenyl ring with one, two, or three cationic chains, respectively, specifically to compare membrane anchorage and pyroptosis-activation capability. ^[activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:125-145, activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:155-167]

The study presents the cationic chains as the structural feature that confers membrane anchoring. It further states that cationic pendant groups can improve aqueous solubility and that the number of these groups affects lipid-membrane anchorage capability; within this design, increasing the number of cationic chains significantly enhanced membrane anchoring, with `TBD-3C` identified as the optimized membrane-anchoring photosensitizer. ^[activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:129-145, activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:442-455]

## Molecular design context

All three molecules share the same `TBD` core, described as `1,1,2,2-tetraphenylethene-benzo[c][1,2,5] thiadiazole-2-(diphenyl methylene) malononitrile`, and differ only in the number of appended cationic groups. The source describes the platform as a donor-acceptor-type photosensitizer structure in which the core supports fluorescence turn-on behavior for imaging and photosensitization for [[photodynamic therapy]], while the cationic chains enable insertion of the probes into the cell membrane. ^[activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:133-141, activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:156-173, activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:446-450]

The source also reports that `TBD-1C`, `TBD-2C`, and `TBD-3C` showed broad absorption from 300 to 500 nm, weak red emission centered at 650 nm, typical AIE behavior, and efficient reactive oxygen species ([[ROS]]) generation under irradiation. Their ABDA decomposition rates were similar, and the authors explicitly note that the different charge counts did not produce a meaningful distinction in ROS-generating ability. ^[activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:167-188]

## Evidence for chain-dependent membrane anchoring

Membrane anchoring was examined first in [[HeLa]] cells by confocal microscopy using a membrane tracker. The reported Pearson correlation coefficients between the red fluorescence of the probes and the green membrane stain were `0.75` for `TBD-1C`, `0.88` for `TBD-2C`, and `0.95` for `TBD-3C`, indicating progressively stronger membrane colocalization as the number of cationic chains increased. The source also notes that `TBD-1C` showed clearly observable red fluorescence in the cytoplasm, consistent with weaker membrane restriction than `TBD-3C`. ^[activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:189-219]

This trend was extended across multiple cancer cell lines, including [[4T1]] breast cancer cells, HeLa cells, and `C6` glioblastoma cells. At the same molar concentration, all three probes stained cell membranes, but `TBD-3C` labeled cells more strongly than `TBD-1C` and `TBD-2C` in both microscopy and [[flow cytometry]] analyses. The source further reports time-dependent membrane labeling by `TBD-3C`, with successful membrane staining achieved within 30 minutes. ^[activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:220-238, activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:279-285]

## Functional consequences for photodynamic cell killing

The study links stronger membrane anchoring to more effective membrane-localized photodynamic damage. In cell viability assays under white-light irradiation, `TBD-3C` had lower reported `IC50` values than `TBD-1C` and `TBD-2C` across `4T1`, `HeLa`, and `C6` cells, while dark cytotoxicity was negligible. The authors attribute the stronger ablation effect of `TBD-3C` to its superior membrane-anchoring capacity rather than to a major difference in ROS generation efficiency. ^[activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:239-258]

Real-time imaging and scanning electron microscopy showed that irradiated cells treated with the TBD-R probes swelled and developed bubble-like protrusions on the plasma membrane. These protrusions are described in the source as pyroptotic bodies. Pyroptosis-related morphology was most obvious with `TBD-3C`, and the authors state that the level of pyroptosis was positively correlated with membrane-anchoring ability because stronger membrane accumulation enabled more in situ ROS production at the membrane, increased membrane damage, and promoted the pyroptotic pathway. ^[activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:292-330]

A similar ordering was observed for lactate dehydrogenase ([[LDH]]) release after irradiation in `4T1` cells: `22.1%` for `TBD-1C`, `59.7%` for `TBD-2C`, and `78.7%` for `TBD-3C`, with negligible release in the dark. Comparable LDH trends were also reported in HeLa and C6 cells, supporting the association between stronger membrane anchoring and stronger pyroptosis-linked membrane damage. ^[activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:331-345]

## Relationship to pyroptosis dominance

At the molecular level, photodynamic treatment with TBD-R increased cleaved [[Gasdermin D|GSDMD]] and cleaved [[Caspase-1]], along with secretion of `IL-1β` and `IL-18`, and `TBD-3C` produced the largest increases among the three photosensitizers. The source summarizes the mechanism as ROS generation under light irradiation leading to caspase-1 activation, GSDMD cleavage, GSDMD pore formation on the membrane, and release of pro-inflammatory cytokines. ^[activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:345-398]

The authors also examined [[apoptosis]] markers and report that pyroptosis and apoptosis coexisted during membrane-targeting PDT, but with opposite trends across the series. Flow-cytometric analysis showed that double-positive pyroptotic cells increased from `TBD-1C` to `TBD-3C`, whereas annexin V single-positive apoptotic cells decreased across the same series. The source therefore concludes that pyroptosis gradually became the dominant cell-death pathway as the number of cationic chains increased. ^[activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:399-440]

## Significance in the reported system

Within this study, cationic-chain-dependent membrane anchoring is presented as the central design variable connecting molecular structure to cellular localization, membrane-localized photodamage, and the balance of cell-death pathways. The reported pattern is that adding more cationic chains strengthened membrane anchoring, and stronger anchoring was associated with greater cancer-cell ablation and a stronger shift toward pyroptosis, with `TBD-3C` as the strongest example in the series. ^[activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:142-149, activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:218-219, activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:320-330, activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:434-463]
