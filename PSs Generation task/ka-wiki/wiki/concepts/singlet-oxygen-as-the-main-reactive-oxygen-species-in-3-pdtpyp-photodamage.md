# Singlet oxygen as the main reactive oxygen species in 3-PdTPyP photodamage

In this study of antimicrobial [[photodynamic therapy]] against rapidly growing mycobacteria, the meta-substituted palladium porphyrin `3-PdTPyP` was identified as the more effective photosensitizer, and the authors specifically concluded that [[singlet oxygen]] was the main reactive oxygen species involved in its photodamage process. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:28-38]

## Experimental basis

The authors investigated reactive oxygen species by adding scavengers during `3-PdTPyP` photoinactivation assays. They used ascorbic acid for singlet oxygen (`1O2`), dimethyl sulfoxide for superoxide radical, tert-butanol for hydroxyl radical, and N-acetylcysteine for hydroperoxyl radical, then measured changes in minimum inhibitory concentration under white LED irradiation. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:179-191]

The key result was that mycobacterial susceptibility to `3-PdTPyP` decreased mainly in the presence of ascorbic acid, the singlet-oxygen quencher. In those conditions, the MIC rose from `2.92 ug/mL` to `23.43 ug/mL` for [[Mycolicibacterium fortuitum]] and from `5.85 ug/mL` to `23.43 ug/mL` for [[Mycobacteroides abscessus subsp. massiliense|M. massiliense]]; it also increased from `2.92 ug/mL` to `11.71 ug/mL` for [[Mycobacteroides abscessus subsp. abscessus|M. abscessus]] and from `5.85 ug/mL` to `11.71 ug/mL` for [[Mycolicibacterium smegmatis]]. The paper interprets this loss of activity in the absence of `1O2` as evidence that singlet oxygen was the main species associated with `3-PdTPyP` activity in mycobacteria. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:243-253, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:373-413, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:458-467]

## Interpretation in the paper

In the discussion, the authors state that the ROS assay examined species from both type I and type II photochemical pathways and that `1O2` emerged as the principal species linked to `3-PdTPyP` action against rapidly growing mycobacteria. They further note that removing this species from solution with ascorbic acid reduced photosensitizer activity and increased MIC values. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:458-467]

The paper places this finding in a broader porphyrin context, noting that singlet oxygen is widely recognized as a leading mediator of photodynamic action in microorganisms and is often the main oxygen species produced by porphyrins. It also cites prior work associating porphyrin-based photoactivity against mycobacteria with `1O2`. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:499-505]

## Relationship to photodamage

According to the source, the high reactivity of singlet oxygen enables it to interact with nearby biomolecules and thereby cause irreversible photodamage in microorganisms. The paper states that `1O2` can react with bacterial membrane lipids and proteins, cross the membrane barrier, and attack intracellular targets including DNA bases; it also notes reported oxidation and mutation of guanine-derived bases and DNA breakage associated with singlet oxygen. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:505-515]

This mechanistic interpretation is consistent with the structural damage observed after `3-PdTPyP` treatment. AFM analysis showed that porphyrin plus white light significantly altered mycobacterial morphology, producing wrinkling, thinning, cavities in the cell wall, increased adhesion forces, and altered electrostatic properties, which the authors linked to photodamage of the bacterial envelope. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:283-314, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:516-545]

## Conclusion

The article concludes that `3-PdTPyP` was the most promising photosensitizer in the series and that its photochemical and photobiological behavior led to local ROS production, especially singlet oxygen. In the authors’ account, this singlet oxygen acted as the main photosensitizing species promoting damage to the mycobacterial cell wall and altering nanomechanical and electrostatic adhesion properties. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:571-593]
