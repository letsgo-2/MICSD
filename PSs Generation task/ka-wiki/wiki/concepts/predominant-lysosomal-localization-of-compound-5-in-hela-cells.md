# Predominant lysosomal localization of compound 5 in HeLa cells

In HeLa cells, compound `5` showed a punctate cytoplasmic distribution by fluorescence microscopy and colocalized mainly with lysosomes. The source states that no significant colocalization with mitochondria or any other cellular compartment was evident, indicating that lysosomes were the predominant observed site of intracellular localization under the reported conditions. ^[rscccc3cc44609c-35.md:239-242]

This localization finding was reported in the context of in vitro photodynamic studies of a newly synthesized, water-soluble, non-aggregating zinc phthalocyanine. In the same HeLa-cell system, the compound produced a strong photodynamic effect after red-light irradiation, while showing low dark toxicity, and the authors identified subcellular mechanistic follow-up as a next step. ^[rscccc3cc44609c-35.md:217-236, rscccc3cc44609c-35.md:249-260]

After light exposure, time-lapse microscopy revealed conspicuous morphological changes in HeLa cells loaded with compound `5`. These changes began by the completion of irradiation and continued afterward, including retraction of filopodia, disruption of the cellular monolayer, cellular shrinkage, and eventual formation of cellular debris. ^[rscccc3cc44609c-35.md:242-248]
