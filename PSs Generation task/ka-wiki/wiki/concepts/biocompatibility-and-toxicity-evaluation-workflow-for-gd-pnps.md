# Biocompatibility and toxicity evaluation workflow for Gd-PNPs

The reported evaluation workflow for [[Gd-PNPs]] combines in vitro biocompatibility testing, blood compatibility assessment, and in vivo toxicity studies in zebrafish and mice. In the source, these studies are presented under “Biocompatibility” as a staged assessment of cytotoxicity, hemocompatibility, and whole-animal biotoxicity. ^[2021-chen-gadolinium-porphyrin-polymer-nanotheranostics-for-imaging-guided-pdt-si.md:27-48, 2021-chen-gadolinium-porphyrin-polymer-nanotheranostics-for-imaging-guided-pdt-si.md:55-66]

## Overview

The workflow begins with cell-based cytotoxicity testing in two tumor cell lines, then evaluates interactions with human blood cells through hemolysis and erythrocyte morphology, and finally extends to animal models using zebrafish larvae and Balb/c mice. The zebrafish study measures survival and hatching outcomes after exposure, while the mouse study examines major organs by H&E staining after intravenous administration. ^[2021-chen-gadolinium-porphyrin-polymer-nanotheranostics-for-imaging-guided-pdt-si.md:28-45, 2021-chen-gadolinium-porphyrin-polymer-nanotheranostics-for-imaging-guided-pdt-si.md:46-66]

## 1. In vitro cytotoxicity assessment

Cytotoxicity was evaluated against HeLa cells and CT 26 cells using a standard [[MTT assay]]. Cells were seeded in 96-well plates at 3.0 x 10^3 cells per well, allowed to adhere overnight, and then treated with Gd-PNPs for 24 hours across concentrations of 0, 50, 100, 150, 200, 250, 300, 350, and 400 micrograms per milliliter. Cell viability was then measured by the MTT assay. ^[2021-chen-gadolinium-porphyrin-polymer-nanotheranostics-for-imaging-guided-pdt-si.md:28-32]

## 2. Hemocompatibility evaluation

Hemocompatibility was assessed using human blood cells. A 0.5 mL blood cell suspension at 1.0 x 10^8 cells per mL was incubated with 0.5 mL Gd-PNP suspensions at concentrations from 0 to 400 micrograms per milliliter for 2 hours at room temperature under mild shaking. After centrifugation at 1000 rpm for 10 minutes, the upper supernatants were collected and measured for absorbance at 541 nm to calculate hemolysis percentage. PBS and ultrapure water were used as the negative and positive controls, respectively. ^[2021-chen-gadolinium-porphyrin-polymer-nanotheranostics-for-imaging-guided-pdt-si.md:33-43]

In addition to the hemolysis measurement, the sedimented cells were re-dissolved in PBS for erythrocyte morphology observation. The supplementary figures also include red blood cell morphology after treatment with PBS, Triton X-100, and Gd-PNPs at 200 and 400 micrograms per milliliter for 2 hours. ^[2021-chen-gadolinium-porphyrin-polymer-nanotheranostics-for-imaging-guided-pdt-si.md:44-45, 2021-chen-gadolinium-porphyrin-polymer-nanotheranostics-for-imaging-guided-pdt-si.md:111-113]

## 3. In vivo zebrafish toxicity study

Biotoxicity was evaluated in zebrafish using 6-day-old larvae. The larvae were placed in 24-well plates at 10 to 12 larvae per well and incubated with 2 mL Gd-PNP solutions in E3 medium at 0, 100, 200, and 400 micrograms per milliliter for 8 hours. At developmental stages from 24 to 96 hpf, the number of live embryos was counted to evaluate in vivo cytotoxicity. ^[2021-chen-gadolinium-porphyrin-polymer-nanotheranostics-for-imaging-guided-pdt-si.md:46-57]

Bright-field and fluorescence images of the zebrafish were recorded with a fluorescent microscope under 535 nm excitation. The study calculated both survival rate and hatching rate based on the number of viable or hatched embryos relative to the total number of embryos. Supplementary Figure S7 reports survival-rate and hatching-rate data for untreated zebrafish and for zebrafish treated with 100, 200, and 400 micrograms per milliliter Gd-PNPs. ^[2021-chen-gadolinium-porphyrin-polymer-nanotheranostics-for-imaging-guided-pdt-si.md:56-62, 2021-chen-gadolinium-porphyrin-polymer-nanotheranostics-for-imaging-guided-pdt-si.md:121-122]

## 4. In vivo mouse toxicity study

In vivo toxicity was further evaluated in Balb/c mice weighing 18 to 22 g, with n = 3. After intravenous injection of Gd-PNPs at 0.8 mg mL^-1, mice were anesthetized with 4% chloral hydrate at 6 mL kg^-1 and dissected after 3 days. The main organs were collected for [[H&E staining]], and mice injected with 100 microliters of normal saline served as controls. ^[2021-chen-gadolinium-porphyrin-polymer-nanotheranostics-for-imaging-guided-pdt-si.md:63-66]

## 5. Supporting measurements related to safety

The supplementary material also reports Gd^3+ leakage from Gd-PNPs at different time points and changes in nanoparticle size after incubation in FBS over different periods. These measurements appear in Figure S5A and Figure S6 as additional characterization relevant to evaluating the material’s behavior under biologically relevant conditions. ^[2021-chen-gadolinium-porphyrin-polymer-nanotheranostics-for-imaging-guided-pdt-si.md:111-117]

## Workflow summary

Taken together, the workflow moves from [[in vitro]] cell viability testing, to blood compatibility testing, to [[in vivo]] organism-level evaluation in zebrafish and mice. The reported readouts are cell viability, hemolysis, erythrocyte morphology, zebrafish survival and hatching, fluorescence and bright-field imaging, and mouse organ histology. ^[2021-chen-gadolinium-porphyrin-polymer-nanotheranostics-for-imaging-guided-pdt-si.md:28-66]
