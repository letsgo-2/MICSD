# Phe-BF3-triggered desilylation as an in vivo bioorthogonal release system

**Phe-BF3-triggered desilylation as an in vivo bioorthogonal release system** is a chemical release strategy in which [[Phe-BF3]] enters cells and induces desilylation of a designed silyl-ether-containing linker, thereby releasing a caged small molecule, drug, or protein in living animals. In the source study, the system was developed to operate in tumors and was used both as a general release platform and as a tool to study the immune consequences of [[pyroptosis]]. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:21-40]

## Overview

The system is based on the observation that Phe-BF3, a cancer PET imaging probe, can promote desilylation of a silyl ether built into an ortho-carbamoylmethylene silyl-phenolic ether linker. Desilylation of that linker triggers fragmentation and release of the attached cargo. The authors first demonstrated this design with a coumarin reporter, where release of free coumarin produced fluorescence and provided a direct readout of linker cleavage. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:42-64, a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:1484-1493]

The study positions this chemistry as bioorthogonal in the sense that it functioned in live cells and mice while showing little reactivity toward tested biological interferents. TBSO-coumarin did not react with hydrogen peroxide, glutathione, or other biologically relevant anions in the initial tests, and the optimized TESO-coumarin likewise was not desilylated by tested biological anions or by other tested fluorine-derived drugs. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:57-64, a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:299-314, a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:1530-1547]

## Mechanistic design

The release motif consisted of a silyl-phenolic ether linked to cargo through a carbamate architecture. The proposed mechanism was that Phe-BF3-catalysed desilylation of the silyl ether triggers decarboxylation and consequent release of the attached molecule. This design was used in both antibody-linked and nanoparticle-linked formats. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:50-58, a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:1484-1493]

Linker optimization compared tert-butyldimethylsilyl (TBS), triethylsilyl (TES), and triisopropylsilyl (TIPS) variants. TESO-coumarin was desilylated more efficiently than TBSO-coumarin, whereas TIPSO-coumarin resisted Phe-BF3-triggered desilylation. The authors attributed the different reactivities to steric hindrance and concluded that TES was the best-performing silyl group among those tested for this bioorthogonal system. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:298-314]

At 10 mM, Phe-BF3-induced desilylation was reported to complete instantly, and the authors suggested that the reaction may proceed through direct fluoride transfer rather than through slow generation of free fluoride from Phe-BF3. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:304-309]

## Evidence for operation in live cells

In BGC823 cells transfected with TBSO-coumarin, treatment with Phe-BF3 produced strong coumarin fluorescence, showing that desilylation could occur inside live cells. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:60-64]

For protein release, the authors attached the optimized linker to gold [[nanoparticles]] and generated NP-GFP constructs. Upon treatment with Phe-BF3 or sodium fluoride, TES-linked NP-GFP released GFP completely, whereas TBS-linked NP-GFP showed only partial release. In mammalian cells, Phe-BF3 induced GFP release in HeLa, EMT6, 4T1, and primary bone-marrow-derived macrophage cells, while sodium fluoride did not do so in cells because it is not cell-permeable. Released GFP regained fluorescence because it was no longer quenched by the nanoparticle. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:315-338]

The source also reports that these tested cell types expressed the LAT1 transporter for Phe-BF3, supporting cellular access by the trigger molecule in the in vitro release experiments. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:329-333, a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:1556-1569]

## In vivo tumor-selective release

The system was extended to living mice using two delivery formats: an [[antibody-drug conjugate]] and nanoparticle-protein conjugates. In nude mice bearing HER2-positive BGC823 tumors, a trastuzumab-SEC-MMAE conjugate showed tumor targeting, while [18F]Phe-BF3 also showed tumor uptake and retention. Phe-BF3 concentrations in tumors were higher than in other tissues and higher than the concentration of the antibody conjugate in the tumor, and marked MMAE release in the tumor was detected when both agents were administered to the same mouse. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:65-88, a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:1498-1526]

In the antibody-drug conjugate format, Phe-BF3 incubation caused about 90% MMAE release from the MMAE-SEC-trastuzumab conjugate, supporting efficient triggered uncaging before the in vivo tumor experiments. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:68-73]

In BALB/c mice bearing 4T1 tumors, [18F]Phe-BF3 again showed tumor enrichment, with the remainder rapidly cleared by renal excretion. Nanoparticle-conjugated cargo accumulated in tumors, and although NP-[89Zr]GFP was also enriched in liver, the distributions of NP-GFP and Phe-BF3 overlapped only in the tumor. Using NP-mNeonGreen-NLS as a reporter, about 15% of tumor cells showed nuclear mNeonGreen signal after Phe-BF3 treatment, whereas control tumors showed no signal. The authors concluded that the nanoparticle-based system was compatible with tumor-selective in vivo release. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:339-355]

## Use as a protein-release platform

A central advance of the study was release of a functional protein cargo rather than only a small-molecule reporter or cytotoxin. The authors conjugated a noncovalent GSDMA3(N+C) complex to nanoparticles through the TES ether linker to generate NP-GSDMA3. After incubation with Phe-BF3, GSDMA3(N+C) was released from the nanoparticle and remained capable of forming pores on liposome membranes. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:470-477, a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:1590-1603]

In cultured HeLa, EMT6, 4T1, and primary macrophage cells, combined treatment with NP-GSDMA3 and Phe-BF3 caused membrane enrichment of gasdermin N domains, pyroptotic morphology, and lactate dehydrogenase release. Neither Phe-BF3 alone nor NP-GSDMA3 alone induced pyroptosis, and a pore-forming-deficient GSDMA3 mutant blocked the effect, indicating that release of active gasdermin from the nanoparticle was the relevant functional event. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:477-490]

## In vivo application to pyroptosis and antitumor immunity

The source used this release system to activate gasdermin in tumors and study [[pyroptosis]] in vivo. NP-GSDMA3 and Phe-BF3 showed convergent tumor distribution in 4T1-bearing mice, and three rounds of combined treatment caused major tumor shrinkage in 4T1 and EMT6 models, whereas NP-GSDMA3 alone, Phe-BF3 alone, or mutant NP-GSDMA3 with Phe-BF3 did not. Mice receiving the active combination showed neither weight loss nor major-organ abnormalities in the reported analyses. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:493-516, a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:1661-1679]

Intratumoral treatment produced regression similar to intravenous treatment, supporting the conclusion that the antitumor effect resulted from gasdermin activation at the tumor site. The study also found that only a small fraction of tumor cells were propidium-iodide-positive in vivo, consistent with about 20% pyroptosis observed in cultured 4T1 cells, yet this limited pyroptotic fraction was sufficient to eliminate the full tumor graft. In an inducible mixed-cell model, tumors were completely arrested even when only 10-30% of inoculated cells carried inducible GSDMA3 activation machinery. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:633-652, a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:803-828]

The antitumor effect depended on immunity. Tumor regression was absent in Nu/Nu mice lacking mature T cells, and depletion of either CD4+ or CD8+ T cells blocked regression. Treated tumors showed increased CD3+, CD4+, and CD8+ T-cell infiltration and reduced proportions of FOXP3+ CD4+ regulatory T cells. Single-cell RNA sequencing further showed increases in CD4+, CD8+, natural killer, and M1 macrophage populations, along with reduced monocyte, neutrophil, myeloid-derived suppressor cell, and Arg1-positive M2-like features. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:834-866]

The study also associated pyroptosis-induced immunity with inflammatory mediators. IL-1beta, IL-18, and HMGB1 levels increased in serum and tumors after treatment, and intratumoral antitumor efficacy was severely inhibited by anti-IL-1beta antibody, while anti-IL-18 had a partial effect and anti-HMGB1 had no effect in that assay. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:867-876, a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:1720-1734]

## Relationship to checkpoint blockade

The authors further used the system at a reduced dosing schedule to test whether pyroptosis-associated inflammation could sensitize a poorly responsive tumor to [[anti-PD1 therapy]]. The 4T1 tumor model did not respond to anti-PD1 alone, and one round of NP-GSDMA3 plus Phe-BF3 alone also did not prevent tumor growth. However, when that single round was followed by anti-PD1 treatment, the mice showed marked tumor shrinkage and reduced tumor weight, indicating synergy between pyroptosis-inducing release and checkpoint blockade in this model. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:877-892]

## Significance in the source study

In the source article, Phe-BF3-triggered desilylation is presented as an in vivo-compatible bioorthogonal chemistry platform for controlled release of fluorophores, cytotoxic drugs, and functional proteins at tumors. The study emphasizes both its chemical utility and its biological application, particularly the use of controlled gasdermin release to reveal that limited tumor-cell pyroptosis can trigger strong antitumor immunity and cooperate with checkpoint immunotherapy. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:893-904]
