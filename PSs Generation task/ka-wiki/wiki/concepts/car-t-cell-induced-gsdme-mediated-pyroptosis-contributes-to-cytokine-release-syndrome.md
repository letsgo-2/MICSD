# CAR-T-cell-induced GSDME-mediated pyroptosis contributes to cytokine release syndrome

[[CAR-T]] immunotherapy is described in the source as a new targeted therapy for hematologic malignancies that has achieved good results in recent years. The same source identifies [[cytokine release syndrome|CRS]] as an important side effect of CAR-T therapy and states that its mechanism was not yet clear. ^[pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:645-650]

Within the review’s discussion of [[pyroptosis]] and antitumor immunity, Liu et al. are cited as showing that CAR-T cells release [[Granzyme B]], which can either activate [[caspase-3]] and thereby cleave [[Gasdermin E|GSDME]], or cleave GSDME directly, leading to pyroptosis in target cells. The source presents this GSDME-dependent pyroptotic death as the mechanism linking CAR-T-cell cytotoxic activity to CRS. ^[pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:651-656, pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:773-776]

The broader mechanistic context given in the review is that GSDME is a gasdermin-family protein closely related to pyroptosis, and that cleavage of GSDME generates an N-terminal fragment that forms pores in the cell membrane. The review also notes that granzyme B can cleave GSDME in a manner similar to caspase-3 and induce pyroptosis. ^[pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:195-205, pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:228-230]

The source further states that knockout of GSDME in tumor cells could eliminate CRS. In the review’s interpretation, this finding provides a new reference point for reducing CRS during clinical use of CAR-T therapy. ^[pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:654-656]
