# Cationic porphyrin charge and aqueous solubility govern antimicrobial photodynamic activity

In this study of tetra-cationic palladium(II) porphyrins against rapidly growing mycobacteria, the authors link antimicrobial [[photodynamic therapy]] performance to two related properties: the presence and placement of positive charges on the porphyrin periphery, and the resulting behavior in aqueous media. They argue that cationic substitution improves interaction with negatively charged microbial structures, while better aqueous solubility supports stronger photoactive function. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:420-457]

## Core claim

The discussion states that introducing positive charges at the peripheral pyridine positions of the porphyrin ring considerably increases aqueous solubility and provides more efficient interaction with negatively charged microbial cell structures. The paper further notes that cationic photosensitizers are known to interact with membrane-associated anionic groups in both Gram-negative and Gram-positive bacteria, and proposes a similar interaction with anionic groups in mycobacterial envelopes. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:429-442]

For the mycobacterial system examined here, the authors suggest that the positive charges of the studied photosensitizers tend to bind anionic groups, probably mycolic-acid-associated structures in terminal lipoproteins, altering membrane electrostatic potential and making cells more susceptible to attack by reactive oxygen species. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:437-442]

## Evidence from isomer comparison

The paper compares two isomeric tetra-cationic Pd(II) porphyrins: `4-PdTPyP`, with peripheral complexes at the para position, and `3-PdTPyP`, with peripheral complexes at the meta position. Under dark conditions, both had the same MIC against all tested rapidly growing mycobacterial strains, `93.75 ug/mL (39 uM)`. Under white LED irradiation, however, `4-PdTPyP` showed only a twofold MIC reduction to `46.87 ug/mL (19.5 uM)`, whereas `3-PdTPyP` reached much lower MIC values of `2.92-5.85 ug/mL (1.21-2.43 uM)`. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:223-231, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:267-276]

The authors identify `3-PdTPyP` as the more active photosensitizer and explicitly relate the weaker activity of `4-PdTPyP` to reduced solubility in aqueous media and a greater tendency to aggregate. They also cite higher hydrophobicity for `4-PdTPyP` than for `3-PdTPyP`, with reported log `P_OW` values of `+0.735` and `+0.180`, respectively. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:420-457]

## Role of solubility and aggregation

According to the discussion, many porphyrin derivatives are poorly soluble in water because of their large hydrophobic cores. The authors state that intrinsic hydrophobicity and strong pi-pi interactions favor aggregation in aqueous media, especially at higher concentrations. They further argue that aggregation reduces the porphyrin's ability to generate reactive oxygen species and therefore lowers bacterial photo-oxidation efficiency. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:449-457]

Within this framework, the higher antimicrobial photodynamic activity of the meta isomer is attributed not only to its cationic character but also to its more favorable aqueous behavior relative to the para isomer. The paper also notes that a similar solubility-activity relationship had been reported previously for related platinum porphyrin isomers. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:443-457]

## Relationship to photodynamic mechanism

The study found that `3-PdTPyP` was the most efficient photosensitizer against the tested rapidly growing mycobacterial strains, and ROS-scavenger experiments indicated that [[singlet oxygen]] was the main species associated with its activity. In the presence of ascorbic acid, used here as a singlet-oxygen quencher, MIC values increased substantially for all tested strains, consistent with reduced photodynamic efficacy when singlet oxygen was suppressed. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:35-38, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:244-253, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:373-413]

The discussion connects this ROS-mediated killing mechanism back to charge and solubility: cationic binding is proposed to improve association with the mycobacterial surface, while lower aggregation in aqueous media preserves the photosensitizer's ROS-generating capacity. Together, these properties help explain why the more water-compatible `3-PdTPyP` showed stronger antimicrobial photodynamic action than `4-PdTPyP`. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:425-457, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:458-467]

## Cell-surface effects consistent with the model

Atomic force microscopy showed that treatment with `3-PdTPyP` plus white light significantly altered mycobacterial morphology, causing wrinkling and thinning of bacilli. The same treatment increased adhesion force to `0.4 uN`, compared with `0.14 uN` in untreated dark controls, and was associated with decreased width and increased length of the bacilli. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:283-299, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:475-492]

Electrostatic force microscopy further indicated loss of electrostatic features around the bacterial perimeter after porphyrin treatment under light exposure, along with signs of electrochemical degradation of the cell wall. In the discussion, these findings are interpreted as evidence that `3-PdTPyP` affected both nanomechanical and electrostatic properties of the bacterial surface, which is consistent with the proposed importance of charge-mediated interactions during photoinactivation. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:299-314, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:516-548]

## Significance

The authors conclude that tetra-cationic porphyrins with peripheral palladium(II) complexes have antimycobacterial potential, with the meta-substituted `3-PdTPyP` emerging as the most promising photosensitizer in the series. In this paper, its superior activity is presented as a consequence of favorable photochemical properties together with cationic interaction at the microbial surface and better aqueous solubility, which together support effective ROS-driven damage to mycobacterial cell walls. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:571-593]
