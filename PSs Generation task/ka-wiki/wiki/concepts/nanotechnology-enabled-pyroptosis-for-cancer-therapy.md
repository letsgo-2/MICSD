# Nanotechnology-enabled pyroptosis for cancer therapy

Nanotechnology-enabled pyroptosis for cancer therapy refers to the use of [[nanomaterials]] and [[nanomedicine]] platforms to deliver or activate pyroptosis-inducing agents in tumors, with the goal of killing cancer cells and, in some cases, enhancing anti-tumor immunity. This strategy is framed as a response to the limits of apoptosis-based therapy, especially in tumors with intrinsic or acquired resistance to apoptosis, and as part of a broader effort to exploit [[pyroptosis]] as an additional programmed cell death mode in cancer treatment. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:28-45, cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:261-292]

[[Pyroptosis]] is described in the source as a programmed cell death process characterized by early DNA fragmentation and chromatin condensation, followed by pore formation, cell swelling with large bubbles, membrane rupture, and release of inflammatory mediators such as `IL-1beta` and `IL-18`. Its canonical pathway involves pattern-recognition-receptor-driven inflammasome assembly, recruitment and activation of [[caspase-1]], and cleavage of [[Gasdermin D|GSDMD]], while non-canonical pyroptosis is mediated by caspase-4/5/11. The review also notes additional pyroptotic routes, including [[caspase-3]]-dependent cleavage of [[Gasdermin E|GSDME]] and caspase-8/GSDMD signaling. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:146-206, cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:309-314]

## Why nanotechnology is used

The source identifies several delivery problems for small-molecule pyroptosis inducers, including rapid blood clearance, non-specific biodistribution, low tumor accumulation, and systemic adverse effects at high dose. It presents nanotechnology as a way to improve safety and efficacy by reducing toxicity, increasing bioavailability and specificity, increasing tumor accumulation through the [[enhanced permeability and retention effect|EPR effect]], reducing non-specific interactions, and enabling active targeting through surface modification. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:263-288]

The review further states that smart nanomedicines can carry pyroptotic reagents into tumor cells and then activate them through endogenous or exogenous stimuli, thereby regulating caspase-related pathways that execute pyroptosis. It highlights stimuli-responsive design as a key therapeutic advantage, especially in relation to tumor features such as low pH, altered enzyme activity, and hypoxia, because such designs may improve selective drug release and limit damage to normal tissues. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:283-288, cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:1046-1081]

## Main mechanistic strategies

A recurring theme in the review is the low expression of [[Gasdermin E|GSDME]] in many tumor cells due to hypermethylation of the `DFNA5` gene, which makes caspase-3-dependent pyroptosis harder to initiate and may contribute to poor efficacy and side effects. One strategy is therefore to combine nanocarrier-based chemotherapy with demethylating drugs such as decitabine (`DAC`) to restore GSDME expression and sensitize tumor cells to pyroptosis. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:306-338, cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:1094-1100]

The review also describes nanoplatforms that induce pyroptosis through distinct upstream triggers, including ROS amplification, glucose starvation, ion release, lysosomal disruption, toxin delivery, direct gasdermin release, and innate immune activation. Across these examples, the downstream execution mechanisms include caspase-3/GSDME signaling, caspase-1-dependent pyroptosis, caspase-11- and `NLRP3`-associated pyroptosis, gasdermin activation more broadly, and mixed pyroptosis-apoptosis responses. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:389-415, cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:478-502, cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:514-571, cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:622-661, cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:676-706, cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:717-771, cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:788-829]

## Representative nanoplatforms

In liposome- and polymer-based systems, the review highlights a tumor-targeted nanoliposome carrying cisplatin (`LipoDDP`) combined with DAC. DAC restored GSDME expression by inhibiting `DFNA5` methylation, while `LipoDDP` activated caspase-3-dependent pyroptosis, producing tumor-cell swelling and leakage of cell contents. In vivo, this combination improved tumor suppression, reduced metastasis, increased dendritic-cell maturation, and promoted conversion of naive `CD8+` T cells to central memory `CD8+` T cells, which the authors interpret as evidence of enhanced systemic immune activation. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:321-354]

A biomimetic nanoparticle with a `PLGA` core and cell-membrane shell co-loaded with `DAC` and indocyanine green (`ICG`) is presented as a photoactivated pyroptosis system. According to the review, tumor-targeting and low immunogenicity came from the membrane shell, while low-dose near-infrared irradiation enabled `ICG` to increase cytoplasmic `Ca2+`, promote cytochrome c release, activate caspase-3, and, with DAC-upregulated GSDME, induce pyroptosis. This treatment suppressed both primary and distant tumors, increased `IL-6` and `TNF-alpha` secretion by BMDCs, elevated `CD4+` and `CD8+` T-cell levels, and showed no obvious tissue injury or weight loss in vivo. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:367-444]

The review also describes ROS-responsive `PICsomes` loaded with glucose oxidase (`GOD`) as therapeutic nanoreactors. In tumors, glucose entered the vesicles and was converted into `H2O2`, which increased membrane permeability, reinforced catalytic glucose oxidation, intensified oxidative stress, and promoted glucose starvation. The reported outcome was pyroptotic tumor-cell death associated with oxidative DNA damage, large-bubble morphology, and increased calreticulin and `HMGB1`, with the platform described as capable of inducing immunostimulatory pyroptotic death. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:453-502]

## Metal-oxide and MOF approaches

Among metal-oxide-based systems, the review summarizes `As2O3` nanoparticles built from `mPEG-PLGA-PLL` for [[hepatocellular carcinoma]]. These particles improved stability, circulation, and tumor accumulation; after uptake, released `As2O3` activated caspase-3, triggered GSDME cleavage, and induced pyroptosis. Compared with free `As2O3`, the nanoparticle formulation also reduced expression of `Dnmt1`, `Dnmt3a`, and `Dnmt3b`, increased GSDME-N levels, enhanced antitumor activity, and showed no obvious systemic toxicity by H&E staining. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:503-542]

The review further describes gastrin-functionalized magnetic iron oxide nanoparticles used for magnetic intra-lysosomal hyperthermia (`MILH`). These particles targeted `CCK2R`-expressing tumor cells, accumulated in lysosomes, and upon alternating magnetic field exposure generated ROS through Fenton chemistry, causing lysosomal membrane collapse, cathepsin-B leakage, and caspase-1-mediated pyroptosis rather than caspase-3-dependent apoptosis. The source notes that this strategy did not increase pro-`IL-1beta` expression in the studied `INR1G9-CCK2R` cells, likely because those cells had inherently low `IL-1beta`, but it proposes that more proinflammatory effects could occur in tumor cells with higher basal pro-`IL-1beta`. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:543-598]

A lipid-coated `MIL-100(Fe)` metal-organic framework (`Lip-MOF`) is presented as an ion-delivery approach. After clathrin-mediated uptake and lysosomal degradation, the particles released large amounts of `Fe3+`, causing lysosomal rupture and pyroptosis mediated by caspase activation, gasdermin cleavage, and `IL-1beta` release. The source reports that pyroptosis-dominant death was supported by the effects of the caspase inhibitor `z-YVAD-FMK`, and it suggests that uptake by macrophages could potentially help stimulate anti-tumor immune responses in acidic tumor microenvironments. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:604-661]

## Protein- and oligonucleotide-based systems

For [[colorectal cancer stem cells]], the review discusses the toxin-based nanoparticle `T22-DITOX-H6`, which uses the `CXCR4` ligand `T22` for selective binding to `CXCR4+` cells. The released pseudomonas exotoxin and diphtheria toxin suppressed `eEF-2`, inhibited protein synthesis, and caused pyroptosis mediated by caspase-11 and `NLRP3`. In vivo, the system increased `NLRP3` and caspase-11 expression in `CXCR4+` tumors, significantly inhibited tumor growth, and showed lower systemic toxicity than `5-FU`, suggesting possible utility against apoptosis-resistant and drug-resistant tumor cells. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:662-708]

The review also presents a nanobioorthogonal system, `NP-GSDMA3`, in which [[Gasdermin A3|GSDMA3]] is linked to nanoparticles through a cleavable triethylsilyl ether linker. A tumor-imaging probe, `Phe-BF3`, selectively entered cells and released GSDMA3, which then formed membrane pores and induced pyroptosis. In 4T1 tumors, only about 20% of cells were reported as propidium-iodide-positive, yet the whole tumor could be erased, which the authors interpret as evidence of a substantial immune contribution. The treatment increased `CD4+` T cells, `CD8+` T cells, and natural killer cells, and when combined with anti-`PD1` therapy it inhibited tumor growth, supporting a role for pyroptosis-induced inflammation in cooperation with immune checkpoint blockade. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:709-771]

An oligonucleotide-based example is `SLR20` nanoparticles, which deliver a stem-loop [[RIG-I]] agonist using a pH-responsive amphiphilic diblock copolymer. In breast tumors, activation of `RIG-I` signaling induced a combination of pyroptosis and intrinsic apoptosis, increased tumor immunogenicity, and, when combined with `aPD-L1`, inhibited tumor growth and metastasis. The review reports increased `RIG-I` expression, increased `STAT1` phosphorylation, evidence of tumor-cell death by `Ki67`, `TUNEL`, and `Annexin V` staining, and induction of IFN-beta-inducible chemokines that recruit T lymphocytes. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:772-829]

## Immunotherapy relevance

The review repeatedly connects nanotechnology-enabled pyroptosis with [[cancer immunotherapy]]. Because pyroptotic cells release proinflammatory intracellular contents, the process is presented as a way to activate immune responses that are otherwise suppressed within the tumor microenvironment. Reported immune effects across the cited platforms include release of `IL-1beta`, `HMGB1`, `IL-6`, `TNF-alpha`, and `IFN-g`, along with increased dendritic-cell activation and higher levels of `CD4+` and `CD8+` T cells. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:355-365, cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:393-415, cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:426-444, cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:727-771, cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:799-829]

The source also emphasizes combinatorial use with checkpoint blockade. In the `NP-GSDMA3` and `SLR20` examples, pyroptosis-associated inflammation was reported to work with anti-`PD1` or `aPD-L1` treatment to improve anti-tumor efficacy. More broadly, the review argues that activating diverse signaling pathways together with chemotherapeutic drugs may strengthen systemic immune responses against tumors. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:759-771, cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:803-829, cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:1103-1121]

## Limitations and safety considerations

The review presents pyroptosis as a potentially useful but double-edged anti-cancer mechanism. It states that pyroptosis may help treat apoptosis-resistant cancers, yet it may also damage normal cells and create a microenvironment favorable to tumor development and metastasis. For that reason, the authors argue that cancer-selective control of pyroptosis remains a major requirement. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:1017-1039]

Several practical limitations for nanomedicine design are also noted. These include marginal physiologic differences between tumors and normal tissues, which create a need for ultrasensitive stimulus thresholds; poor delivery into hypoxic regions far from blood vessels; undesirable nanocarrier toxicity to normal organs; and technical issues such as complex synthesis and colloidal instability during circulation. The review suggests tumor-microenvironment-responsive shells, hypoxia-responsive prodrug release, and supramolecular nanomaterials assembled through programmable self-assembly as possible directions to address these constraints. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:1048-1093]

Beyond therapeutic systems, the source includes a substantial toxicology section showing that some nanoparticles can induce pyroptosis in normal cells and tissues, including mesoporous silica nanoparticles in liver, certain metal oxide nanoparticles in phagocytic cells, indium-tin-oxide nanoparticles in macrophages, carbon black nanoparticles, and silver nanoparticles. The review uses these examples to argue that biosafety assessment and toxicity databases are necessary alongside therapeutic development. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:830-851, cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:852-1016, cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:1122-1141]

## Outlook

According to the review, nanotechnology may help overcome several limitations of conventional pyroptosis-inducing agents and expand the use of pyroptosis in cancer treatment through tumor targeting, stimulus responsiveness, and immunotherapy combination strategies. The authors identify demethylation-assisted restoration of GSDME, broader study of other gasdermin-family proteins, and immune-oriented combinations as promising areas, while also stressing the need for more precise tumor selectivity and stronger nanomaterial safety assessment. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:1040-1121, cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:1122-1141]
