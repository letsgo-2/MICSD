# 3-PdTPyP as the lead antimycobacterial photodynamic sensitizer

`3-PdTPyP` is the meta-substituted tetra-cationic palladium(II) porphyrin identified in this study as the most effective [[photosensitizer]] against rapidly growing [[mycobacteria]] under [[antimicrobial photodynamic therapy]] conditions. The paper compares `3-PdTPyP` with the para isomer `4-PdTPyP` against four standard rapidly growing mycobacterial strains and reports that `3-PdTPyP` showed the strongest light-dependent antimycobacterial activity. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:27-38, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:223-231]

## Study context

The study evaluated tetra-cationic palladium(II) porphyrins against standard strains of [[Mycobacteroides abscessus subsp. abscessus]], [[Mycolicibacterium fortuitum]], [[Mycobacteroides abscessus subsp. massiliense]], and [[Mycolicibacterium smegmatis]]. Photodynamic experiments used white LED light at `400-800 nm`, `50 mW/cm^2`, and a total dose of `270 J/cm^2` over `90 min`, with matched light-only and dark controls. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:28-33, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:132-141, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:143-148]

Under dark conditions, both porphyrins had the same minimum inhibitory concentration (`93.75 ug/mL`, `39 uM`) against all tested strains. Under irradiation, `4-PdTPyP` improved only to `46.87 ug/mL` (`19.5 uM`), while `3-PdTPyP` showed much lower MICs ranging from `2.92` to `5.85 ug/mL` (`1.21` to `2.43 uM`), establishing it as the lead compound in the series. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:223-231, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:267-276]

## Antimycobacterial activity

For `M. fortuitum` and `M. abscessus`, the light-condition MIC of `3-PdTPyP` was `2.92 ug/mL` (`1.21 uM`). For `M. massiliense` and `M. smegmatis`, the MIC was `5.85 ug/mL` (`2.43 uM`). These values were substantially lower than those of `4-PdTPyP`, whose irradiated MIC remained `46.87 ug/mL` (`19.5 uM`) for all four organisms. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:267-276]

Time-kill testing focused on `3-PdTPyP` because it gave the best initial results. At MIC and `2xMIC`, most tested rapidly growing mycobacterial strains were inhibited after two white-light irradiation sessions at `0` and `24 h`. For `M. smegmatis`, bactericidal action at MIC was observed after `48 h`, requiring three irradiation sessions at `0`, `24`, and `48 h`. The `1/2 MIC` condition still reduced growth to some extent relative to the control. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:169-178, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:232-242]

The discussion and conclusion sections emphasize that `3-PdTPyP` could eradicate mycobacterial growth with only two visible-light sessions in most strains tested, which the authors present as a relevant finding given the prolonged and multi-agent treatment typically required for rapidly growing mycobacterial infections. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:549-570, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:571-593]

## Reactive oxygen species mechanism

The ROS-scavenger experiments were designed specifically around `3-PdTPyP` to identify the main reactive species involved in photoinactivation. The study used ascorbic acid for [[singlet oxygen]], DMSO for superoxide radical, `t-BuOH` for hydroxyl radical, and N-acetylcysteine for hydroperoxyl radical. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:179-191]

The strongest loss of activity occurred in the presence of ascorbic acid, indicating that singlet oxygen was the main ROS associated with `3-PdTPyP` activity. In the presence of the singlet-oxygen quencher, the MIC rose to `23.43 ug/mL` (`9.74 uM`) for `M. fortuitum` and `M. massiliense`, and to `11.71 ug/mL` (`4.87 uM`) for `M. abscessus` and `M. smegmatis`. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:243-253, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:373-413]

The authors interpret these results as evidence that `3-PdTPyP` acts mainly through a type II photodynamic process dominated by singlet oxygen. In the conclusion, they state that the meta-substituted isomer led to local ROS production, especially singlet oxygen, which functioned as the principal photosensitizing species in the observed antimycobacterial effect. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:458-467, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:571-580]

## Cell-surface damage and biophysical effects

[[Atomic force microscopy]] was used to examine the effect of `3-PdTPyP` on `M. smegmatis`. The AFM images indicated that porphyrin plus white-light exposure played a key role in the cell-death pathway: untreated bacteria in the dark preserved their morphology, porphyrin in the dark slightly affected morphology, light alone caused similar mild changes, and porphyrin under light significantly altered morphology by wrinkling and thinning the bacilli. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:192-214, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:255-287]

AFM nanomechanical measurements showed increased adhesion forces after `3-PdTPyP` treatment, especially under light. Untreated bacteria in the dark had an adhesion force of `0.14 +/- 0.03 uN`, rising to `0.17 +/- 0.03 uN` with light alone, `0.25 +/- 0.05 uN` with porphyrin in the dark, and `0.4 +/- 0.16 uN` with porphyrin plus light. Under porphyrin treatment, bacterial width decreased while length increased, with the light-plus-porphyrin condition reaching an average length of `3.60 +/- 0.33 um` and width of `0.43 +/- 0.05 um`. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:288-298, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:475-492]

The paper further reports that treated bacteria showed electrostatic changes consistent with surface degradation. After porphyrin treatment followed by light exposure, AFM electric-force maps showed dark spots across the bacterial surface, and the discussion interprets these as evidence of cavities and significant electrochemical degradation of the cell wall together with altered nanomechanical and electrostatic adhesion properties. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:299-314, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:516-548]

## Why `3-PdTPyP` led the series

The discussion attributes the stronger performance of `3-PdTPyP` relative to `4-PdTPyP` to physicochemical differences. The authors state that positive charges on the porphyrin improve solubility in aqueous media and interaction with negatively charged microbial structures, and they specifically associate the lower activity of `4-PdTPyP` with reduced aqueous solubility, greater aggregation tendency, and higher reported `log POW` relative to `3-PdTPyP`. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:429-456]

In the paper’s overall interpretation, `3-PdTPyP` emerged as the most promising lead because it combined the lowest irradiated MICs, bactericidal activity within two irradiation sessions for most strains, singlet-oxygen-centered photochemistry, and measurable structural damage to mycobacterial cell walls. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:35-38, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:420-428, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:571-580]

## Therapeutic relevance

The study frames rapidly growing mycobacterial disease as a setting where alternative localized therapies are needed because diagnosis may be delayed, standard treatment is prolonged, and these organisms do not respond to classic tuberculosis regimens. Within that context, the authors suggest that porphyrin-based [[antimicrobial photodynamic therapy]] may be useful for localized infections, especially skin and soft-tissue disease, and that combining this approach with conventional antimycobacterial therapy could help shorten treatment time and improve adherence. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:98-119, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:549-570, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:580-593]
