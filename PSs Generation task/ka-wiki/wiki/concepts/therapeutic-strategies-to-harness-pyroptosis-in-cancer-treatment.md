# Therapeutic strategies to harness pyroptosis in cancer treatment

[[Pyroptosis]] is being investigated as a way to address two major barriers in cancer treatment: tumor resistance to [[apoptosis]] and the immunosuppressive [[tumor microenvironment]]. The reviewed literature describes pyroptosis as a lytic, inflammatory form of programmed cell death that can promote marked tumor regression by releasing pro-inflammatory cytokines and immunogenic material, thereby supporting immune-cell activation and infiltration. At the same time, the same inflammatory properties can support tumor-promoting microenvironments in some contexts, so therapeutic use is presented as context-dependent rather than universally beneficial. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:13-25, pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:49-58, pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:332-361]

## Therapeutic rationale

The source frames pyroptosis-based therapy as a way to "heat" immune-silent or "cold" tumors. In this model, inducing pyroptosis in tumor cells releases cytokines and immunogenic cellular contents that recruit and activate immune cells, shifting tumors toward a more inflamed, immune-responsive state. The review further notes that tumor killing by infiltrating immune cells may then reinforce pyroptosis-related antitumor immunity in a positive feedback loop. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:526-548, pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:845-850, pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:859-860]

This therapeutic logic depends on the machinery of gasdermin-mediated membrane pore formation. The review identifies canonical and non-canonical inflammasome routes centered on [[Gasdermin D|GSDMD]], as well as alternative pathways such as [[Gasdermin E|GSDME]] cleavage by caspase-3, which can convert an apoptotic signal into pyroptotic death in some treatment settings. The balance between apoptosis and pyroptosis is described as dependent in part on gasdermin expression levels. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:212-241, pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:242-267, pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:287-330]

## Direct induction of pyroptosis in tumor cells

One strategy is direct delivery of functional gasdermin proteins to tumors. The review describes a bioorthogonal nanoparticle system that selectively delivered the mouse gasdermin isoform Gsdma3 into multiple cancer cell types and produced pyroptosis in a fraction of tumor cells. In mouse 4T1 and EMT6 models, repeated treatment caused marked tumor shrinkage, and even pyroptosis in less than 15% of 4T1 tumor cells was reported to eliminate the entire mammary tumor graft. The antitumor effect depended on pore-forming gasdermin function and was lost in T-cell-deficient mice, indicating that direct pyroptosis induction can operate through immune-mediated tumor clearance rather than only direct cell killing. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:549-597]

Another direct approach uses tumor-cell-derived microparticles to deliver cytotoxic drugs that trigger pyroptosis. The review cites methotrexate-loaded microparticles delivered into cholangiocarcinoma cells, where they induced GSDME-mediated pyroptosis, activated patient-derived macrophages, and recruited neutrophils for tumor-directed destruction. In extrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma patients, infusion of this system into the bile duct lumen was associated with neutrophil activation and resolution of biliary obstruction in 25% of patients. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:861-870]

## Repurposing and adapting existing anticancer therapies

The review highlights several existing or emerging anticancer treatments that induce pyroptosis as part of their effect. In melanoma, combined [[BRAF]] and [[MEK]] inhibition was reported to induce GSDME-mediated pyroptosis, alongside immune-cell infiltration, immune activation, and melanoma regression. This places targeted therapy not only as a cytostatic or apoptotic treatment, but also as a potential trigger of inflammatory tumor-cell death. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:871-874, pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:829-837]

Similarly, several small-molecule inhibitors used against KRAS-, EGFR-, or ALK-mutant lung cancers were found to induce pyroptotic death through caspase-3-mediated GSDME cleavage after activation of the mitochondrial intrinsic apoptosis pathway. The review interprets this as evidence that apoptosis and pyroptosis can be co-regulated, and suggests that engaging pyroptosis may improve the efficacy of targeted therapies, although the effect is reduced when apoptotic function remains intact. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:883-891]

The source also summarizes other drug-based strategies that appear to push cancer cells toward pyroptosis. Metformin was reported to inhibit cancer-cell proliferation by indirectly activating pyroptosis through caspase-3, via mitochondrial dysfunction, AMPK/SIRT1/NF-kappaB signaling, Bax accumulation, cytochrome c release, and subsequent GSDME cleavage. In breast cancer cells, a [[RIG-I]] agonist triggered the extrinsic apoptosis pathway together with pyroptosis, activated STAT1 and NF-kappaB, and upregulated lymphocyte-recruiting chemokines; in mice, this was accompanied by reduced metastasis and tumor growth and increased tumor lymphocytes. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:875-882, pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:892-898]

Compounds that promote an apoptosis-to-pyroptosis switch are another therapeutic category discussed in the review. A newly synthesized NF-kappaB inhibitor, `13d`, was reported to arrest cancer cells in G2/M phase and promote this switch, producing a robust in vivo antitumor effect with low toxicity. A second compound, `L61H10`, was also described as likely inducing an NF-kappaB-mediated apoptosis-to-pyroptosis switch and showing therapeutic potential in lung cancer. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:899-906]

## Leveraging immune-cell-mediated pyroptosis

The review also presents strategies that harness the ability of cytotoxic lymphocytes to trigger pyroptosis in cancer cells. [[Natural killer cell|NK cells]] and [[cytotoxic T lymphocyte|cytotoxic T lymphocytes]] can deliver granzymes that cleave gasdermins in target cells, producing pyroptosis through distinct gasdermin-granzyme pairs depending on cellular context. Reported mechanisms include GzmA cleavage of [[Gasdermin B|GSDMB]] and GzmB-driven cleavage or activation linked to GSDME-mediated pyroptosis. These findings support therapy designs that rely on immune effectors not only to kill tumor cells directly, but also to amplify inflammatory antitumor signaling. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:660-699, pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:752-828]

This principle has been extended to engineered cell therapies. The review notes that CAR-T cells can induce GSDME-mediated pyroptosis in leukemic and solid tumor cells through perforin and GzmB release. It also describes engineered NK92 cells bearing a chimeric costimulatory converting receptor that turns inhibitory PD-1 signaling into activating signaling; these CCCR-NK92 cells rapidly killed H1299 lung cancer cells through GSDME-mediated pyroptosis in vitro and significantly inhibited tumor growth in vivo. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:798-828, pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:934-944]

## Combination strategies with immune checkpoint blockade

The source emphasizes combining pyroptosis induction with checkpoint-targeted immunotherapy. In one example, human GSDMB expression alone did not change CT26 tumor growth in mice, but blocking PD-1/PD-L1 interaction slightly reduced control tumor growth and almost completely suppressed the growth of GSDMB-expressing CT26 tumors. Similar findings were reported in a B16-F10 melanoma model, supporting the view that checkpoint blockade can enable cytotoxic lymphocytes to access and exploit pyroptosis-sensitive tumor states more effectively. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:723-751]

More broadly, the review concludes that pyroptosis induction can synergize with [[PD-1]] inhibitors to convert cold tumors into hot tumors. This is presented as one of the most promising combinatorial directions because it addresses a major obstacle to immunotherapy: the immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment and inhibitory receptor signaling. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:930-949]

## Restoring pyroptosis competence in tumors

A recurring obstacle is that many cancers downregulate gasdermin expression or carry non-functional gasdermin variants. The review identifies this as a major limitation for pyroptosis-based treatment because successful induction often depends on intact gasdermin substrates. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:907-910, pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:960-966]

To address this, one strategy uses epigenetic reactivation. The review describes combining decitabine-mediated demethylation of GSDME with nanoliposomes carrying chemotherapy drugs that activate caspase-3. This approach reversed GSDME silencing, induced pyroptosis, suppressed tumor growth, metastasis, and recurrence, and stimulated immune responses through cytokine release. The same section notes, however, that loss-of-function GSDME mutations may limit the usefulness of epigenetic restoration in some patients. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:913-929]

Where restoration of endogenous gasdermins is not feasible, the review suggests that nanotechnology-based delivery of functional gasdermin proteins directly into cancer cells may offer a way around defective or absent endogenous proteins. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:924-929]

## Immune consequences relevant to therapy

Across the studies summarized in the review, successful therapeutic pyroptosis is repeatedly linked to broader remodeling of the tumor immune environment. Reported effects include increased infiltration of CD3-positive T cells, CD4-positive and CD8-positive lymphocytes, NK cells, M1 macrophages, and activated dendritic cells, as well as decreases in regulatory T cells, myeloid-derived suppressor cells, monocytes, neutrophils in some tumor settings, and M2 macrophages. Pyroptosis was also associated with release of IL-1beta, IL-18, HMGB1, ATP, and upregulation of immunostimulatory effector genes. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:568-597, pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:757-797, pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:816-837]

These immune effects are why the review treats pyroptosis as overlapping with [[immunogenic cell death]]. It states that pyroptosis can function as a form of immunogenic cell death in some cases because dying tumor cells provide both antigenic and adjuvant signals that amplify adaptive immunity. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:526-546, pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:793-797]

## Limitations and safety considerations

The review consistently warns that pyroptosis is double-edged. Depending on cancer type, genetics, cell type, and duration of induction, pyroptosis-related components can either suppress tumors or support tumor progression, metastasis, therapeutic resistance, and immunosuppressive microenvironments. This means pyroptosis cannot be treated as uniformly anticancer across all settings. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:205-211, pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:332-361, pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:417-425]

Safety is another concern. The review cites cytokine release syndrome in CAR-T-cell settings as an adverse effect linked to GSDME-facilitated pyroptosis and argues that future clinical use will likely work best in combination regimens tailored to specific patients and tumor types rather than as a one-size-fits-all intervention. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:798-828, pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:950-973]

## Overall outlook

The review presents pyroptosis as a promising therapeutic lever for cancer treatment because it can bypass apoptosis resistance, inflame otherwise immune-silent tumors, and strengthen antitumor immunity. The most developed strategies include direct gasdermin delivery, pyroptosis-inducing drug and nanoparticle systems, targeted therapies and small molecules that trigger gasdermin cleavage, epigenetic restoration of gasdermin expression, engineered immune-cell therapies, and combinations with checkpoint blockade. The source concludes that progress in molecular, genetic, epigenetic, and delivery technologies may make these strategies increasingly practical within precision oncology. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:49-58, pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:838-949, pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:950-973]
