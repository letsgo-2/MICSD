# Crosstalk between pyroptosis and other programmed cell death pathways

[[Pyroptosis]] is a form of programmed cell death (PCD), and the source frames its interaction with other PCD pathways as a major current and future research focus. In keyword clustering, pyroptosis research is linked not only with inflammasome signaling and [[Gasdermin D]] but also with [[apoptosis]], [[autophagy]], [[necroptosis]], and [[ferroptosis]], indicating sustained interest in how these death programs relate to one another. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:38-40, a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:377-397, a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:919-922]

## Overview

The source divides cell death into programmed and non-programmed forms. Programmed cell death includes [[apoptosis]], [[autophagy]], [[pyroptosis]], [[necroptosis]], and [[ferroptosis]], whereas necrosis is listed as non-programmed. It further states that these pathways do not act in isolation and that multiple lines of evidence show interconnection between different programmed cell death pathways at several levels. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:760-770]

Within the pyroptosis literature, apoptosis is one of the most frequent associated keywords, and necroptosis and ferroptosis also appear in keyword analyses and clustering results. The source therefore presents crosstalk with other death pathways as part of the field’s core knowledge structure rather than a peripheral topic. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:377-397, a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:456-467, a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:571-583]

## Pyroptosis and apoptosis

The source describes bidirectional crosstalk between [[pyroptosis]] and [[apoptosis]]. It states that pyroptotic stimuli can activate apoptosis through multiple mechanisms, while apoptotic stimuli can avoid or even inactivate pyroptosis. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:768-784]

One example involves DPP8/9 inhibition in monocytes and macrophages. According to the source, DPP8/9 inhibitors selectively induced caspase-1-dependent pyroptosis, but in the absence of [[Gasdermin D|GSDMD]], active caspase-1 instead induced apoptosis through caspase-3/7. The same study also found that caspase-3/7 and inflammatory caspases cleave GSDMD at different sites, and that caspase-3/7 cleavage can inactivate GSDMD, thereby specifically blocking pyroptosis. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:773-784]

The source also highlights a route from apoptotic machinery into pyroptosis through [[caspase-8]]. In pathogen-triggered settings, caspase-8-mediated cell death was reported to engage the inflammatory GSDMD pathway, and after *Yersinia* infection of macrophages, caspase-8 cleaved GSDMD and triggered pyroptosis. A related study cited in the source found that during TAK1 inhibition, caspase-8 activation cleaved GSDMD in mouse macrophages, producing pyroptosis; when GSDMD was absent and membrane rupture was delayed, the death morphology shifted to apoptosis. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:785-796]

A further connection between apoptosis and pyroptosis involves [[Gasdermin E|GSDME]]. The source states that GSDME can be cleaved by caspase-3, with a cleavage outcome very similar to caspase-1 cleavage of GSDMD, releasing an N-terminal pore-forming fragment that induces pyroptosis. Because caspase-3 is also a central apoptotic caspase, this finding links apoptotic execution machinery to pyroptotic membrane pore formation. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:810-815]

The same source notes a context-dependent consequence of this mechanism in chemotherapy. Due to DNA methylation-mediated silencing, GSDME is not expressed in cancer cells but is highly expressed in many normal tissues. After chemotherapy activates caspase-3, normal cells can undergo GSDME-dependent pyroptosis, whereas cancer cells lacking GSDME die by apoptosis; GSDME-deficient mice were protected from several chemotherapy-associated toxicities, suggesting that pyroptosis in normal tissues may contribute to treatment side effects. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:816-829]

## Pyroptosis, necroptosis, and pathway switching

The source presents [[caspase-8]] as an important regulator connecting [[apoptosis]], [[necroptosis]], and [[pyroptosis]]. It cites work suggesting that caspase-8 activity functions as a key switch that determines cell fate by enabling crosstalk among these pathways. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:796-803]

Specifically, enzyme-inactive caspase-8 could not induce apoptosis and instead mainly drove lytic death through a necroptosis mechanism. When necroptosis was blocked, however, inactive caspase-8 induced pyroptosis, leading to a severe inflammatory reaction. The source uses these observations to argue that strict boundaries do not exist between apoptotic and inflammatory caspases in downstream regulation, and that pathogens and their secretion-system components may affect several signaling proteins at once and engage multiple death pathways simultaneously. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:799-808]

## Broader landscape of programmed cell death crosstalk

Beyond apoptosis and necroptosis, the source places [[autophagy]] and [[ferroptosis]] within the same programmed cell death landscape as pyroptosis. In its keyword clustering, apoptosis, autophagy, necrosis, and ferroptosis group together as a major research cluster associated with pyroptosis, reflecting broad interest in comparative and interconnected death-pathway biology. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:384-397]

The article does not provide detailed mechanistic discussion for pyroptosis crosstalk with autophagy or ferroptosis in the cited section, but it does identify the relationship between pyroptosis and other forms of programmed cell death as an active area of study and a likely future direction for the field. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:38-40, a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:906-922]

## Research significance

The source emphasizes that understanding crosstalk among programmed cell death pathways has clinical implications. It states that this knowledge may help clinical diagnosis and treatment, and it also illustrates how pathway switching can shape therapeutic effects and toxicities, as in chemotherapy-induced GSDME-dependent pyroptosis in normal tissues. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:809-829]

More broadly, the article concludes that the mechanism of pyroptosis, its crosstalk with other forms of programmed cell death, and its roles in disease together represent one of the central value areas of current pyroptosis research. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:905-922]
