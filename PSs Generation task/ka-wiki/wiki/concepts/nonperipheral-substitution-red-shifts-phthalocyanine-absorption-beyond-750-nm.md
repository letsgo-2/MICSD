# Nonperipheral substitution red-shifts phthalocyanine absorption beyond 750 nm

Nonperipheral substitution red-shifts phthalocyanine absorption beyond 750 nm refers to the observation that moving substituents on the [[phthalocyanine]] macrocycle from peripheral (`beta`) to nonperipheral (`alpha`) positions can shift the main Q-band absorption markedly to longer wavelengths. In the cited study, this design change was applied to octacationic zinc phthalocyanines bearing `[(triethylammonio)ethyl]sulfanyl` substituents, with the nonperipheral analogue identified as especially favorable for far-red absorption relevant to [[photodynamic therapy]]. ^[jm5014852-114.md:63-75, jm5014852-114.md:87-95, jm5014852-114.md:140-146]

The paper states that substituent position on the phthalocyanine ring substantially influences the wavelength of the main absorption band. In DMF, the Q-band maxima were `704 nm` for the peripherally substituted `1Zn-Et` and `756 nm` for the nonperipherally substituted `2Zn-Et`, while the aza analogue `3Zn-Et` absorbed at `656 nm`. The authors explicitly note that moving substituents from peripheral to nonperipheral positions produces a substantial bathochromic shift, giving a red-shift of more than `50 nm` for `2Zn-Et` relative to `1Zn-Et`. ^[jm5014852-114.md:87-90, jm5014852-114.md:140-153]

From the standpoint of optical performance, `2Zn-Et` was highlighted as particularly interesting because its Q-band maximum lies above `750 nm`, which the authors describe as highly advantageous for PDT. The study frames this as important because light penetration in tissue increases at longer wavelengths, especially close to or above `700 nm`, and concludes that `2Zn-Et` is an attractive candidate for further development because it absorbs strongly at wavelengths over `750 nm`, which are more permeable to activating light. ^[jm5014852-114.md:63-68, jm5014852-114.md:144-146, jm5014852-114.md:679-683]

The same comparison also showed that nonperipheral substitution affected more than wavelength alone. The nonperipherally substituted zinc derivative `2Zn-Et` was essentially nonaggregated in water, and the authors state that moving substituents to nonperipheral positions significantly reduced aggregation tendency. In addition, nonperipheral substitution favored singlet oxygen production: `2Zn-Et` had a reported `PhiDelta` of `0.91`, compared with `0.68` for the peripheral analogue `1Zn-Et`. ^[jm5014852-114.md:154-185, jm5014852-114.md:198-206, jm5014852-114.md:225-231]

In the biological evaluation, the zinc complexes were phototoxic in the order `2Zn-Et > 1Zn-Et > 3Zn-Et`, consistent with their singlet oxygen production. For HeLa cells, the reported `IC50` values were `0.31 uM` for `2Zn-Et` and `0.54 uM` for `1Zn-Et`, while both compounds were selected for further mechanistic study because of favorable therapeutic ratios. This places the >`750 nm` absorption of the nonperipheral zinc phthalocyanine alongside strong photodynamic activity, though the page’s central point is the wavelength shift itself. ^[jm5014852-114.md:290-313, jm5014852-114.md:322-330, jm5014852-114.md:364-377]
