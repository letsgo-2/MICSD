# Concurrent mitochondria-dependent apoptosis and pyroptosis induced by benzimidazoles

Benzimidazoles such as flubendazole, mebendazole, and fenbendazole were reported to inhibit proliferation of human glioblastoma cells and to induce two forms of programmed cell death at the same time: pyroptosis and mitochondria-dependent apoptosis. The paper’s overall conclusion is that these compounds may be promising candidates for GBM treatment because they arrest the cell cycle and trigger concurrent apoptotic and pyroptotic death. ^[benzimidazoles-induce-concurrent-apoptosis-and-pyroptosis-of-human-glioblastoma-cells-via-arresting-cell-cycle.md:20-31, benzimidazoles-induce-concurrent-apoptosis-and-pyroptosis-of-human-glioblastoma-cells-via-arresting-cell-cycle.md:750-755]

## What was studied

The study tested benzimidazole compounds against U87 and U251 human glioblastoma cells, with flubendazole, mebendazole, and fenbendazole showing potent, dose-dependent anti-GBM activity. Only the benzimidazole class was active among the azole compounds screened. ^[benzimidazoles-induce-concurrent-apoptosis-and-pyroptosis-of-human-glioblastoma-cells-via-arresting-cell-cycle.md:19-31, benzimidazoles-induce-concurrent-apoptosis-and-pyroptosis-of-human-glioblastoma-cells-via-arresting-cell-cycle.md:387-406]

## Cell-cycle arrest

Benzimidazoles dose-dependently arrested GBM cells in the G2/M phase. The study links this effect to the [[P53/P21/cyclin B1 pathway]], with flubendazole increasing P53 and P21 and decreasing cyclin B1 expression. ^[benzimidazoles-induce-concurrent-apoptosis-and-pyroptosis-of-human-glioblastoma-cells-via-arresting-cell-cycle.md:24-25, benzimidazoles-induce-concurrent-apoptosis-and-pyroptosis-of-human-glioblastoma-cells-via-arresting-cell-cycle.md:455-471]

## Pyroptosis

The compounds triggered pyroptosis through the [[NF-κB/NLRP3/GSDMD pathway]]. Supporting observations included balloon-like membrane protrusions, increased LDH release, increased NLRP3 and caspase-1 expression, increased GSDMD-N, and increased nuclear translocation of NF-κB. The authors describe this as benzimidazole-induced pyroptosis of GBM cells. ^[benzimidazoles-induce-concurrent-apoptosis-and-pyroptosis-of-human-glioblastoma-cells-via-arresting-cell-cycle.md:25-26, benzimidazoles-induce-concurrent-apoptosis-and-pyroptosis-of-human-glioblastoma-cells-via-arresting-cell-cycle.md:512-597]

## Mitochondria-dependent apoptosis

Alongside pyroptosis, benzimidazoles also induced mitochondria-dependent apoptosis. The study reports reduced mitochondrial membrane potential, decreased Bcl-2 and Bcl-xL, increased Bad and Bim, and activation of apoptotic effectors including cleaved PARP, cleaved caspase-3, and cleaved caspase-7. ^[benzimidazoles-induce-concurrent-apoptosis-and-pyroptosis-of-human-glioblastoma-cells-via-arresting-cell-cycle.md:26-26, benzimidazoles-induce-concurrent-apoptosis-and-pyroptosis-of-human-glioblastoma-cells-via-arresting-cell-cycle.md:598-615]

## Evidence for both death programs

The pan-caspase inhibitor Z-VAD-FMK blocked flubendazole-induced cytotoxicity, altered morphology, and the annexin V/PI patterns associated with apoptosis and pyroptosis. The authors used this to support the claim that benzimidazoles induce concurrent apoptotic and pyroptotic cell death rather than a single death mode. ^[benzimidazoles-induce-concurrent-apoptosis-and-pyroptosis-of-human-glioblastoma-cells-via-arresting-cell-cycle.md:627-644]

## In vivo result

In a nude mouse U87 xenograft model, flubendazole suppressed tumor growth in a dose-dependent manner without obvious adverse effects. ^[benzimidazoles-induce-concurrent-apoptosis-and-pyroptosis-of-human-glioblastoma-cells-via-arresting-cell-cycle.md:26-30, benzimidazoles-induce-concurrent-apoptosis-and-pyroptosis-of-human-glioblastoma-cells-via-arresting-cell-cycle.md:645-665]
