# Limited pyroptosis fraction can eradicate whole 4T1 tumours

“Limited pyroptosis fraction can eradicate whole 4T1 tumours” refers to the finding that inducing [[Pyroptosis]] in only a minority of tumour cells was sufficient to eliminate an entire [[4T1]] mammary tumour graft in mice. In this study, a bioorthogonal delivery system was used to activate gasdermin-mediated pyroptosis selectively in tumour cells, and the authors reported that pyroptosis in less than 15% of tumour cells could clear the whole tumour. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:26-41]

## Experimental basis

The study combined tumour-targeting nanoparticle delivery with a Phe-BF3-triggered bioorthogonal release system. Phe-BF3 accumulated in 4T1 tumours, nanoparticle-conjugated cargo also reached the tumour, and the overlap of these distributions enabled selective intratumoural release of linked proteins. Using a nuclear mNeonGreen reporter, the authors estimated that about 15% of tumour cells in Phe-BF3-treated tumours showed evidence of release activity. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:339-355]

To test pyroptosis directly, the authors conjugated the noncovalent [[Gasdermin|GSDMA3]](N+C) complex to nanoparticles and released it with Phe-BF3. In cultured 4T1 cells, combined treatment with NP–GSDMA3 and Phe-BF3 produced pyroptotic morphology, gasdermin N-domain membrane enrichment, and measurable cell death, whereas either component alone or a pore-forming-deficient mutant control did not. About 20% of treated 4T1 cells underwent pyroptosis in vitro. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:470-490]

## Effect in 4T1 tumours

In BALB/c mice bearing subcutaneous 4T1 tumours, tumour volume increased by about 20-fold over two weeks in PBS-treated controls. By contrast, three rounds of NP–GSDMA3 plus Phe-BF3 treatment caused marked tumour shrinkage, and tumour burden became negligible by day 25. Control treatments with NP–GSDMA3 alone, Phe-BF3 alone, or NP–GSDMA3(mut) plus Phe-BF3 did not produce tumour shrinkage. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:502-510]

Intratumoural administration of NP–GSDMA3 with Phe-BF3 caused the same regression as intravenous administration, supporting the conclusion that the anti-tumour effect depended on gasdermin activation at the tumour site. Tumours from treated mice contained more propidium-iodide-positive pyroptotic cells than controls, but these positive cells represented only a small fraction of the total tumour-cell population, consistent with the approximately 20% pyroptosis seen in cultured 4T1 cells. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:633-643]

The authors further tested fractional pyroptosis using a mixed-cell graft model. They generated doxycycline-inducible 4T1-GSDMA3 dox-on cells and mixed them with normal 4T1 cells before implantation. Intratumoural doxycycline completely stopped tumour growth even when only 10% or 30% of the inoculated cells were the inducible pyroptosis-competent population, whereas tumours with 0% inducible cells did not show the same response. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:643-650, a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:803-810]

## Immune dependence

The study attributed whole-tumour eradication by limited pyroptosis to anti-tumour immunity rather than direct killing alone. In athymic Nu/Nu mice lacking mature T cells, NP–GSDMA3 plus Phe-BF3 did not cause tumour regression. Likewise, depletion of either CD4+ or CD8+ T cells blocked the tumour regression seen in immunocompetent mice. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:834-846]

Consistent with this immune mechanism, treated 4T1 tumours showed increased infiltration by CD3+ T cells, including both CD4+ and CD8+ populations, while the fraction of FOXP3+ CD4+ regulatory T cells decreased. Single-cell RNA sequencing of tumour-infiltrating CD45+ cells also showed increased CD4+, CD8+, natural killer, and M1 macrophage populations, alongside reduced monocyte, neutrophil, myeloid-derived suppressor cell, and Arg1-positive M2-like features. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:836-859]

## Inflammatory mediators and checkpoint sensitization

Serum and tumour levels of IL-1beta and IL-18 increased after NP–GSDMA3 plus Phe-BF3 treatment, and HMGB1 levels also rose. Neutralizing IL-1beta severely inhibited the tumour regression induced by treatment, while anti-IL-18 had a partial effect and anti-HMGB1 had no effect, leading the authors to conclude that IL-1beta is required for the anti-tumour immunity triggered by tumour-cell pyroptosis. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:867-876]

The 4T1 model was described as a “cold” tumour that did not respond to anti-PD1 alone. A single reduced round of NP–GSDMA3 plus Phe-BF3 also did not prevent tumour growth by itself, but when followed by anti-PD1 treatment it produced marked tumour shrinkage and lower tumour weight. This indicates that limited pyroptosis-driven inflammation could sensitize 4T1 tumours to [[Immune checkpoint blockade|anti-PD1 therapy]]. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:877-892]

## Interpretation

Within this study, the key result is that direct pyroptotic death of only a limited subset of tumour cells was enough to trigger a broader immune response capable of eliminating the full 4T1 tumour. The authors present this as evidence that pyroptosis-induced inflammation can drive robust antitumour immunity and can work together with checkpoint blockade. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:33-41, a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:893-904]
