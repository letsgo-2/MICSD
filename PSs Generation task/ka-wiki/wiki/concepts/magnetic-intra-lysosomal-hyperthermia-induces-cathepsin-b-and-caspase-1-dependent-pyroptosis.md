# Magnetic intra-lysosomal hyperthermia induces cathepsin-B- and caspase-1-dependent pyroptosis

Magnetic intra-lysosomal hyperthermia (MILH) is described in the source as a lysosome-directed cancer therapy strategy triggered by magnetic nanoparticles. In the reviewed study, Gigoux et al. developed magnetic iron oxide nanoparticles coated with PEG-amine and functionalized with gastrin and NHSDY647-PEG1, referred to as Gastrin-MNPs, to selectively target tumor cells and induce pyroptotic cell death after exposure to an alternating magnetic field. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:543-551]

The nanoplatform was designed to target [[CCK2R]]-expressing tumor cells. According to the source, PEG coating was used to prolong blood circulation and improve biocompatibility, while gastrin served as a specific ligand for CCK2R on the tumor-cell surface. Guided by this targeting interaction, Gastrin-MNPs were selectively endocytosed by INR1G9-CCK2R cells and then transported into lysosomes, where they accumulated. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:551-558]

Upon exposure to an alternating magnetic field, the accumulated nanoparticles initiated Fenton reactions and generated reactive oxygen species within cytolysosomes. The source states that this ROS production was the initiating event in MILH, leading to lysosomal membrane collapse and leakage of the lysosomal enzyme [[cathepsin-B]] into the cytosol. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:558-567]

The resulting cell death was reported to proceed through a [[pyroptosis]] pathway rather than a caspase-3-dependent apoptotic pathway. The source says that MILH-triggered death relied on cathepsin-B activity, which activated a [[caspase-1]]-dependent pyroptotic program in these tumor cells. The review summarizes this mechanism as a sequence linking Fenton reaction, cathepsin-B, and caspase-1. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:560-571, cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:594-598]

The source further notes that MILH-induced caspase-1-dependent pyroptosis did not increase pro-IL-1beta expression in INR1G9-CCK2R cells. It attributes this to the inherently low level of IL-1beta in that cell line, while suggesting that MILH could have proinflammatory effects in other tumor cells that express higher levels of pro-IL-1beta. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:582-589]

Similar MILH-induced death was also observed in AGS-CCK2R, HEK-CCK2R, and AR4-2J tumor cell lines, despite their low CCK2R expression, and the source states that these cells died through the same caspase-1-dependent pyroptosis pathway. The review presents these findings as a mechanistic demonstration that MILH can eradicate tumor cells through a lysosome-centered pathway built on ROS generation, cathepsin-B leakage, and caspase-1 activation. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:589-598]
