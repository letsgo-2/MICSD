# DNA minor-groove binding and nucleus targeting by CQPP

`CQPP` is described as a dual-targeted fluorescent probe that can visualize both [[lipid droplets]] and the [[cell nucleus]]. In the study, its nucleus-associated behavior was tied to strong nucleic-acid binding and was used alongside lipid-droplet imaging to monitor cellular changes during ferroptosis. ^[polaritysensitive-ratiometric-fluorescence-probe-for-monitoring-lipid-dropletsnucleus-change-during-ferroptosis.md:49-58, polaritysensitive-ratiometric-fluorescence-probe-for-monitoring-lipid-dropletsnucleus-change-during-ferroptosis.md:160-171]

## Design basis for nucleus targeting

The probe design explicitly incorporated a cationic quinolinium unit because such groups can interact with nucleic acids through electrostatic interaction. The authors further added an extended aromatic system containing a `1-(pyridin-4-yl)piperazine` group to partially mimic [[Hoechst 33342]], with the stated aim of improving nucleic-acid binding and supporting the probe’s ratiometric fluorescence properties. ^[polaritysensitive-ratiometric-fluorescence-probe-for-monitoring-lipid-dropletsnucleus-change-during-ferroptosis.md:148-168]

The resulting `D-pi-A` structure, named `CQPP`, was therefore designed to simultaneously visualize [[lipid droplets]] and the nucleus in separate channels while also responding to microenvironment polarity. The paper presents balanced lipophilicity/hydrophilicity together with high nucleic-acid binding ability as key design factors for achieving this dual-targeting behavior. ^[polaritysensitive-ratiometric-fluorescence-probe-for-monitoring-lipid-dropletsnucleus-change-during-ferroptosis.md:145-147, polaritysensitive-ratiometric-fluorescence-probe-for-monitoring-lipid-dropletsnucleus-change-during-ferroptosis.md:160-171]

## Evidence for DNA binding

`CQPP` showed a strong fluorescence response to DNA in titration experiments with `ds26DNA`. Relative to free `CQPP`, addition of DNA caused the emission peak at `670 nm` to increase by about 11-fold, while the `470 nm` peak remained unchanged. The reported binding constant was `3.97 x 10^6 M^-1`, which the authors interpret as evidence of good affinity for DNA. ^[polaritysensitive-ratiometric-fluorescence-probe-for-monitoring-lipid-dropletsnucleus-change-during-ferroptosis.md:268-274]

The probe also showed a marked fluorescence-lifetime response upon DNA binding. When `0.1` equivalent of `ds26DNA` was added, the fluorescence lifetime increased from `0.63 ns` to `2.10 ns`, indicating that nucleic-acid interaction substantially alters the photophysical behavior of `CQPP`. ^[polaritysensitive-ratiometric-fluorescence-probe-for-monitoring-lipid-dropletsnucleus-change-during-ferroptosis.md:275-278]

## Minor-groove binding mode

Molecular modeling indicated that the DNA response of `CQPP` arises from binding in the minor groove of DNA. The study further states that both electrostatic interactions and hydrogen bonding contribute to the interaction between `CQPP` and DNA. ^[polaritysensitive-ratiometric-fluorescence-probe-for-monitoring-lipid-dropletsnucleus-change-during-ferroptosis.md:278-283]

This proposed binding mode is consistent with the probe’s nucleus-associated fluorescence lifetime in cells. In fluorescence lifetime imaging, the nuclear region corresponded to the longer-lifetime population, and the fitted lifetime for the nucleic-acid-associated region was `2.11 ns`, closely matching the DNA-bound lifetime measured in solution. ^[polaritysensitive-ratiometric-fluorescence-probe-for-monitoring-lipid-dropletsnucleus-change-during-ferroptosis.md:410-419]

## Evidence for nucleus targeting in cells

In live `HT-1080` cells after `2 h` incubation, `CQPP` produced bright red fluorescence in the nucleus, while dotted green fluorescence appeared in the cytoplasm. Co-localization with [[Hoechst 33342]] gave a Pearson correlation coefficient of `0.91`, which the authors used to verify that the red-emitting channel of `CQPP` was localized in the nucleus. ^[polaritysensitive-ratiometric-fluorescence-probe-for-monitoring-lipid-dropletsnucleus-change-during-ferroptosis.md:309-313, polaritysensitive-ratiometric-fluorescence-probe-for-monitoring-lipid-dropletsnucleus-change-during-ferroptosis.md:327-329]

The paper also links nucleus targeting to the probe’s physicochemical balance. Measured octanol/water partition coefficients for `Nile Red`, `CQPP`, and `Hoechst 33342` were `1.75`, `-1.80`, and `-3.15`, respectively, and the authors state that the appropriate lipophilicity/hydrophilicity of `CQPP` contributes to its dual targeting of lipid droplets and nucleus. ^[polaritysensitive-ratiometric-fluorescence-probe-for-monitoring-lipid-dropletsnucleus-change-during-ferroptosis.md:330-339]

## Role of nucleus targeting in ferroptosis imaging

The study highlights nucleus imaging as useful because ferroptosis does not involve the nuclear changes that are characteristic of apoptosis. Using the nucleus as a reference, `CQPP` was applied to distinguish increasing lipid-droplet polarity during ferroptosis from apoptotic nuclear alterations. ^[polaritysensitive-ratiometric-fluorescence-probe-for-monitoring-lipid-dropletsnucleus-change-during-ferroptosis.md:123-143, polaritysensitive-ratiometric-fluorescence-probe-for-monitoring-lipid-dropletsnucleus-change-during-ferroptosis.md:361-365]

In fluorescence lifetime imaging, erastin-induced ferroptosis produced a new short-lifetime population in the lipid-droplet region, while the nuclear lifetime region changed little. By contrast, in cisplatin-treated apoptotic cells, the nucleus showed obvious morphological and lifetime changes. The authors therefore used nuclear lifetime as a stable reference for interpreting lipid-droplet polarity changes during ferroptosis. ^[polaritysensitive-ratiometric-fluorescence-probe-for-monitoring-lipid-dropletsnucleus-change-during-ferroptosis.md:420-435]
