# Pyroptosis-related gene signatures as diagnostic, prognostic, and treatment-response biomarkers in cancer

[[Pyroptosis]]-related genes (PRGs) are presented in the source as a growing biomarker class in cancer, with potential use in diagnosis, prognosis, and prediction of treatment efficacy. The review argues that expression levels of these genes are linked to tumor progression, therapeutic response, and features of the [[tumor microenvironment]], and that they may support more personalized cancer treatment strategies. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:1566-1589]

## Overview

The source frames pyroptosis and [[inflammasome]] signaling as closely connected to tumor immunity and the tumor microenvironment, which is why pyroptosis-related markers are being explored in oncology. Because pyroptosis can exert either procancer or anticancer effects depending on context, PRG-based biomarkers are relevant not only to tumor biology in general but also to how cancers interact with immune infiltration, inflammation, and therapy response. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:534-543, pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:545-565, pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:611-622]

In the review’s conclusion, the authors state that pyroptosis-related genes may help predict both tumor prognosis and treatment efficacy, and that their expression levels could serve as biomarkers in personalized cancer treatment. They also state that use of PRGs as new and accurate diagnostic and prognostic tools for tumors is anticipated, while emphasizing that more in-depth research and clinical validation are still needed. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:1573-1589]

## Biological rationale for biomarker use

Pyroptosis is an inflammatory form of programmed cell death driven by gasdermins, caspases, and inflammatory mediators such as `IL-1beta`, `IL-18`, and `HMGB1`. In cancer, these pathways influence immune-cell recruitment, cytokine production, and remodeling of the tumor microenvironment, which provides a mechanistic basis for why PRG expression patterns could correlate with clinical behavior and treatment response. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:223-231, pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:292-305, pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:611-622]

The review also highlights that inflammasome regulation is important for cancer pathogenesis, progression, and therapeutic response. In particular, inflammasomes such as `NLRP3`, `NLRP1`, `NLRC4`, and `AIM2`, along with gasdermins like `GSDMD`, `GSDME`, and `GSDMB`, are discussed as functionally relevant molecules whose activity can shape antitumor immunity or tumor-promoting inflammation. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:445-458, pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:459-533, pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:683-723]

## Diagnostic and prognostic value

The review explicitly states that PRGs may become new and accurate diagnostic and prognostic markers for tumors. This statement is forward-looking rather than disease-specific, but it places PRG signatures within the broader effort to stratify cancers by molecular features tied to pyroptosis and inflammasome activity. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:1584-1589]

In lung cancer, the review reports that `GSDME`, `caspase 3`, and `caspase 8` are more abundantly expressed in tumor tissue, and that elevated `GSDME` expression is associated with a higher postoperative survival rate and a lower lymph node metastasis rate. It also names `NOD1`, `NLRP1`, `NLRP2`, `NLRP7`, and `caspase 6` as new pyroptosis-related prognostic markers in lung adenocarcinoma, noting that these genes are closely linked to immune infiltration. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:854-866]

In breast cancer, the review states that tumors showed lower levels of `caspase 1`, `IL-1beta`, and `GSDMD` than adjacent normal tissue in one prognostic study. It further reports that `GSDMB` is a significant predictor of poor prognosis and therapeutic response in `HER2`-positive breast cancer, that `GSDME` methylation is a promising biomarker for diagnosis and prognosis, and that a three-gene regression model involving `IL18`, `GSDMC`, and `TIRAP` independently stratified prognosis, with high-risk scores associated with poorer progression, overall survival, and relapse outcomes. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:1067-1088]

In gastric cancer, the review describes two PRG-based prognostic models. One study identified four pyroptosis-related `lncRNAs` (`HAND2-AS1`, `LINC01354`, `RP11-276H19.1`, and `PGM5-AS1`) whose high expression correlated with poor prognosis. Another developed a ten-gene prognostic model comprising `BIRC2`, `CD274`, `IRGM`, `ANXA2`, `GBP5`, `TXNIP`, `POP1`, `GBP1`, `DHX9`, and `TLR2`, and the review states that this pyroptosis-related risk signal could predict prognosis and identify immune-cell infiltration in high-risk tissues. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:1178-1189]

In endometrial cancer, the review reports that a nine-`lncRNA` pyroptosis-related risk model was established from `TCGA` data to forecast prognosis and assess response to immunotherapy. It also notes a four-gene panel (`ELANE`, `GPX4`, `GSDMD`, and `TIRAP`) associated with prognosis and capable of forecasting the immune microenvironment as well as prognosis. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:1313-1324]

In cervical cancer, `CHMP4C`, `GZMB`, and `TNF` are described as differentially expressed pyroptosis-related genes that can predict outcome. In ovarian cancer, the review states that higher `NLRP3` expression is associated with worse overall survival, and it describes additional prognostic frameworks including a 31-PRG analysis with derived prognostic models and risk patterns, as well as a 51-PRG `Pyrsig` score that clarified the immunologic and prognostic relevance of pyroptosis in ovarian cancer. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:1360-1363, pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:1446-1460]

## Treatment-response biomarkers

The review presents PRGs not only as prognostic markers but also as indicators of therapy sensitivity and resistance. Across cancers, the authors repeatedly connect pyroptosis-related molecules to responses to chemotherapy, immunotherapy, and combination regimens. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:1566-1589, pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:1590-1617]

`GSDME` is the clearest treatment-response biomarker discussed in the source. The review states that low `GSDME` levels caused by hypermethylation impair tumor-cell pyroptosis, may stimulate tumor growth, and may enhance resistance to chemotherapy while hindering immunotherapy effectiveness. It further notes that targeting `GSDME` methylation and restoring `GSDME` expression, for example with decitabine (`DAC`), may improve antitumor treatment efficacy. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:1590-1609]

In lung cancer, the review states that pyroptosis contributes to chemotherapy resistance and reports that `GSDME` overexpression increases chemosensitivity, whereas reduced `GSDME` expression is seen in lung cancer tissue. It also notes that higher `GSDME` expression facilitates chemokine release that affects T-cell activation and infiltration, and that cisplatin may offer particular benefit in lung cancers with high `GSDME` expression. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:780-797]

In breast cancer, the review reports that `GSDMB` predicts both poor prognosis and poor therapeutic response in `HER2`-positive disease, and that its expression promotes resistance to trastuzumab. It also states that `GSDME` methylation is a promising biomarker for diagnosis and prognosis, linking epigenetic regulation of a pyroptosis effector to potential clinical stratification. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:1073-1080]

In ovarian cancer, `AIM2` is described as a predictor of antiangiogenic therapy effectiveness, with higher `AIM2` levels in bevacizumab-resistant patients associated with poorer progression-free survival. The review also links `NLRP3` to chemoresistance, noting that its downregulation increases drug sensitivity in gemcitabine-resistant cell lines, while overexpression induces `IL-1beta`, `EMT`, and `Wnt/beta-catenin` signaling. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:1399-1406, pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:1446-1452]

The review additionally links pyroptosis-related risk models to immunotherapy stratification. In endometrial cancer, a nine-`lncRNA` PRG risk model is specifically described as useful for assessing immunotherapy response, while in breast cancer a low-risk PRG score is associated with immune-cell infiltration and immune checkpoints, suggesting a connection between PRG signatures and the immune context relevant to checkpoint-based treatment. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:1081-1086, pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:1313-1316]

## Relationship to the tumor microenvironment

A recurring theme in the source is that the biomarker value of PRGs is tied to the immune microenvironment. The review states that PRGs and inflammasomes influence infiltration by `CD8+` and `CD4+` T cells, `NK` cells, `TAMs`, `MDSCs`, and `Treg` cells, and that some prognostic signatures correlate with immune infiltration or checkpoint-related states. This supports the idea that PRG signatures may function partly as readouts of tumor-immune ecology rather than only tumor-intrinsic death pathways. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:574-606, pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:741-770, pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:861-866, pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:1081-1086, pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:1184-1188, pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:1317-1324]

The review also emphasizes that pyroptosis can convert tumors toward a more immunologically active state. In particular, `GSDME` is described as suppressing tumors by producing `IL-18`, `IL-1beta`, and `HMGB1` and converting “cold” tumors into “hot” tumors, with increased `NK`-cell and `CD8+` T-cell infiltration and improved antitumor immunity. This helps explain why PRG expression can have both prognostic and treatment-response relevance. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:693-721]

## Limitations and current status

The source is explicit that clinical use of PRG signatures remains an evolving area. Although the review describes multiple candidate genes, methylation markers, and multigene or `lncRNA`-based models across tumor types, it also states that more in-depth research is required to define the exact mechanisms by which PRGs affect tumor growth and treatment response, and that clinical trials are needed to validate these genes as predictive biomarkers in cancer patients. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:1578-1587]

Another limitation emphasized in the review is the context dependence of pyroptosis itself. Because pyroptosis and inflammasome signaling can be either tumor-suppressive or tumor-promoting depending on cancer type, microenvironment, and inflammatory state, PRG signatures should be interpreted within disease-specific and therapeutic context rather than assumed to have uniform meaning across cancers. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:545-565, pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:615-621, pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:1618-1635]
