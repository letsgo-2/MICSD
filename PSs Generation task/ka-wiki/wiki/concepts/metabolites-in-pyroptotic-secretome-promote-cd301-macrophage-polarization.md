# Metabolites in pyroptotic secretome promote CD301+ macrophage polarization

The study reports that the [[pyroptotic secretome]] from macrophages undergoing `Pyro−1` pyroptosis promotes polarization of macrophages toward a `CD301+` phenotype associated with tissue repair. In the article’s summary, the authors state that pyroptotic metabolites are linked to immune-cell infiltration into wounds and to polarization toward `CD301+` macrophages, positioning this effect as part of the tissue-repair activity of the secretome. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:50-59]

## Experimental context

To isolate pyroptosis-associated factors independent of `IL-1β` and `IL-1α`, the authors used `Nlrp1b`-transgenic mouse bone-marrow-derived macrophages treated with anthrax lethal toxin, a condition they call `Pyro−1`. In this model, cells undergo pyroptosis without `TLR` priming and without release of `IL-1β` or `IL-1α`, enabling comparison of live, pyroptotic, and apoptotic secretomes generated from related systems. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:120-129, oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:131-143]

Untargeted metabolomics of `Pyro−1` supernatants identified both metabolites unique to the pyroptotic secretome and metabolites shared with the apoptotic secretome but present at higher levels in `Pyro−1` conditions. The authors note that several detected metabolites, including [[spermidine]], [[putrescine]], taurine, methyladenosine, and fumarate, had previously been linked to tissue repair or anti-inflammatory signaling, leading them to propose that metabolites in the pyroptotic secretome contribute to the beneficial wound-healing effects of `Pyro−1` supernatants. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1087-1118]

## Evidence for CD301+ polarization

In ex vivo polarization assays, incubating `M0` target macrophages with `Pyro−1` supernatants for 24 hours increased both the percentage of `CD301+` cells and the per-cell surface level of `CD301`. This effect was also seen with `Pyro−1-18` supernatants, indicating that `IL-18` was largely dispensable for induction of the `CD301+` phenotype. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1502-1508, oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1459-1470]

The source distinguishes this `CD301+` polarization effect from the oxylipin-dependent component of wound repair. Supernatants from aspirin-treated `Pyro−1` cells, which reduced `PGE2`, remained equally effective at inducing `CD301+` macrophages by both frequency and surface-density measures. On that basis, the authors conclude that components other than `PGE2`, such as metabolites, mediate the polarization effect. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1508-1515]

The paper also states that `CD301` induction by `Pyro−1` supernatants required [[Gasdermin D|GSDMD]] pore activity. This places macrophage-polarizing activity downstream of the pyroptotic release process rather than simply reflecting baseline macrophage products. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1513-1515]

## In vivo wound-site findings

In full-thickness skin wounds collected on day 3, treatment with `Pyro−1` supernatants increased total `CD45+` cells and increased `CD45+ CD64+` and `CD45+ F4/80+` macrophage populations per wound area. Within these wound macrophage compartments, the number of `CD301+` cells per unit wound area was markedly increased in `Pyro−1`-treated wounds. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1123-1130, oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1491-1494, oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1515-1522]

The authors further note that `Pyro−1` treatment did not significantly alter the inflammatory marker `Ly6C` within the `CD64+` or `F4/80+` wound populations. They also observed higher `CD301` expression in the `Ly6C`-intermediate `CD64+` population than in the `Ly6C^high` `CD64+` population under `Pyro−1` conditions. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1522-1527]

## Functional relevance

The paper frames `CD301+` wound macrophages as a pro-reparative population. It cites prior work that dermal mononuclear phagocytes expressing `CD301` are associated with wound repair, and notes that `CD301+ CD206+` wound macrophages tend to express `IL-10` and `TGFβ`, which support re-epithelialization and fibroblast recruitment. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1495-1502]

In wounds treated with the `Pyro−1` secretome, the authors detected more `IL-27`-expressing cells within `CD45+ CD64+` and `CD45+ F4/80+` populations. Blocking `IL-27` diminished the wound-healing benefit of `Pyro−1` supernatants, supporting the conclusion that `IL-27`-producing `CD301+` macrophages induced by the pyroptotic secretome significantly enhance wound healing. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1528-1537]

## Interpretation

In the discussion, the authors summarize this mechanism as a metabolite-driven contribution of the `Pyro−1` secretome to wound repair. They state that metabolites in `Pyro−1` supernatants promote wound-site `CD45+ F4/80+ CD301+ IL-27+` macrophages, a subpopulation linked to healing, while oxylipins such as `PGE2` account for only part of the overall repair-associated program. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1556-1602]
