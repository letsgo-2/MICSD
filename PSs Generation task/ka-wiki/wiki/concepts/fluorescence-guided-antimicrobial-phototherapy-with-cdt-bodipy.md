# Fluorescence-guided antimicrobial phototherapy with CDT-BODIPY

Fluorescence-guided antimicrobial phototherapy with [[CDT-BODIPY]] refers to the use of a water-soluble donor-acceptor-donor conjugated oligomer for imaging-guided light-activated killing of microbes. In the source study, CDT-BODIPY combined dual-wavelength fluorescence with synchronous [[photodynamic therapy]] and [[photothermal therapy]], enabling antimicrobial treatment to be guided by optical localization of the targeted microorganisms. ^[intramolecular-charge-transfer-based-conjugated-oligomer-with-fluorescence-efficient-photodynamics-and-photothermal-activities.md:25-29, intramolecular-charge-transfer-based-conjugated-oligomer-with-fluorescence-efficient-photodynamics-and-photothermal-activities.md:115-130]

CDT-BODIPY was designed as a D-A-D structured conjugated oligomer containing two cyclopenta-dithiophene (CDT) donor units and a BODIPY acceptor. The source states that intramolecular charge transfer in this architecture supported balanced emission, reactive oxygen species generation, and photothermal conversion, while quaternary ammonium side chains improved aqueous solubility and promoted affinity for negatively charged microbial surfaces. ^[intramolecular-charge-transfer-based-conjugated-oligomer-with-fluorescence-efficient-photodynamics-and-photothermal-activities.md:16-29, intramolecular-charge-transfer-based-conjugated-oligomer-with-fluorescence-efficient-photodynamics-and-photothermal-activities.md:286-306, intramolecular-charge-transfer-based-conjugated-oligomer-with-fluorescence-efficient-photodynamics-and-photothermal-activities.md:320-326]

## Fluorescence features

The oligomer showed dual emission that supports fluorescence guidance. In water, CDT-BODIPY exhibited blue and red emission peaks at 463 and 730 nm, and the authors note that these emissions provide a convenient basis for fluorescence imaging-guided therapy. They further state that the 730 nm red emission can help avoid biological background fluorescence. ^[intramolecular-charge-transfer-based-conjugated-oligomer-with-fluorescence-efficient-photodynamics-and-photothermal-activities.md:350-358, intramolecular-charge-transfer-based-conjugated-oligomer-with-fluorescence-efficient-photodynamics-and-photothermal-activities.md:394-398]

When microorganisms were stained with CDT-BODIPY and examined by confocal laser scanning microscopy, strong fluorescence was detected in both the blue and red channels for [[Escherichia coli|E. coli]], [[Staphylococcus aureus|S. aureus]], and [[Candida albicans|C. albicans]]. These fluorescence signals overlapped well with the microorganisms, supporting the conclusion that CDT-BODIPY bound efficiently and could be used to precisely locate microbial cells for imaging-guided treatment. ^[intramolecular-charge-transfer-based-conjugated-oligomer-with-fluorescence-efficient-photodynamics-and-photothermal-activities.md:510-543]

## Phototherapeutic activity

Under 660 nm laser irradiation, CDT-BODIPY generated reactive oxygen species, with total ROS increasing over time and depending on both CDT-BODIPY concentration and laser power density. Probe-based measurements and EPR data indicated production of [[singlet oxygen]] and superoxide radical, while no evidence of hydroxyl radical was found; the authors therefore concluded that the photodynamic process likely involved both type I and type II mechanisms. ^[intramolecular-charge-transfer-based-conjugated-oligomer-with-fluorescence-efficient-photodynamics-and-photothermal-activities.md:399-454]

CDT-BODIPY also showed photothermal behavior under 660 nm irradiation. In aqueous solution, temperature rose with both concentration and laser power density, and the measured photothermal conversion efficiency was 33.1%. The source states that the observed temperature increase was sufficient to kill bacteria. ^[intramolecular-charge-transfer-based-conjugated-oligomer-with-fluorescence-efficient-photodynamics-and-photothermal-activities.md:455-476]

The study frames the antimicrobial effect as a combined PDT/PTT process triggered by a single wavelength. According to the authors, the low bandgap of CDT-BODIPY supported photothermal conversion, while the small singlet-triplet energy gap promoted intersystem crossing and ROS generation, allowing one material to deliver both heat and oxidative damage after light exposure. ^[intramolecular-charge-transfer-based-conjugated-oligomer-with-fluorescence-efficient-photodynamics-and-photothermal-activities.md:492-509, intramolecular-charge-transfer-based-conjugated-oligomer-with-fluorescence-efficient-photodynamics-and-photothermal-activities.md:606-617]

## Antimicrobial performance

The antimicrobial experiments tested CDT-BODIPY against [[Escherichia coli|E. coli]], [[Staphylococcus aureus|S. aureus]], and [[Candida albicans|C. albicans]]. Under 660 nm laser irradiation, growth of all three microorganisms was effectively inhibited. The paper reports that more than 95% of microbes were killed even at 4 ug/mL, and inhibition increased to 99% at 10 ug/mL; the minimal bactericidal concentration was about 10 ug/mL for all three organisms. ^[intramolecular-charge-transfer-based-conjugated-oligomer-with-fluorescence-efficient-photodynamics-and-photothermal-activities.md:544-583]

The source also reports some dark toxicity. Without light irradiation, CDT-BODIPY caused moderate toxicity toward E. coli and S. aureus, with less than 50% bacterial death, and nearly 90% growth inhibition of C. albicans at 10 ug/mL. The authors attribute this baseline effect mainly to the quaternary ammonium groups and note that fungal cell-wall differences may contribute to the stronger dark response in C. albicans. ^[intramolecular-charge-transfer-based-conjugated-oligomer-with-fluorescence-efficient-photodynamics-and-photothermal-activities.md:551-574]

## Binding and proposed killing mechanism

The authors expected CDT-BODIPY to bind efficiently to microbial surfaces because many pathogens carry negatively charged cell-wall components, and CDT-BODIPY bears positively charged quaternary ammonium side chains. Confocal imaging supported this binding, and the paper states that close association with microbial cells would help the locally generated ROS and heat act more effectively on the membrane. ^[intramolecular-charge-transfer-based-conjugated-oligomer-with-fluorescence-efficient-photodynamics-and-photothermal-activities.md:510-536]

Scanning electron microscopy showed that untreated microbes had clear shapes and relatively smooth surfaces, whereas CDT-BODIPY plus laser treatment produced deformed cells, collapsed membranes, and visible holes. Based on these observations, the authors propose that CDT-BODIPY first binds to microorganisms, then upon irradiation generates local hyperthermia and toxic ROS that damage membranes and inactivate biomacromolecules; together with the intrinsic effect of the quaternary ammonium groups, this yields broad-spectrum killing. ^[intramolecular-charge-transfer-based-conjugated-oligomer-with-fluorescence-efficient-photodynamics-and-photothermal-activities.md:584-605]

## Significance in the study

In the study's conclusion, CDT-BODIPY is presented as a water-soluble BODIPY-based conjugated oligomer that enables fluorescence-guided antimicrobial phototherapy through single-wavelength-triggered synchronous PDT and PTT. The reported combination of dual emission, microbial binding, ROS generation, photothermal conversion, and broad-spectrum biocidal activity is the basis for describing it as an imaging-guided antimicrobial phototherapeutic platform. ^[intramolecular-charge-transfer-based-conjugated-oligomer-with-fluorescence-efficient-photodynamics-and-photothermal-activities.md:606-626]
