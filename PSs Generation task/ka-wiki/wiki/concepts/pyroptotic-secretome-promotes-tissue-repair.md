# Pyroptotic secretome promotes tissue repair

The pyroptotic secretome was reported in this study to promote tissue repair when released from macrophages undergoing pyroptosis without IL-1β or IL-1α release, a condition the authors call `Pyro−1`. Using this system, the study found that pyroptotic supernatants induced wound-healing-associated programs, enhanced cell migration, accelerated wound closure in vitro, and improved tissue repair in vivo, supporting a repair-promoting role for factors released during [[pyroptosis]] beyond canonical inflammatory cytokines. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:43-59, oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:120-129]

## Experimental context

To isolate effects independent of IL-1β, the authors used a model in which anthrax lethal toxin triggered the [[NLRP1B inflammasome]] in `Nlrp1b`-transgenic mouse bone-marrow-derived macrophages, causing pyroptosis without TLR priming and without IL-1β or IL-1α release. In matched `Casp1/11`-deficient macrophages exposed to the same trigger, the cells underwent apoptosis instead, allowing comparison among live-cell, pyroptotic, and apoptotic secretomes generated under parallel conditions. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:120-143]

When these `Pyro−1` supernatants were added to naive target macrophages, RNA-seq showed notable transcriptional changes without induction of inflammatory cytokines such as `IL-6` or `TNF`. Instead, the upregulated programs were enriched for migration, proliferation, and immune-response categories, including genes previously linked to wound healing and tissue repair such as `Thbs1`, `Vegf`, `Nr4a1`, `S1pr1`, and `Tgm2`. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:144-180]

The repair-associated transcriptional effects were largely independent of `IL-18`, which was present in `Pyro−1` supernatants, because supernatants from `Il18`-deficient pyroptotic macrophages still induced key wound-healing genes. Protein depletion with proteinase K also did not abolish induction of `Thbs1`, `Vegf`, `Nr4a1`, and `Tgm2`, pointing to non-protein mediators in the pyroptotic secretome. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:170-194]

## Oxylipins and metabolites as repair mediators

Bioinformatic analysis of the `Pyro−1` gene signature pointed to upstream lipid-mediated signaling, especially [[Prostaglandin E2|PGE2]]-dependent signaling. Consistent with that prediction, lipidomics showed enrichment of oxylipins, including `PGE2`, in `Pyro−1` supernatants but not in control supernatants or apoptotic supernatants generated with the same trigger. About half of the genes induced by `Pyro−1` supernatants overlapped with genes induced by direct `PGE2` treatment of macrophages, suggesting that `PGE2` accounted for a substantial but incomplete portion of the response. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:195-230]

Untargeted metabolomics further showed that the pyroptotic secretome contained both metabolites unique to `Pyro−1` conditions and metabolites shared with apoptotic secretomes, often at higher levels in `Pyro−1` samples. Some identified metabolites, including spermidine, putrescine, taurine, methyladenosine, and fumarate, had previously been linked to tissue repair or anti-inflammatory signaling, leading the authors to conclude that metabolites likely contribute alongside oxylipins to the beneficial wound-healing effects. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1087-1118]

## Effects on migration and wound closure

In vitro, `Pyro−1` supernatants increased wound closure in primary mouse fibroblast monolayers compared with live-cell or apoptotic supernatants, and this effect remained evident with `Il18`-deficient pyroptotic supernatants. The same supernatants also increased migration of primary fibroblasts and primary macrophages in Transwell assays, matching the migration-associated gene signatures seen in target macrophages. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:231-243]

The study also extended this observation to human myeloid cells. Supernatants from human `CD14+` monocytes driven into pyroptosis with an `NLRC4` agonist accelerated fibroblast wound closure, and `PGE2` was detectable in those human pyroptotic supernatants. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:244-254]

## De novo PGE2 production during pyroptosis

The authors found that `PGE2` became detectable in `Pyro−1` supernatants by 90 minutes after the pyroptotic trigger and increased further by 120 minutes. Pharmacologic inhibition experiments indicated that this `PGE2` was generated de novo during pyroptosis and depended strongly on `COX2` activity, rather than simply reflecting release of pre-existing intracellular stores. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:508-510, oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:731-743, oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:993-1004]

Mechanistically, direct activation of [[caspase-1]] in inducible `THP-1` cells was sufficient to drive `PGE2` release, placing prostaglandin synthesis downstream of caspase-1 activation. Release of `PGE2` also required [[Gasdermin D|GSDMD]] pore activity, because it was lost in `Gsdmd`-deficient macrophages and after pharmacologic inhibition of GSDMD pores with disulfiram. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1007-1028]

## In vivo tissue repair

In a colonic punch-biopsy model in which wound healing depends on localized bioactive `PGE2`, local administration of `Pyro−1` supernatants strongly promoted wound repair even when systemic `COX2` inhibition delayed healing. The treated wounds showed claudin-4-positive wound-associated epithelial cells spanning the wound bed, supporting the in vivo bioactivity of pyroptosis-derived `PGE2`. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1029-1047]

In a full-thickness skin-wound model, intradermal administration of `Pyro−1` supernatants on days 0 to 2 improved wound closure, with the strongest benefit observed on days 2 and 4 during the inflammatory phase of repair. Histologic analysis showed reduced granulation tissue thickness, increased proliferation within the wound bed, and increased revascularization and fibroblast repopulation, even though keratinocyte migration and proliferation in the wound epithelial tongue were not significantly changed. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1048-1071]

Supernatants from aspirin-treated pyroptotic cells, which lacked prostaglandin production, retained only a partial pro-healing effect in the skin model. This partial preservation was consistent with the conclusion that `PGE2` and related oxylipins account for part, but not all, of the repair activity, with additional benefit attributable to other components such as metabolites. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1072-1078, oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1572-1576]

## Immune-cell recruitment and macrophage polarization

During the early inflammatory phase of skin repair, wounds treated with `Pyro−1` supernatants contained increased numbers of `CD45+` immune cells as well as increased `CD45+CD64+` and `CD45+F4/80+` macrophage populations. The authors also observed increases in non-hematopoietic `CD45−` cells, consistent with greater endothelial and myofibroblast presence in the wound bed. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1119-1129, oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1491-1494]

`Pyro−1` supernatants also promoted polarization of macrophages toward a `CD301+` reparative phenotype ex vivo and increased `CD301+` macrophages in wounds in vivo. This effect was largely independent of `IL-18` and also independent of `PGE2`, because aspirin-treated pyroptotic supernatants still induced `CD301`, whereas activity required `GSDMD` pores. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1502-1522]

The study further linked this macrophage response to `IL-27`. Wounds treated with `Pyro−1` supernatants contained more `IL-27`-expressing cells within `CD64+` and `F4/80+` macrophage populations, and blocking `IL-27` reduced the wound-healing benefit of `Pyro−1` supernatants. The authors therefore identify `IL-27`-producing `CD301+` macrophages as an important downstream component of pyroptotic-secretome-mediated repair. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1528-1537]

## Interpretation

The study argues that pyroptosis is not solely tissue damaging. In this `Pyro−1` setting, the pyroptotic secretome combined oxylipins and metabolites that induced migration- and proliferation-linked gene programs, promoted wound closure, and supported reparative immune-cell responses. `PGE2` explained part of the effect through de novo synthesis downstream of caspase-1 and release through GSDMD pores, while metabolites appeared to contribute particularly to immune-cell infiltration and reparative macrophage polarization. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1555-1602]

These findings support the concept that localized pyroptotic cell death can balance tissue injury with regeneration, and that components of the pyroptotic secretome may have therapeutic potential in wound repair. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1595-1602]
