# WNK1 as a negative regulator of NLRP3 inflammasome activation

[[WNK1]] is described as a negative regulator of the [[NLRP3 inflammasome]] in macrophages. In the source study, loss of WNK1 protein or kinase activity increased NLRP3 activation and [[pyroptosis]], while myeloid WNK1 deficiency in mice increased IL-1beta production after NLRP3 stimulation. The authors conclude that WNK1 tempers NLRP3 activation by balancing intracellular chloride and potassium during inflammasome activation. ^[chloride-sensing-by-wnk1-regulates-nlrp3-inflammasome-activation-and-pyroptosis.md:17-25]

## Overview

The study frames NLRP3 activation as a process influenced by ionic changes, including K+ and Cl- efflux, and investigates WNK kinases because they regulate intracellular ion homeostasis and cell volume. Within that context, the authors report that WNK1 suppresses NLRP3-dependent inflammation through its action on cation-Cl- cotransporters, adding a regulatory layer to the NLRP3 activation pathway. ^[chloride-sensing-by-wnk1-regulates-nlrp3-inflammasome-activation-and-pyroptosis.md:125-133]

## Evidence in macrophages

Pharmacologic inhibition of WNK kinases with WNK463 in LPS-primed bone marrow-derived macrophages increased NLRP3 activation induced by ATP, nigericin, or monosodium urate crystals, as shown by higher caspase-1 activation, IL-1beta production, and ASC oligomerization. The same treatments also increased propidium iodide uptake and LDH release, indicating increased pyroptosis. ^[chloride-sensing-by-wnk1-regulates-nlrp3-inflammasome-activation-and-pyroptosis.md:141-154]

A more specific test with the WNK1 inhibitor WNK-IN-11 gave similar results. WNK-IN-11 increased caspase-1 activation, IL-1beta production, propidium iodide uptake, and LDH release in response to ATP, nigericin, or monosodium urate, and it increased the number of cells containing NLRP3 specks, indicating enhanced NLRP3 oligomerization. ^[chloride-sensing-by-wnk1-regulates-nlrp3-inflammasome-activation-and-pyroptosis.md:170-208]

Genetic evidence was consistent with the inhibitor data. Tamoxifen-induced conditional deletion of WNK1 in immortalized macrophages increased NLRP3 activation after priming with Pam3CSK4, Poly I:C, or LPS followed by ATP, which the authors interpret as an effect on the activation step rather than the priming step. WNK1-deficient cells also showed increased LDH release, and after MSU stimulation they showed increased caspase-1 activation, IL-1beta release, and propidium iodide uptake. ^[chloride-sensing-by-wnk1-regulates-nlrp3-inflammasome-activation-and-pyroptosis.md:230-260]

Primary macrophages from myeloid-specific Wnk1 conditional knockout mice likewise showed increased NLRP3 activation and pyroptosis compared with wild-type controls after stimulation with ATP, nigericin, or MSU. This was supported by increased caspase-1 activation, ASC oligomerization, IL-1beta release, LDH release, and propidium iodide uptake. ^[chloride-sensing-by-wnk1-regulates-nlrp3-inflammasome-activation-and-pyroptosis.md:262-283, chloride-sensing-by-wnk1-regulates-nlrp3-inflammasome-activation-and-pyroptosis.md:364-374]

## Mechanism

The proposed mechanism centers on WNK1 as a chloride-sensitive kinase that responds to low intracellular Cl-. According to the study, WNK1 activates [[OXSR1]] and [[STK39]], which then activate cation-Cl- cotransporters such as [[SLC12A2]], [[SLC12A1]], and [[SLC12A3]] to help maintain intracellular Na+, K+, and Cl- concentrations. The authors propose that when NLRP3 stimuli lower intracellular Cl-, WNK1-driven cotransporter activity offsets Cl- and K+ loss and thereby dampens NLRP3 activation. ^[chloride-sensing-by-wnk1-regulates-nlrp3-inflammasome-activation-and-pyroptosis.md:392-403, chloride-sensing-by-wnk1-regulates-nlrp3-inflammasome-activation-and-pyroptosis.md:815-819]

Experiments in chloride-free medium supported this model. In wild-type macrophages, nigericin stimulation in Cl--free medium increased IL-1beta production, LDH release, and caspase-1 activation compared with normal medium. In WNK1-deficient macrophages, chloride-free conditions did not further increase these readouts above the already elevated response seen in normal medium, and the authors interpret this as evidence that loss of WNK1 and loss of cotransporter activity produce similar effects on NLRP3 activation. ^[chloride-sensing-by-wnk1-regulates-nlrp3-inflammasome-activation-and-pyroptosis.md:420-451]

Direct ion measurements further linked WNK1 to control of intracellular chloride and potassium during NLRP3 activation. At baseline, wild-type and conditional WNK1-knockout macrophages had similar intracellular Cl- levels, but after nigericin, ATP, or imiquimod treatment, wild-type cells lost less intracellular Cl- than WNK1-deficient cells. WNK1-deficient cells also showed greater loss of intracellular K+, supporting the conclusion that without WNK1, macrophages cannot adequately offset Cl- and K+ loss during NLRP3 activation. ^[chloride-sensing-by-wnk1-regulates-nlrp3-inflammasome-activation-and-pyroptosis.md:452-493]

The study further concludes that both Cl- and K+ efflux contribute to NLRP3 activation. In ion-substitution experiments, strong activation was associated with simultaneous decline of intracellular Cl- and K+, and excess extracellular Cl- or K+ blocked intracellular K+ decline and inhibited NLRP3 activation and IL-1beta generation in both wild-type and WNK1-deficient cells. ^[chloride-sensing-by-wnk1-regulates-nlrp3-inflammasome-activation-and-pyroptosis.md:494-544, chloride-sensing-by-wnk1-regulates-nlrp3-inflammasome-activation-and-pyroptosis.md:663-672]

## Pathway specificity

The source reports that WNK1 regulation was not a general effect on all inflammasomes. WNK1 inhibition or deletion did not affect [[NLRC4 inflammasome|NLRC4]], [[NLRP1 inflammasome|NLRP1]], or non-canonical [[caspase-11]] inflammasome activity, although it did increase [[AIM2 inflammasome|AIM2]] activity by a mechanism that, unlike NLRP3 regulation, was not controlled by chloride or potassium. ^[chloride-sensing-by-wnk1-regulates-nlrp3-inflammasome-activation-and-pyroptosis.md:291-304]

The study also tested [[WNK4]], another chloride-sensitive WNK family member. WNK4 knockout did not increase NLRP3 activation, and combined WNK1/WNK4 deficiency did not enhance the phenotype beyond WNK1 deficiency alone, indicating that WNK1 rather than WNK4 is the critical family member in this context. ^[chloride-sensing-by-wnk1-regulates-nlrp3-inflammasome-activation-and-pyroptosis.md:596-618]

## In vivo relevance

To assess physiological relevance, the authors used an MSU-induced peritonitis model, a NLRP3-dependent innate immune response. Compared with wild-type littermates, myeloid WNK1 conditional knockout mice had higher IL-1beta levels in peritoneal exudates and increased neutrophil infiltration after both 6-hour and 16-hour MSU challenge, supporting an in vivo role for WNK1 in limiting NLRP3-dependent inflammation. ^[chloride-sensing-by-wnk1-regulates-nlrp3-inflammasome-activation-and-pyroptosis.md:572-595, chloride-sensing-by-wnk1-regulates-nlrp3-inflammasome-activation-and-pyroptosis.md:699-705]

## Clinical implications

The paper highlights potential implications for therapies that inhibit the WNK1 pathway. Inhibitors of WNK1, OXSR1/STK39, or downstream cation-Cl- cotransporters all increased NLRP3 activation in the study, and hydrochlorothiazide enhanced ATP- and MSU-induced NLRP3 activation and pyroptosis in macrophages. The authors suggest that targeting this pathway for hypertension treatment could worsen NLRP3-mediated inflammatory conditions such as [[gout]]. ^[chloride-sensing-by-wnk1-regulates-nlrp3-inflammasome-activation-and-pyroptosis.md:323-338, chloride-sensing-by-wnk1-regulates-nlrp3-inflammasome-activation-and-pyroptosis.md:380-391, chloride-sensing-by-wnk1-regulates-nlrp3-inflammasome-activation-and-pyroptosis.md:682-694, chloride-sensing-by-wnk1-regulates-nlrp3-inflammasome-activation-and-pyroptosis.md:738-779]
