# Dose-dependent caspase-3 activity switches M-TOP-treated cells between apoptosis and pyroptosis

The study reports that [[M-TOP]] can shift treated cancer cells between [[apoptosis]] and [[pyroptosis]] by altering the level of [[caspase-3]] activity after photodynamic treatment. In the authors' framework, M-TOP is a mitochondria-targeting, near-infrared-activatable type-I photodrug that modulates apoptotic and pyroptotic signaling crosstalk, with lower doses favoring apoptosis and higher doses favoring pyroptosis through the caspase-3/[[Gasdermin E|GSDME]] pathway. ^[mitochondria-targeting-type-i-photodrug-harnessing-caspase-3-activity-for-pyroptotic-oncotherapy.md:18-31, mitochondria-targeting-type-i-photodrug-harnessing-caspase-3-activity-for-pyroptotic-oncotherapy.md:101-114]

## Core finding

In U251-MG cells, most cells underwent apoptotic death when treated with a low dose of M-TOP (`0.25 uM`) and irradiated for `5-10 min`. By contrast, a higher M-TOP dose (`1 uM`) with `5 min` irradiation predominantly induced pyroptotic death. The source states that the threshold for switching from apoptosis to pyroptosis was `0.5 uM` under `5 min` of `640 nm` irradiation at `15 mW/cm^2`. ^[mitochondria-targeting-type-i-photodrug-harnessing-caspase-3-activity-for-pyroptotic-oncotherapy.md:208-220]

This switch was associated with the activation level of caspase-3. The paper states that the apoptosis-to-pyroptosis transition correlated with caspase-3 activation, consistent with the observed expression of full-length caspase-3 in western blot analysis. ^[mitochondria-targeting-type-i-photodrug-harnessing-caspase-3-activity-for-pyroptotic-oncotherapy.md:216-220]

## Mechanistic basis

M-TOP localized to mitochondria in the tested cells, and after irradiation it generated peroxygen radicals rather than singlet oxygen. The authors report that these radicals affected the mitochondrial respiratory chain, triggered cytochrome c release, and activated caspase-3, as shown by decreased full-length caspase-3 and increased cleaved caspase-3. ^[mitochondria-targeting-type-i-photodrug-harnessing-caspase-3-activity-for-pyroptotic-oncotherapy.md:132-146, mitochondria-targeting-type-i-photodrug-harnessing-caspase-3-activity-for-pyroptotic-oncotherapy.md:165-172]

Activated caspase-3 was linked to both apoptotic and pyroptotic downstream markers. M-TOP phototreatment produced cleaved PARP, indicating apoptotic signaling, and cleaved GSDME, indicating pyroptotic signaling. The figure description further states that direct interaction between the photodrug and mitochondria activates caspase-3 and leads to pyroptotic pore formation through the GSDME-mediated pathway, whereas gentler activation yields apoptotic bodies indicative of apoptosis. ^[mitochondria-targeting-type-i-photodrug-harnessing-caspase-3-activity-for-pyroptotic-oncotherapy.md:172-186]

## Phenotypic evidence

Under conditions associated with pyroptosis, cells treated with M-TOP and light showed shrinkage together with large bubble formation on the cell membrane, which the paper describes as indicative of pyroptotic cell death. This was accompanied by dosage-dependent release of [[lactate dehydrogenase|LDH]] and [[IL-1beta]], consistent with pore formation and leakage of cytoplasmic contents. These pyroptotic characteristics were reported only for the M-TOP-treated group and not for the other organelle-targeting comparison photodrugs. ^[mitochondria-targeting-type-i-photodrug-harnessing-caspase-3-activity-for-pyroptotic-oncotherapy.md:147-164]

For the lower-dose condition, the Figure 3 description states that `0.25 uM` M-TOP with `10 min` irradiation produced dominant apoptotic morphological changes and relatively weak activation of caspase-3 over a `24 h` incubation period. For the higher-dose condition, `1 uM` M-TOP with `5 min` irradiation produced dominant pyroptotic morphological change and relatively strong activation of caspase-3 over a `3 h` incubation period. ^[mitochondria-targeting-type-i-photodrug-harnessing-caspase-3-activity-for-pyroptotic-oncotherapy.md:270-280]

## Cell-line scope

The source states that this type-I pyroptotic oncotherapy strategy was also verified in various human and mouse cancer cell lines. All tested cell types except [[MCF-7]] showed membrane swelling and bubble formation after M-TOP phototreatment under the defined critical parameters, and subsequent analysis linked susceptibility to pyroptosis with GSDME expression in those cell lines. ^[mitochondria-targeting-type-i-photodrug-harnessing-caspase-3-activity-for-pyroptotic-oncotherapy.md:221-226, mitochondria-targeting-type-i-photodrug-harnessing-caspase-3-activity-for-pyroptotic-oncotherapy.md:276-279]

## Therapeutic context

The paper frames this dose-dependent switch as a way to modulate signaling between noninflammatory apoptosis and pro-inflammatory pyroptosis. It states that controlling this balance could help prevent immune hyperactivation and related adverse effects while preserving the immunotherapeutic value of pyroptotic cancer cell death. ^[mitochondria-targeting-type-i-photodrug-harnessing-caspase-3-activity-for-pyroptotic-oncotherapy.md:208-210, mitochondria-targeting-type-i-photodrug-harnessing-caspase-3-activity-for-pyroptotic-oncotherapy.md:227-237]

More broadly, the abstract and conclusion describe M-TOP-mediated switching as part of a photodynamic strategy that enables spatiotemporal control of inflammatory responses. The authors argue that the shift from apoptosis to pyroptosis can evoke a controlled inflammatory response and contribute to robust but balanced antitumor immunity. ^[mitochondria-targeting-type-i-photodrug-harnessing-caspase-3-activity-for-pyroptotic-oncotherapy.md:25-31, mitochondria-targeting-type-i-photodrug-harnessing-caspase-3-activity-for-pyroptotic-oncotherapy.md:357-377]
