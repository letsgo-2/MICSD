# Type-I ROS plus photothermal membrane damage drives D1-induced caspase-1/GSDMD pyroptosis

`D1` is a tumor-membrane-targeted photosensitive dimer reported to trigger [[pyroptosis]] through the combined action of [[type-I reactive oxygen species|type-I ROS]] generated during [[photodynamic therapy]] and membrane-damaging heat generated during [[photothermal therapy]]. In the study’s overall model, light-activated D1 localizes to the tumor cell membrane, produces ROS under irradiation, and uses its photothermal effect to reinforce membrane injury, together driving an immunogenic pyroptotic cell-death program. ^[pyroptosismediated-synergistic-photodynamic-and-photothermal-immunotherapy-enabled-by-a-tumormembranetargeted-photosensitive-dimer.md:21-31, pyroptosismediated-synergistic-photodynamic-and-photothermal-immunotherapy-enabled-by-a-tumormembranetargeted-photosensitive-dimer.md:140-157]

## D1 properties relevant to pyroptosis

The source describes D1 as an aggregation-induced emission photosensitive dimer with strong membrane-targeting behavior, improved aggregation relative to the monomer `M1`, and the ability to support both photodynamic and photothermal action under light irradiation. D1 generated multiple ROS species in aqueous solution, including hydroxyl radical and superoxide radical identified as type-I ROS, while also showing a strong photothermal response under 520 nm laser irradiation. ^[pyroptosismediated-synergistic-photodynamic-and-photothermal-immunotherapy-enabled-by-a-tumormembranetargeted-photosensitive-dimer.md:147-157, pyroptosismediated-synergistic-photodynamic-and-photothermal-immunotherapy-enabled-by-a-tumormembranetargeted-photosensitive-dimer.md:229-263]

In cells, D1 showed strong localization to the plasma membrane. Colocalization with the membrane dye `DiO` gave a Pearson correlation coefficient of `0.90`, and the authors attribute this targeting to electrostatic interaction between positively charged D1 and negatively charged cell membranes. The paper presents this membrane localization as important because ROS act over a short distance, so generating damage directly at the membrane favors rupture and release of intracellular contents. ^[pyroptosismediated-synergistic-photodynamic-and-photothermal-immunotherapy-enabled-by-a-tumormembranetargeted-photosensitive-dimer.md:121-131, pyroptosismediated-synergistic-photodynamic-and-photothermal-immunotherapy-enabled-by-a-tumormembranetargeted-photosensitive-dimer.md:288-305]

## ROS generation at the membrane

In 4T1 cells, D1 produced strong intracellular ROS signals under light, detected with `DCFH-DA`, and this signal was suppressed by the ROS inhibitor `N-acetylcysteine`, supporting light-dependent ROS generation in cells. Flow-cytometry measurements agreed with confocal imaging, and the fluorescence signal in the D1-plus-light group was reported as about 40 times that of the control group. ^[pyroptosismediated-synergistic-photodynamic-and-photothermal-immunotherapy-enabled-by-a-tumormembranetargeted-photosensitive-dimer.md:306-322]

The mechanistic interpretation given by the authors is that D1-generated ROS activate [[caspase-1]] at the cell membrane, which then cleaves [[Gasdermin D|GSDMD]] into the pore-forming `GSDMD-N` fragment. This links membrane-localized photodynamic injury to the canonical pore-formation machinery of pyroptosis. ^[pyroptosismediated-synergistic-photodynamic-and-photothermal-immunotherapy-enabled-by-a-tumormembranetargeted-photosensitive-dimer.md:400-414]

## Caspase-1/GSDMD pyroptosis mechanism

The paper frames pyroptosis as a lytic cell-death pathway in which caspase-1 cleavage of GSDMD generates `GSDMD-N`, which forms membrane pores and causes swelling, membrane rupture, and release of inflammatory contents. In D1-treated 4T1 cells under light irradiation, western blotting showed significant increases in cleaved caspase-1 and cleaved `GSDMD-N`, while ELISA and release assays detected associated pyroptosis markers including `IL-1beta`, `IL-18`, and `LDH`. ^[pyroptosismediated-synergistic-photodynamic-and-photothermal-immunotherapy-enabled-by-a-tumormembranetargeted-photosensitive-dimer.md:75-93, pyroptosismediated-synergistic-photodynamic-and-photothermal-immunotherapy-enabled-by-a-tumormembranetargeted-photosensitive-dimer.md:400-414]

Microscopy findings were consistent with pyroptosis rather than apoptosis. After light irradiation of D1-stained cancer cells, the cells swelled instead of shrinking, bubbles formed from the plasma membrane and progressively expanded, and Trypan Blue staining indicated loss of plasma-membrane integrity. Flow cytometry with annexin V-FITC and `PI` further indicated a large pyroptotic population, with total mortality in the D1-plus-light group reaching `99.1%`. ^[pyroptosismediated-synergistic-photodynamic-and-photothermal-immunotherapy-enabled-by-a-tumormembranetargeted-photosensitive-dimer.md:353-363, pyroptosismediated-synergistic-photodynamic-and-photothermal-immunotherapy-enabled-by-a-tumormembranetargeted-photosensitive-dimer.md:386-400]

## Contribution of photothermal membrane damage

The study explicitly states that the photothermal effect of D1 accelerates the pyroptosis process. The authors report that D1-generated heat enhanced membrane permeability and promoted faster release of inflammatory cytokines and other cell contents after pore formation. ^[pyroptosismediated-synergistic-photodynamic-and-photothermal-immunotherapy-enabled-by-a-tumormembranetargeted-photosensitive-dimer.md:409-414, pyroptosismediated-synergistic-photodynamic-and-photothermal-immunotherapy-enabled-by-a-tumormembranetargeted-photosensitive-dimer.md:441-464]

A direct comparison of irradiation conditions was used to separate photodynamic-only and combined photodynamic-photothermal treatment. The paper states that `0.1 W cm^-2` irradiation produced only a photodynamic effect, whereas `0.3 W cm^-2` produced both photodynamic and photothermal effects. Under the combined condition, tumor cells showed stronger DNA damage, expression of DNA-damage-repair proteins, and higher uptake of the fluorescent probe `berberine`, which the authors interpret as evidence that photothermal treatment further increased membrane permeability beyond photodynamic treatment alone. ^[pyroptosismediated-synergistic-photodynamic-and-photothermal-immunotherapy-enabled-by-a-tumormembranetargeted-photosensitive-dimer.md:426-450]

## Link to immunogenic cell death

The pyroptotic response induced by D1 was associated with hallmarks of [[immunogenic cell death]]. In D1-treated 4T1 cells under light, the source reports increased surface exposure of `CRT` and redistribution of `HMGB1` from the nucleus to the whole cell, indicating release-associated ICD signaling. The paper interprets these changes as evidence that light-induced pyroptosis by D1 efficiently induces ICD and helps initiate adaptive immune responses. ^[pyroptosismediated-synergistic-photodynamic-and-photothermal-immunotherapy-enabled-by-a-tumormembranetargeted-photosensitive-dimer.md:416-425]

## In vivo consequence of the mechanism

In the bilateral 4T1 mouse model, D1 plus light completely regressed the treated primary tumor within 7 days and completely inhibited growth of the untreated distant tumor. The authors connect these outcomes to pyroptosis-mediated photoimmunotherapy, supported by increased dendritic-cell maturation, higher `CD4+` and `CD8+` T-cell infiltration, increased distant-tumor `CD8+` T cells, memory T-cell markers, and elevated serum proinflammatory cytokines including `IL-18` and `IL-1beta`. ^[pyroptosismediated-synergistic-photodynamic-and-photothermal-immunotherapy-enabled-by-a-tumormembranetargeted-photosensitive-dimer.md:494-507, pyroptosismediated-synergistic-photodynamic-and-photothermal-immunotherapy-enabled-by-a-tumormembranetargeted-photosensitive-dimer.md:537-607]
