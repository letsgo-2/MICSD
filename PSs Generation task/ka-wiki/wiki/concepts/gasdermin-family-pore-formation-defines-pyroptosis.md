# Gasdermin-family pore formation defines pyroptosis

[[Pyroptosis]] is a form of programmed cell death whose defining feature is dependence on the [[gasdermin family]] and the inflammatory response that accompanies it. The reviewed source describes pyroptosis as a regulated or programmed necrotic cell death distinguished by formation of pores in the cytoplasmic membrane by gasdermin proteins, often, but not always, downstream of inflammatory caspase activation. ^[pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:16-24, pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:102-109]

## Core definition

The source traces an evolution in how pyroptosis was defined: it was first recognized as a rapid lytic, inflammatory cell death distinct from apoptosis, then associated with caspase-1, and later redefined after discovery of gasdermin-mediated membrane pore formation. By 2018, the cited nomenclature standard summarized pyroptosis as regulated cell death that relies on gasdermin-family pore formation in the cell membrane rather than on caspase activation alone. ^[pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:83-109, pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:155-174]

## Why gasdermins are central

Gasdermin proteins are presented as the decisive executioners of pyroptosis. They contain an N-terminal domain and a C-terminal domain linked by a flexible region; in the uncleaved state, the C-terminal domain suppresses the pore-forming activity of the N-terminal domain. When cleavage releases the N-terminal fragment, it oligomerizes in membranes, drills pores, and induces pyroptosis. ^[pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:113-124]

This pore-forming mechanism is the basis for the article’s framing that gasdermin-family activity, rather than any single upstream trigger, defines pyroptosis. The review notes that multiple gasdermin N-terminal domains have pore-forming capacity and that, except for Pejvakin, gasdermin-family members discussed in the paper share this functional theme. ^[pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:98-109, pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:231-234, pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:732-735]

## Canonical inflammasome pathway

In the canonical [[inflammasome]] pathway, sensors such as [[NLRP1]], [[NLRP3 inflammasome|NLRP3]], [[NLRC4]], [[AIM2]], and pyrin assemble inflammasome complexes by recruiting [[ASC]] and pro-caspase-1. Activated caspase-1 then cleaves [[Gasdermin D|GSDMD]] to generate the N-terminal fragment that forms membrane pores and executes pyroptosis. Caspase-1 also processes pro-[[IL-1beta]] and pro-[[IL-18]], which are then released through these pores. ^[pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:125-149]

The same section notes that [[IL-1alpha]] and [[HMGB1]] can also be released during pyroptosis. This links gasdermin pore formation directly to the inflammatory output that distinguishes pyroptotic death from many other death programs. ^[pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:143-149]

## Noncanonical inflammasome pathway

The noncanonical pathway centers on caspase-11 in mice and caspase-4/5 in humans. In this pathway, intracellular [[lipopolysaccharide|LPS]] directly activates these caspases, which then cleave GSDMD to produce the pore-forming N-terminal fragment and trigger pyroptosis. ^[pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:150-192]

The review also describes a link between this pathway and canonical inflammasome signaling: LPS-driven caspase-11 activation can open pannexin-1, promote potassium efflux, activate NLRP3, and thereby engage caspase-1-mediated pyroptosis as well. Even in this branch, gasdermin pore formation remains the terminal event that defines the death process. ^[pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:184-192]

## Other gasdermins that can execute pyroptosis

Although [[Gasdermin D|GSDMD]] is emphasized in canonical and noncanonical inflammasome signaling, the review states that other gasdermins also contribute to pyroptosis. [[Gasdermin E|GSDME]] can be cleaved by caspase-3 after chemotherapeutic or ROS-linked signaling, producing GSDME-NT and membrane pores. In this context, GSDME can function as a switch between apoptosis and pyroptosis, with high GSDME expression favoring pyroptosis. ^[pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:193-230]

The paper further describes pore-forming roles for [[GSDMA]], [[GSDMB]], and [[GSDMC]]. GSDMA-NT and mouse Gsdma3-NT bind membrane lipids and induce pore formation; GSDMB-NT can induce pyroptosis, and granzyme A cleavage at Lys244 yields a pore-forming fragment; GSDMC can be cleaved by caspase-8 and convert TNF-alpha-induced apoptosis to pyroptosis in breast cancer cells. ^[pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:231-271]

A summary figure described in the source lists the cleavage relationships across the family: granzyme A cleaves GSDMB, caspase-3 and granzyme B cleave GSDME, caspase-1/4/5/11 cleave GSDMD, and caspase-8 cleaves GSDMC, while the activator that cleaves GSDMA remained unknown in the reviewed literature. ^[pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:390-391]

## Distinguishing feature relative to other cell death programs

In the review’s comparison table, pyroptosis is distinguished from [[apoptosis]], [[necroptosis]], and [[autophagy]] by gasdermin-dependent membrane pore formation and inflammatory mediator release. The table lists pore formation, swelling, release of IL-18, IL-1beta, IL-1alpha, HMGB1, and LDH, and the presence of gasdermin-family pore-forming proteins as defining characteristics of pyroptosis. ^[pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:342-389]

## Relevance in cancer

The article frames pyroptosis as increasingly relevant to cancer biology and treatment because many tumor-associated interventions can induce gasdermin-dependent pyroptosis, and expression of pyroptosis-related proteins often differs between tumor and normal tissues. The authors suggest that gasdermins, caspases, [[NLRP3 inflammasome|NLRP3]], and [[ASC]] may serve as biomarkers or therapeutic targets. ^[pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:19-24, pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:736-750]

The review also highlights an immunologic dimension: pyroptosis in tumor cells can promote [[antitumor immunity]]. In GSDME-expressing tumors, pyroptosis-generated damage-associated molecular patterns recruit immune cells, increase tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes, and enhance macrophage phagocytic capacity. It further notes that even a small number of pyroptotic tumor cells may be sufficient to remodel the tumor immune microenvironment and trigger strong T-cell-mediated antitumor responses. ^[pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:602-644]
