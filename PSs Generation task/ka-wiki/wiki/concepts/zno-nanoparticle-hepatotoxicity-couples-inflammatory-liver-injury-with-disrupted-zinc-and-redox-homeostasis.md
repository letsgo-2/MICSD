# ZnO nanoparticle hepatotoxicity couples inflammatory liver injury with disrupted zinc and redox homeostasis

[[Zinc oxide nanoparticles|ZnO nanoparticles]] were reported to cause liver toxicity characterized by inflammatory injury together with disturbed zinc handling and impaired redox balance. In the study, ZnO NPs disrupted zinc homeostasis in rat liver, induced oxidative stress in rat liver and [[HepG2]] cells, and were linked to inflammatory hepatocyte death through canonical [[pyroptosis]] signaling. ^[oxidative-stress-related-canonical-pyroptosis-pathway-as-a-target-of-liver-toxicity-triggered-by-zinc-oxide-nanoparticles.md:39-44, oxidative-stress-related-canonical-pyroptosis-pathway-as-a-target-of-liver-toxicity-triggered-by-zinc-oxide-nanoparticles.md:65-72]

## Overview

The paper frames the liver as a major toxic target of ZnO NPs because these particles can accumulate there after exposure and trigger oxidative and inflammatory damage. The authors set out to test whether hepatocyte injury involved pyroptosis and whether oxidative stress contributed to that process, using both Sprague-Dawley rats and HepG2 cells. ^[oxidative-stress-related-canonical-pyroptosis-pathway-as-a-target-of-liver-toxicity-triggered-by-zinc-oxide-nanoparticles.md:139-180, oxidative-stress-related-canonical-pyroptosis-pathway-as-a-target-of-liver-toxicity-triggered-by-zinc-oxide-nanoparticles.md:189-208]

## Experimental context

In vivo, rats received intraperitoneal ZnO NP suspensions at `12.5`, `25`, or `50 mg/kg` body weight for 7 consecutive days, with a solvent-treated control group for comparison. In vitro, HepG2 cells were exposed to ZnO NPs at `0-25 ug/mL`, and a working concentration near the 50% inhibitory level was set at `20 ug/mL` for mechanistic experiments. ^[oxidative-stress-related-canonical-pyroptosis-pathway-as-a-target-of-liver-toxicity-triggered-by-zinc-oxide-nanoparticles.md:251-274, oxidative-stress-related-canonical-pyroptosis-pathway-as-a-target-of-liver-toxicity-triggered-by-zinc-oxide-nanoparticles.md:339-367, oxidative-stress-related-canonical-pyroptosis-pathway-as-a-target-of-liver-toxicity-triggered-by-zinc-oxide-nanoparticles.md:575-584]

The ZnO NPs used in the study were described as agglomerated polygonal and columnar particles with an average primary diameter of about `46.31 +/- 12.04 nm`, a hydrodynamic size of `178.6 +/- 53.16 nm`, and a zeta potential of `21.2 +/- 6.46 mV` at pH 7.0. Dissolved zinc release in DMEM was low and largely stable across 1 to 24 hours, but increased under more acidic conditions. ^[oxidative-stress-related-canonical-pyroptosis-pathway-as-a-target-of-liver-toxicity-triggered-by-zinc-oxide-nanoparticles.md:463-495]

## Zinc accumulation and disrupted homeostasis

After 7 days of exposure, zinc content increased across multiple rat tissues, indicating disrupted systemic zinc homeostasis. The liver showed the highest zinc burden among the measured organs, with the reported tissue ranking `liver > spleen > kidney > lung > heart > brain`, supporting the liver as the primary target organ of Zn penetration in this model. ^[oxidative-stress-related-canonical-pyroptosis-pathway-as-a-target-of-liver-toxicity-triggered-by-zinc-oxide-nanoparticles.md:495-509]

In HepG2 cells, total intracellular zinc increased in a dose-dependent manner after ZnO NP exposure. The study also observed undissolved nanoparticles in the cytoplasm and increased intracellular `Zn2+` fluorescence, which the authors interpreted as evidence that ZnO NPs contributed to zinc accumulation both as particles and through ion release after cellular uptake, potentially including conversion in acidic lysosomes. ^[oxidative-stress-related-canonical-pyroptosis-pathway-as-a-target-of-liver-toxicity-triggered-by-zinc-oxide-nanoparticles.md:558-574]

## Inflammatory liver injury

ZnO NP-treated rats showed signs consistent with inflammatory liver injury, including loose stools, reduced food intake, weight loss, and reduced organ coefficient in the high-dose group. Hematology also shifted toward inflammation, with increased white blood cells, neutrophils, monocytes, and lymphocytes in the medium- and high-dose groups. ^[oxidative-stress-related-canonical-pyroptosis-pathway-as-a-target-of-liver-toxicity-triggered-by-zinc-oxide-nanoparticles.md:512-519]

Histopathology and ultrastructure in rat liver showed disordered hepatocyte arrangement, vacuolar degeneration, portal-vein congestion, inflammatory-cell infiltration, tissue calcification, nuclear pyknosis, mitochondrial swelling, and mitochondrial cristae fracture or loss. Serum liver injury markers `ALT`, `AST`, and `gamma-GT` were also significantly elevated relative to controls. ^[oxidative-stress-related-canonical-pyroptosis-pathway-as-a-target-of-liver-toxicity-triggered-by-zinc-oxide-nanoparticles.md:520-538]

In HepG2 cells, ZnO NPs reduced viability in a time- and dose-dependent manner and produced morphologic changes including swelling, detachment from neighboring cells, and reduced cell density. Cellular inflammatory signaling was also elevated, with increased `HMGB1` at `15 ug/mL` and increased `NF-kappaB` at `20 ug/mL`. ^[oxidative-stress-related-canonical-pyroptosis-pathway-as-a-target-of-liver-toxicity-triggered-by-zinc-oxide-nanoparticles.md:575-584, oxidative-stress-related-canonical-pyroptosis-pathway-as-a-target-of-liver-toxicity-triggered-by-zinc-oxide-nanoparticles.md:605-620]

## Redox imbalance and oxidative stress

The study found that ZnO NPs impaired antioxidant defenses and increased oxidative stress in both rat liver and HepG2 cells. In rat liver, exposure increased `MDA` and decreased `SOD`, `GSH`, and `CAT`, while in HepG2 cells ZnO NPs increased ROS accumulation, reduced `CAT` and `SOD`, and raised `MDA`. ^[oxidative-stress-related-canonical-pyroptosis-pathway-as-a-target-of-liver-toxicity-triggered-by-zinc-oxide-nanoparticles.md:531-538, oxidative-stress-related-canonical-pyroptosis-pathway-as-a-target-of-liver-toxicity-triggered-by-zinc-oxide-nanoparticles.md:594-604]

In HepG2 cells, antioxidant-response proteins `Nrf2` and `HO-1` were up-regulated at ZnO NP concentrations of at least `15 ug/mL`. The authors interpreted this together with the ROS and antioxidant-enzyme changes as evidence that ZnO NPs triggered oxidative stress by suppressing antioxidant capacity. ^[oxidative-stress-related-canonical-pyroptosis-pathway-as-a-target-of-liver-toxicity-triggered-by-zinc-oxide-nanoparticles.md:598-604]

## Link to canonical pyroptosis

The liver injury was coupled to activation of the canonical [[NLRP3 inflammasome]]-[[caspase-1]]-[[Gasdermin D|GSDMD]] pathway. In rat liver, ZnO NPs increased `NLRP3`, `ASC`, cleaved caspase-1, `GSDMD-NT`, `IL-1beta`, and `IL-18`, with supporting immunohistochemistry, immunofluorescence, and increased TUNEL positivity. ^[oxidative-stress-related-canonical-pyroptosis-pathway-as-a-target-of-liver-toxicity-triggered-by-zinc-oxide-nanoparticles.md:539-557]

In HepG2 cells, ZnO NPs increased `NLRP3`, `ASC`, and cleaved caspase-1 in a dose- and time-dependent manner, increased co-localization consistent with inflammasome assembly, and elevated `GSDMD-NT`, `IL-1beta`, and `IL-18`. Additional evidence of pyroptotic membrane injury included increased `LDH` release, increased Annexin V/PI double-positive cells, membrane pore formation on scanning electron microscopy, and increased TUNEL signal. ^[oxidative-stress-related-canonical-pyroptosis-pathway-as-a-target-of-liver-toxicity-triggered-by-zinc-oxide-nanoparticles.md:605-620, oxidative-stress-related-canonical-pyroptosis-pathway-as-a-target-of-liver-toxicity-triggered-by-zinc-oxide-nanoparticles.md:621-644]

## Evidence that oxidative stress and caspase-1 are functionally involved

Pharmacologic inhibition supported a functional link between oxidative stress, canonical pyroptosis, and hepatocyte injury. The caspase-1 inhibitor `VX-765` reduced cleaved caspase-1, `GSDMD-NT`, mature `IL-1beta`, and `IL-18`, improved membrane integrity, lowered LDH leakage, reduced Annexin V/PI double positivity and TUNEL positivity, and improved cell viability in ZnO NP-treated HepG2 cells. ^[oxidative-stress-related-canonical-pyroptosis-pathway-as-a-target-of-liver-toxicity-triggered-by-zinc-oxide-nanoparticles.md:645-667]

The antioxidant `N-acetyl-L-cysteine` (`NAC`) reduced ROS, restored `CAT` and `SOD`, lowered `MDA`, promoted the `Nrf2/HO-1` pathway, improved cell viability, decreased LDH release, and down-regulated `NLRP3`, `ASC`, caspase-1, and `GSDMD`, including reduced cytoplasmic `GSDMD-NT` aggregation. The authors therefore concluded that antioxidation protected against ZnO NP-induced pyroptosis in hepatocytes. ^[oxidative-stress-related-canonical-pyroptosis-pathway-as-a-target-of-liver-toxicity-triggered-by-zinc-oxide-nanoparticles.md:668-841]

## Interpretation

Within this study, ZnO nanoparticle hepatotoxicity is presented as a coupled process: liver-targeted zinc accumulation and disturbed zinc homeostasis occur alongside oxidative-stress injury, inflammatory signaling, and caspase-1-dependent pyroptotic cell death. The paper concludes that this mechanism helps explain ZnO NP liver toxicity and may inform both nanoparticle risk assessment and antioxidant-based protective strategies. ^[oxidative-stress-related-canonical-pyroptosis-pathway-as-a-target-of-liver-toxicity-triggered-by-zinc-oxide-nanoparticles.md:1169-1180, oxidative-stress-related-canonical-pyroptosis-pathway-as-a-target-of-liver-toxicity-triggered-by-zinc-oxide-nanoparticles.md:1195-1198]
