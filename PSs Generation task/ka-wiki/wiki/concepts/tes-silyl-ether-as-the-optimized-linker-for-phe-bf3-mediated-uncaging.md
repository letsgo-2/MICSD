# TES silyl ether as the optimized linker for Phe-BF3-mediated uncaging

**TES silyl ether** was identified in the source study as the preferred silyl group for a [[Phe-BF3]]-triggered uncaging system based on desilylation of a silyl-ether-containing carbamate linker. The authors compared [[TES silyl ether|TES]], [[TBS silyl ether|TBS]], and [[TIPS silyl ether|TIPS]] variants in coumarin-caged probes and concluded that TES gave the best overall performance for the bioorthogonal system. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:89-90, a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:299-314]

## Optimization rationale

To optimize the original linker design, the study replaced the initial TBS group in the coumarin conjugate with either TES or TIPS and then evaluated Phe-BF3-catalyzed desilylation. In these comparisons, TIPS-substituted coumarin resisted desilylation, whereas TESO-coumarin was desilylated much more efficiently than the TBS analogue. The authors interpreted the different reactivities of TES, TBS, and TIPS as evidence of a steric hindrance effect, consistent with fluoride-mediated desilylation chemistry. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:89-90, a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:299-314]

At `10 mM`, Phe-BF3-induced desilylation was reported to be completed instantly. The paper states that this rapid reaction rate suggests a direct fluoride-transfer process rather than release of free fluoride from Phe-BF3, because free-fluoride generation from Phe-BF3 is described as having extremely slow kinetics. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:304-309]

All three tested coumarin probes, TESO-coumarin, TBSO-coumarin, and TIPSO-coumarin, showed no spontaneous desilylation under the tested conditions. TESO-coumarin was also reported not to be desilylated by other agents, including fluorine-derived drugs, supporting its use as the optimized bioorthogonal triggerable linker in this system. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:310-314]

## Experimental evidence

The optimization experiments are summarized in Extended Data Fig. 3, which describes Phe-BF3-catalyzed release of coumarin from TESO-, TBSO-, and TIPSO-coumarin constructs using fluorescence and liquid chromatography assays. The same figure caption states that desilylation efficiency was compared for both Phe-BF3 and sodium fluoride across the three silyl groups, and that TESO-coumarin was additionally tested against biologically relevant nucleophilic anions and FDA-approved organofluorines. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:1530-1547]

The Methods section specifies that TESO-coumarin, TBSO-coumarin, and TIPSO-coumarin were each treated with `150 uM` Phe-BF3 in PBS containing `5% DMSO` at `37 °C`, followed by HPLC analysis at `5` and `240 min`. For bioorthogonality testing, TESO-coumarin was exposed to `H2O2`, `GSH`, and other biologically relevant anions, and for fluorine-donor comparison it was treated with Phe-BF3 or other organofluorines under the same general assay conditions. Reaction yields were determined by HPLC peak integration. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:1076-1103]

## Use in nanoparticle-linked cargo release

The optimized TES-based linker was carried forward into nanoparticle conjugates. The study states that TES-linked NP-GFP released GFP completely upon treatment with Phe-BF3 or sodium fluoride, whereas release from the corresponding TBS-linked NP-GFP construct was only partial. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:323-326]

In mammalian cells, NP-GFP followed by Phe-BF3 treatment produced evident GFP release in HeLa, EMT6, 4T1, and primary BMDM cells, whereas sodium fluoride did not produce release in cells because it is not cell-permeable. The released GFP became fluorescent after loss of nanoparticle quenching, showing that the TES-based uncaging strategy could release functional protein cargo intracellularly. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:326-338]

The Methods further state that the nanoparticle conjugates `NP-GFP`, `NP-mNeonGreen-NLS`, and `NP-GSDMA3` were prepared using an `N3-OTES-maleimide linker` identified as the TESO-linker. This indicates that the optimized TES linker was used not only for fluorescent-protein release assays but also for the gasdermin delivery system used later in the study. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:1055-1069, a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:1173-1196]

The gasdermin conjugate was specifically described as being generated through a TES ether linker, and Phe-BF3 incubation released `GSDMA3(N + C)` from `NP-GSDMA3`. The released protein retained pore-forming activity on liposome membranes and, in cell assays, enabled pyroptosis after combined treatment with NP-GSDMA3 and Phe-BF3. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:470-490]

## Significance within the Phe-BF3 uncaging platform

Within this study, TES was the optimized linker choice because it combined higher sensitivity to Phe-BF3-triggered desilylation with lack of spontaneous cleavage and resistance to competing biologically relevant or drug-like fluorinated agents tested by the authors. The source therefore presents TES as the most effective balance of reactivity and orthogonality among the tested silyl ethers for Phe-BF3-mediated uncaging. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:299-314, a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:1530-1547]
