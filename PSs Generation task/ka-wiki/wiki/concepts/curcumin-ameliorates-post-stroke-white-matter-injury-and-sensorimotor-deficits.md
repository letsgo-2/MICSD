# Curcumin ameliorates post-stroke white matter injury and sensorimotor deficits

Curcumin was studied as a post-stroke intervention in a mouse model of transient [[middle cerebral artery occlusion|middle cerebral artery occlusion/reperfusion (MCAO/R)]]. In this study, mice received curcumin at `150 mg/kg` intraperitoneally immediately after reperfusion and then daily for 7 days. The reported outcome was that curcumin reduced post-stroke [[white matter injury]], decreased brain tissue loss, and improved sensorimotor performance over the following 21 days. ^[omcl1552127-125.md:30-44, omcl1552127-125.md:215-221]

## Experimental context

The in vivo experiments used adult male C57BL/6J mice subjected to 60 minutes of MCAO followed by reperfusion. Mice were assigned to sham, `MCAO+vehicle`, and `MCAO+curcumin` groups, and the same cohort was used across outcome measures including infarct volume, neurobehavioral testing, white matter injury, and protein-expression analyses. Sensorimotor function was assessed with the [[adhesive removal test]] before surgery and at 3, 10, and 21 days after stroke. ^[omcl1552127-125.md:157-172, omcl1552127-125.md:173-193, omcl1552127-125.md:229-240]

White matter injury was evaluated at 21 days using [[Luxol fast blue]] staining in the ipsilateral perilesional [[corpus callosum]] and [[striatum]], while brain tissue loss was assessed by MAP-2 immunostaining. Acute infarct volume was measured at 3 days by TTC staining. ^[omcl1552127-125.md:241-271, omcl1552127-125.md:272-283, omcl1552127-125.md:469-480]

## Effects on white matter and tissue loss

Curcumin post-treatment significantly reduced infarct volume at 3 days after MCAO and decreased brain tissue loss at 21 days relative to vehicle-treated stroke mice. The study describes these findings as protection extending from the early stage into longer-term phases after stroke. ^[omcl1552127-125.md:746-774]

The source also reports improved white matter integrity after curcumin treatment. In vehicle-treated stroke mice, Luxol fast blue optical density was markedly reduced in the corpus callosum and striatum 21 days after MCAO, whereas curcumin significantly increased the staining intensity in both regions. The authors interpret this as suppression of stroke-induced demyelination. ^[omcl1552127-125.md:775-784]

## Effects on sensorimotor deficits

Curcumin improved performance in the adhesive removal test, which the study used to measure tactile response and sensorimotor asymmetry. MCAO prolonged both the time to touch and the time to remove the adhesive tape for up to 21 days, while curcumin treatment markedly reduced these times across the testing period. ^[omcl1552127-125.md:229-240, omcl1552127-125.md:766-773]

In the paper’s summary, these behavioral improvements are presented alongside reduced white matter lesions and tissue loss, indicating that post-stroke curcumin treatment was associated with better long-term functional recovery in this model. ^[omcl1552127-125.md:31-44, omcl1552127-125.md:2475-2481]

## Relationship to microglial pyroptosis

The study links these structural and functional benefits to reduced [[microglia]]/macrophage [[pyroptosis]]. Twenty-one days after stroke, curcumin significantly reduced the number of `GSDMD+ Iba1+` and `caspase-1+ Iba1+` microglia/macrophages in the ischemic cortex, corpus callosum, and striatum, and also lowered protein levels associated with pyroptosis and [[NLRP3 inflammasome]] activation, including `GSDMD-N`, cleaved caspase-1, `IL-1beta`, and `NLRP3`. ^[omcl1552127-125.md:33-43, omcl1552127-125.md:785-815, omcl1552127-125.md:916-956]

In primary microglia stimulated with LPS plus ATP, curcumin similarly reduced NLRP3, cleaved caspase-1, GSDMD-N, `IL-1beta`, and `IL-18`, supporting a direct anti-pyroptotic effect in the cell type emphasized by the study. The authors therefore frame curcumin’s protection against post-stroke white matter injury and sensorimotor deficits as being at least partly mediated by inhibition of microglial pyroptosis. ^[omcl1552127-125.md:224-228, omcl1552127-125.md:935-956, omcl1552127-125.md:2272-2281]

## Proposed mechanism

Mechanistically, the paper reports that curcumin suppressed activation of the [[NF-kappaB]] pathway in vivo and in vitro. After MCAO or LPS+ATP stimulation, phosphorylation of `IKKalpha/beta`, `IkappaBalpha`, and `p65` increased, and curcumin reversed these changes. The authors propose that curcumin inhibits NF-kappaB signaling, thereby limiting downstream NLRP3 inflammasome activation and microglial pyroptosis. ^[omcl1552127-125.md:1655-1824, omcl1552127-125.md:2393-2419]

Additional support for the NLRP3-dependent component came from knockdown experiments. In mice, AAV-based `NLRP3 shRNA` reduced white matter lesions and improved adhesive-removal performance after MCAO, and it enhanced the protective effects of curcumin. In primary microglia, `NLRP3` siRNA likewise strengthened curcumin’s inhibitory effects on pyroptosis-related proteins. ^[omcl1552127-125.md:1635-1654, omcl1552127-125.md:1721-1728, omcl1552127-125.md:2295-2310]

## Conclusion

In this study, post-stroke curcumin treatment was associated with less white matter damage in the corpus callosum and striatum, reduced brain tissue loss, and improved sensorimotor recovery over 21 days after ischemic stroke in mice. The source attributes these benefits, at least in part, to suppression of `NF-kappaB` signaling, inhibition of the `NLRP3` inflammasome, and reduced microglia/macrophage pyroptosis. ^[omcl1552127-125.md:41-44, omcl1552127-125.md:2452-2455, omcl1552127-125.md:2475-2481]
