# Photothermal conversion by CDT-BODIPY

Photothermal conversion by CDT-BODIPY refers to the compound’s ability to convert 660 nm light into heat in aqueous solution. In the source study, CDT-BODIPY was a water-soluble donor-acceptor-donor conjugated oligomer designed from two [[cyclopenta-dithiophene]] (CDT) units and a [[BODIPY]] acceptor, with photothermal behavior arising from its low bandgap and intramolecular charge transfer (ICT) character. ^[intramolecular-charge-transfer-based-conjugated-oligomer-with-fluorescence-efficient-photodynamics-and-photothermal-activities.md:13-29, intramolecular-charge-transfer-based-conjugated-oligomer-with-fluorescence-efficient-photodynamics-and-photothermal-activities.md:115-130]

The photothermal performance was measured by irradiating aqueous CDT-BODIPY with a 660 nm laser at different power densities and monitoring temperature rise. In one heating/cooling test, a 0.3 mg/mL solution reached an 18 C increase after 12 minutes at 1.5 W/cm2, while water showed almost no temperature elevation. The photothermal conversion efficiency was calculated as 33.1%. ^[intramolecular-charge-transfer-based-conjugated-oligomer-with-fluorescence-efficient-photodynamics-and-photothermal-activities.md:214-218, intramolecular-charge-transfer-based-conjugated-oligomer-with-fluorescence-efficient-photodynamics-and-photothermal-activities.md:455-476]

The study attributes this behavior to the compound’s narrow HOMO-LUMO gap, which supports nonradiative decay and heat generation, while also preserving red fluorescence and ROS-producing photodynamics. The authors note that CDT-BODIPY formed micelles or aggregates in water and that its amphiphilic structure likely contributed to reduced emission efficiency in aqueous media. ^[intramolecular-charge-transfer-based-conjugated-oligomer-with-fluorescence-efficient-photodynamics-and-photothermal-activities.md:321-326, intramolecular-charge-transfer-based-conjugated-oligomer-with-fluorescence-efficient-photodynamics-and-photothermal-activities.md:477-509]

In the reported antibacterial application, the photothermal effect worked together with ROS generation and quaternary ammonium-mediated binding to suppress [[Escherichia coli]], [[Staphylococcus aureus]], and [[Candida albicans]] under 660 nm irradiation. The source describes this as a synchronous PDT/PTT effect guided by dual-wavelength fluorescence imaging. ^[intramolecular-charge-transfer-based-conjugated-oligomer-with-fluorescence-efficient-photodynamics-and-photothermal-activities.md:510-543, intramolecular-charge-transfer-based-conjugated-oligomer-with-fluorescence-efficient-photodynamics-and-photothermal-activities.md:544-605, intramolecular-charge-transfer-based-conjugated-oligomer-with-fluorescence-efficient-photodynamics-and-photothermal-activities.md:607-626]
