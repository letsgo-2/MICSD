# Canonical inflammasome-induced pyroptosis via caspase-1 and GSDMD

Canonical inflammasome-induced pyroptosis is a form of [[pyroptosis]] that is mostly triggered by inflammasome complexes and proceeds through activation of [[caspase-1]] and cleavage of [[Gasdermin D|GSDMD]]. In the reviewed source, pyroptosis is described as regulated cell death that depends on gasdermin-family pore formation in the cytoplasmic membrane and is often associated with inflammatory caspase activation. ^[pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:104-109, pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:125-146]

## Core pathway

Several major inflammasomes are listed in the source as participants in the canonical pathway, including [[NLRP1 inflammasome|NLRP1]], [[NLRP3 inflammasome|NLRP3]], [[NLRC4 inflammasome|NLRC4]], [[AIM2 inflammasome|AIM2]], and pyrin. These inflammasomes sense diverse pathogenic stimuli and engage homotypic or heterotypic `PYD/CARD` interactions to recruit [[ASC]] and pro-caspase-1 into an inflammasome complex. ^[pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:127-139]

Once assembled, the inflammasome activates caspase-1. Active caspase-1 then cleaves GSDMD to generate the N-terminal fragment, `GSDMD-NT`, which is the pore-forming effector in this pathway. The source also notes an exception within the canonical context: [[NLRC4 inflammasome|NLRC4]] can directly bind caspase-1 and induce GSDMD cleavage without relying on ASC. ^[pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:139-143]

The source explains that gasdermin proteins contain inhibitory C-terminal and pore-forming N-terminal domains linked by a flexible region. In the uncleaved state, the C-terminal domain suppresses the N-terminal domain; after cleavage, the released N-terminal fragment oligomerizes in biomembranes and forms pores, thereby executing pyroptosis. ^[pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:113-124]

## Cytokine maturation and release

In parallel with GSDMD cleavage, active caspase-1 processes pro-`IL-1beta` and pro-`IL-18` into their active forms by recognizing tetrapeptide sequences. These cytokines are then released from the cell through the membrane pores formed by GSDMD. ^[pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:143-146, pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:219-222]

The source further states that pyroptosis is associated not only with release of `IL-1beta` and `IL-18`, but also with release of `IL-1alpha` and `HMGB1`. This places canonical inflammasome-driven pyroptosis within a broader inflammatory-output program rather than a membrane-rupture event alone. ^[pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:147-149, pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:383-387]

## Morphologic and functional features

Historically, the source describes pyroptosis as a caspase-1-mediated programmed cell death characterized by nuclear constriction, DNA fragmentation, cell swelling, and eventual rupture, together with inflammatory factor release. In its comparison table, pyroptosis is further distinguished by membrane pore formation, inflammatory signaling, and release of `IL-18`, `IL-1beta`, `IL-1alpha`, `HMGB1`, and `LDH`. ^[pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:90-99, pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:373-389]

Compared with [[apoptosis]], the source presents pyroptosis as faster and more strongly inflammatory. It contrasts pyroptotic swelling and membrane pore formation with apoptotic shrinkage and preservation of membrane integrity. ^[pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:29-32, pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:373-389]

## Position within pyroptosis mechanisms

The reviewed article treats the canonical inflammasome pathway as one major route to pyroptosis and distinguishes it from the [[noncanonical inflammasome-induced pyroptosis]] pathway, which is driven by caspase-11 in mice or caspase-4/5 in humans. In contrast, the canonical route centers on inflammasome assembly and caspase-1 activation upstream of GSDMD cleavage. ^[pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:125-146, pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:152-192]

The source also places this pathway in the broader history of pyroptosis research: caspase-1-dependent inflammatory cell death was recognized before GSDMD was identified as a key pyroptosis substrate, and later work clarified that gasdermin-mediated pore formation is the execution mechanism. ^[pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:83-109, pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:94-101]
