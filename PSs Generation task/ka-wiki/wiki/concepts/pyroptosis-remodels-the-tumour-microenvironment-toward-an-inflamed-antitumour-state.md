# Pyroptosis remodels the tumour microenvironment toward an inflamed antitumour state

Pyroptosis is presented in the source as a proinflammatory form of cell death whose induction in a subset of tumour cells can trigger broad antitumour immunity. Using a bioorthogonal system to release active gasdermin in tumours, the study found that pyroptosis in fewer than 15% of tumour cells was sufficient to clear entire 4T1 mammary tumour grafts, and that this effect correlated with augmented antitumour immune responses. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:21-41]

## Overview

In this study, pyroptosis did not act only as a direct tumour-cell killing mechanism. The data indicate that local gasdermin activation at the tumour site reshaped the tumour microenvironment into a more inflamed and immune-active state, with tumour-wide regression despite pyroptosis occurring in only a minority of cells. Intratumoural delivery produced the same tumour regression as intravenous delivery, supporting the interpretation that the effect depended on tumour-site pyroptosis and its local immune consequences. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:633-652]

## Evidence for tumour microenvironment remodeling

After treatment with NP–GSDMA3 and Phe-BF3, tumours contained more propidium-iodide-positive pyroptotic cells than controls, but these cells still represented only a small fraction of the full tumour-cell population. Even so, whole-tumour growth was halted or reversed, including in a model where only 10-30% of inoculated cells were engineered to undergo inducible pyroptosis. This mismatch between the fraction of pyroptotic cells and the scale of tumour clearance supports a tumour-microenvironment-mediated antitumour effect rather than a purely cell-autonomous killing effect. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:638-652]

Flow-cytometry analysis showed markedly increased infiltration of `CD3+` T cells in treated tumours. Both `CD4+` and `CD8+` T-cell populations increased, while the proportion of `FOXP3+ CD4+` regulatory T cells decreased. These changes were not seen with pore-forming-deficient mutant gasdermin, linking the immune remodeling specifically to pyroptotic activity. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:834-846]

Single-cell RNA sequencing of `CD45+` tumour leukocytes further showed a broader shift in immune composition. Treated tumours had increased [[CD4-positive T cells|CD4+]] T cells, [[CD8-positive T cells|CD8+]] T cells, and natural killer cells, alongside decreased monocytes, neutrophils, and myeloid-derived suppressor cells. The [[macrophage polarization]] pattern also shifted toward an antitumour state, with increased M1 macrophages and fewer `Arg1`-positive M2-like cells. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:847-856]

The transcriptional state of tumour-infiltrating immune cells also became more inflammatory and cytotoxic. Genes encoding chemotactic cytokines such as `Ccl5`, `Cxcl9`, and `Cxcl10`, as well as lymphocyte-activation genes including `Cd69` and `Klrk1`, were upregulated after pyroptosis-inducing treatment. Antitumour effector genes including `Ifng`, `Gzma`, `Gzmb`, `Gzmk`, and `Fasl` were also increased, whereas protumoural or immunosuppressive genes such as `Csf1`, `Vegfa`, `Arg1`, `Cd274` (`PD-L1`), and `Pdcd1lg2` (`PD-L2`) were downregulated. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:856-866]

## Inflammatory mediators linked to the shift

The study reports increased `IL-1beta`, `IL-18`, and `HMGB1` in both serum and tumour after pyroptosis-inducing treatment. Functional neutralization experiments showed that blocking `IL-1beta` severely inhibited the tumour regression caused by pyroptosis, whereas blocking `IL-18` had only a partial effect and blocking `HMGB1` had no effect in this assay. The authors therefore identify `IL-1beta`, probably from activated macrophages, as required for the antitumour immunity induced by tumour-cell pyroptosis. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:867-876, a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:1720-1734]

## Dependence on adaptive immunity

The remodeled microenvironment was functionally dependent on adaptive immune cells. Tumour regression was absent in athymic `Nu/Nu` mice lacking mature T cells, and depletion of either `CD4+` or `CD8+` T cells blocked the tumour regression induced by pyroptosis. In this system, both helper and cytotoxic T-cell compartments were therefore required for the antitumour effect. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:834-846]

## Conversion of a cold tumour toward an inflamed state

The source explicitly frames low or ineffective tumour inflammation as a reason some tumours are resistant to checkpoint blockade and describes such tumours as "cold." In the 4T1 model, anti-PD1 alone had no meaningful effect, and one round of pyroptosis induction alone also did not prevent tumour growth. However, when that same limited pyroptosis-inducing treatment was followed by anti-PD1, tumour volume and weight were markedly reduced, indicating that pyroptosis-induced inflammation converted the tumour microenvironment into a state more responsive to [[immune checkpoint blockade]] and more supportive of antitumour immunity. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:877-892]

## Interpretation

Within this paper, pyroptosis remodels the tumour microenvironment toward an inflamed antitumour state by increasing effector lymphocyte infiltration, reducing suppressive immune populations, shifting macrophage features toward M1-like states, elevating inflammatory mediators, and inducing immune gene programs associated with chemotaxis, activation, and cytotoxic function. The resulting antitumour effect extends beyond the pyroptotic cells themselves and can sensitize otherwise resistant tumours to [[PD-1 blockade]]. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:33-41, a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:847-892]
