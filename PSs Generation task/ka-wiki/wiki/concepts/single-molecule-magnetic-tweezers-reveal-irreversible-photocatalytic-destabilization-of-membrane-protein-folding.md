# Single-molecule magnetic tweezers reveal irreversible photocatalytic destabilization of membrane protein folding

This study used single-molecule magnetic tweezers to test how intracellular membrane-focused [[oxidative photocatalysis]] affects the stability of a membrane protein fold. The authors report that photocatalysis by the amphiphilic membrane-localized catalyst `BTP` oxidatively damaged membrane proteins, and that the tweezer assay directly showed marked destabilization of membrane protein folding. ^[oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:33-45, oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:149-160]

## Experimental system

For the folding measurements, the authors used the E. coli rhomboid protease `GlpG`, a six-transmembrane-helix protein described as a widely studied model for membrane-protein folding and stability. In the single-molecule assay, an individual GlpG molecule was embedded in a bicelle lipid bilayer disc, then repeatedly unfolded and refolded by magnetic tweezers through force scans between `1 pN` and `50 pN`. ^[oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:321-367, oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:389-391, oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:913-940]

The assay design was intended to isolate changes in folding stability from bulk aggregation effects. The paper states that this single-molecule approach excludes aggregation events, allowing the experiment to focus specifically on the effect of photocatalytic oxidation on protein stability. ^[oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:382-388]

## What the tweezers showed

Under blue-light exposure without `BTP`, GlpG underwent reproducible reversible unfolding for more than one hundred pulling cycles, with unfolding forces distributed around `34 +/- 5.8 pN`. This established the baseline behavior of the intact membrane protein in the reconstituted bilayer environment. ^[oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:392-398]

When `BTP` was added and blue light was applied, the unfolding forces dropped sharply to below `15 pN` at a critical pulling-cycle point labeled `C0`; at `20 uM BTP`, unfolding even became unmeasurable. After this transition, the unfolding-force values did not recover to the normal level observed before damage. ^[oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:399-403]

The authors interpret this behavior as evidence of irreversible oxidative damage to the membrane protein fold. Fresh bicelles injected after the `C0` point did not restore normal unfolding behavior, even though fresh bicelles can rapidly reconstitute an intact membrane environment. On that basis, the paper concludes that `BTP` photocatalysis caused irreversible destabilization of the native fold rather than a merely reversible change in membrane surroundings. ^[oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:404-411]

## Relationship to bulk stability assays

The single-molecule findings were consistent with bulk biochemical measurements on GlpG. In SDS-PAGE assays, GlpG band intensity decreased and higher-molecular-weight aggregates increased only when `BTP` and blue light were combined, indicating destabilization and aggregation after oxidative damage. Thermal denaturation assays likewise showed reduced resistance to unfolding, with the transition midpoint temperature falling from `75 °C` without `BTP` to `35 °C` at `150 uM BTP`. ^[oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:369-381]

Together, these experiments support the paper’s broader claim that membrane-focused photocatalysis can directly damage membrane-protein structure. In the discussion, the authors state that the single-molecule tweezer approach verified disruption of membrane protein folding and link this destabilization to downstream accumulation of misfolded proteins, stress in protein-quality-control pathways, and eventual [[pyroptosis]]. ^[oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:678-692]

## Context in the study

Within the larger study, this result provided a mechanistic bridge between localized oxidative chemistry and cellular stress responses. The paper argues that `BTP` generates `H2O2` and hydroxyl radicals from water, that oxidation occurs preferentially in membrane proteins rather than lipids in cells, and that many of the oxidized targets are associated with [[protein quality control]], including pathways related to the [[endoplasmic reticulum]], [[Golgi apparatus]], and [[mitochondria]]. ^[oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:204-246, oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:300-313, oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:419-458, oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:459-494]

The authors therefore position irreversible folding destabilization as an early physical consequence of photocatalytic oxidation that helps explain later cellular phenotypes. These downstream effects include accumulation of misfolded proteins, maladaptive unfolded protein response, cation mobilization, activation of caspase-4/5, cleavage of [[Gasdermin D|GSDMD]], and non-canonical [[pyroptosis]]. ^[oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:495-527, oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:653-676, oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:678-699]
