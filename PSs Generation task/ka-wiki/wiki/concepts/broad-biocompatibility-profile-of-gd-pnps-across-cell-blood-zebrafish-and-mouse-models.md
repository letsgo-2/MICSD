# Broad biocompatibility profile of Gd-PNPs across cell, blood, zebrafish, and mouse models

[[Gd-PNPs]] were reported to have a broad biocompatibility profile across in vitro and in vivo systems in a study of gadolinium-porphyrin supramolecular nanotheranostics for [[fluorescence imaging]], [[magnetic resonance imaging]], and [[photodynamic therapy]]. The biocompatibility assessment covered chemical stability, cultured mammalian cells, human red blood cells, [[zebrafish]] development, and histology of major organs in mice. ^[d1nr04489c-110.md:377-383, d1nr04489c-110.md:396-426, d1nr04489c-110.md:590-606]

## Overview

The study evaluated biocompatibility using several complementary readouts. In vitro, the authors used MTT assays in HeLa and CT 26 cells and a hemolysis assay in human blood cells. In vivo, they used zebrafish and BALB/c mice as animal models for biotoxicity evaluation, with zebrafish morphology, survival, and hatching as developmental readouts and mouse organ histology as a tissue-level safety readout. ^[d1nr04489c-110.md:186-212, d1nr04489c-110.md:377-426]

A design feature intended to reduce material-associated toxicity was the addition of a negatively charged `mPEG-PGA` outer layer. The paper states that this coating was introduced to minimize possible surface charge-induced biotoxicity and that it reduced the particle surface potential from `+50.3 mV` to about `-0.1 mV`. ^[d1nr04489c-110.md:309-317]

## Chemical and colloidal stability

The source reports that Gd-PNPs released only a negligible amount of free `Gd3+` during up to 48 hours of incubation in a leakage assay using xylenol orange as the indicator. The authors interpret this as evidence of good chemical stability and as supportive of biocompatibility. ^[d1nr04489c-110.md:377-383]

Gd-PNPs also showed good [[colloidal stability]] in fetal bovine serum, with no obvious size change and no observable precipitation for up to 48 hours. The conclusions section further states that the self-assembly strategy improved colloidal stability and dispersity. ^[d1nr04489c-110.md:393-395, d1nr04489c-110.md:598-600]

## Cell compatibility

In cultured cells, Gd-PNPs showed low dark cytotoxicity in both HeLa and CT 26 cells. The MTT assay showed that cell viability remained above `88%` even at `400 ug/mL` Gd-PNPs. ^[d1nr04489c-110.md:383-386]

Additional qualitative support came from imaging experiments in CT 26 cells. The paper notes that cell morphology remained intact during the time-dependent uptake study, which the authors cite as further evidence of good biocompatibility during cellular internalization. ^[d1nr04489c-110.md:432-439]

## Blood compatibility

The hemolysis assay using human red blood cells indicated good [[hemocompatibility]]. At concentrations up to `400 ug/mL`, Gd-PNPs produced only about `5.6%` hemolysis. ^[d1nr04489c-110.md:386-389]

Microscopy of red blood cells provided consistent qualitative evidence: the cells remained intact even after treatment with `400 ug/mL` Gd-PNPs. ^[d1nr04489c-110.md:390-392]

## Zebrafish biotoxicity

The study used zebrafish as an in vivo model for biotoxicity evaluation and describes zebrafish as useful for this purpose because of their short generation time, rapid development, low maintenance cost, and similarities with humans. ^[d1nr04489c-110.md:396-399]

According to the source, Gd-PNP treatment did not affect zebrafish development. Treated zebrafish showed morphologies similar to untreated controls at different developmental stages, and no lesion or teratogenesis was observed. ^[d1nr04489c-110.md:399-404]

Quantitative developmental outcomes were likewise favorable. Even at `400 ug/mL`, treated zebrafish showed survival and hatching rates above `90%`, supporting the conclusion of low biotoxicity in this model. ^[d1nr04489c-110.md:404-407]

In a separate imaging experiment, zebrafish showed successful internalization of Gd-PNPs, visible as bright red fluorescence in the yolk sac, without noticeable lesions. ^[d1nr04489c-110.md:440-445]

## Mouse organ safety

In mice, an H&E staining assay of major organs showed no observable organ damage or inflammation after Gd-PNP treatment. The examined organs included the heart, liver, spleen, lungs, and kidneys. ^[d1nr04489c-110.md:408-412]

The paper summarizes these combined findings by stating that Gd-PNPs caused no obvious side effects in the examined animals and were therefore considered safe enough for further evaluation in imaging-guided [[photodynamic therapy]]. The conclusion section similarly describes the in vivo study as demonstrating excellent biocompatibility. ^[d1nr04489c-110.md:411-426, d1nr04489c-110.md:602-605]

## Context within the therapeutic study

Biocompatibility findings were consistent with later therapeutic observations in CT 26 tumor-bearing mice. During the in vivo photodynamic therapy experiment, no obvious decrease in body weight was observed across the treatment period, which the authors interpreted as indicating no systemic side effects of Gd-PNPs under the tested conditions. ^[d1nr04489c-110.md:561-567]

Overall, the source presents Gd-PNPs as a nanotheranostic platform with safety signals spanning molecular stability, cell viability, blood compatibility, normal zebrafish development, and absence of histologically detectable organ injury in mice. ^[d1nr04489c-110.md:377-426, d1nr04489c-110.md:590-606]
