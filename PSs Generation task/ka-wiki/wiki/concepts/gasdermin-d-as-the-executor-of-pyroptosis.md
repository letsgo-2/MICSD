# Gasdermin D as the executor of pyroptosis

[[Gasdermin D]] (GSDMD) is described as an executor of [[pyroptosis]], the pro-inflammatory form of programmed cell death characterized by plasma membrane pore formation, release of inflammatory contents, cell swelling, and lysis. In the pyroptosis field, GSDMD became especially important after its identification in 2015 as the protein responsible for forming pores in the plasma membrane during this process. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:43-46, a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:88-104]

Pyroptosis can be triggered through both canonical and non-canonical [[inflammasome]] pathways. The source states that canonical activation proceeds through caspase-1, while non-canonical activation proceeds through caspase-4, caspase-5, and caspase-11; both routes can lead to pyroptosis. In this framework, inflammatory caspases directly cleave and activate GSDMD, linking inflammasome activation to membrane pore formation and cell death. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:88-95, a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:584-591, a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:646-672]

In 2015, Feng Shao and colleagues identified GSDMD through CRISPR-Cas9 screens of caspase-11- and caspase-1-mediated pyroptosis in mouse bone marrow macrophages. The study proposed that GSDMD is a central downstream mediator of caspase-1 and caspase-4/5/11 and is essential for interleukin-1 beta secretion, helping define the molecular basis of pyroptosis and supporting a view of pyroptosis as gasdermin-mediated programmed necrosis. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:674-680, a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:518-532]

GSDMD is a member of the [[gasdermin family]], which also includes gasdermin A, B, C, E, and DFNB59. Under steady-state conditions, GSDMD is generally located in the cytoplasm in an auto-inhibited state. When activated inflammatory caspases cleave GSDMD, they generate an N-terminal P30 fragment and a C-terminal P20 fragment. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:680-686]

The N-terminal fragment, GSDMD-NT, binds specifically to the lipid bilayer of the cell membrane and forms small pores about 10-15 nm in diameter, often referred to as gasdermin pores. These pores disrupt membrane integrity, allow active secretion of pro-inflammatory mediators such as [[IL-1beta]] and [[IL-18]], and permit water influx that drives cell swelling, lysis, and pyroptotic death. The C-terminal fragment contributes to autoinhibition by suppressing the cytotoxicity of the N-terminal fragment and promoting its solubility when bound. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:686-695]

Because GSDMD pore formation is the execution step of pyroptosis, it sits at the point where upstream inflammatory sensing is converted into cell destruction and cytokine release. The source also notes that the assembly and activation of inflammasomes are critical to pyroptosis, since they activate inflammatory caspases that then process both cytokines and GSDMD. Among inflammasomes associated with pyroptosis, [[NLRP3 inflammasome]] is identified as the most extensively studied. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:88-104, a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:592-608, a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:650-658]

Structural work cited in the source further clarifies how GSDMD executes pyroptosis. GSDMD pores were reported to contain 31 to 34 subunits, with a 33-subunit pore observed at the highest resolution. The pore includes a large beta-barrel transmembrane structure and a globular domain on the cytosolic side, while a prepore state lacking the beta-barrel may represent an intermediate during pore assembly. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:696-712]

The source also summarizes evidence that charge interactions are important for GSDMD function. In addition to interactions involving the alpha1 helix, GSDMD has charge-rich regions important for membrane binding, and a hydrophobic beta1-beta2 loop affects pore formation efficiency. The pore interior accumulates negative charges, and this was used to explain preferential release of mature interleukin-1 over its precursor. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:712-724]

GSDMD-dependent execution of pyroptosis is not entirely irreversible at the membrane level. After activation of canonical or non-canonical inflammasomes, calcium influx through GSDMD pores can initiate membrane repair by recruiting the ESCRT machinery to damaged membrane regions. Inhibition of ESCRT-III significantly increases the rate of pyroptosis, indicating that endogenous repair mechanisms can counteract GSDMD-driven membrane damage. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:725-733]

The literature surveyed in the source places GSDMD at the center of pyroptosis research. In keyword analysis, GSDMD appears among the most frequent terms, and co-cited reference analysis shows that several of the top references in the field focus on gasdermin proteins, especially GSDMD. The most frequently co-cited reference was “Cleavage of GSDMD by inflammatory caspases determines pyroptotic cell death,” reflecting the importance of this discovery to the field’s development. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:395-397, a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:416-420, a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:464-467, a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:561-570]

The source further describes GSDMD as relevant to crosstalk between pyroptosis and other cell death pathways. In monocytes and macrophages, active caspase-1 induced apoptosis in the absence of GSDMD, which supported the conclusion that GSDMD was the caspase-1 substrate responsible for pyroptosis in those cells. Other studies cited in the source also reported caspase-8-mediated cleavage of GSDMD in infection-related settings, reinforcing GSDMD’s role as a downstream effector at the intersection of inflammatory and apoptotic signaling. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:770-789, a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:791-809]

Because GSDMD is the pore-forming effector of pyroptosis, it has also emerged as a therapeutic target. The source notes that researchers have sought inhibitors of GSDMD to block pyroptosis, including necrosulfonamide and disulfiram, both reported to inhibit GSDMD pore formation by covalently modifying a key cysteine residue, Cys191 in human or Cys192 in mouse. Even so, the source emphasizes that large-scale clinical data on the safety and efficacy of pyroptosis inhibitors are still lacking. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:847-888]
