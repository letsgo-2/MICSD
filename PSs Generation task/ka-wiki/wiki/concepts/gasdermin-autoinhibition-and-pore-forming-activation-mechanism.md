# Gasdermin autoinhibition and pore-forming activation mechanism

Gasdermin-mediated [[pyroptosis]] depends on a conserved structural arrangement found in GSDMA, GSDMB, GSDMC, GSDMD, and GSDME. These proteins contain an N-terminal pore-forming domain and a C-terminal regulatory domain connected by a linker region. Under normal conditions, the linker permits the C-terminal domain to fold over the N-terminal domain and suppress its lethal activity, establishing an autoinhibited state. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:224-232]

Activation occurs when caspases or granzymes cleave the gasdermin protein at the linker site. This cleavage disrupts the autoinhibitory arrangement, freeing the N-terminal fragment from repression by the C-terminal domain. In the review’s summary of pyroptosis pathways, activated caspase-1 and caspase-4/5 are described as cleaving autoinhibited GSDMD at its linker region to release the N-terminal domain from the repressor C-terminal domain, and similar cleavage logic is noted for other gasdermins in alternative pathways. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:230-233, pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:299-307]

Once liberated, the N-terminal fragment translocates to cellular membranes, including the plasma membrane and mitochondrial membranes. After membrane binding, the N-terminal domain oligomerizes and forms beta-barrel transmembrane pores. These pores are the core execution step of gasdermin-mediated pyroptosis. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:233-239]

Pore formation has two major consequences. First, it enables the secretion or release of pro-inflammatory contents, including [[IL-1beta]] and [[IL-18]]. Second, it disrupts osmotic barrier function, causing increased osmotic pressure, cell swelling, membrane rupture, and eventual lysis. These features distinguish pyroptotic death from apoptosis despite some shared nuclear changes such as chromatin condensation and DNA fragmentation. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:186-197, pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:236-239, pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:301-304]

In the principal pyroptosis pathways, this mechanism is most clearly described for [[Gasdermin D|GSDMD]]. In the canonical [[inflammasome]] pathway, DAMP or PAMP sensing leads to inflammasome assembly, caspase-1 activation, cleavage of GSDMD, and release of mature IL-1beta and IL-18 through GSDMD-N-formed membrane pores. In the non-canonical pathway, human caspase-4/5 or mouse caspase-11 directly respond to intracellular LPS and trigger pyroptosis through GSDMD cleavage; the resulting potassium efflux can then activate the NLRP3 inflammasome and promote caspase-1 activity. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:242-267, pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:268-286]

The same general activation logic extends beyond GSDMD. The review notes alternative pathways in which caspase-3 cleaves [[Gasdermin E|GSDME]], caspase-8 can cleave GSDMD, GSDME, or GSDMC in particular contexts, granzyme B can cleave GSDME, and granzyme A can cleave GSDMB. Across these examples, proteolytic cleavage relieves autoinhibition and enables the pore-forming N-terminal fragment to drive membrane permeabilization and pyroptotic death. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:287-330]

This mechanism helps explain why gasdermins are central effectors of pyroptosis rather than merely markers of the pathway. The decisive event is not only upstream caspase activation but the conversion of an autoinhibited gasdermin into an active membrane pore-forming fragment that executes inflammatory cell death. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:217-239]
