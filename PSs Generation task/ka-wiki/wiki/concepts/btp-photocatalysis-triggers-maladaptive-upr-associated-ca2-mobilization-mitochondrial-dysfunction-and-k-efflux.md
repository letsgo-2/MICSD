# BTP photocatalysis triggers maladaptive UPR-associated Ca2+ mobilization, mitochondrial dysfunction, and K+ efflux

`BTP` is an intracellular membrane-localized amphiphilic photocatalyst that oxidatively damages membrane proteins, especially proteins linked to [[protein quality control]]. In the study, this membrane-focused oxidation was proposed to impair protein folding stability, promote accumulation of misfolded proteins, and aggravate stress responses in the [[endoplasmic reticulum]], [[Golgi apparatus]], and [[mitochondria]]. ^[oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:149-160, oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:459-494]

Proteomic analysis linked BTP-induced oxidation to protein quality control pathways. Among the strongly oxidized membrane proteins, many localized to the ER, Golgi apparatus, and mitochondria, and 97 proteins were grouped into networks involving [[unfolded protein response|UPR]] and ER-associated degradation, ER-Golgi transport, mitochondrial trafficking and transport, and lipid metabolism. The authors therefore focused on deterioration of protein quality control and escalation of ER and mitochondrial UPR stress as a central consequence of BTP photocatalysis. ^[oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:463-494]

The paper explicitly connects this stress state to cation mobilization. It states that destabilization of protein structures related to protein quality control leads to irreversible accumulation of misfolded proteins and enhanced stress on the ER, Golgi apparatus, and mitochondria, and notes that failure of protein quality control with subsequent maladaptive UPR has been reported to trigger `Ca2+` mobilization. ^[oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:495-503]

Using the `Ca2+` indicator Rhod-2, the authors observed a considerable increase in mitochondrial `Ca2+` concentration after light exposure during BTP photocatalysis. In time-series confocal microscopy, the Rhod-2 signal rose markedly between 20 and 30 seconds, and line-cut analysis showed that the increased Rhod-2 signal merged with the MitoTracker signal, supporting mitochondrial `Ca2+` uptake. Flow-cytometry measurements with Rhod-2 showed the same overall tendency. ^[oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:503-513, oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:605-617]

The study associates this `Ca2+` loading with mitochondrial structural damage. It states that accumulation of misfolded proteins and `Ca2+` leads to osmotic swelling and mitochondrial dysfunction, and the authors directly observed mitochondrial swelling together with fission and fusion after BTP photocatalysis, describing these changes as part of the mitochondrial protein quality control response. ^[oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:514-519, oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:617-620]

Functional evidence of mitochondrial injury came from the mitochondrial membrane potential assay. After BTP photocatalysis, TMRE fluorescence was dramatically diminished, which the authors interpreted as damage to mitochondrial membrane potential and mitochondrial function. ^[oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:519-523, oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:621-626]

In parallel with mitochondrial `Ca2+` uptake and dysfunction, the authors detected intracellular `K+` efflux by flow cytometry using ION `K+` Green-2. They summarize these findings by stating that oxidative photocatalysis induces maladaptive UPR and cation mobilization in response to oxidative damage of intracellular membranes. ^[oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:523-527, oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:627-631]

In the discussion, the paper presents these events as part of a broader mechanism in which oxidation of protein quality control-related membrane proteins disrupts folding stability, stimulates accumulation of misfolded proteins, and is followed by ER stress, maladaptive UPR, and cation mobilization. These responses are then positioned upstream of caspase-4/5 activation, [[Gasdermin D|GSDMD]] cleavage, and non-canonical [[pyroptosis]]. ^[oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:677-692]
