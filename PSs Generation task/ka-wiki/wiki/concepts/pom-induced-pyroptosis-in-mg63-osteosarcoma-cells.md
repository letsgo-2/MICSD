# POM-induced pyroptosis in MG63 osteosarcoma cells

POM-induced pyroptosis in this study refers to a form of regulated cell death observed in MG63 osteosarcoma cells treated with the polyoxometalate compound {BiW8}. The authors report that {BiW8} inhibited MG63 cell proliferation, induced morphological changes consistent with pyroptosis, increased LDH release, reduced ATP levels, and raised intracellular ROS while lowering GSH content. ^[biw8o30-exerts-antitumor-effect-by-triggering-pyroptosis-and-upregulating-reactive-oxygen-species.md:136-145, biw8o30-exerts-antitumor-effect-by-triggering-pyroptosis-and-upregulating-reactive-oxygen-species.md:248-262, biw8o30-exerts-antitumor-effect-by-triggering-pyroptosis-and-upregulating-reactive-oxygen-species.md:294-343, biw8o30-exerts-antitumor-effect-by-triggering-pyroptosis-and-upregulating-reactive-oxygen-species.md:347-374]

## Compound and model

The compound studied was a 10-nuclear heteroatom-modified polyoxometalate, abbreviated {BiW8}. It was tested against several cell lines, but MG63 cells showed the most pronounced growth inhibition and were selected for further study. ^[biw8o30-exerts-antitumor-effect-by-triggering-pyroptosis-and-upregulating-reactive-oxygen-species.md:136-146, biw8o30-exerts-antitumor-effect-by-triggering-pyroptosis-and-upregulating-reactive-oxygen-species.md:248-262]

## Evidence for pyroptosis

The authors used transmission electron microscopy, LDH release, and ATP assays to characterize the death phenotype. MG63 cells treated with 127.3 μM {BiW8} showed cell swelling, membrane rupture, content leakage, nuclear condensation, and loss of nucleoli, which the authors distinguish from typical apoptotic morphology and interpret as pyroptosis. ^[biw8o30-exerts-antitumor-effect-by-triggering-pyroptosis-and-upregulating-reactive-oxygen-species.md:294-343]

## Oxidative stress and glutathione

{BiW8} increased ROS levels in MG63 cells to about twice the control level. The compound also reduced intracellular GSH over time, with GSH content falling by 59%, 65%, 75%, 94%, and 97% at 6, 12, 24, 36, and 48 hours, respectively. The source further reports reductions in total glutathione and GSSG after 48 hours. ^[biw8o30-exerts-antitumor-effect-by-triggering-pyroptosis-and-upregulating-reactive-oxygen-species.md:347-374, biw8o30-exerts-antitumor-effect-by-triggering-pyroptosis-and-upregulating-reactive-oxygen-species.md:410-429]

## Transcriptomic context

RNA sequencing and qRT-PCR identified upregulated pyroptosis-related genes, including CASP3, CASP4, CASP5, GSDMB, GSDME, IL18, NLRP3, NLRC4, NLRP10, and TXNIP. The same analyses also showed downregulation of genes involved in glutathione metabolism and DNA repair/replication, supporting the authors’ view that {BiW8} affects multiple antitumor pathways. ^[biw8o30-exerts-antitumor-effect-by-triggering-pyroptosis-and-upregulating-reactive-oxygen-species.md:439-487, biw8o30-exerts-antitumor-effect-by-triggering-pyroptosis-and-upregulating-reactive-oxygen-species.md:502-564]

## Interpretation

The paper concludes that {BiW8} exerts antitumor effects in MG63 cells by triggering pyroptosis, suppressing glutathione generation, depleting GSH, increasing ROS, and inhibiting DNA repair. The authors state that this is the first report identifying POM-induced pyroptosis as a mechanism of cancer cell death. ^[biw8o30-exerts-antitumor-effect-by-triggering-pyroptosis-and-upregulating-reactive-oxygen-species.md:502-567]
