# Ultra-rapid photopyroptosis triggered within 10 seconds at low light dose

Ultra-rapid photopyroptosis triggered within 10 seconds at low light dose refers to a reported photodynamic cell-death response in which a designed photosensitizer induced pyroptotic morphology after only `10 s` of irradiation, with a stated light dose of `0.4 J cm^-2`. The study presents this as an unusually efficient form of [[photopyroptosis]] compared with earlier approaches that required substantially higher light doses, reported as up to `60 J cm^-2`. ^[a-general-molecular-structural-design-for-highly-efficient-photopyroptosis-that-can-be-activated-within-10-s-irradiation.md:23-26, a-general-molecular-structural-design-for-highly-efficient-photopyroptosis-that-can-be-activated-within-10-s-irradiation.md:548-550]

## Overview

The source frames this phenomenon as part of a general molecular design strategy for photosensitizers. That strategy clamps cationic moieties such as pyridinium or imidazolium onto a photosensitive core to strengthen electrostatic interactions with mitochondria, promote targeting of both the inner and outer mitochondrial membranes, and maximize site-specific photogenerated reactive oxygen species for [[GSDME]]-mediated pyroptosis. ^[a-general-molecular-structural-design-for-highly-efficient-photopyroptosis-that-can-be-activated-within-10-s-irradiation.md:18-25, a-general-molecular-structural-design-for-highly-efficient-photopyroptosis-that-can-be-activated-within-10-s-irradiation.md:112-123]

The reported efficiency is tied to three practical parameters the paper highlights for photopyroptosis: reagent concentration, excitation wavelength, and light dosage. The selected representative compound, `1d`, was chosen because it can be excited by red light and was described as outperforming previously reported related photosensitizers in this combined design space. ^[a-general-molecular-structural-design-for-highly-efficient-photopyroptosis-that-can-be-activated-within-10-s-irradiation.md:131-145]

## Structural basis

According to the source, compounds following the design rule showed strong electrostatic interaction with an anionic model and were associated with stronger mitochondrial targeting and pyroptotic behavior after irradiation. In contrast, reference compounds lacking cationic periphery or lacking a photosensitive core did not show the same pyroptotic phenomena, even when some shared photosensitive cores could still produce singlet oxygen. ^[a-general-molecular-structural-design-for-highly-efficient-photopyroptosis-that-can-be-activated-within-10-s-irradiation.md:190-214, a-general-molecular-structural-design-for-highly-efficient-photopyroptosis-that-can-be-activated-within-10-s-irradiation.md:227-242]

For compound `1d`, the paper reports a strong ATP-model binding constant and states that electrostatic interaction strength substantially affects mitochondrial targeting. The study links this targeting to centralized ROS photoproduction from mitochondria, which it presents as beneficial for triggering pyroptosis efficiently. ^[a-general-molecular-structural-design-for-highly-efficient-photopyroptosis-that-can-be-activated-within-10-s-irradiation.md:231-239, a-general-molecular-structural-design-for-highly-efficient-photopyroptosis-that-can-be-activated-within-10-s-irradiation.md:260-273]

## Cellular mechanism

In EMT-6 cells, `1d` colocalized most strongly with mitochondria relative to other tested organelles, with a reported Pearson correlation coefficient of `0.91`. High-resolution imaging further showed localization at both the inner and outer mitochondrial membranes. ^[a-general-molecular-structural-design-for-highly-efficient-photopyroptosis-that-can-be-activated-within-10-s-irradiation.md:243-256]

After irradiation, the `1d + light` condition showed markedly increased intracellular ROS, reported as more than `16-fold` higher fluorescence intensity than the `1d` condition without irradiation. The source attributes this to dominant ROS photoproduction rather than structural alteration of the molecule and states that mitochondrial targeting concentrates ROS generation at the site most relevant for initiating pyroptosis. ^[a-general-molecular-structural-design-for-highly-efficient-photopyroptosis-that-can-be-activated-within-10-s-irradiation.md:260-273]

The study also tested ROS specificity using scavengers and found that sodium azide and Tiron caused only slight decreases in cell viability, which the authors interpret as indicating that singlet oxygen and superoxide anion accounted for the main part of total ROS induced by `1d`. Additional readouts included elevated LDH release, reduced intracellular ATP activity, and increased extracellular ATP content after `1d + light` treatment, all of which were presented as evidence of cell death. ^[a-general-molecular-structural-design-for-highly-efficient-photopyroptosis-that-can-be-activated-within-10-s-irradiation.md:279-293]

## Evidence for pyroptosis

The paper reports multiple features consistent with pyroptosis after irradiation of `1d`-treated cells. These include cell bubbling observed by confocal microscopy, membrane fragmentation in Bio-TEM images, loss of mitochondrial membrane potential, shrinkage of the nucleus, outflow of cellular contents, mitochondrial structural damage, and increased HMGB-1 expression. ^[a-general-molecular-structural-design-for-highly-efficient-photopyroptosis-that-can-be-activated-within-10-s-irradiation.md:194-202, a-general-molecular-structural-design-for-highly-efficient-photopyroptosis-that-can-be-activated-within-10-s-irradiation.md:318-339]

At the protein level, the source states that control cells and cells treated with `1d` in the dark showed no significant difference in caspase-3 and GSDME levels, whereas the `1d + light` group showed high caspase-3 levels and obvious `GSDME-N` expression. The authors interpret this as activation of a ROS-caspase-3-[[Gasdermin E|GSDME]] pathway leading to pyroptosis. ^[a-general-molecular-structural-design-for-highly-efficient-photopyroptosis-that-can-be-activated-within-10-s-irradiation.md:99-101, a-general-molecular-structural-design-for-highly-efficient-photopyroptosis-that-can-be-activated-within-10-s-irradiation.md:340-347]

## Ultra-rapid triggering

The defining observation is that, after `4 h` incubation with `1d`, EMT-6, Hepa1-6, and MCF-7 tumor cells showed bubbling and eventual rupture under `640 nm` irradiation. Under confocal laser irradiation, the study reports that cells could swell and show membrane bubbling after only `10 s`, and that the process continued even after the laser was turned off. ^[a-general-molecular-structural-design-for-highly-efficient-photopyroptosis-that-can-be-activated-within-10-s-irradiation.md:356-363, a-general-molecular-structural-design-for-highly-efficient-photopyroptosis-that-can-be-activated-within-10-s-irradiation.md:442-448]

Flow-cytometry analysis with annexin V and propidium iodide showed that the pyroptotic proportion of EMT-6 cells increased continuously after light irradiation, with the majority of cells becoming annexin V-positive and PI-positive within `5 h`. The source also states that under CLSM laser irradiation, nearly all observed cells showed bubbling morphology at the indicated observation time point. ^[a-general-molecular-structural-design-for-highly-efficient-photopyroptosis-that-can-be-activated-within-10-s-irradiation.md:363-380]

The study further reports that the laser power density required for pyroptosis could be reduced from `40 mW cm^-2` to `2 mW cm^-2`, although longer irradiation was then needed. In the conclusion, the authors summarize the key result as pyroptosis triggered within `10 s` under CLSM laser at `0.4 J cm^-2`. ^[a-general-molecular-structural-design-for-highly-efficient-photopyroptosis-that-can-be-activated-within-10-s-irradiation.md:376-380, a-general-molecular-structural-design-for-highly-efficient-photopyroptosis-that-can-be-activated-within-10-s-irradiation.md:548-550]

## Dynamic progression

Because the compounds are luminogenic, the study also visualized the photopyroptotic process through changing photoluminescence. The signal first marked mitochondrial localization, then spread through the cytoplasm after loss of mitochondrial membrane potential, then shifted to the nucleus as membrane integrity was lost, and finally became quenched after loss of cellular contents. ^[a-general-molecular-structural-design-for-highly-efficient-photopyroptosis-that-can-be-activated-within-10-s-irradiation.md:389-401]

## In vivo immune and anti-tumor context

The source connects this ultra-rapid photopyroptosis to anti-tumor immunity. Transcriptome analysis of control, `1d`, and `1d + light` cell samples showed upregulation of immune-related genes in the `1d + light` group, with enrichment in pathways including `IL-17`, `TNF`, and `NOD-like receptor` signaling. ^[a-general-molecular-structural-design-for-highly-efficient-photopyroptosis-that-can-be-activated-within-10-s-irradiation.md:402-429]

In EMT-6 tumor-bearing mice, `1d + light` treatment produced strong tumor restraint with a reported tumor inhibition rate of `79.5%` on day 16, while body weight remained nearly unchanged and major organs showed no significant histopathological abnormalities. Blood counts associated with inflammatory response increased, and tumors showed higher proportions of `CD4+` and `CD8+` T cells, along with cleaved caspase-3, `GSDME` N-terminal signal, `CD8`, and granzyme B in tumor sections. The paper states that these results were achieved using the synthetic molecule alone, without other immunoadjuvants. ^[a-general-molecular-structural-design-for-highly-efficient-photopyroptosis-that-can-be-activated-within-10-s-irradiation.md:457-523]

A second in vivo model using Hepa1-6 tumors also showed strong efficacy, with the `1d + light` group reported to achieve a tumor inhibition rate of `88.7%` and no obvious tissue damage in major organs. ^[a-general-molecular-structural-design-for-highly-efficient-photopyroptosis-that-can-be-activated-within-10-s-irradiation.md:524-535]

## Significance in the source

The paper presents this result as evidence that mitochondrial-targeted photosensitizer design can make photopyroptosis unusually fast and light-efficient. In the authors' framing, the combination of photosensitive core, cationic moieties, strong electrostatic interaction, dual-membrane mitochondrial localization, and centralized ROS generation enables a highly efficient trigger that may benefit tumor immunotherapy. ^[a-general-molecular-structural-design-for-highly-efficient-photopyroptosis-that-can-be-activated-within-10-s-irradiation.md:537-554]
