# Pyroptosis converts cold 4T1 tumours to anti-PD1-responsive tumours

## Overview

In this study, the authors report that inducing [[pyroptosis]] in a [[4T1]] mouse mammary tumour model converted a checkpoint-blockade-resistant, "cold" tumour into one that responded to [[anti-PD1 therapy]]. Using a bioorthogonal system based on Phe-BF3-triggered release of nanoparticle-conjugated [[GSDMA3]], they found that pyroptosis-driven inflammation sensitized 4T1 tumours to anti-PD1 treatment and produced marked tumour shrinkage when the two interventions were combined. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:33-40, a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:885-892]

The paper frames this result in the context of poor checkpoint-blockade responses in tumours with low or ineffective inflammation. It explicitly states that 4T1 tumours are known to be cold, that they did not respond to anti-PD1 alone, and that pyroptosis-induced inflammation within the tumour microenvironment can synergize with checkpoint blockade to induce antitumour immunity. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:877-892]

## Experimental basis

The authors used a Phe-BF3 desilylation system to release active GSDMA3 from nanoparticle conjugates selectively in tumours. In BALB/c mice bearing subcutaneous 4T1 tumours, NP-GSDMA3 and Phe-BF3 converged in the tumour, and treatment with the combination caused tumour-cell pyroptosis and strong tumour regression, whereas NP-GSDMA3 alone, Phe-BF3 alone, or mutant pore-defective GSDMA3 with Phe-BF3 did not. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:499-510, a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:634-643]

The study also showed that only a minority of tumour cells needed to undergo pyroptosis for a much broader antitumour effect to occur. In vivo, propidium-iodide-positive pyroptotic cells represented only a small fraction of the tumour-cell population, consistent with about 20% pyroptosis observed in cultured 4T1 cells. In a separate doxycycline-inducible system, mixing only 10% or 30% pyroptosis-competent 4T1-GSDMA3 dox-on cells into the inoculum was still sufficient to stop tumour growth after doxycycline injection. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:638-649]

## Conversion of a cold tumour state

The source links 4T1 resistance to anti-PD1 with an insufficiently inflamed tumour microenvironment. One round of NP-GSDMA3 plus Phe-BF3 alone did not prevent tumour growth, and anti-PD1 alone also failed. However, when that single pyroptosis-inducing treatment was followed by anti-PD1 administration, the tumours showed marked shrinkage in volume together with reduced tumour weight. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:885-890, a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:1353-1356]

This finding supports the specific claim that pyroptosis converted the 4T1 tumours from an anti-PD1-unresponsive state to an anti-PD1-responsive one within the experimental system used here. The paper's interpretation is that local inflammatory changes induced by tumour-cell pyroptosis created conditions that allowed checkpoint blockade to work effectively in a tumour model that otherwise did not respond. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:885-892]

## Immune changes associated with sensitization

The authors attribute the anti-PD1 sensitization effect to stronger antitumour immunity after pyroptosis. In treated 4T1 tumours, CD3+ T-cell infiltration increased markedly, with increases in both CD4+ and CD8+ populations and a decrease in the proportion of FOXP3+ CD4+ regulatory T cells. These changes were not seen with the pore-defective GSDMA3 mutant plus Phe-BF3. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:834-846]

Functional immune dependence was tested directly. Tumour regression did not occur in athymic Nu/Nu mice lacking mature T cells, and depletion of either CD4+ or CD8+ T cells blocked the tumour regression induced by NP-GSDMA3 and Phe-BF3. The authors therefore conclude that both cytotoxic T cells and CD4+ T helper cells are required for the antitumour effect triggered by pyroptosis. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:834-846]

Single-cell RNA sequencing of tumour-infiltrating CD45+ immune cells further showed a shift toward a more antitumour immune landscape after treatment. Compared with PBS controls, pyroptosis-inducing treatment increased CD4+, CD8+, and natural killer cell populations, decreased monocytes, neutrophils, and myeloid-derived suppressor cells, increased M1 macrophages, reduced Arg1-positive M2-like cells, upregulated chemotactic cytokine genes such as `Ccl5`, `Cxcl9`, and `Cxcl10`, and increased lymphocyte activation and effector genes including `Cd69`, `Klrk1`, `Ifng`, `Gzma`, `Gzmb`, `Gzmk`, and `Fasl`. At the same time, immunosuppressive or protumour genes including `Csf1`, `Vegfa`, `Arg1`, `Cd274` (PD-L1), and `Pdcd1lg2` (PD-L2) were downregulated. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:847-865]

## Cytokine and mediator context

The study reports that serum and tumour levels of [[IL-1beta|IL-1b]] and [[IL-18]], as well as [[HMGB1]], increased after NP-GSDMA3 plus Phe-BF3 treatment. Cytokine-neutralization experiments showed that blocking IL-1b severely inhibited tumour regression, whereas blocking IL-18 had a partial effect and blocking HMGB1 had no effect. The authors interpret this to mean that IL-1b is required for the antitumour immunity induced by pyroptosis of tumour cells, probably through activation of host immune cells such as macrophages. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:867-876]

## Interpretation

Within this paper, conversion of cold 4T1 tumours to anti-PD1-responsive tumours refers to an experimentally demonstrated sensitization process in which induced tumour-cell pyroptosis reshaped the tumour immune microenvironment and enabled subsequent checkpoint blockade to reduce tumour burden. The evidence presented ties this conversion to inflammatory mediator release, increased lymphocyte infiltration and activation, reduced immunosuppressive cell states, and dependence on T-cell immunity and IL-1b signaling. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:834-892]
