# ROS generation by type-I photodrugs under 640 nm irradiation

ROS generation by type-I photodrugs under 640 nm irradiation refers to the measurement of reactive oxygen species produced by the study’s photodrugs after red-light exposure, using separate indicators for general ROS, [[singlet oxygen]] (`^1O2`), and [[superoxide]] (`O2-.`). In the supporting methods, the authors identify `DCFH-DA` as the probe for general ROS, `SOSG` for singlet oxygen, and `DHE` for superoxide detection. ^[microsoft-word-supporting-information-reversion.md:233-241]

For cellular ROS imaging, cells in 35 mm confocal dishes were treated with `0.5 uM` photodrug for `30 min`, washed to remove excess compound, then incubated with `10 uM DCFH-DA`, `1 uM SOSG`, or `10 uM DHE` for another `30 min`. After further washing, confocal images were captured immediately after `1 min` of `640 nm` LED irradiation at `15 mW/cm^2`. ^[microsoft-word-supporting-information-reversion.md:242-247]

The source explains the readout logic of each probe. `DCFH-DA` reports general ROS through formation of fluorescent `2',7'-dichlorofluorescein`, monitored by confocal microscopy at excitation `488 nm` and emission `525 nm`. `SOSG` initially shows weak blue fluorescence but emits green in the presence of singlet oxygen and is described as insensitive to radicals. `DHE` was used to assess superoxide anion radical generation through conversion to `2-hydroxyethidium`, detected at excitation `488 nm` and emission `590-620 nm`. ^[microsoft-word-supporting-information-reversion.md:234-241]

The supporting figures also include a solution-phase ROS comparison. Figure S19 is described as showing `DCFH-DA` emission at `525 nm`, `SOSG` emission at `525 nm`, and `DHE` emission at `600 nm` for PBS solutions containing `1 uM` photodrug after `10 s` of `640 nm` irradiation at `15 mW/cm^2`; methylene blue was used as a positive control for singlet oxygen generation. ^[microsoft-word-supporting-information-reversion.md:450-453]

Within the study’s broader framing, these measurements were part of the characterization of a [[mitochondria-targeting]] type-I photodrug platform. Table S2 classifies the present work as a “Mitochondrial-targeting Type-I photodrug,” notes low oxygen dependency, and lists light irradiation conditions of `15 mW/cm^2` for `2-10 min` for this work. ^[microsoft-word-supporting-information-reversion.md:523-565]
