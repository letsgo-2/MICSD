# 1Zn-Et combines exceptionally low dark toxicity with strong photodynamic selectivity

`1Zn-Et` is an octacationic zinc phthalocyanine photosensitizer reported to pair very low toxicity in the absence of light with strong activity under photodynamic treatment. In the study, it was evaluated alongside related zinc, magnesium, and aza-analogue phthalocyanines, and emerged as one of the two compounds selected for deeper mechanistic follow-up because of its high [[photodynamic therapy|photodynamic]] selectivity. ^[jm5014852-114.md:255-264, jm5014852-114.md:364-378]

## Dark toxicity

In dark-toxicity testing, `1Zn-Et` showed much lower inherent toxicity than the related `2Zn-Et` and `3Zn-Et` compounds. The authors state that the nonperipherally substituted `2Zn-Et` was the most toxic, followed by the aza analogue `3Zn-Et`, whereas the peripherally substituted `1Zn-Et` had more than one order of magnitude lower toxicity. They further note that, to their knowledge, `1Zn-Et` may be considered one of the photosensitizers with the lowest dark-toxicity values reported in the literature. ^[jm5014852-114.md:265-289]

The measured `TC50` values for `1Zn-Et` were `1510 +/- 14 uM` in [[3T3]] cells and `1639 +/- 110 uM` in [[HeLa]] cells. These values were substantially higher than those reported for `2Zn-Et` (`147 +/- 11 uM` in 3T3 and `192 +/- 14 uM` in HeLa) and `3Zn-Et` (`249 +/- 34 uM` in 3T3 and `226 +/- 19 uM` in HeLa), supporting the conclusion that `1Zn-Et` had the lowest dark toxicity among the zinc complexes tested. ^[jm5014852-114.md:273-289, jm5014852-114.md:322-330]

## Photodynamic activity

Under irradiation (`lambda > 570 nm`, `12.4 mW cm^-2`, `15 min`, `11.2 J cm^-2`), `1Zn-Et` showed submicromolar phototoxicity across multiple malignant cell lines. Its reported `IC50` values were `0.32 +/- 0.048 uM` in [[SK-MEL-28]], `0.54 +/- 0.090 uM` in HeLa, and `0.69 +/- 0.045 uM` in [[HCT 116]], while the value in 3T3 cells was `0.34 +/- 0.088 uM`. Across the zinc complexes, phototoxic potency ranked `2Zn-Et < 1Zn-Et < 3Zn-Et`, matching their singlet-oxygen production trends. ^[jm5014852-114.md:290-315, jm5014852-114.md:322-330]

The study also found that photodynamic killing by `1Zn-Et` increased with light dose. Using a fixed concentration corresponding to its `IC50`, longer irradiation reduced HeLa-cell viability further, consistent with light-dependent activation rather than high constitutive toxicity. ^[jm5014852-114.md:354-363, jm5014852-114.md:1141-1148]

## Selectivity

The paper used the `TC50/IC50` ratio in HeLa cells as a therapeutic or selectivity index. For `1Zn-Et`, this ratio was `3035`, compared with `619` for `2Zn-Et` and `61` for `3Zn-Et`. The authors interpret the high ratios of `1Zn-Et` and `2Zn-Et` as evidence that both are promising PDT candidates, with `1Zn-Et` showing the strongest selectivity because its very low dark toxicity was paired with strong phototoxicity. ^[jm5014852-114.md:322-330, jm5014852-114.md:364-378]

In the conclusion, the authors again emphasize that the peripherally substituted phthalocyanines were characterized by exceptionally low dark toxicity and specifically state that, with a HeLa `TC50` of `>1600 uM`, `1Zn-Et` ranks among the least toxic photosensitizers reported to date. They also identify `1Zn-Et` and `2Zn-Et` as the compounds with the best selectivity in the series. ^[jm5014852-114.md:636-649, jm5014852-114.md:679-683]

## Context within the compound series

`1Zn-Et` was not the most phototoxic compound in the series; `2Zn-Et` generally had lower `IC50` values. However, `1Zn-Et` combined strong photodynamic potency with a far higher dark-toxicity margin, which is why its overall selectivity exceeded that of `2Zn-Et`. This distinction matters because the study treated dark toxicity and `TC50/IC50` ratio as key indicators of photosensitizer quality, not phototoxic potency alone. ^[jm5014852-114.md:280-289, jm5014852-114.md:308-315, jm5014852-114.md:364-378]
