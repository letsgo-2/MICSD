# Lysosome-targeted PDT leverages lysosomal membrane permeabilization and cathepsin signaling

[[Photodynamic therapy]] can be strengthened by directing [[photosensitizer]] activity to lysosomes, because reactive oxygen species act over very short distances and therefore have greater effect when generated at vulnerable subcellular sites. In the reviewed source, lysosomes are presented as a major organelle target for enhanced PDT because localized ROS can damage lysosomal membranes, disturb lysosomal function, and activate downstream cell-death signaling. ^[targeted-photodynamic-therapy-enhancing-efficacy-through-specific-organelle-engagement.md:19-24, targeted-photodynamic-therapy-enhancing-efficacy-through-specific-organelle-engagement.md:123-142, targeted-photodynamic-therapy-enhancing-efficacy-through-specific-organelle-engagement.md:237-249]

Lysosomes are described as organelles rich in hydrolytic enzymes, including cathepsins, that are essential for cellular catabolism and homeostasis. The review notes that tumor cells often contain more abundant lysosomes with altered morphology and elevated lysosomal activity, and that lysosome-localized photosensitizers can selectively damage these organelles in tumors, causing cathepsin release into the cytoplasm and promoting tumor-cell death. ^[targeted-photodynamic-therapy-enhancing-efficacy-through-specific-organelle-engagement.md:170-176, targeted-photodynamic-therapy-enhancing-efficacy-through-specific-organelle-engagement.md:1029-1040]

A central mechanism is [[lysosomal membrane permeabilization]] (`LMP`). According to the source, ROS can alter lysosomal membrane permeability and integrity, which disrupts lysosomal function and can initiate a cathepsin-mediated lysosomal cell-death pathway distinct from caspase-mediated apoptosis. The review therefore frames lysosomal ROS generation as a therapeutic strategy that exploits membrane damage to trigger lysosome-dependent death programs. ^[targeted-photodynamic-therapy-enhancing-efficacy-through-specific-organelle-engagement.md:172-175, targeted-photodynamic-therapy-enhancing-efficacy-through-specific-organelle-engagement.md:241-249, targeted-photodynamic-therapy-enhancing-efficacy-through-specific-organelle-engagement.md:1041-1047]

The cathepsin axis is emphasized in two related ways. First, ROS-driven LMP releases cathepsins that directly contribute to cell death. Second, lysosomal damage can engage inflammatory signaling: the review states that damaged lysosomes release cathepsin B, which activates the [[NLRP3 inflammasome]] and caspase-1, ultimately causing [[pyroptosis]] through `GSDMD-N`. In the source’s summary table, lysosomal targeting is specifically linked to altered membrane permeability, cathepsin-mediated damage, pyroptosis activation, impaired homeostasis, and the `cathepsin B`/`NLRP3 inflammasome` pathway. ^[targeted-photodynamic-therapy-enhancing-efficacy-through-specific-organelle-engagement.md:241-258, targeted-photodynamic-therapy-enhancing-efficacy-through-specific-organelle-engagement.md:306-318]

The review also describes how sustained lysosomal retention of photosensitizers can reinforce this mechanism. It states that preventing photosensitizer leakage from lysosomes helps maintain constant LMP under irradiation, producing unrecoverable cellular damage and improving PDT efficiency. This rationale is illustrated by `BDQ-NP`, a lysosome-targeted nanophotosensitizer whose pH-dependent lysosomal accumulation enabled ROS-mediated lipid peroxidation, continuous LMP, and disruption of lysosomal and mitochondrial function through a cathepsin B-mediated mitochondrial damage pathway. In that example, cathepsin B cleavage of pro-apoptotic proteins promoted mitochondrial outer-membrane permeabilization, cytochrome c release, and caspase-dependent apoptosis. ^[targeted-photodynamic-therapy-enhancing-efficacy-through-specific-organelle-engagement.md:1041-1047, targeted-photodynamic-therapy-enhancing-efficacy-through-specific-organelle-engagement.md:1048-1075]

Another example in the review, `TPIMBS NPs`, further connects lysosomal damage to combined death signaling. After lysosomal localization and light-triggered ROS production, these nanoparticles caused lysosomal disruption and release of both `Ag+` and cathepsin B. The released cathepsin B was reported to contribute to mitochondrial damage and to inflammasome activation through the `NLRP3 inflammasome/caspase-1/GSDMD-N` pathway, while `Ag+` added a chemotherapeutic effect by inhibiting thioredoxin reductase and DNA polymerase. ^[targeted-photodynamic-therapy-enhancing-efficacy-through-specific-organelle-engagement.md:1099-1131]

The source further links lysosome-targeted PDT to antitumor immunity through pyroptosis and [[immunogenic cell death]]. It notes that lysosomes play a critical role in pyroptosis activation, and highlights `BDPd NPs`, a morpholine-bearing lysosomal photosensitizer that accumulated in lysosomes, caused strong lysosomal damage under NIR irradiation, and triggered pyroptosis through both `caspase-3/GSDME` and `NLRP3/GSDMD` pathways. The same system also activated ICD marker exposure, promoted dendritic-cell maturation, blocked self-protective autophagic degradation by disrupting lysosomal function, and in mouse breast-tumor models suppressed tumor growth, provoked robust antitumor immune responses, and prevented recurrence after light activation. ^[targeted-photodynamic-therapy-enhancing-efficacy-through-specific-organelle-engagement.md:249-258, targeted-photodynamic-therapy-enhancing-efficacy-through-specific-organelle-engagement.md:1137-1179]

Overall, the review presents lysosome-targeted PDT as an organelle-focused strategy that uses ROS to compromise lysosomal membranes, release cathepsins, impair homeostasis, and route tumor cells into lysosome-dependent death programs, including apoptosis-linked mitochondrial damage and inflammasome-associated pyroptosis. The source also identifies this lysosomal route as an alternative to classical caspase-apoptosis-centered killing and as a way to enhance pyroptosis-related immunity within PDT. ^[targeted-photodynamic-therapy-enhancing-efficacy-through-specific-organelle-engagement.md:243-249, targeted-photodynamic-therapy-enhancing-efficacy-through-specific-organelle-engagement.md:306-316, targeted-photodynamic-therapy-enhancing-efficacy-through-specific-organelle-engagement.md:1698-1707]
