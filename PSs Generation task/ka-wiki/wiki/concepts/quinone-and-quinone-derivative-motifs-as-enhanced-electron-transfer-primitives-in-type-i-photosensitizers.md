# Quinone and quinone-derivative motifs as enhanced electron-transfer primitives in type-I photosensitizers

Quinone and quinone-derivative motifs are presented in the review as one of the three main direct-design routes for [[type-I photosensitizer|type-I photosensitizers]]. In that framework, they are treated as “enhanced electron transfer capacity” primitives that can facilitate the electron-transfer process responsible for generating [[superoxide radical|superoxide radicals]] and related type-I [[reactive oxygen species]]. ^[the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:388-415, the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:749-755]

The review states that introducing substrates with suitable oxidation-reduction potential is an effective way to promote electron transfer in type-I photodynamic systems, and it identifies quinone and its derivatives as the most promising candidates among such motifs because of their redox-cycle behavior and electron-transfer capacity. This placement makes quinone motifs a direct molecular strategy for strengthening type-I photochemistry rather than an indirect strategy based on suppressing type-II energy transfer. ^[the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:749-755, the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:871-888]

## Role within the direct strategy

In the review’s overall scheme, direct strategy aims to enhance electron transfer rather than lower `T1` to inhibit [[type-II photosensitizer|type-II]] behavior. The authors divide this direct strategy into three construction methods: forming an electron-rich microenvironment, providing an electron-deficient intermediate transmitter, and introducing an enhanced electron-transfer primitive through quinone and its derivatives. ^[the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:405-423]

This framing matters because the review describes electron transfer as the core of type-I [[photodynamic therapy]]. In one pathway, the excited triplet photosensitizer acquires an electron from its surroundings and then transfers that electron to oxygen; in another, it transfers electrons first to an electron-deficient intermediary, which then passes electrons to oxygen. Quinone-based motifs are positioned as a more direct means to strengthen this electron-transfer step itself. ^[the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:390-415]

## Carvacrol-derived TQ as an electron-transfer mediator

One example in the review combines the natural substrate carvacrol (`CA`) with classical type-II photosensitizers (`PS1`, `PS2`, `PS3`). In this system, `CA` is described as a biologically active molecule sensitive to [[singlet oxygen]], which can be activated in situ and then converted into `TQ`. The resulting `TQ` acts as an electron-transfer mediator between the photosensitizer and molecular oxygen, increasing type-I behavior and enabling more efficient generation of `O2·−`. ^[the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:756-762]

The reported measurements for `CA/PS1` support that shift. Relative to `PS1` alone, the combination produced about a 433-fold fluorescence enhancement for `DCFH` and a 5.5-fold enhancement for `DHR123`, which the review cites as evidence that pairing `PS1` with `CA` yields unexpected type-I ROS generation capacity for a normally type-II photosensitizer. ^[the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:763-767]

Additional mechanistic data in the review support the mediator role of `TQ`. Increasing `TQ` concentration quenched `PS1` fluorescence, and the Stern-Volmer quenching constant was reported as `1.92 x 10^10 M^-1 s^-1`, which the authors interpret as evidence that `TQ` can effectively quench the excited state of `PS1` through electron transfer. The `TQ/PS1` system also showed stronger photocurrent than either component alone, indicating an enhanced charge-transfer rate. ^[the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:767-775]

The review further notes that the `LUMO` level of `PS1` (`-2.61 eV`) lies between those of `TQ` (`-3.31 eV`) and `CA` (`-0.26 eV`), and states that this ordering was consistent with the spectral results. In the presented interpretation, the quinone-derived intermediate helps establish energetically favorable electron-transfer relationships within the photosensitizing system. ^[the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:775-778]

## Quinone-fused cyclopenta[2,1-b]indoles as pure type-I photosensitizers

The review also describes a series of quinone-fused cyclopenta[2,1-b]indoles as “pure” type-I photosensitizers. In that study, 20 compounds were tested and all produced large amounts of ROS, while `EPR` measurements showed signals consistent with both `O2·−` and `OH·`. ^[the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:779-784, the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:807-809]

For the representative compound `3af`, the `HOMO` was mainly located on the indole unit and the `LUMO` on the naphthoquinone moiety. The calculated `ΔES1-T2` value of `0.2904 eV` was reported to facilitate [[intersystem crossing|ISC]], while cyclic voltammetry showed electrochemical behavior similar to `p-benzoquinone` and an obvious reduction peak at `-0.935 V`. Because this reduction potential was well below `Ered(O2/O2·−) = -0.33 V`, the review concludes that `3af` retained excellent electron-transfer ability associated with the quinone unit. ^[the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:809-822]

## Anthraquinone-containing polymeric systems

A further example uses trianiline (`TPA`) as an electron donor and anthraquinone (`AQ`) as an acceptor to build three polymeric photosensitizers: `MP`, `SP`, and `HP`. The review reports that these materials increased `DHR123` photoluminescence intensity by 56-, 12-, and 116-fold, respectively, indicating superoxide generation across the series. ^[the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:823-830]

## Combined strategies with quinone derivatives

The review also highlights that quinone derivatives can be integrated with the other two direct-strategy elements. One cited system combined a cationized quinone derivative with `BSA`, so that the final photosensitizer assembly simultaneously used an electron-deficient intermediate-transmitter feature, an enhanced electron-transfer motif, and an electron-rich microenvironment. In that example, cationization increased ROS production, and `BSA` immobilization further promoted ROS generation, especially `OH·`. ^[the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:831-841]

## Significance and current limits

In the review’s summary, quinone and quinone-derivative motifs remain the clearest examples of electron-transfer-capacity primitives for rational type-I photosensitizer design. The authors also note, however, that this class is still rare: among such primitives, only quinone structures had been explored so far in the surveyed literature. They argue that developing new intermediates with strong electron-transfer ability is an important direction for building type-I photosensitizers more effectively. ^[the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:886-888, the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:909-918]

The review additionally cautions that direct-strategy approaches must balance stronger electron transfer against unwanted oxidation of biomolecules and other off-target reactivity. That limitation applies to quinone-based systems as part of the broader challenge of optimizing type-I photosensitizers for therapeutic performance while minimizing side effects. ^[the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:842-870]
