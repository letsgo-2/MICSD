# IL-27-producing CD301+ macrophages mediate repair downstream of pyroptotic secretome

[[Pyroptotic secretome|Pyroptotic supernatants]] generated from macrophages undergoing `Pyro-1` pyroptosis were reported to promote tissue repair rather than only inflammation. In the study, these supernatants improved wound closure in vitro and in vivo, and the authors linked part of this reparative effect to immune-cell infiltration and polarization toward [[CD301+ macrophages]]. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:46-59, oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1123-1128]

`Pyro-1` refers to a macrophage pyroptosis model induced through the [[NLRP1B inflammasome]] without TLR priming and without release of `IL-1beta` or `IL-1alpha`. This system was used to isolate effects of the pyroptotic secretome that are independent of those cytokines. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:120-129, oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:131-143]

## Role in wound repair

In a full-thickness skin-wounding model, intradermal administration of `Pyro-1` supernatants on days 0, 1, and 2 after injury improved wound closure, with the strongest benefit observed during the inflammatory phase on days 2 and 4. The treated wound bed also showed increased cell proliferation, revascularization, and fibroblast repopulation. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1048-1071]

The reparative effect was not explained solely by oxylipins such as [[Prostaglandin E2|PGE2]]. Supernatants from aspirin-treated `Pyro-1` cells, which reduced prostaglandin production, retained partial wound-healing activity, leading the authors to investigate additional metabolite-driven mechanisms. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1072-1078, oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1450-1457]

## Induction of CD301+ macrophages

During the early inflammatory phase of repair, wounds treated with `Pyro-1` supernatants showed increased numbers of `CD45+` cells, including `F4/80+` and `CD64+` macrophage populations. This indicated that the pyroptotic secretome altered immune-cell accumulation at the wound site. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1119-1128, oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1458-1461]

The authors focused on [[CD301+ macrophages]] because prior work had identified dermal `CD301+` wound macrophages as pro-reparative. Ex vivo, incubation of naive macrophages with `Pyro-1` or `Pyro-1-18` supernatants increased both the proportion of `CD301+` cells and the amount of surface `CD301` per cell, showing that this polarization was largely independent of `IL-18`. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1495-1508, oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1461-1467]

This macrophage polarization was also reported to be independent of `PGE2`. Aspirin-treated `Pyro-1(Asp)` supernatants remained equally effective at inducing `CD301+` macrophages, which the authors interpreted as evidence that components other than `PGE2`, including metabolites, drive this effect. By contrast, induction of `CD301` required [[Gasdermin D|GSDMD]] pore activity. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1508-1515]

In vivo, day-3 skin wounds treated with `Pyro-1` supernatants contained markedly increased numbers of `CD301+` cells within both `CD45+CD64+` and `CD45+F4/80+` populations per unit wound area. `Pyro-1` treatment did not significantly change the inflammatory marker `Ly6C` in these macrophage populations, and `Ly6C`-intermediate `CD64+` cells showed higher `CD301` expression than `Ly6C-high` `CD64+` cells. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1515-1527]

## IL-27 production and functional relevance

The study next examined [[IL-27]] because it has been linked to cell proliferation and skin wound repair and is secreted by `CD301+` macrophages. In wounds treated with the `Pyro-1` secretome, the frequency of `IL-27`-expressing cells increased within both the `CD45+CD64+` and `CD45+F4/80+` `CD301+` macrophage populations. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1528-1532, oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1471-1475]

To test whether `IL-27` was functionally required for the wound-healing benefit, the authors administered an antibody blocking `IL-27` in mouse skin wounds. `IL-27` blockade diminished the enhanced healing associated with `Pyro-1` supernatants, supporting the conclusion that `IL-27-producing CD301+ macrophages` are important mediators of repair downstream of the pyroptotic secretome. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1533-1537, oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1475-1478]

## Mechanistic context

The broader study concluded that the `Pyro-1` secretome contains both oxylipins and metabolites that coordinate tissue repair. Within that model, [[PGE2]] accounted for part of the reparative program, while metabolites were proposed to promote wound-site `CD45+F4/80+CD301+IL-27+` macrophages, linking pyroptotic cell death to a pro-repair immune response. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1555-1602]
