# Pyroptosis can occur without IL-1beta or IL-1alpha release

[[Pyroptosis]] is commonly described as a lytic cell-death program coupled to [[inflammasome]] activation, [[caspase-1]] engagement, and maturation and secretion of inflammatory cytokines such as [[IL-1beta]] and [[IL-1alpha]]. The source, however, states that inflammasome activation can also occur without IL-1beta or IL-1alpha release, indicating that cytokine release is not an obligatory feature of every pyroptotic context. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:39-45, oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:90-95]

## Experimental context

To isolate pyroptotic effects independent of IL-1beta, the study used a model based on the [[NLRP1B inflammasome]]. In bone-marrow-derived macrophages from `Nlrp1b`-transgenic mice, anthrax lethal toxin (`LeTox`) triggered pyroptotic death without prior TLR priming and without release of IL-1beta or IL-1alpha. The authors refer to this state as `Pyro−1`, specifically to denote pyroptosis occurring in the absence of signal 1 triggers and without IL-1beta or IL-1alpha release. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:120-129, oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:131-138]

The paper further notes that this system creates a useful comparison framework. When `Nlrp1b`-transgenic macrophages were instead engineered to lack [[caspase-1]] and caspase-11, the same `LeTox` trigger no longer induced pyroptosis and instead induced apoptosis, allowing comparison of live, pyroptotic, and apoptotic secretomes under the same upstream stimulus. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:138-143]

## What is still released

Although `Pyro−1` lacks IL-1beta and IL-1alpha release, it is not described as secretion-free. The source states that [[IL-18]] is constitutively expressed in macrophages and is released during pyroptosis, and the `Pyro−1` supernatants did contain IL-18. Follow-up experiments using `Il18`-deficient cells showed that several gene-expression effects of `Pyro−1` supernatants were largely IL-18-independent. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:170-181]

The study also reports that `Pyro−1` supernatants contained non-cytokine mediators, including oxylipins such as [[Prostaglandin E2|PGE2]] and multiple metabolites. These factors were linked to migration, proliferation, wound-healing gene signatures, and tissue-repair effects, showing that pyroptotic cells can influence surrounding tissue even when IL-1beta and IL-1alpha are absent from the secretome. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:46-59, oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:156-169, oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:213-230]

## Significance in this study

Using the `Pyro−1` model, the authors found that supernatants from pyroptotic macrophages upregulated gene programs associated with migration, proliferation, immune response, wound healing, and tissue repair in target macrophages. These supernatants also promoted fibroblast and macrophage migration, accelerated in vitro wound closure, and improved tissue repair in vivo. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:46-59, oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:144-169, oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:231-254]

In the discussion, the authors frame these findings as evidence that a pyroptotic secretome lacking IL-1beta can still be biologically active and can promote tissue repair. They argue that pyroptotic cell death may balance tissue injury with regeneration, and that beneficial effects can arise from oxylipins and metabolites released during pyroptosis even when canonical IL-1beta or IL-1alpha release is absent. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1555-1602]
