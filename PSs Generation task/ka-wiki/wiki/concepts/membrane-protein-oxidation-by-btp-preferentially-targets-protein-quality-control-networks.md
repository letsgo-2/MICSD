# Membrane protein oxidation by BTP preferentially targets protein-quality-control networks

`BTP` is an intracellular membrane-localized amphiphilic photocatalyst that generates reactive oxidizing species near organelle membranes. In the reported cell-based proteomic analysis, its oxidative effects were concentrated in membrane proteins rather than cytosolic proteins, and the authors highlight this as a membrane-focused oxidative stress response. ^[oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:116-120, oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:257-286, oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:432-442]

## Overview

The study proposes that `BTP`-driven oxidation occurs mainly in membrane proteins associated with [[protein quality control]], rather than broadly and evenly across the proteome. Using label-free quantitative mass spectrometry, the authors found that oxidized proteins were much more frequent among membrane-specific proteins than among cytosolic proteins, supporting the conclusion that intracellular membrane oxidation is the dominant effect of `BTP` photocatalysis. ^[oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:153-160, oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:414-458]

## Evidence for preferential membrane targeting

The initial oxidized-methionine screen classified detected proteins by Gene Ontology subcellular location and showed a greater number and proportion of oxidized proteins in membrane-specific proteins than in cytosolic proteins: `339` versus `40`, corresponding to `24.5%` and `3.6%`, respectively. Among organelles, the proportions of oxidized membrane proteins were reported as `31.1%` for mitochondria, `19.1%` for endoplasmic reticulum, `21.1%` for nucleus, and `7.7%` for Golgi apparatus. ^[oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:425-442]

A secondary search covering oxidative modifications across `17` amino acids reinforced the same pattern. Under stricter criteria, membrane-specific proteins were the most oxidized class, followed by membrane-cytosolic proteins and then cytosolic proteins, and oxidation defined by tryptophan and histidine modifications was especially prevalent in membrane-specific proteins. ^[oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:443-458]

## Enrichment in protein-quality-control functions

To evaluate the functional impact of oxidation, the authors selected `250` oxidized membrane-associated proteins using a conservative threshold. These proteins were predominantly located in the [[endoplasmic reticulum]], [[mitochondria]], nucleus, and [[Golgi apparatus]], matching the earlier localization analysis. Notably, the ER, Golgi apparatus, and mitochondria together accounted for `58.1%` of the oxidized membrane proteins, and the paper emphasizes these three organelles as central to [[protein quality control]]. ^[oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:459-468]

Within this set of `250` oxidized membrane proteins, `97` were assigned to four functional networks: `(i)` unfolded protein response and [[ER-associated degradation]], `(ii)` ER-Golgi transport, `(iii)` mitochondrial trafficking and transport, and `(iv)` lipid metabolism. The authors focused on these networks because they are tied to cellular quality-control capacity, including handling misfolded proteins, organelle trafficking, and stress-responsive homeostasis. ^[oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:469-490]

Gene Ontology biological-process clustering further supported enrichment in quality-control pathways, with oxidative damage linked to processes related to cellular quality control through the ER and mitochondrial unfolded protein response. Based on the folding-stability experiments and proteomic profile together, the authors hypothesized that oxidation-induced dysfunction of these membrane proteins deteriorates protein-quality-control systems and escalates unfolded protein response stress. ^[oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:487-494]

## Relationship to membrane protein destabilization

The proteomic emphasis on [[protein quality control]] was paired with direct evidence that `BTP` photocatalysis destabilizes membrane protein structure. In a model assay using the membrane protein `GlpG`, oxidation under `BTP` plus blue light decreased the native gel band, increased aggregate bands, and sharply reduced thermal stability, with the transition midpoint temperature dropping from `75 °C` without `BTP` to `35 °C` at `150 uM BTP`. ^[oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:369-381]

Single-molecule magnetic tweezer experiments further showed that `BTP` photocatalysis drastically reduced GlpG unfolding forces and caused irreversible loss of normal folding behavior after a critical point in the pulling cycle. The authors interpret these results as evidence of irreversible oxidative damage to membrane proteins, providing a mechanistic basis for why oxidation of PQC-related membrane proteins could lead to accumulation of misfolded proteins. ^[oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:382-411]

## Cellular consequences in the study

The paper connects this preferential oxidation of PQC-related membrane proteins to downstream stress in the ER, Golgi apparatus, and mitochondria. It states that destabilization of these proteins leads to irreversible accumulation of misfolded proteins, enhanced stress responses, maladaptive unfolded protein response signaling, and cation mobilization, including mitochondrial `Ca2+` uptake and intracellular `K+` efflux. ^[oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:495-527]

In the study’s overall model, these quality-control failures precede activation of non-canonical inflammasome caspases `4` and `5`, cleavage of [[Gasdermin D|GSDMD]], and subsequent [[pyroptosis]]. The discussion summarizes the sequence as oxidation and destabilization of PQC-related membrane proteins, followed by misfolded-protein accumulation, ER stress, maladaptive unfolded protein response, cation mobilization, and caspase-4/5-induced pyroptotic cell death. ^[oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:153-170, oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:672-692]
