I’m extracting only the bilateral 4T1 / immune-response claims from the Cu-TBB paper and turning them into a concise wiki entry with source line citations on each paragraph.# Systemic antitumor immunity from Cu-TBB treatment in bilateral 4T1 tumors

Systemic antitumor immunity from Cu-TBB treatment in this study refers to the ability of a locally activated Cu-TBB treatment to suppress a distant, untreated 4T1 tumor in a bilateral mouse model. The paper frames this effect as immune-mediated rather than a direct drug effect, because Cu-TBB was designed to be activated in the tumor microenvironment and the bilateral setup allowed the distant lesion to serve as a readout of systemic response. ^[copperbacteriochlorin-nanosheet-as-a-specific-pyroptosis-inducer-for-robust-tumor-immunotherapy.md:17-29, copperbacteriochlorin-nanosheet-as-a-specific-pyroptosis-inducer-for-robust-tumor-immunotherapy.md:527-536]

## Bilateral 4T1 model

The in vivo antitumor study used bilateral 4T1-bearing BALB/c mice. Primary tumors were established on the right flank, and distant tumors were grafted on the left flank later in the experiment so that primary-tumor treatment and distant-tumor outcome could be tracked separately. Cu-TBB was administered intravenously, followed by 750 nm laser irradiation, and both primary and distant tumor growth were monitored over time. ^[copperbacteriochlorin-nanosheet-as-a-specific-pyroptosis-inducer-for-robust-tumor-immunotherapy.md:526-536, copperbacteriochlorin-nanosheet-as-a-specific-pyroptosis-inducer-for-robust-tumor-immunotherapy.md:552-556]

## Local treatment and distant tumor control

In the bilateral model, Cu-TBB significantly inhibited primary tumor growth, and the Cu-TBB + Laser group nearly eradicated the primary tumors. The paper also reports significant inhibition of distant tumor growth, with the Cu-TBB + Laser group showing a limited number of metastatic lung nodules after 30 days, supporting a systemic antitumor effect beyond the treated site. ^[copperbacteriochlorin-nanosheet-as-a-specific-pyroptosis-inducer-for-robust-tumor-immunotherapy.md:557-603]

## Immune activation

The authors linked this antitumor effect to pyroptosis-driven immunogenicity. In vivo, Cu-TBB treatment increased N-GSDMD, cleaved caspase-1, IL-1beta, and IL-18 in tumors, and also increased CRT expression and serum HMGB1, which the paper identifies as hallmarks of immunogenic cell death. These changes were accompanied by higher proportions of mature dendritic cells in the spleen and increased CD8+ and CD4+ T-cell populations, along with stronger tumor infiltration by CD8+ and CD4+ T cells. ^[copperbacteriochlorin-nanosheet-as-a-specific-pyroptosis-inducer-for-robust-tumor-immunotherapy.md:604-665]

## Interpretation

The study concludes that Cu-TBB-induced ROS triggered GSDMD-mediated pyroptosis in primary tumors, which enhanced tumor immunogenicity and activated T-cell-dependent adaptive immune responses. Within that framework, the distant-tumor inhibition in bilateral 4T1 mice is presented as evidence of systemic antitumor immunity arising from local pyroptotic tumor destruction. ^[copperbacteriochlorin-nanosheet-as-a-specific-pyroptosis-inducer-for-robust-tumor-immunotherapy.md:673-707]
