# Enhanced tumor accumulation and retention of Gd-PNPs versus free Gd-TCPP

[[Gd-PNPs]] showed stronger and more sustained tumor localization than free [[Gd-TCPP]] in CT26 tumor-bearing mice after intravenous administration. The study compared time-dependent in vivo fluorescence distributions of Gd-PNPs and equivalent TCPP-derived material and found that Gd-PNPs accumulated progressively in tumors, reached their strongest fluorescence signal at 12 hours, and remained detectable at 24 hours, whereas Gd-TCPP showed major tumor accumulation at 1 hour followed by gradual signal loss by 12 hours. ^[d1nr04489c-110.md:448-457]

Relative to free Gd-TCPP, tumor fluorescence in Gd-PNP-treated mice was reported to be about 1.6-fold stronger. Ex vivo fluorescence imaging at 24 hours further showed that tumors from Gd-PNP-treated mice had the strongest signal among examined tissues, while in Gd-TCPP-treated mice the strongest signal was observed in the liver rather than the tumor. This indicates a shift toward greater tumor-associated retention for the nanoparticle formulation and less dominant signal in metabolic organs compared with the free agent. ^[d1nr04489c-110.md:456-463]

The MRI results were consistent with improved tumor persistence of Gd-PNPs. In vivo T1-weighted imaging showed positive contrast enhancement at tumor sites for both [[Gd-PNPs]] and [[Gadobutrol]], but the strongest tumor MR signal for Gd-PNPs occurred at 6 hours after injection, compared with 1 hour for Gadobutrol. Quantitatively, Gd-PNPs produced up to 3.5-fold tumor MR signal enhancement, whereas Gadobutrol produced up to 2.5-fold enhancement. ^[d1nr04489c-110.md:467-475, d1nr04489c-110.md:488-493]

The authors attributed the more efficient tumor accumulation and longer retention time of Gd-PNPs, relative to free Gd-TCPP, to the [[enhanced permeability and retention effect|EPR effect]]. The paper also notes that nanomaterials can exploit the EPR effect and help transport metalloporphyrins despite their intrinsic solubility limitations, providing the rationale for formulating Gd-TCPP into supramolecular nanoparticles. ^[d1nr04489c-110.md:112-117, d1nr04489c-110.md:505-510]

This enhanced tumor accumulation and retention was part of the broader in vivo performance of Gd-PNPs in the study. In the conclusions, the authors state that Gd-PNPs exhibited efficient tumor accumulation and long retention time at tumor sites, which supported [[fluorescence imaging]], [[magnetic resonance imaging]], and imaging-guided [[photodynamic therapy]] in CT26 tumor-bearing mice. ^[d1nr04489c-110.md:602-606]
