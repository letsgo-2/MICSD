# Cu+-driven ROS cascade chemistry in Cu-TBB

Cu+-driven ROS cascade chemistry in [[Cu-TBB]] refers to the tumor-activated redox sequence initiated after glutathione (GSH) reduces Cu2+ within the Cu-TBB nanosheet, releasing Cu+ and enabling successive reactive oxygen species generation. In the study, Cu-TBB remains in an “off” state under normal conditions, but in a glutathione-rich [[tumor microenvironment]] it disassembles into Cu+ and TBB, allowing Cu+-mediated redox chemistry and, under 750 nm irradiation, additional photodynamic ROS production from released TBB. ^[copperbacteriochlorin-nanosheet-as-a-specific-pyroptosis-inducer-for-robust-tumor-immunotherapy.md:140-155, copperbacteriochlorin-nanosheet-as-a-specific-pyroptosis-inducer-for-robust-tumor-immunotherapy.md:228-246]

## Core reaction sequence

The paper describes the first step as the reaction of Cu-TBB with GSH, which forms Cu+, releases TBB, and generates oxidized glutathione (GSSG). Spectroscopically, this was supported by a redshift attributed to S-Cu2+ charge transfer and by the appearance of the characteristic GSSG absorption at 254 nm after 30 minutes. ^[copperbacteriochlorin-nanosheet-as-a-specific-pyroptosis-inducer-for-robust-tumor-immunotherapy.md:228-239, copperbacteriochlorin-nanosheet-as-a-specific-pyroptosis-inducer-for-robust-tumor-immunotherapy.md:249-251]

Once formed, Cu+ reacts with molecular oxygen to regenerate Cu2+ and produce superoxide (`O2−•`). Electron spin resonance detected no signal for Cu-TBB alone, but showed a clear superoxide signal after incubation of Cu-TBB with GSH, indicating that GSH activation enabled Cu-catalyzed superoxide formation. ^[copperbacteriochlorin-nanosheet-as-a-specific-pyroptosis-inducer-for-robust-tumor-immunotherapy.md:239-248, copperbacteriochlorin-nanosheet-as-a-specific-pyroptosis-inducer-for-robust-tumor-immunotherapy.md:250-251]

The generated superoxide then participates in downstream cascade steps. The authors state that endogenous superoxide dismutase (SOD) disproportionation converts superoxide into oxygen and hydrogen peroxide, and that superoxide can also react with hydrogen peroxide through the Haber-Weiss reaction to yield oxygen and highly toxic hydroxyl radicals (`·OH`). In parallel, Cu+ can react with hydrogen peroxide through a Fenton-like process to generate additional `·OH`. ^[copperbacteriochlorin-nanosheet-as-a-specific-pyroptosis-inducer-for-robust-tumor-immunotherapy.md:147-151, copperbacteriochlorin-nanosheet-as-a-specific-pyroptosis-inducer-for-robust-tumor-immunotherapy.md:330-349]

## Cellular evidence

In cells, DHE staining showed strong superoxide-associated fluorescence in the `Cu-TBB + GSH` group, while PBS, GSH alone, and Cu-TBB alone showed only weak signal attributed to endogenous superoxide. The same treatment also produced DHE fluorescence under hypoxia, which the authors interpret as consistent with oxygen regeneration during the cascade. ^[copperbacteriochlorin-nanosheet-as-a-specific-pyroptosis-inducer-for-robust-tumor-immunotherapy.md:320-338]

The study further reports that GSH-treated HeLa cells produced detectable intracellular hydrogen peroxide, and that bright HPF fluorescence for hydroxyl radicals was observed specifically in the `Cu-TBB + GSH` group. Malondialdehyde levels also increased with higher GSH concentration, supporting substantial oxidative stress during activation of the cascade. ^[copperbacteriochlorin-nanosheet-as-a-specific-pyroptosis-inducer-for-robust-tumor-immunotherapy.md:339-359]

The authors summarize the intracellular chemistry as a multi-path sequence: Cu-TBB disassembly by GSH forms Cu+; Cu+ reduces oxygen to superoxide; Cu+-dependent Fenton-like chemistry produces hydroxyl radicals; and superoxide is further converted through SOD disproportionation and Haber-Weiss chemistry to yield more oxygen and `·OH`. They conclude that these Cu2+-driven cascade reactions can generate large amounts of ROS in cancer cells. ^[copperbacteriochlorin-nanosheet-as-a-specific-pyroptosis-inducer-for-robust-tumor-immunotherapy.md:360-371]

## Relationship to photodynamic ROS generation

The Cu+-driven cascade is one arm of Cu-TBB ROS production, distinct from but coordinated with the photodynamic activity of released TBB. After GSH activation, TBB fluorescence recovered and ROS generation under 750 nm irradiation increased with GSH-dependent release of TBB; additional assays supported production of both singlet oxygen (`1O2`) and superoxide from the photodynamic pathway. ^[copperbacteriochlorin-nanosheet-as-a-specific-pyroptosis-inducer-for-robust-tumor-immunotherapy.md:252-282]

In vitro, laser irradiation increased superoxide fluorescence beyond that seen from Cu-driven chemistry alone, and singlet oxygen probe readouts were positive only in the `Cu-TBB + GSH + Laser` group. The paper therefore presents Cu-TBB as combining Cu-driven cascade chemistry with [[photodynamic therapy]] to produce abundant `1O2`, `O2−•`, and `·OH`. ^[copperbacteriochlorin-nanosheet-as-a-specific-pyroptosis-inducer-for-robust-tumor-immunotherapy.md:372-387]

## Biological consequence in the study

According to the study, ROS generated through the Cu-driven cascade, with further enhancement by photodynamic activation, reduced cancer-cell viability and triggered [[pyroptosis]]. The reported pathway was caspase-1-dependent and [[Gasdermin D|GSDMD]]-mediated, with detection of cleaved caspase-1, N-GSDMD, and increased IL-1beta and IL-18 in treated cells. ^[copperbacteriochlorin-nanosheet-as-a-specific-pyroptosis-inducer-for-robust-tumor-immunotherapy.md:388-397, copperbacteriochlorin-nanosheet-as-a-specific-pyroptosis-inducer-for-robust-tumor-immunotherapy.md:439-462]

The same ROS-associated pyroptotic response was linked to increased release of [[damage-associated molecular patterns|DAMPs]] such as HMGB1 and LDH, surface exposure of CRT, dendritic-cell maturation, and stronger T-cell-associated antitumor immunity in vivo. The source frames the Cu+-driven ROS cascade chemistry as part of the mechanism by which Cu-TBB enhances tumor immunogenicity while remaining selectively activatable in glutathione-rich tumors. ^[copperbacteriochlorin-nanosheet-as-a-specific-pyroptosis-inducer-for-robust-tumor-immunotherapy.md:463-488, copperbacteriochlorin-nanosheet-as-a-specific-pyroptosis-inducer-for-robust-tumor-immunotherapy.md:605-665, copperbacteriochlorin-nanosheet-as-a-specific-pyroptosis-inducer-for-robust-tumor-immunotherapy.md:673-706]
