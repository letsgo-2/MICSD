# Hexyl-substituted Zn-porphyrin produces stronger respiration suppression and phototoxicity than shorter-chain analogs

In LS174T adenocarcinoma cells, increasing the alkyl chain length on ortho Zn(II) `N`-alkylpyridylporphyrins from methyl to butyl to hexyl increased lipophilicity and was associated with stronger photodynamic effects. The study reports that the methyl analog showed the weakest suppression of MTT reduction, while extending the chain to six carbons produced a dramatic gain in photodynamic efficiency after illumination. ^[debpone0108238-19.md:293-309, debpone0108238-19.md:312-320]

A similar chain-length dependence was observed for cellular respiration. After illumination, respiration decreased in the same rank order seen for MTT reduction, and no respiration suppression occurred in dark controls or with light alone. The source interprets oxygen-consumption loss as reflecting [[mitochondria|mitochondrial]] damage because aerobic oxygen consumption in these cells depends mainly on mitochondrial respiration. ^[debpone0108238-19.md:323-330]

Experiments with isolated mitochondria strengthened this pattern. After preincubation and illumination, the amphiphilic hexyl derivative [[ZnTnHex-2-PyP]] produced much stronger respiratory suppression than the shorter-chain analogs: `1 uM` ZnTnHex-2-PyP was sufficient to completely suppress mitochondrial respiration, whereas the hydrophilic methyl analog [[ZnTM-2-PyP]] at `5 uM` suppressed respiration by only about 40%. No effect was observed without illumination or in the absence of Zn porphyrin. ^[debpone0108238-19.md:331-345, debpone0108238-19.md:363-365]

The study links this stronger activity to differences in subcellular distribution. Hydrophilic methyl and ethyl analogs localized mainly to lysosomes with little mitochondrial uptake, the butyl analog showed greater distribution to mitochondria and endoplasmic reticulum, and extending the chain to six carbons strongly increased mitochondrial uptake while eliminating detectable lysosomal partition of the hexyl derivative. ^[debpone0108238-19.md:366-405]

The hexyl derivative also showed stronger photoinactivation of [[cytochrome c oxidase]] than the shorter-chain compounds. In intact cells, `5 uM` ZnTnHex-2-PyP caused about 50% loss of cytochrome c oxidase activity, whereas ZnTM-2-PyP was ineffective at the same concentration and the butyl analog showed intermediate activity. In isolated mitochondria, `1 uM` ZnTnHex-2-PyP produced cytochrome c oxidase inactivation comparable to that seen with `5-10 uM` in intact cells, while the methyl analog failed to inactivate the enzyme even after prolonged incubation at `5 uM`. ^[debpone0108238-19.md:406-424, debpone0108238-19.md:447-484]

The authors argue that increased lipophilicity does not only improve uptake, but also shifts sub-mitochondrial localization toward the inner mitochondrial membrane and the vicinity of complex IV. They conclude that the greater phototoxicity and respiratory inhibition of the hexyl-substituted porphyrin are explained by its more effective mitochondrial targeting and its proximity to critical respiratory-chain components. ^[debpone0108238-19.md:485-491, debpone0108238-19.md:538-545, debpone0108238-19.md:558-582]
