# Caspase-3/GSDME functions as an apoptosis-to-pyroptosis switch in chemotherapy-treated cancer cells

[[Pyroptosis]] is a form of programmed cell death that depends on gasdermin-family pore formation in the plasma membrane and is distinct from [[apoptosis]], which is classically associated with caspase-driven non-lytic cell death. In cancer treatment contexts, the reviewed source highlights GSDME as a key mediator linking these two death programs. ^[pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:17-24, pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:102-109, pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:343-389]

## Core mechanism

The source states that conventional chemotherapeutic drugs such as cisplatin and paclitaxel can activate [[caspase-3]]. Activated caspase-3 then cleaves [[Gasdermin E|GSDME]] to generate the N-terminal fragment, GSDME-NT, which forms pores in the cell membrane and drives pyroptotic death. ^[pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:201-205, pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:321-322]

Because caspase-3 is originally described here as an apoptosis-related enzyme, the caspase-3-to-GSDME pathway provides a mechanism by which a signal commonly associated with apoptosis can instead culminate in pyroptosis. The review explicitly describes GSDME as a "switch" that determines whether chemotherapy-treated cancer cells undergo apoptosis or pyroptosis. ^[pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:205-210]

## Switch behavior in cancer cells

According to the source, cancer cells with high GSDME expression undergo pyroptosis after treatment with chemotherapeutic drugs, whereas cells with low or absent GSDME expression undergo apoptosis under the same general type of treatment. This expression-dependent difference is the basis for describing caspase-3/GSDME as an apoptosis-to-pyroptosis switch. ^[pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:206-211]

The review also notes a related reactive-oxygen-species pathway in which intracellular iron-dependent ROS is sensed by Tom20, leading to Bax translocation, cytochrome c release, caspase-9 activation, and then caspase-3 activation. In this pathway as summarized by the source, active caspase-3 again induces GSDME-mediated pyroptosis. ^[pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:211-229, pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:321-322]

## Evidence in chemotherapy-treated cancers

In [[non-small-cell lung cancer]], the review cites work showing that both cisplatin and paclitaxel could induce apoptosis and pyroptosis in A549 cells through caspase-3/GSDME activation, with cisplatin having the stronger effect. The same section notes that cisplatin-induced GSDME-mediated pyroptosis was associated with increased immune-factor release, including MIP-1a, MIP-1b, MIP-2, and IP-10. ^[pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:300-310, pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:664-670, pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:1046-1054]

In [[gastric cancer]], the review reports that 5-fluorouracil triggered pyroptosis in SGC-7901 and MKN-45 cells through activation of caspase-3 and induction of GSDME-mediated pyroptosis. It also states that in SGC-7901 cells treated with BIX-01294 plus cisplatin, GSDME knockout converted pyroptosis into apoptosis, directly supporting the switch model. ^[pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:524-531, pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:692-695]

The review further describes GSDME-dependent pyroptosis in [[osteosarcoma]], where dioscin treatment generated GSDME-NT and its antitumor effect was reduced when GSDME was knocked down by siRNA. The source adds that pyroptosis and apoptosis can cooperate under some conditions in osteosarcoma cells. ^[pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:405-417, pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:671-677]

## Immunologic relevance

The source links GSDME-expressing tumor-cell pyroptosis to [[antitumor immunity]]. It states that damage-associated molecular patterns released during pyroptosis can recruit immune cells into the tumor microenvironment, increase tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes, and enhance macrophage phagocytic capacity. ^[pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:602-609]

In the same discussion, the review notes that cisplatin-induced GSDME-mediated pyroptosis may promote antitumor effects through release of immune factors that recruit T cells into tumors. More broadly, it argues that even pyroptosis in a small number of tumor cells can reshape the tumor immune microenvironment and activate a strong T-cell-mediated antitumor response. ^[pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:305-310, pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:636-644]

## Context

The review presents the caspase-3/GSDME pathway as one of several gasdermin-dependent routes to pyroptosis in cancer, alongside canonical and noncanonical [[inflammasome]]/[[Gasdermin D|GSDMD]] pathways and a caspase-8/[[GSDMC]] pathway. Within that broader framework, caspase-3/GSDME is notable because it directly links a classical apoptotic executor caspase to lytic, inflammatory cell death during chemotherapy. ^[pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:193-204, pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:264-271, pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:658-660]
