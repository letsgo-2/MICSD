# Pyroptosis is a context-dependent double-edged regulator of the tumor microenvironment

[[Pyroptosis]] is an inflammatory form of programmed cell death that is closely linked to [[tumor immunity]] and the [[tumor microenvironment|tumor microenvironment (TME)]]. In cancer, the review describes pyroptosis as a “double-edged sword” because it can either support tumor progression or strengthen antitumor immunity depending on the biological context and pattern of activation. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:534-568]

The source states that long-term pyroptosis of cancer cells in an adverse TME can accelerate disease development. In this setting, chronic pyroptosis-derived proinflammatory cytokines help construct and maintain an inflammatory environment that encourages tumor growth. As an example, the review notes that in ovarian cancer, early microenvironmental signals may induce [[DAMPs]], which can activate [[AIM2 inflammasome|AIM2]] and [[NLRP3 inflammasome|NLRP3]] inflammasomes; subsequent NF-kappaB-driven overexpression of inflammasome components and cytokine precursors contributes to inflammasome activation, pyroptosis, IL-1beta and IL-18 maturation, and chronic inflammation in advanced stages. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:546-560]

By contrast, acute and widespread pyroptotic activation can promote antitumor effects. The review states that this pattern causes major immune-cell invasion, strengthens antitumor immunity, prevents tumor growth, and triggers substantial cancer cell death. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:561-565]

The review also presents inflammasomes as major regulators of how pyroptosis reshapes the TME. [[NLRP3 inflammasome|NLRP3]] is described as facilitating infiltration of myeloid populations such as [[myeloid-derived suppressor cell|MDSCs]] and [[tumor-associated macrophage|TAMs]], which benefits tumor progression. In tumors with high inflammasome signaling, suppressing inflammasomes increased CD8+ and CD4+ T-cell infiltration, reduced TAM infiltration, and amplified the therapeutic effect of PD-L1 inhibition. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:572-587]

Other inflammasomes can also shape the TME in divergent ways. Activation of [[NLRP1 inflammasome|NLRP1]] by dipeptidyl peptidase inhibition is reported to induce proinflammatory cytokine secretion, activate Th1 responses, engage the CXCL10/CXCR3 axis, increase T- and NK-cell infiltration, strengthen anti-PD1 efficacy, and inhibit tumor progression. [[NLRC4 inflammasome|NLRC4]] is described as essential in a melanoma model for cytokine generation in TAMs and for development of IFN-gamma-producing CD8+ and CD4+ T cells that impede tumor progression. In contrast, [[AIM2 inflammasome|AIM2]] can inhibit chronic STING signaling by inducing pyroptotic cell death, and the review also cites evidence that AIM2 reduced CD4+ effector T cells, increased Treg cells, promoted IL-1 and IL-18 production, and enhanced tumor progression in vivo. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:588-606]

According to the review, pyroptosis influences the TME largely through inflammatory mediators released during cell death, especially [[IL-1beta]], [[IL-18]], and [[HMGB1]]. These mediators are reported to have both tumor-promoting and tumor-suppressive effects, which the authors cite as evidence that pyroptosis has a complicated, context-dependent impact on the TME. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:611-621]

Within the TME, IL-1beta is described as having dual functions tied to tumor growth. It can activate innate immune cells and antigen-presenting cells, polarize CD4+ T cells toward Th1 and Th17 phenotypes, support acute inflammatory anticancer defenses, and stimulate dendritic-cell expansion and tumor-antigen presentation that triggers cytotoxic CD8+ T-cell responses. At the same time, IL-1beta can enhance TAMs and MDSCs within an immunosuppressive environment, thereby promoting tumor growth. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:623-644]

[[IL-18]] is likewise presented as both procancer and anticancer. The review states that IL-18 promotes IFN production, enhances Th1 responses, and activates T cells and NK cells to generate IFN-gamma, all of which can support tumor immunity. However, IL-18 can also support angiogenesis, metastasis, growth, and immune evasion, and in an immune-suppressive microenvironment it can further boost tumor growth. The cited example is that IL-18 can enhance monocytic MDSCs, suppress T-cell expansion and IFN production, while NLRP3-mediated IL-18 can also increase NK-cell maturation, FasL expression, and killing of FasL-sensitive malignancies. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:645-663]

[[HMGB1]] released during pyroptosis is also described as functionally ambivalent in cancer. The review notes that extracellular HMGB1 can activate NLRP3 inflammasomes and NF-kappaB signaling, and that HMGB1 signaling through TLR2 and TLR4 induces cytokines needed for CD8+ T-cell activation. HMGB1 from dying tumor cells can interact with TLR4 on mature dendritic cells to initiate tumor-antigen processing and anticancer immune responses. At the same time, the review states that HMGB1 can increase neo-angiogenesis and, within an immunosuppressive TME, enhance the viability and recruitment of MDSCs, thereby supporting tumor-cell survival. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:664-682]

The gasdermin family is also linked to TME remodeling through pyroptosis. The review highlights [[Gasdermin D|GSDMD]] and [[Gasdermin E|GSDME]] as pore-forming executioners whose activation releases cytokines and reshapes immune infiltration. In particular, GSDME-mediated pyroptosis is described as converting “cold” tumors into “hot” tumors by producing IL-18, IL-1beta, and HMGB1, thereby promoting antitumor immunity. In mouse tumors expressing GSDME, loss of GSDME increased tumor development, whereas ectopic expression inhibited tumor growth; this was associated with more frequent and better-functioning NK cells and CD8+ T cells in tumors, along with more effective TAM phagocytosis. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:683-704]

The same section also notes that pyroptosis can have costs even when therapeutically induced. For example, CAR T-cell-delivered granzyme can cleave GSDME and activate caspase 3 in target cells, while pyroptosis-associated signals can activate macrophage caspase 1 and GSDMD cleavage, causing cytokine release and accompanying [[cytokine release syndrome|CRS]]. This reinforces the review’s broader conclusion that manipulating pyroptosis in cancer has therapeutic promise but may also injure surrounding normal tissues through excessive inflammation. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:705-712, pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:1618-1635]

Overall, the review concludes that pyroptosis-associated inflammatory factors can modify the TME in either procancer or anticancer directions. It therefore presents pyroptosis and inflammasomes as promising but complex therapeutic targets whose value depends on understanding how specific pathways, mediators, and tumor contexts shape immune responses and tumor progression. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:1661-1688]
