# Gadolinium-porphyrin polymer nanotheranostics (Gd-PNPs)

**Gadolinium-porphyrin polymer nanotheranostics (Gd-PNPs)** are a gadolinium-porphyrin-based polymer nanotheranostic platform described for fluorescence and magnetic resonance imaging-guided [[photodynamic therapy]]. The supplementary material identifies them as supramolecular nanotheranostics and frames their use in imaging-guided PDT. ^[2021-chen-gadolinium-porphyrin-polymer-nanotheranostics-for-imaging-guided-pdt-si.md:8-10, 2021-chen-gadolinium-porphyrin-polymer-nanotheranostics-for-imaging-guided-pdt-si.md:95-98]

Gd-PNPs were studied in the context of [[biocompatibility]], in vivo toxicity, tumor-bearing mouse models, and singlet oxygen generation under illumination. The experimental work included cell assays, blood compatibility testing, zebrafish and mouse toxicity evaluation, tumor model establishment in Balb/c mice, and optical detection of singlet oxygen production. ^[2021-chen-gadolinium-porphyrin-polymer-nanotheranostics-for-imaging-guided-pdt-si.md:27-38, 2021-chen-gadolinium-porphyrin-polymer-nanotheranostics-for-imaging-guided-pdt-si.md:46-66, 2021-chen-gadolinium-porphyrin-polymer-nanotheranostics-for-imaging-guided-pdt-si.md:67-75]

## Preparation and related materials

The source material indicates that Gd-PNPs are related to a chelation reaction between TCPP and Gd3+ to prepare Gd-TCPP, and that synthesis of the supramolecular nanotheranostics was optimized using nanoparticle diameter as a key parameter. The optimization variables shown were the mass ratio of PEI/TCPP and reaction time. ^[2021-chen-gadolinium-porphyrin-polymer-nanotheranostics-for-imaging-guided-pdt-si.md:95-98]

Associated characterization in the supplementary figures included 1H NMR spectra of TCPP and Gd-TCPP, 1H NMR spectra of mPEG-PGA, deconvoluted XPS spectra of Gd-FPNPs for C 1s, N 1s, and O 1s, and fluorescence decay profiles comparing Gd-TCPP and Gd-PNPs. ^[2021-chen-gadolinium-porphyrin-polymer-nanotheranostics-for-imaging-guided-pdt-si.md:90-91, 2021-chen-gadolinium-porphyrin-polymer-nanotheranostics-for-imaging-guided-pdt-si.md:102-107]

## Biocompatibility and toxicity evaluation

In vitro cytotoxicity was evaluated against HeLa cells and CT 26 cells using a standard MTT assay. Cells were exposed to Gd-PNPs at concentrations from 0 to 400 μg mL-1 for 24 hours before cell viability measurement. ^[2021-chen-gadolinium-porphyrin-polymer-nanotheranostics-for-imaging-guided-pdt-si.md:28-32]

Hemocompatibility was evaluated using human blood cells. Blood cell suspensions were incubated with Gd-PNPs at concentrations from 0 to 400 μg mL-1 for 2 hours at room temperature, after which hemolysis percentage was calculated from absorbance at 541 nm. PBS and ultrapure water served as the negative and positive controls, and erythrocyte morphology was also observed after treatment. ^[2021-chen-gadolinium-porphyrin-polymer-nanotheranostics-for-imaging-guided-pdt-si.md:33-45]

The source also reports in vivo biotoxicity testing in zebrafish and Balb/c mice. For zebrafish, 6-day-old larvae were incubated with Gd-PNP solutions at 0, 100, 200, and 400 μg mL-1, and survival and hatching rates were assessed during embryonic development from 24 to 96 hours post-fertilization. Bright-field and fluorescence images were recorded under 535 nm excitation. ^[2021-chen-gadolinium-porphyrin-polymer-nanotheranostics-for-imaging-guided-pdt-si.md:46-62]

For mice, in vivo toxicity was further evaluated after intravenous injection of 0.8 mg mL-1 Gd-PNPs. The animals were dissected after 3 days, and major organs were collected for H&E staining; mice injected with normal saline were used as controls. ^[2021-chen-gadolinium-porphyrin-polymer-nanotheranostics-for-imaging-guided-pdt-si.md:63-66]

## Tumor model and imaging context

CT 26 tumor-bearing mouse models were established by subcutaneous injection of CT 26 cell suspension into the backs of Balb/c mice. The mice were used for experiments when tumor volume reached approximately 150-200 mm3. ^[2021-chen-gadolinium-porphyrin-polymer-nanotheranostics-for-imaging-guided-pdt-si.md:67-72]

The imaging context in the source includes both fluorescence and [[magnetic resonance imaging]]. A supplementary table reports exact tumor MRI signal values at multiple time points from 0 to 12 hours for both Gd-PNPs and gadobutrol, indicating direct comparison of tumor MRI signal behavior over time. ^[2021-chen-gadolinium-porphyrin-polymer-nanotheranostics-for-imaging-guided-pdt-si.md:130-171]

## Singlet oxygen generation

The capability of Gd-PNPs to generate [[singlet oxygen]] under illumination was evaluated using a well-established protocol based on imidazole and N,N-dimethyl-4-nitrosoaniline (RNO). In this mechanism, imidazole captures 1O2 to form a trans-annular peroxide intermediate, which then induces bleaching of RNO and produces an absorbance change at 440 nm measurable by UV-Vis spectroscopy. ^[2021-chen-gadolinium-porphyrin-polymer-nanotheranostics-for-imaging-guided-pdt-si.md:73-86]

A supplementary figure also reports the 1O2 quantum yield of TCPP, Gd-TCPP, and Gd-PNPs. ^[2021-chen-gadolinium-porphyrin-polymer-nanotheranostics-for-imaging-guided-pdt-si.md:126-126]

## Stability-related measurements

The supplementary material includes measurements of Gd3+ leakage from Gd-PNPs at different time points and the size change of Gd-PNPs after incubation in FBS over different periods. These figures indicate that leakage behavior and size stability in serum-containing conditions were assessed as part of material characterization. ^[2021-chen-gadolinium-porphyrin-polymer-nanotheranostics-for-imaging-guided-pdt-si.md:111-117]
