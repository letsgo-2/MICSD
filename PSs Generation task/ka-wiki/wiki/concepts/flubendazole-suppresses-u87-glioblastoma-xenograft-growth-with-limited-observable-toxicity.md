# Flubendazole suppresses U87 glioblastoma xenograft growth with limited observable toxicity

[[Flubendazole]] was evaluated in an in vivo [[glioblastoma multiforme|glioblastoma]] xenograft experiment using subcutaneous [[U87]] tumors in nude mice. In this study, U87 cells were implanted into the right flank of female BALB/c-nu mice, and treatment began after tumors reached an average volume of about `100 mm^3`. Mice were assigned to no treatment, [[temozolomide]] at `25 mg/kg` intraperitoneally, or flubendazole at `12.5`, `25`, or `50 mg/kg` intraperitoneally once daily for 3 weeks. Body weight and tumor volume were recorded every 2 days, and tumor and organ weights were measured at the end of the experiment. ^[benzimidazoles-induce-concurrent-apoptosis-and-pyroptosis-of-human-glioblastoma-cells-via-arresting-cell-cycle.md:309-333]

Flubendazole dose-dependently inhibited growth of the U87 xenograft tumors relative to vehicle-treated mice. The source states that both tumor volume and tumor weight were significantly decreased with flubendazole administration, and that the antitumor effect was almost equivalent to the positive control drug [[temozolomide]]. At the highest tested dose, `50 mg/kg`, the tumor had almost disappeared after 24 days of administration. ^[benzimidazoles-induce-concurrent-apoptosis-and-pyroptosis-of-human-glioblastoma-cells-via-arresting-cell-cycle.md:645-655]

Observable toxicity was limited under the reported treatment conditions. The paper reports no significant change in body weight during treatment and no apparent change in the relative weight, appearance, or morphology of major organs including the heart, liver, kidney, lung, and spleen. It also states that physiological blood tests showed no obvious systemic toxicity. ^[benzimidazoles-induce-concurrent-apoptosis-and-pyroptosis-of-human-glioblastoma-cells-via-arresting-cell-cycle.md:656-665]

Within the broader study, these in vivo findings were presented alongside in vitro evidence that benzimidazoles inhibit proliferation of [[U87]] and U251 glioblastoma cells, arrest the cell cycle at G2/M, and induce concurrent apoptotic and pyroptotic cell death. The authors concluded that flubendazole, in particular, may be a promising anti-[[GBM]] candidate with no observable side effects in this xenograft setting. ^[benzimidazoles-induce-concurrent-apoptosis-and-pyroptosis-of-human-glioblastoma-cells-via-arresting-cell-cycle.md:26-30, benzimidazoles-induce-concurrent-apoptosis-and-pyroptosis-of-human-glioblastoma-cells-via-arresting-cell-cycle.md:715-718, benzimidazoles-induce-concurrent-apoptosis-and-pyroptosis-of-human-glioblastoma-cells-via-arresting-cell-cycle.md:746-749]
