# Hypoxia-accelerated water-oxidation photocatalysis by BTP generates H2O2 and hydroxyl radicals

`BTP` is described as an intracellular membrane-localized organic photocatalyst whose light-driven redox chemistry oxidizes water to generate `H2O2` and highly oxidizing [[hydroxyl radicals]]. The study specifically reports that this water-oxidation pathway is accelerated under hypoxia, because oxygen competes with water as a quencher of the excited photocatalyst. ^[oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:32-37, oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:149-151, oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:204-229]

## Photocatalytic basis

The authors synthesized `BTP` as a donor-acceptor molecule built from benzothiadiazole and triphenylamine. Based on measured photophysical and electrochemical properties, they propose that light excitation produces an excited state of BTP capable of accepting electrons from water and transferring electrons to oxygen, establishing photocatalytic cycles that support membrane oxidation. ^[oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:172-182, oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:183-190]

The reported excited-state reduction potential of `BTP*` was `1.47 V` versus the normal hydrogen electrode, which the authors state is sufficient to drive a two-electron water-oxidation pathway yielding `H2O2` at physiological pH. In fluorescence-quenching experiments, increasing water content progressively reduced BTP fluorescence and shortened the excited-state lifetime, which the study interprets as evidence for reductive quenching of `BTP*` by water oxidation. ^[oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:191-203]

## Evidence for H2O2 generation

Using a peroxidase/`DPD` assay, the study found that photoexcited `BTP` generated `H2O2` in aqueous solution, whereas `BTP*` did not generate `H2O2` in `DMSO` without water. The authors therefore conclude that `H2O2` was produced from water during BTP photocatalysis. ^[oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:204-209]

The same assay showed that hypoxia substantially accelerated photocatalytic `H2O2` production. The paper attributes this to oxygen reduction competing with water oxidation: oxygen can quench the excited photocatalyst, so lowering oxygen availability favors the water-oxidation route and increases `H2O2` generation. ^[oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:209-216]

## Evidence for hydroxyl-radical generation

After accepting an electron, `BTP` forms `BTP•−`, which the authors propose can transfer an electron to nearby `H2O2` to produce [[hydroxyl radicals]]. In this model, hypoxia enhances `•OH` formation indirectly by increasing `H2O2` production through reduced oxygen quenching of `BTP*`. ^[oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:216-229]

A hydroxyphenyl fluorescein (`HPF`) assay showed that BTP photocatalysis generated `•OH`, with stronger signal under hypoxic conditions and when `H2O2` was supplemented. `•OH` production was also impaired in the absence of water, supporting the conclusion that hydroxyl radicals arose from water-mediated photocatalysis and from `H2O2` generated in that process. ^[oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:220-229, oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:787-803]

The source further states that electron paramagnetic resonance spectroscopy with `BMPO` detected the `BMPO-OH` spin adduct after BTP photocatalysis with `H2O2`, providing additional support for hydroxyl-radical formation. ^[oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:229-232, oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:804-825]

## Relationship to other reactive oxygen species

The study notes that when oxygen is reduced by excited `BTP`, [[superoxide]] (`O2•−`) can also be produced, and oxidative quenching can support amino-acid oxidation. However, the paper distinguishes the water-oxidation branch by emphasizing that BTP photocatalysis generates `H2O2` and `•OH` and does not produce [[singlet oxygen]]. ^[oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:233-246]

## Cellular relevance in the study

Within cells, the authors position this chemistry as the basis for intracellular membrane-focused oxidative stress. They state that BTP oxidizes water to generate highly oxidizing radical species, including hydroxyl radicals, even under hypoxia, and link that chemistry to oxidative damage of membrane proteins and downstream biological effects. ^[oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:149-160, oxidative-photocatalysis-on-membranes-triggers-non-canonical-pyroptosis.md:678-692]
