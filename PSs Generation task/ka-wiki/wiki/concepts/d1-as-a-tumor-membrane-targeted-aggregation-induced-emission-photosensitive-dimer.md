# D1 as a tumor-membrane-targeted aggregation-induced emission photosensitive dimer

`D1` is a tumor-cell-membrane-targeted [[aggregation-induced emission]] photosensitive dimer developed for synergistic [[photodynamic therapy]] and [[photothermal therapy]] with an immunotherapy objective. In the source study, it is presented as a dimeric photosensitizer that localizes to tumor cell membranes, generates reactive oxygen species under light, and promotes [[pyroptosis]]-mediated [[immunogenic cell death]] in poorly immunogenic [[4T1]] tumors. ^[pyroptosismediated-synergistic-photodynamic-and-photothermal-immunotherapy-enabled-by-a-tumormembranetargeted-photosensitive-dimer.md:21-32, pyroptosismediated-synergistic-photodynamic-and-photothermal-immunotherapy-enabled-by-a-tumormembranetargeted-photosensitive-dimer.md:139-169]

## Molecular design and photophysical features

The authors designed `D1` as a donor-pi-acceptor conjugated dimer built from triphenylamine, thiophene, and pyridinium-containing chromophores linked by a flexible octyl chain. They report that this dimeric design supports strong intramolecular charge transfer, a small calculated singlet-triplet energy gap, and near-infrared aggregation-induced emission, all of which were presented as favorable for ROS generation and phototherapy. ^[pyroptosismediated-synergistic-photodynamic-and-photothermal-immunotherapy-enabled-by-a-tumormembranetargeted-photosensitive-dimer.md:143-149, pyroptosismediated-synergistic-photodynamic-and-photothermal-immunotherapy-enabled-by-a-tumormembranetargeted-photosensitive-dimer.md:181-211]

In solution, `D1` showed a main absorption peak at `497 nm` and an emission peak at `675 nm`. It also displayed significant aggregation-induced emission in poor solvents, self-assembled into roughly spherical nanostructures with an average diameter of about `110.3 nm`, and had a positive zeta potential of `+10.68 +/- 0.66 mV`, which the authors associate with favorable interaction with negatively charged cancer cells. ^[pyroptosismediated-synergistic-photodynamic-and-photothermal-immunotherapy-enabled-by-a-tumormembranetargeted-photosensitive-dimer.md:212-228]

## Reactive oxygen species and photothermal behavior

`D1` generated multiple reactive oxygen species under light, including [[singlet oxygen]], hydroxyl radical, and superoxide radical. The study reports a singlet-oxygen quantum yield of `89.4%`, along with effective type-I ROS generation confirmed by indicator assays and electron paramagnetic resonance measurements. ^[pyroptosismediated-synergistic-photodynamic-and-photothermal-immunotherapy-enabled-by-a-tumormembranetargeted-photosensitive-dimer.md:229-257]

The same material also showed strong photothermal performance under `520 nm` irradiation. In DMSO, irradiation for `180 s` raised the temperature to `138.4 °C`, the calculated photothermal conversion efficiency was about `64.19%`, and the signal remained stable over four heating-cooling cycles. In vitro, D1-stained cell clusters reached `58.5 °C` under `0.3 W cm^-2` irradiation for `3 min`, whereas negligible heating occurred without the photothermal agent. ^[pyroptosismediated-synergistic-photodynamic-and-photothermal-immunotherapy-enabled-by-a-tumormembranetargeted-photosensitive-dimer.md:258-287]

## Tumor-membrane targeting

Confocal colocalization experiments in [[HeLa]] cells showed that `D1` overlapped strongly with the membrane stain DiO, with a Pearson correlation coefficient of `0.90`, supporting its membrane-targeting behavior. The authors attribute this localization to electrostatic attraction between positively charged `D1` and negatively charged cell membranes, together with limited rapid passage across the phospholipid bilayer. Similar membrane targeting was also observed in [[4T1]] and HeLa cells, and flow cytometry indicated uptake into the cancer cell membrane within `30 min`. ^[pyroptosismediated-synergistic-photodynamic-and-photothermal-immunotherapy-enabled-by-a-tumormembranetargeted-photosensitive-dimer.md:288-305]

## In vitro antitumor and pyroptotic activity

In [[4T1]] cells, `D1` produced strong intracellular ROS signals under light, while the ROS inhibitor `N-acetylcysteine` suppressed the signal. Quantitatively, the fluorescence associated with ROS production in the light-treated `D1` group was reported to be `40` times higher than in the control group. ^[pyroptosismediated-synergistic-photodynamic-and-photothermal-immunotherapy-enabled-by-a-tumormembranetargeted-photosensitive-dimer.md:306-322]

Dark toxicity was low in this assay range: more than `90%` of 4T1 cells remained viable in `0-50 uM` `D1` without irradiation. After `520 nm` laser exposure at `0.3 W cm^-2` for `1 min`, however, cell survival fell substantially as `D1` concentration increased, and live/dead staining showed extensive light-dependent killing. Flow-cytometric analysis further indicated that a large fraction of cells underwent pyroptosis, with total mortality in the irradiated `D1` group reaching `99.1%`. ^[pyroptosismediated-synergistic-photodynamic-and-photothermal-immunotherapy-enabled-by-a-tumormembranetargeted-photosensitive-dimer.md:323-363]

Morphological and molecular assays were consistent with pyroptosis rather than apoptosis. Upon irradiation, D1-treated cells swelled, formed bubbles from the plasma membrane, and showed membrane disruption. Western blotting showed increased cleaved [[Gasdermin D|GSDMD-N]] and cleaved [[caspase-1]], while LDH, `IL-1beta`, and `IL-18` release also increased. The authors interpret these results as ROS-triggered activation of caspase-1 at the membrane, cleavage of GSDMD, pore formation, and pyroptotic cell death accelerated by the photothermal effect. ^[pyroptosismediated-synergistic-photodynamic-and-photothermal-immunotherapy-enabled-by-a-tumormembranetargeted-photosensitive-dimer.md:382-415]

## Immunogenic cell death and immune activation

The study further links `D1`-induced pyroptosis to [[immunogenic cell death]]. In irradiated D1-treated 4T1 cells, calreticulin (`CRT`) exposure on the cell surface increased and `HMGB1` redistributed from the nucleus throughout the cells, which the authors present as evidence that light-triggered pyroptosis induced ICD and initiated adaptive immune responses. ^[pyroptosismediated-synergistic-photodynamic-and-photothermal-immunotherapy-enabled-by-a-tumormembranetargeted-photosensitive-dimer.md:416-425]

Photothermal treatment was reported to reinforce this process. Compared with a lower-power condition interpreted as photodynamic-only treatment, the higher-power condition that added a photothermal component produced stronger `gammaH2AX` DNA-damage signaling, increased expression of DNA-repair proteins `MDC1` and `Ku70`, and greater uptake of the fluorescent drug indicator berberine. The authors interpret these findings as evidence that photothermal treatment further increases membrane permeability and strengthens ICD-related content release. ^[pyroptosismediated-synergistic-photodynamic-and-photothermal-immunotherapy-enabled-by-a-tumormembranetargeted-photosensitive-dimer.md:426-464]

## In vivo antitumor effects

In a [[BALB/c]] mouse 4T1 tumor model using intratumoral injection, `D1` fluorescence remained at the tumor site `24 h` after administration. Under irradiation of the D1-accumulated tumor, tumor temperature rose by about `65 °C` within `1 min` and stayed near that level for the following `5 min`, indicating a strong in vivo photothermal response. Over the `14`-day study period, body weights increased steadily, and organ histology plus blood biochemistry did not indicate obvious liver or kidney damage, which the authors interpret as favorable biosafety and biocompatibility. ^[pyroptosismediated-synergistic-photodynamic-and-photothermal-immunotherapy-enabled-by-a-tumormembranetargeted-photosensitive-dimer.md:465-493]

In a bilateral axillary 4T1 model used to assess systemic effects, light-treated `D1` completely regressed the primary treated tumor within `7 days` and completely inhibited growth of the untreated distant tumor. Histological and immunohistochemical analyses also showed extensive tumor destruction, severe tumor-cell death, reduced angiogenesis by `CD31` staining, and near-loss of proliferative capacity by `Ki67` staining. ^[pyroptosismediated-synergistic-photodynamic-and-photothermal-immunotherapy-enabled-by-a-tumormembranetargeted-photosensitive-dimer.md:494-528]

## Comparison with M1

The monomer `M1`, which shares the same chromophore, served as a control. Relative to `M1`, `D1` showed stronger aggregation-induced emission behavior, self-assembly, higher solid-state fluorescence quantum yield, better ROS generation and photothermal performance, and greater cancer-cell killing efficiency. In vivo, `M1` also showed weaker tumor retention after intratumoral injection, which the authors associate with weaker aggregation behavior and a reduced enhanced permeability and retention effect compared with `D1`. ^[pyroptosismediated-synergistic-photodynamic-and-photothermal-immunotherapy-enabled-by-a-tumormembranetargeted-photosensitive-dimer.md:364-381, pyroptosismediated-synergistic-photodynamic-and-photothermal-immunotherapy-enabled-by-a-tumormembranetargeted-photosensitive-dimer.md:529-536]

## Immune correlates in vivo

Immune analyses of tumors from the `D1+L` group showed increased `CD103` expression, consistent with increased dendritic-cell maturation, and higher `CD4` and `CD8` T-cell signals in the primary tumor. In the bilateral model, the proportion of `CD8+` T cells reached `8.37%` in primary tumors and `22.1%` in distant tumors after D1 photodynamic treatment, while `CD44+/CD62L-` memory-marker expression on distant-tumor `CD3+CD8+` T cells reached `22.4%`. Serum `IL-6`, `IL-18`, `IL-1beta`, `TNF-alpha`, and `IFN-gamma` levels were also highest in the `D1+L` group, supporting the study's conclusion that D1 elicited strong systemic and long-term antitumor immune responses. ^[pyroptosismediated-synergistic-photodynamic-and-photothermal-immunotherapy-enabled-by-a-tumormembranetargeted-photosensitive-dimer.md:537-607]

## Significance in the study

The authors conclude that `D1` functions as a membrane-targeted dimeric photosensitizer whose aggregation behavior improves ROS generation, photothermal performance, and tumor retention relative to the matched monomer. In their interpretation, these features enable efficient pyroptosis, strong ICD, dendritic-cell maturation, T-cell activation, immune memory, elimination of primary tumors, and inhibition of distant tumors and metastasis in the 4T1 model. ^[pyroptosismediated-synergistic-photodynamic-and-photothermal-immunotherapy-enabled-by-a-tumormembranetargeted-photosensitive-dimer.md:608-635]
