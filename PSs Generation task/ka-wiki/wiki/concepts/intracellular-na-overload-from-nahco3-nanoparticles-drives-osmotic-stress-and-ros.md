# Intracellular Na+ overload from NaHCO3 nanoparticles drives osmotic stress and ROS

`NaHCO3` nanoparticles (`NaHCO3 NPs`) were described as intracellular `Na+` reservoirs in tumor cells. In the study, the particles were designed to release high amounts of `Na+` ions after cellular uptake, leading to a rise in intracellular osmolarity and disruption of ionic homeostasis. The authors present this ion-overload effect as a central part of the nanoparticles’ antitumor mechanism, alongside their role in neutralizing tumor acidity. ^[sodium-bicarbonate-nanoparticles-for-amplified-cancer-immunotherapy-by-inducing-pyroptosis-and-regulating-lactic-acid-metabolism.md:61-66, sodium-bicarbonate-nanoparticles-for-amplified-cancer-immunotherapy-by-inducing-pyroptosis-and-regulating-lactic-acid-metabolism.md:124-140, sodium-bicarbonate-nanoparticles-for-amplified-cancer-immunotherapy-by-inducing-pyroptosis-and-regulating-lactic-acid-metabolism.md:149-151]

## Mechanism

The source reports that `NaHCO3 NPs` rapidly released `Na+` ions in buffer, with accumulated release reaching about `85%` within `24 h` at both `pH 7.4` and `pH 6.5`. Based on intracellular `Na+` imaging with the `ENG-2` probe, cells treated with `NaHCO3 NPs` showed a markedly stronger `Na+` signal than control or free sodium bicarbonate (`SoB`) groups, supporting the conclusion that the nanoparticles act as intracellular ion reservoirs. ^[sodium-bicarbonate-nanoparticles-for-amplified-cancer-immunotherapy-by-inducing-pyroptosis-and-regulating-lactic-acid-metabolism.md:181-187, sodium-bicarbonate-nanoparticles-for-amplified-cancer-immunotherapy-by-inducing-pyroptosis-and-regulating-lactic-acid-metabolism.md:240-247]

According to the paper, the rise in intracellular osmolarity and homeostatic imbalance after `Na+` accumulation further enhanced [[reactive oxygen species|ROS]]. Using the `DCFH-DA` probe, the authors observed obvious green fluorescence in the `NaHCO3 NP` group, which they interpret as oxidative stress caused by ion overload. ^[sodium-bicarbonate-nanoparticles-for-amplified-cancer-immunotherapy-by-inducing-pyroptosis-and-regulating-lactic-acid-metabolism.md:247-253]

## Cellular consequences

The study links this osmotic and oxidative stress to strong tumor-cell toxicity. In `4T1` cells, `NaHCO3 NPs` produced concentration-dependent cytotoxicity, with viability falling below `10%` at `400 ug/mL` and an `IC50` of `168 ug/mL`. The paper notes a sharp viability drop between `100 ug/mL` and `200 ug/mL`, and attributes this change to overdose of `Na+` ions causing an immediate intracellular osmolarity imbalance. The same section states that `L929` cells were less affected, which the authors suggest may reflect the higher intracellular ion concentrations of cancer cells and their greater sensitivity to osmotic disturbance. ^[sodium-bicarbonate-nanoparticles-for-amplified-cancer-immunotherapy-by-inducing-pyroptosis-and-regulating-lactic-acid-metabolism.md:208-231]

Morphologic and ultrastructural findings were consistent with osmotic injury. `NaHCO3 NP`-treated cells showed obvious swelling with large bubbles, and this swelling increased with concentration. Bio-TEM images also showed cytomembrane rupture, cellular cavitation, and mitochondrial damage with swelling and cavitation, which the authors attribute to osmotic-pressure imbalance and ROS generation. ^[sodium-bicarbonate-nanoparticles-for-amplified-cancer-immunotherapy-by-inducing-pyroptosis-and-regulating-lactic-acid-metabolism.md:253-279]

## Link to [[pyroptosis]]

The source places the `Na+`-overload and `ROS` response upstream of [[pyroptosis]]. It states that effective ion overload can activate pyroptosis, and reports increased `caspase-1`, increased cleaved `caspase-1`, and upregulation of the `GSDMD-N` fragment after `NaHCO3 NP` treatment, indicating activation of a `caspase-1/GSDMD`-dependent pyroptotic pathway. ^[sodium-bicarbonate-nanoparticles-for-amplified-cancer-immunotherapy-by-inducing-pyroptosis-and-regulating-lactic-acid-metabolism.md:253-292]

Consistent with membrane pore formation and loss of membrane integrity during pyroptosis, the `NaHCO3 NP` group showed substantially increased release of `LDH` and higher `IL-1beta` release than control and free `SoB` groups. The paper also reports induction of [[immunogenic cell death|ICD]] markers, including increased `ATP` release and calreticulin exposure, linking this stress response to broader inflammatory and immune-stimulatory tumor-cell death. ^[sodium-bicarbonate-nanoparticles-for-amplified-cancer-immunotherapy-by-inducing-pyroptosis-and-regulating-lactic-acid-metabolism.md:292-332]

## Context in the study

Within the overall study, intracellular `Na+` overload was one of two coordinated functions assigned to `NaHCO3 NPs`: the nanoparticles both neutralized tumor acidity and triggered ion-driven intracellular stress. The authors argue that this combination contributed to immune activation in vivo, including greater dendritic-cell maturation, increased tumor-infiltrating `CD4+` and `CD8+` T cells, reduced immunosuppressive cell populations, and inhibition of primary and distal tumor growth as well as lung metastasis. ^[sodium-bicarbonate-nanoparticles-for-amplified-cancer-immunotherapy-by-inducing-pyroptosis-and-regulating-lactic-acid-metabolism.md:382-490, sodium-bicarbonate-nanoparticles-for-amplified-cancer-immunotherapy-by-inducing-pyroptosis-and-regulating-lactic-acid-metabolism.md:492-506]
