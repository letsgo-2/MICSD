I’m pulling the claims directly from the provided CA-Re source and shaping them into a concise concept page with paragraph-end citations.# Hypoxia-active photodynamic efficacy of CA-Re

**Hypoxia-active photodynamic efficacy of [[CA-Re carbonic anhydrase-anchored rhenium(I) photosensitizer|CA-Re]]** refers to the reported ability of this CAIX-anchored rhenium(I) photosensitizer to maintain strong light-triggered anti-cancer activity under low-oxygen conditions, where conventional [[photodynamic therapy]] is often impaired. The source frames this as a key design objective because tumor hypoxia both restricts PDT efficiency and contributes to an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment. ^[a-carbonic-anhydrase-ix-caixanchored-rheniumi-photosensitizer-evokes-pyroptosis-for-enhanced-antitumor-immunity.md:17-22, a-carbonic-anhydrase-ix-caixanchored-rheniumi-photosensitizer-evokes-pyroptosis-for-enhanced-antitumor-immunity.md:41-63]

## Why hypoxia matters

The source explains that standard PDT can proceed through an oxygen-dependent type-II pathway that generates singlet oxygen or through a type-I pathway that generates other reactive oxygen species. It also states that tumor hypoxia seriously limits PDT efficiency, creating a need for photosensitizers that remain effective under hypoxic conditions. ^[a-carbonic-anhydrase-ix-caixanchored-rheniumi-photosensitizer-evokes-pyroptosis-for-enhanced-antitumor-immunity.md:41-63]

CA-Re was designed as a [[carbonic anhydrase IX]]-anchored photosensitizer for this setting. The article states that CAIX is a transmembrane protein overexpressed in tumors rather than normal tissues and that its transcription is regulated by hypoxia through HIF-1alpha, linking the targeting strategy directly to hypoxic tumor biology. ^[a-carbonic-anhydrase-ix-caixanchored-rheniumi-photosensitizer-evokes-pyroptosis-for-enhanced-antitumor-immunity.md:86-95]

## Basis for activity under hypoxia

The source attributes CA-Re's hypoxia-compatible PDT behavior to two connected features: it can generate ROS through both type-I and type-II pathways, and it remains anchored on the cell membrane, where in situ ROS production promotes lipid peroxidation and membrane damage. The authors report relatively low singlet-oxygen quantum yields but also detect hydroxyl-radical-associated HPF fluorescence and EPR evidence consistent with superoxide radical anion generation, concluding that CA-Re can operate through both photodynamic pathways. ^[a-carbonic-anhydrase-ix-caixanchored-rheniumi-photosensitizer-evokes-pyroptosis-for-enhanced-antitumor-immunity.md:183-216]

Confocal imaging showed CA-Re on the cell membrane rather than in the cytoplasm after 3 hours and even 24 hours of incubation in MDA-MB-231 cells. The source further reports molecular docking consistent with CAIX binding, reduced cellular uptake after acetazolamide competition, and higher uptake under hypoxia than normoxia, supporting the view that CAIX-associated membrane anchoring contributes to localized ROS production and improved PDT efficiency in hypoxic tumor cells. ^[a-carbonic-anhydrase-ix-caixanchored-rheniumi-photosensitizer-evokes-pyroptosis-for-enhanced-antitumor-immunity.md:217-266]

## In vitro hypoxic phototoxicity

In MDA-MB-231 cells under hypoxia at 1% O2, CA-Re showed a dark IC50 above 100 uM and a light IC50 of 20 nM under 425 nm irradiation, corresponding to a phototoxicity index of 5000. Under 525 nm irradiation, the light IC50 under hypoxia was 190 nM with a phototoxicity index of 526. The source presents these values as evidence that CA-Re retains nanomolar-level phototoxicity even in hypoxic conditions. ^[a-carbonic-anhydrase-ix-caixanchored-rheniumi-photosensitizer-evokes-pyroptosis-for-enhanced-antitumor-immunity.md:267-288, a-carbonic-anhydrase-ix-caixanchored-rheniumi-photosensitizer-evokes-pyroptosis-for-enhanced-antitumor-immunity.md:307-327, a-carbonic-anhydrase-ix-caixanchored-rheniumi-photosensitizer-evokes-pyroptosis-for-enhanced-antitumor-immunity.md:405-423]

The same table shows that under normoxia, CA-Re had a 425 nm light IC50 of 7.3 nM and a 525 nm light IC50 of 76 nM in MDA-MB-231 cells, while remaining much less toxic in the dark. The hypoxic values are higher than the normoxic values, but they remain in the nanomolar range, which the authors emphasize as unusually strong PDT activity under low oxygen. ^[a-carbonic-anhydrase-ix-caixanchored-rheniumi-photosensitizer-evokes-pyroptosis-for-enhanced-antitumor-immunity.md:267-288, a-carbonic-anhydrase-ix-caixanchored-rheniumi-photosensitizer-evokes-pyroptosis-for-enhanced-antitumor-immunity.md:307-327, a-carbonic-anhydrase-ix-caixanchored-rheniumi-photosensitizer-evokes-pyroptosis-for-enhanced-antitumor-immunity.md:405-423]

## Reactive species associated with hypoxic efficacy

Cellular ROS analysis in irradiated MDA-MB-231 cells indicated that singlet oxygen and hydroxyl radicals accounted for only a small part of the total ROS signal, while quenchers for superoxide, peroxynitrite, and lipid peroxidation markedly reduced the signal. Additional assays showed increased DHE fluorescence and dose-dependent malondialdehyde formation after CA-Re plus light treatment. The source therefore identifies superoxide-associated chemistry and lipid peroxidation as major contributors to CA-Re-mediated cell killing. ^[a-carbonic-anhydrase-ix-caixanchored-rheniumi-photosensitizer-evokes-pyroptosis-for-enhanced-antitumor-immunity.md:328-377]

This ROS profile is relevant to hypoxia-active PDT because the paper explicitly contrasts oxygen-dependent singlet-oxygen production with oxygen-independent ROS generation and concludes that CA-Re follows both type-I and type-II pathways. In the article's framing, that mixed ROS behavior helps explain why strong phototoxicity is preserved under hypoxia. ^[a-carbonic-anhydrase-ix-caixanchored-rheniumi-photosensitizer-evokes-pyroptosis-for-enhanced-antitumor-immunity.md:41-45, a-carbonic-anhydrase-ix-caixanchored-rheniumi-photosensitizer-evokes-pyroptosis-for-enhanced-antitumor-immunity.md:183-216]

## 525 nm performance and in vivo relevance

The authors highlight 525 nm irradiation as more biocompatible and already used clinically for another photosensitizer, and they selected this wavelength for in vivo work. Under this irradiation condition, CA-Re still showed nanomolar phototoxicity in hypoxic MDA-MB-231 cells and, in hypoxic 4T1 cells, a light IC50 of 220 nM with a phototoxicity index of 455, which the source describes as comparable to the MDA-MB-231 result under the same treatment. ^[a-carbonic-anhydrase-ix-caixanchored-rheniumi-photosensitizer-evokes-pyroptosis-for-enhanced-antitumor-immunity.md:307-327, a-carbonic-anhydrase-ix-caixanchored-rheniumi-photosensitizer-evokes-pyroptosis-for-enhanced-antitumor-immunity.md:515-525]

In the bilateral 4T1 mouse model, intratumoral CA-Re plus 525 nm light inhibited primary tumor growth by 78% over 16 days and also suppressed untreated distant tumors by 73%, alongside immune activation readouts including dendritic-cell maturation and increased tumor-infiltrating CD4+ and CD8+ T cells. These in vivo findings are part of the study's broader claim that hypoxia-compatible PDT with CA-Re can support both local tumor control and systemic anti-tumor immunity. ^[a-carbonic-anhydrase-ix-caixanchored-rheniumi-photosensitizer-evokes-pyroptosis-for-enhanced-antitumor-immunity.md:536-625]

## Interpretation in the source

In the conclusion, the authors state that CA-Re combines low dark toxicity with nanomolar-level phototoxicity under hypoxia and describe it as one of the most hypoxia-active photosensitizers in their comparison frame. They link this efficacy to CAIX-guided membrane anchoring, in situ ROS production, membrane rupture, and downstream [[pyroptosis]]-associated immunogenicity. ^[a-carbonic-anhydrase-ix-caixanchored-rheniumi-photosensitizer-evokes-pyroptosis-for-enhanced-antitumor-immunity.md:626-645]
