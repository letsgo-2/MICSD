# Microglial pyroptosis contributes to secondary white matter damage after ischemic stroke

[[White matter injury]] is a major pathological process in [[ischemic stroke]]. In patients, it is characterized by demyelination and loss of axonal integrity and is linked to long-term sensorimotor deficits and cognitive impairment. The source also states that brain inflammation after stroke drives secondary injury and worsens white matter disruption. ^[omcl1552127-125.md:65-86]

The paper describes microglia/macrophage-triggered inflammatory responses as important contributors to secondary white matter injury and later white matter repair after stroke. During disease progression, microglia/macrophages shift from an early anti-inflammatory, protective state toward a later chronic detrimental proinflammatory state. Proinflammatory microglia can secrete cytokines, exacerbate oligodendrocyte death and demyelination, and thereby aggravate white matter injury. ^[omcl1552127-125.md:87-105]

Within this inflammatory context, the source identifies microglial [[pyroptosis]] as a proinflammatory programmed cell death process that participates in stroke-induced inflammatory responses. It further states that emerging evidence suggests [[NLRP3 inflammasome]]-mediated microglial pyroptosis plays a crucial role in brain inflammation and functional recovery after stroke, making inhibition of inflammasome-dependent microglial pyroptosis a potential strategy for white matter repair. ^[omcl1552127-125.md:105-133]

The mechanism outlined in the paper centers on inflammasome activation. Pyroptosis is described as depending on inflammasomes formed by NLRP proteins, ASC, and caspases; specifically, NLRP3 oligomerization with ASC and pro-caspase-1 activates the NLRP3 inflammasome, leading to caspase-1 cleavage. Activated caspase-1 then cleaves pro-IL-1beta and pro-IL-18 into mature cytokines and cleaves [[Gasdermin D|GSDMD]] into GSDMD-N, which forms membrane pores that permit release of IL-1beta and IL-18 and promote proinflammatory responses. ^[omcl1552127-125.md:108-125]

In the mouse MCAO model used here, stroke increased pyroptosis-related signals in peri-infarct tissue 21 days after ischemia. Western blot showed elevated NLRP3, cleaved caspase-1, GSDMD-N, pro-IL-1beta, and IL-1beta in peri-infarct regions after MCAO, consistent with increased inflammasome activation and pyroptosis after stroke. ^[omcl1552127-125.md:785-815]

The study also localized these changes to microglia/macrophage populations. Immunofluorescence showed increased proportions of GSDMD-positive Iba1-positive cells and caspase-1-positive Iba1-positive cells in the ipsilateral cortex, corpus callosum, and striatum 21 days after stroke, which the authors present as evidence of stroke-induced microglial pyroptosis and caspase-1 activation in white matter-associated and adjacent regions. ^[omcl1552127-125.md:916-956]

The paper directly links this process to white matter pathology. In the discussion and schematic summary, the authors state that microglial pyroptosis-mediated proinflammatory responses aggravate stroke-induced white matter damage, and they describe microglia-mediated inflammatory responses as having a critical impact on secondary damage and spontaneous white matter repair after stroke. ^[omcl1552127-125.md:1866-1868, omcl1552127-125.md:2025-2039, omcl1552127-125.md:2364-2372]

Additional support for that link comes from the NLRP3 knockdown experiments. In vivo, AAV-based NLRP3 shRNA reduced pyroptosis-associated proteins in peri-infarct tissue, attenuated stroke-induced white matter lesions, and improved sensorimotor recovery; the authors conclude from these findings that reducing NLRP3 inflammasome activation lessens white matter damage and functional deficits after stroke. ^[omcl1552127-125.md:1635-1654, omcl1552127-125.md:1883-1893]

The authors therefore frame microglial pyroptosis as part of a broader inflammatory cascade that contributes to secondary white matter injury after ischemic stroke. In their model, stroke activates [[NF-kappaB]], which promotes NLRP3-related inflammatory signaling and pyroptosis in microglia; the resulting release of proinflammatory mediators is associated with worsened white matter injury and poorer functional recovery. ^[omcl1552127-125.md:1655-1824, omcl1552127-125.md:2282-2310, omcl1552127-125.md:2364-2372]
