# GSDMD pore-dependent release of PGE2

GSDMD pore-dependent release of [[PGE2]] refers to the finding that, during pyroptosis, prostaglandin E2 is released through a process that requires [[Gasdermin D|GSDMD]] pore formation. In the cited study, PGE2 synthesis was induced de novo during pyroptosis downstream of [[caspase-1]], and its later release depended on pores opened by GSDMD. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:53-56, oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1007-1028]

## Overview

The study investigated pyroptotic secretomes generated under conditions that avoided IL-1beta and IL-1alpha release, termed `Pyro−1`, and found that these supernatants contained oxylipins including PGE2. Lipidomics showed that PGE2 was present in `Pyro−1` supernatants but absent from control supernatants and from apoptotic supernatants generated from macrophages exposed to the same trigger. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:43-59, oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:213-230]

This observation was placed in the broader context of [[pyroptosis]] as a lytic cell death program driven by gasdermin-family membrane pores, with GSDMD described as the primary executor of inflammasome-induced pyroptosis. The paper notes that inflammasome activation leads to caspase-1-mediated cleavage and release of inflammatory cytokines through GSDMD membrane pores, and then extends this framework to include PGE2 release. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:60-67, oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1016-1028]

## De novo synthesis during pyroptosis

The authors tested whether released PGE2 came from pre-existing intracellular stores or was newly generated during pyroptosis. In macrophages treated overnight with cyclooxygenase inhibitors, PGE2 levels in pyroptotic supernatants were strongly reduced; inhibition by the COX2-selective inhibitor `NS-398` was sufficient to strongly reduce PGE2 release, suggesting a greater requirement for COX2. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:731-743]

When reversible inhibitors were washed off before pyroptosis induction, PGE2 release recovered to levels seen in untreated `Pyro−1` supernatants, whereas aspirin, an irreversible inhibitor, continued to block PGE2 release. The authors interpret this as strong evidence that cyclooxygenase activity is induced during pyroptosis and leads to de novo PGE2 generation and release. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:993-999]

Kinetic aspirin-addition experiments further suggested that PGE2 generation and release occur during later stages of pyroptosis. Adding aspirin as late as 60 minutes after the LeTox trigger still significantly reduced PGE2 in `Pyro−1` supernatants, and the paper notes that later addition was not technically feasible because cells had already begun releasing contents by about 75 minutes. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1000-1006]

## Evidence for pore-dependent release

Time-course measurements showed that PGE2 was already detectable in `Pyro−1` supernatants at 90 minutes after LeTox trigger and was significantly higher by 120 minutes. In the same system, cells became permeable to `7-AAD` by 90 minutes, whereas LDH release was best detected around 120 minutes, placing PGE2 release within the evolving membrane-permeabilization phase of pyroptosis. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:260-270, oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:504-510]

To test the role of GSDMD directly, the authors used both genetic and pharmacologic approaches. Pyroptosis induction in `Gsdmd-KO` macrophages did not lead to PGE2 release into supernatants, whereas control macrophages secreted significant amounts of PGE2. Likewise, pretreatment with disulfiram, described in the paper as an inhibitor of GSDMD pores, completely inhibited PGE2 release from `Nlrp1b-Tg` macrophages. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1016-1028]

On this basis, the study concludes that PGE2 release from pyroptotic cells requires GSDMD pore formation. In the paper’s discussion, this is summarized as de novo oxylipin production downstream of caspase-1 activation, with PGE2 release dependent on GSDMD pores and occurring during the final stages of pyroptosis. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1026-1028, oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1567-1572]

## Relationship to caspase-1

The paper also tested whether PGE2 release depends on caspase-1 activity upstream of GSDMD pore formation. In human THP-1 monocytic cells engineered to express an inducible caspase-1, direct caspase-1 activation was sufficient to trigger significant PGE2 release. The authors state that this supports de novo prostaglandin synthesis during pyroptosis occurring downstream of caspase-1 activation. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1007-1015]

## Functional significance in the study

Within this study, PGE2 was treated as a functional component of the pyroptotic secretome rather than only a biochemical marker. Bioinformatic comparison suggested that PGE2-dependent signaling accounted for about half of the gene-expression signature induced by `Pyro−1` supernatants in target macrophages, and the overlapping genes included many of the highly upregulated wound-healing-associated genes. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:204-230]

The study further showed that `Pyro−1` supernatants promoted fibroblast wound closure in vitro and improved colonic wound repair in vivo, and that supernatants retained less benefit when generated under aspirin treatment. In a colonic punch-biopsy model whose repair depended on localized bioactive PGE2, `Pyro−1` supernatants promoted wound repair and were associated with claudin-4-positive wound-associated epithelial cells, which the authors used as evidence that the released PGE2 remained bioactive in vivo. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:231-254, oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1029-1047, oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1072-1078]
