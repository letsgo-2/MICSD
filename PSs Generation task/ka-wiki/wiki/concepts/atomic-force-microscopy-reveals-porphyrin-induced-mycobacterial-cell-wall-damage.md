# Atomic force microscopy reveals porphyrin-induced mycobacterial cell-wall damage

In this study of rapidly growing [[mycobacteria]], [[atomic force microscopy]] (AFM) was used to evaluate how the tetra-cationic palladium porphyrin `3-PdTPyP` affected *Mycolicibacterium smegmatis* under dark and white-light conditions. The authors report that AFM provided topographic, nanomechanical, and electrostatic information and showed that porphyrin treatment, especially when combined with white-light exposure, was associated with marked damage to the mycobacterial cell wall. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:33-35, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:192-214, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:516-523]

## AFM setup

The AFM analysis was performed after 24 hours of incubating *M. smegmatis* in the presence or absence of `3-PdTPyP` and under different light-exposure conditions. The measurements included surface topography, adhesion mapping from force-distance curves, and electrical force microscopy to assess surface charge distribution. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:193-214]

## Topographic evidence of cell-wall damage

AFM topographic images showed that untreated bacteria in the dark preserved their rod-shaped morphology. The porphyrin alone slightly affected bacterial morphology, producing grooves on the top surface, and white light alone produced similar mild effects. By contrast, porphyrin treatment under light significantly altered morphology, with bacilli described as wrinkled and thinned. In the discussion, the authors further state that AFM revealed significant changes in cell-wall morphology, including the formation of cavities, and that topographic images confirmed bacterial inactivation or destruction after treatment. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:255-287, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:516-520]

## Nanomechanical changes

AFM nanomechanical measurements showed increased adhesion forces after `3-PdTPyP` treatment. Untreated bacteria under dark conditions had an adhesion force of `0.14 +/- 0.03 uN`, increasing slightly to `0.17 +/- 0.03 uN` with light alone. With porphyrin treatment, adhesion increased to `0.25 +/- 0.05 uN` in the dark and `0.4 +/- 0.16 uN` under light. At the same time, bacterial width decreased from `0.75 +/- 0.03 um` in the dark control to `0.46 +/- 0.05 um` with porphyrin in the dark and `0.43 +/- 0.05 um` with porphyrin plus light, while length increased up to `3.60 +/- 0.33 um` under light plus porphyrin. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:288-298, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:475-492]

The authors interpret the higher adhesion as a consequence of structural disruption of the mycobacterial membrane. They state that cavities and cell-wall fragments produced by treatment could promote stronger interaction between the AFM tip and the wall, increasing contact area and therefore adhesion force. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:525-535]

## Electrostatic changes

Electrical force microscopy indicated that the mycobacterium-substrate interface was electrostatically charged and that untreated cells showed stronger positive electrostatic forces around the bacterial perimeter, particularly in the dark without porphyrin. After porphyrin treatment followed by light exposure, dark spots spread over the bacterial surface, which the authors interpret as evidence of significant electrochemical degradation of the cell wall under these conditions. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:299-314]

In the discussion, the authors add that porphyrin treatment decreased the electrostatic component of bacteria-substrate adhesion and that many positive spots observed under light-plus-porphyrin conditions correlated with the membrane cavities seen in topographic images. They present AFM as a tool not only for high-resolution topographic mapping but also for studying physicochemical, nanomechanical, and interfacial changes in bacteria after photochemical treatment. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:536-548]

## Relationship to porphyrin photodamage

The AFM findings are presented alongside evidence that `3-PdTPyP` was the more active of the two tested palladium porphyrins and that its antimicrobial photodynamic activity involved reactive oxygen species, especially [[singlet oxygen]]. In the paper's conclusion, the authors state that the meta-substituted isomer `3-PdTPyP` promoted damage to the mycobacterial cell wall and altered nanomechanical and electrostatic adhesion properties. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:223-231, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:244-253, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:571-580]
