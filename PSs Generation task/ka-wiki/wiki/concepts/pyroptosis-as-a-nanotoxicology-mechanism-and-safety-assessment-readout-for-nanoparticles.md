# Pyroptosis as a nanotoxicology mechanism and safety-assessment readout for nanoparticles

Pyroptosis is presented in the source as a relevant mechanism for evaluating nanoparticle toxicity, especially because many engineered nanomaterials are now used in consumer products and biomedical applications while their repeated or heavy use raises questions about biosafety and occupational risk. The review explicitly includes pyroptosis-based toxicological assessment of inorganic nanoparticles because of its close relationship to human health and frames toxicological assessment as necessary for identifying nanoparticle hazards and improving biosafety. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:293-302, cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:830-850]

In this context, pyroptosis is a form of programmed cell death with inflammatory consequences. Morphologically, it combines early apoptosis-like DNA fragmentation and chromatin condensation with later necrosis-like membrane pore formation, cell swelling with large bubbles, membrane rupture, and release of intracellular contents and inflammatory cytokines such as [[IL-1beta]] and [[IL-18]]. Mechanistically, the review describes canonical [[pyroptosis]] as driven by pattern-recognition-receptor sensing of danger signals, inflammasome assembly, [[caspase-1]] activation, and [[Gasdermin D|GSDMD]] cleavage, while non-canonical pyroptosis involves caspase-4/5/11-mediated GSDMD cleavage. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:146-206, cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:309-314]

As a safety-assessment readout, the review uses pyroptosis-associated markers to connect nanoparticle exposure to tissue injury and inflammatory hazard. Across the examples discussed, relevant readouts include [[NLRP3 inflammasome]] activation, cleaved caspase-1, GSDMD involvement, secretion of IL-1beta, release of cytosolic enzymes such as [[LDH]], reactive oxygen species generation, membrane rupture, and histologic or functional evidence of organ injury. These measurements are used to distinguish pyroptotic injury from other death programs and to identify particle classes that may pose biosafety risks. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:872-883, cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:894-911, cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:917-954, cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:964-973, cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:997-1016]

## Why pyroptosis matters in nanotoxicology

The review argues that nanoparticle safety assessment is urgent because nanomaterials such as metal oxides, rare-earth oxides, silicon dioxide, and silver nanoparticles are widely used in products and in diagnosis or drug delivery, yet frequent exposure may create risks for users and workers. Within that framing, pyroptosis is not treated only as a mechanistic curiosity; it is discussed as a biologically meaningful route by which nanoparticles can damage normal tissues and thereby as a useful lens for hazard evaluation. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:832-850]

The source also emphasizes that pyroptosis is potentially harmful to normal organs and tissues, and in its concluding discussion specifically warns that several commonly used nanomaterials can injure normal cells through pyroptosis-linked mechanisms. It therefore recommends emphasizing safety assessments and building a stronger toxicity database to support regulation and reduce occupational disease. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:88-95, cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:1034-1039, cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:1122-1140]

## Nanoparticle examples

### Mesoporous silica nanoparticles

For [[mesoporous silica nanoparticles]], the review describes significant concern over toxicological hazards in biological applications and cites in vivo evidence of morphological and functional liver damage. It states that MSN-treated hepatic tissue showed obvious pyroptosis in hepatocytes, indicating activation of the [[NLRP3 inflammasome]] and the pyroptosis pathway in liver tissue. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:852-866]

The mechanistic evidence for MSN hepatotoxicity included attenuation of NLRP3 inflammasome activation, cleaved caspase-1, IL-1beta, and serum ALT, AST, and LDH in NLRP3-deficient mice compared with wild-type mice. The review also notes that cellular ROS increased after MSN administration, supporting ROS involvement in MSN-induced hepatotoxicity. Together, these findings are presented as evidence that pyroptosis-related readouts can explain nanoparticle-associated liver inflammation and damage. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:872-883]

### Metal oxide nanoparticles

For a broader panel of [[metal oxide nanoparticles]], including rare-earth oxides and transition-metal oxides, the review highlights the liver and mononuclear phagocyte system as common targets and therefore important contexts for safety evaluation. In the summarized study, a panel of 29 particles was tested in Kupffer-like cells, macrophages, and hepatocytes to establish comparative toxicological profiles. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:885-893]

The review reports that rare-earth oxides, except CeO2, activated caspase-1 in KUP5 cells, whereas Hepa 1-6 cells did not respond similarly, and that several rare-earth and transition-metal oxides also activated caspase-3 and caspase-7. After GSDMD knockdown, LDH release from KUP5 cells exposed to CeO2, Gd2O3, La2O3, Y2O3, or Co3O4 decreased, which the review interprets as evidence that these particles induced pyroptosis in phagocytic cells. It further states that pyroptosis appeared to be a feature of phagocytic cells rather than primary hepatocytes, illustrating how cell type shapes nanotoxicity readouts. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:894-911]

### Indium-tin-oxide nanoparticles

The review treats [[indium-tin-oxide nanoparticles]] as especially important for occupational safety because exposure to indium compounds is associated with pulmonary alveolar proteinosis and interstitial lung disease. In the summarized work, intraperitoneal inoculation with ITO nanoparticles increased IL-1beta in peritoneal fluid, indicating that ITO nanoparticles activated NLRP3 inflammasomes and exacerbated inflammation in mice. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:912-923]

Additional readouts linked ITO exposure to pyroptotic death in alveolar macrophage-like MH-S cells. Endosome enlargement was time- and dose-dependent, treated cells secreted TNF-alpha, and exposed 51Cr-labeled cells released 51Cr, while the caspase-1 inhibitor Ac-YVAD reduced that release. The review states that LDH assays and other analyses indicated the death program was neither apoptosis nor necroptosis but caspase-1-dependent pyroptosis with plasma-membrane rupture and intracellular-content release. It also notes that mesenchymal stem cells reduced this proinflammatory pyroptosis and increased IL-10 secretion. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:923-954]

### Carbon black nanoparticles

For [[carbon black nanoparticles]], the review focuses on inhalation-related risk and macrophage injury. It states that carbon black nanoparticles induced LDH release from RAW264.7 cells, indicating reduced plasma-membrane integrity and cell death. Both the caspase-1 inhibitor YVAD and the pyroptosis inhibitor glycine attenuated LDH release, which the review cites as evidence that carbon black nanoparticles induced pyroptosis. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:955-973]

The source frames this result as important specifically for toxicological assessment of the impact of carbon-based particles on human health. In other words, inhibition-sensitive pyroptosis readouts are used here as part of the argument that environmentally relevant particle exposure can be mechanistically tied to inflammatory cell death. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:967-973]

### Silver nanoparticles

For [[silver nanoparticles]], the review says that potential human toxicity remained incompletely understood and required molecular and cellular investigation. In the summarized experiments, 15 nm silver nanoparticles induced rapid degradation of ATF-6 in THP-1 cells, suggesting endoplasmic-reticulum-stress-related events. They affected activation of caspase-4 and caspase-7, but not caspase-3, supporting a death mechanism distinct from apoptosis. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:974-1004]

The review further reports that NLRP3 knockdown weakened NLRP3 protein expression and IL-1 secretion, while caspase-4 was crucial for pro-IL-1 and acted independently of caspase-1 activation. Inhibition of ATF-6 processing reduced caspase-1 processing and activation, PI-positive cells, and IL-1 secretion. The source concludes from these data that AgNP15 triggered ATF-6 decomposition followed by NLRP3 inflammasome activation, adjusted by caspase-4, in THP-1 cells. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:1005-1016]

## Common safety-assessment readouts

Across the examples in the review, pyroptosis-based nanotoxicology assessment relies on a recurring set of measurements: inflammasome activation status, caspase processing or inhibition sensitivity, gasdermin dependence, inflammatory cytokine release, membrane-damage markers such as LDH or 51Cr release, ROS generation, and tissue-level injury markers such as liver enzymes or histologic damage. These readouts are used comparatively across particle classes and cell types to identify whether a nanoparticle provokes inflammatory programmed cell death and where that hazard is most pronounced. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:872-883, cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:894-911, cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:917-954, cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:964-973, cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:997-1016]

## Significance for nanoparticle biosafety

The review’s overall position is that pyroptosis should be taken seriously in nanoparticle safety work because it links nanomaterial exposure to inflammatory tissue injury in normal cells and organs. Its conclusion specifically cites MSNs, ITO nanoparticles, and AgNP15 as examples of nanomaterials whose harmful effects involve pyroptotic mechanisms, and it argues for stronger safety assessment and a more comprehensive toxicity database to support safer use and regulation of nanomaterials. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:1122-1140]
