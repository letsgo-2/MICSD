# Superoxide and lipid peroxidation dominate CA-Re phototoxicity

## Overview

In the study, [[CA-Re carbonic anhydrase-anchored rhenium(I) photosensitizer|CA-Re]] generated both type-I and type-II reactive oxygen species under irradiation, but the cellular data indicate that its phototoxicity was dominated mainly by superoxide and lipid peroxidation rather than by singlet oxygen or hydroxyl radicals. The authors used intracellular ROS readouts, ROS scavenger experiments, superoxide imaging, and malondialdehyde measurement to identify which species contributed most strongly to photodynamic damage. ^[a-carbonic-anhydrase-ix-caixanchored-rheniumi-photosensitizer-evokes-pyroptosis-for-enhanced-antitumor-immunity.md:183-216, a-carbonic-anhydrase-ix-caixanchored-rheniumi-photosensitizer-evokes-pyroptosis-for-enhanced-antitumor-immunity.md:328-371]

## Evidence from ROS characterization

Photophysical measurements showed that CA-Re could sensitize singlet oxygen, hydroxyl-radical-related signals, and superoxide-associated signals under light exposure. However, the reported singlet oxygen quantum yield was low, especially in PBS, while EPR using DMPO detected a characteristic signal consistent with superoxide radical anion formation in the presence of irradiated CA-Re. These results supported the view that CA-Re had mixed ROS behavior with a substantial type-I component. ^[a-carbonic-anhydrase-ix-caixanchored-rheniumi-photosensitizer-evokes-pyroptosis-for-enhanced-antitumor-immunity.md:183-216]

In MDA-MB-231 cells, total intracellular ROS measured with DCF increased strongly after CA-Re plus 425 nm irradiation, but scavenger experiments showed unequal contributions from different reactive species. Pre-incubation with sodium azide and d-mannitol, used as singlet oxygen and hydroxyl radical scavengers, caused only slight decreases in DCF fluorescence. By contrast, tiron, ebselen, and Trolox, used to quench superoxide, peroxynitrite, and lipid peroxidation-related species, effectively suppressed the ROS signal. The authors concluded from these experiments that superoxide and lipid peroxidation accounted for the vast majority of CA-Re PDT-induced ROS. ^[a-carbonic-anhydrase-ix-caixanchored-rheniumi-photosensitizer-evokes-pyroptosis-for-enhanced-antitumor-immunity.md:328-353]

## Direct support for superoxide and lipid peroxidation

Additional cellular assays were used to confirm the dominant species. Confocal imaging with the superoxide indicator DHE showed negligible fluorescence in the dark and substantially increased fluorescence after irradiation, consistent with intracellular superoxide generation during CA-Re photodynamic treatment. ^[a-carbonic-anhydrase-ix-caixanchored-rheniumi-photosensitizer-evokes-pyroptosis-for-enhanced-antitumor-immunity.md:354-361]

Lipid peroxidation was verified by measuring intracellular malondialdehyde using a thiobarbituric acid assay. MDA levels were negligible in control cells but increased in a dose-dependent manner after CA-Re plus irradiation, indicating strong oxidative damage to cellular lipids. The study therefore identified lipid peroxidation, together with superoxide, as the cytotoxic chemistry most responsible for CA-Re-induced cancer cell death under PDT conditions. ^[a-carbonic-anhydrase-ix-caixanchored-rheniumi-photosensitizer-evokes-pyroptosis-for-enhanced-antitumor-immunity.md:362-371]

## Mechanistic context

The source links this ROS pattern to [[CAIX anchoring through sulfonamide-based carbonic anhydrase inhibition|CAIX-anchored]] membrane localization. CA-Re remained on the cell membrane rather than entering the cytoplasm, and the authors state that it was highly possible for lipid peroxidation to occur in membrane phospholipids because of attack by nearby superoxide or direct electron transfer from the excited rhenium complex. This membrane-focused oxidative damage was presented as a basis for membrane rupture and downstream [[Pyroptosis]]-related events. ^[a-carbonic-anhydrase-ix-caixanchored-rheniumi-photosensitizer-evokes-pyroptosis-for-enhanced-antitumor-immunity.md:217-266, a-carbonic-anhydrase-ix-caixanchored-rheniumi-photosensitizer-evokes-pyroptosis-for-enhanced-antitumor-immunity.md:372-377]

The study also reported that [[GPX4]] expression was substantially down-regulated after CA-Re photodynamic treatment. Because GPX4 is described in the paper as a lipid peroxidation defense enzyme with a negative regulatory effect on GSDMD cleavage and pyroptosis, the authors suggested that extensive lipid oxidation might exhaust GPX4 and thereby favor pyroptotic cell death. ^[a-carbonic-anhydrase-ix-caixanchored-rheniumi-photosensitizer-evokes-pyroptosis-for-enhanced-antitumor-immunity.md:397-435]

## Significance within the study

Within the reported mechanism, dominance of superoxide and lipid peroxidation helps explain why CA-Re produced efficient phototoxicity under hypoxia and promoted membrane damage rather than a conventional apoptotic phenotype. The paper presents this oxidative profile as part of the chain linking membrane-anchored ROS generation to membrane rupture, [[Gasdermin D|GSDMD]] activation, release of immunogenic signals, and enhanced anti-tumor immunity. ^[a-carbonic-anhydrase-ix-caixanchored-rheniumi-photosensitizer-evokes-pyroptosis-for-enhanced-antitumor-immunity.md:115-132, a-carbonic-anhydrase-ix-caixanchored-rheniumi-photosensitizer-evokes-pyroptosis-for-enhanced-antitumor-immunity.md:378-450, a-carbonic-anhydrase-ix-caixanchored-rheniumi-photosensitizer-evokes-pyroptosis-for-enhanced-antitumor-immunity.md:626-645]
