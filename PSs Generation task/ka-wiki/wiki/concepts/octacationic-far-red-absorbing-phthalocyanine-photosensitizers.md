# Octacationic far-red-absorbing phthalocyanine photosensitizers

Octacationic far-red-absorbing phthalocyanine photosensitizers are a set of water-soluble, octasubstituted [[phthalocyanine]]-based [[photosensitizer]]s developed for [[photodynamic therapy]]. In the source study, they include zinc, magnesium, and metal-free phthalocyanines bearing eight `[(triethylammonio)ethyl]sulfanyl` substituents arranged at either peripheral or nonperipheral positions, along with an aza analogue used for comparison. The work focused on achieving strong absorption in the far-red region, improving water solubility, reducing aggregation, and examining photodynamic anticancer activity and the mode of cell death induced after irradiation. ^[jm5014852-114.md:19-24, jm5014852-114.md:63-75, jm5014852-114.md:91-95]

## Design rationale

The source frames these compounds as a response to two common limitations of phthalocyanines in PDT: limited water solubility with aggregation in aqueous media, and the shallow tissue penetration of activating light. Because longer wavelengths penetrate tissue more deeply, the study aimed to develop water-soluble phthalocyanines absorbing near or above 700 nm. Eight cationic substituents were introduced to increase water solubility and to reduce aggregation through electrostatic repulsion. ^[jm5014852-114.md:47-62, jm5014852-114.md:63-75, jm5014852-114.md:91-93]

The compounds were designed to compare the effects of substituent position and central metal. Specifically, the study examined peripheral versus nonperipheral substitution, compared zinc, magnesium, and metal-free cores, and included an aza analogue to broaden the structure-activity analysis. The source states that both substituent position and aza substitution substantially influence the wavelength of the main absorption band. ^[jm5014852-114.md:69-75, jm5014852-114.md:87-95]

## Representative compounds and optical properties

Among the quaternized zinc derivatives, `1Zn-Et` had a Q-band maximum at `704 nm`, `2Zn-Et` at `756 nm`, and the aza analogue `3Zn-Et` at `656 nm` in DMF. The source identifies `2Zn-Et` as especially notable because its Q-band maximum lies above `750 nm`, which it describes as highly advantageous for PDT. Moving substituents from peripheral to nonperipheral positions produced a strong bathochromic shift, while aza substitution caused a hypsochromic shift relative to the parent phthalocyanines. ^[jm5014852-114.md:140-153, jm5014852-114.md:217-233]

The quaternized derivatives were highly water-soluble. Reported aqueous solubilities were greater than `1.5 mM` for `1Zn-Et` and `3Zn-Et`, and about `0.5 mM` for `2Zn-Et`. In water, `2Zn-Et` and `3Zn-Et` showed spectra resembling those in DMF, consistent with essentially nonaggregated species, whereas `1Zn-Et` showed spectral changes typical of H-aggregation. The study concludes that aza substitution and nonperipheral substitution help reduce aggregation, and that having eight charged substituents appears important for monomerization in water. ^[jm5014852-114.md:154-185]

## Photophysical behavior

The source reports that zinc complexes consistently had higher [[singlet oxygen]] quantum yields than the corresponding magnesium complexes, while nonperipheral substitution favored singlet oxygen production over peripheral substitution. `2Zn-Et` was highlighted as the most photophysically promising compound, with a reported singlet oxygen quantum yield (`ΦΔ`) of `0.91`. By contrast, metal-free derivatives produced singlet oxygen and fluorescence only weakly and were excluded from biological evaluation. ^[jm5014852-114.md:188-206, jm5014852-114.md:217-239]

The aza analogue behaved differently because of photoinduced electron transfer. The source states that `3Zn` had extremely low `ΦΔ` and `ΦF` values because PET between the peripheral tertiary amino substituents and the macrocyclic core deactivated excited states. Alkylation blocked PET and improved the quantum yields of `3Zn-Et`, but they remained relatively low compared with the parent zinc phthalocyanines. ^[jm5014852-114.md:209-216, jm5014852-114.md:249-254]

## In vitro photodynamic activity

The study evaluated dark toxicity in [[HeLa]] and `3T3` cells and phototoxicity in `3T3`, HeLa, `SK-MEL-28`, and `HCT 116` cells after irradiation with light at `λ > 570 nm` for `15 min` at `11.2 J/cm^2`. The magnesium complexes `1Mg-Et` and `2Mg-Et` showed no phototoxicity up to `100 uM`, whereas the zinc complexes were active in the order `2Zn-Et < 1Zn-Et < 3Zn-Et` by `IC50`, matching their relative singlet oxygen production. Across the tested malignant cell lines, `SK-MEL-28` was the most susceptible, followed by HeLa and `HCT 116`. ^[jm5014852-114.md:290-312, jm5014852-114.md:322-331, jm5014852-114.md:1121-1140]

For HeLa cells, reported `IC50` values were `0.31 uM` for `2Zn-Et`, `0.54 uM` for `1Zn-Et`, and `3.70 uM` for `3Zn-Et`, while the magnesium derivatives remained above `100 uM`. Dark toxicity differed much more strongly by structure: `1Zn-Et` had a HeLa `TC50` of `1639 uM`, `2Zn-Et` `192 uM`, and `3Zn-Et` `226 uM`. The resulting `TC50/IC50` ratios in HeLa were `3035` for `1Zn-Et`, `619` for `2Zn-Et`, and `61` for `3Zn-Et`, leading the authors to identify `1Zn-Et` and `2Zn-Et` as the best candidates for further mechanistic study. ^[jm5014852-114.md:274-289, jm5014852-114.md:322-331, jm5014852-114.md:364-378]

Phototoxicity also depended on light dose. When the concentration of selected zinc complexes was fixed at the corresponding `IC50`, increasing irradiation from `5` to `25 min` decreased HeLa cell viability further, showing fluence dependence of the photodynamic effect. The source also reports intracellular ROS formation during irradiation with `1Zn-Et` and `2Zn-Et`, with stronger ROS generation for `2Zn-Et`; it interprets this as evidence that photoprocess type I contributed alongside type II, although singlet oxygen likely remained the dominant pathway. ^[jm5014852-114.md:354-363, jm5014852-114.md:379-400, jm5014852-114.md:1141-1169]

## Cellular localization and mode of damage

In HeLa cells, both `1Zn-Et` and `2Zn-Et` localized primarily to vesicles that colocalized with lysosomes. The source attributes this localization most likely to endocytic uptake of bulky, highly charged compounds. After irradiation, lysosomal fluorescence rapidly disappeared and the photosensitizer redistributed into the cytoplasm, indicating lysosomal rupture as an early event. ^[jm5014852-114.md:401-417, jm5014852-114.md:462-470, jm5014852-114.md:1090-1102]

This lysosomal damage was followed by broader organelle injury. The study observed mitochondrial rounding and loss of mitochondrion-specific fluorescence, changes in cell shape, plasma membrane damage marked by propidium iodide entry, and later nuclear involvement. The authors interpret this sequence as primary lysosomal photodamage followed by redistribution of the still-activated photosensitizer and secondary injury to mitochondria, plasma membrane, and finally the nucleus. ^[jm5014852-114.md:471-487, jm5014852-114.md:495-535]

## Cell death response

The source concludes that these compounds, particularly `1Zn-Et` and `2Zn-Et`, induced predominantly necrosis-like cell death after photodynamic treatment. Time-lapse morphology showed rapid conversion from spindle-like cells to saccular forms with debris formation, while flow cytometry with annexin V-FITC and propidium iodide showed both apoptotic and necrotic populations but with a strong increase in debris consistent with severe necrotic damage. ^[jm5014852-114.md:481-487, jm5014852-114.md:536-573]

Caspase measurements suggested that classical apoptosis was not the dominant mechanism. Initiator caspases 8 and 9 were generally not activated before executioner caspases 3/7, which instead rose notably around `4 h` after irradiation. The source proposes that lysosomal rupture and release of cathepsins or other proteases may have caused secondary activation of executioner caspases. It also reports that autophagy was not detectably increased under the irradiation protocol used. ^[jm5014852-114.md:574-626]

## Interpretation

Within this study, the most promising octacationic far-red-absorbing phthalocyanine photosensitizers were the zinc complexes, especially the nonperipherally substituted `2Zn-Et` and the peripherally substituted `1Zn-Et`. `2Zn-Et` combined absorption above `750 nm` with very high singlet oxygen yield and strong phototoxicity, while `1Zn-Et` combined submicromolar phototoxicity with exceptionally low dark toxicity. The study therefore presents these octacationic phthalocyanines as effective PDT candidates whose biological action is linked to early lysosomal damage and predominantly necrosis-like cell death. ^[jm5014852-114.md:203-206, jm5014852-114.md:309-318, jm5014852-114.md:370-378, jm5014852-114.md:627-683]
