# Dual tumor-promoting and tumor-suppressive roles of IL-1β, IL-18, and HMGB1 in pyroptotic tumor microenvironments

[[Pyroptosis]] releases inflammatory mediators including [[IL-1beta]], [[IL-18]], and [[HMGB1]], and these factors shape the [[tumor microenvironment]] in ways that can either support or restrain cancer. The source describes pyroptosis as a double-edged process in cancer: chronic pyroptosis-derived inflammation can help maintain a tumor-promoting environment, whereas acute and widespread pyroptotic activation can drive immune-cell infiltration, strengthen antitumor immunity, and increase cancer cell death. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:223-230, pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:541-565, pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:612-621]

The review specifically states that [[IL-1beta]], [[IL-18]], and [[HMGB1]] can each have either tumor-promoting or tumor-suppressive effects in the tumor microenvironment, making their impact context dependent rather than uniformly beneficial or harmful. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:612-621]

## IL-1β

In the tumor microenvironment, IL-1β has dual functions linked to tumor growth. On the antitumor side, IL-1β signaling activates innate immune cells and antigen-presenting cells, promotes polarization and activation of CD4+ T cells toward Th1 and Th17 states, and is described as beneficial in acute inflammation and in evoking specific anticancer defenses. The review also notes that low IL-1β in `NLRP1(-/-)` mice is associated with increased inflammation and cancer burden. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:623-633]

IL-1β is also described as tumor-promoting under immunosuppressive conditions. The source states that IL-1β enhances [[tumor-associated macrophage|TAM]] and [[myeloid-derived suppressor cell|MDSC]] populations in an immunosuppressive environment, thereby promoting tumor growth. At the same time, IL-1β can stimulate the expansion of dendritic cells derived from monocytes and promote hyperactive dendritic cells that present tumor lysates to lymphocytes, triggering antigen-specific cytotoxic CD8+ T-cell responses. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:633-644]

## IL-18

IL-18 is presented as a systemic multifunctional cytokine with both procancer and anticancer properties. Its antitumor side includes promotion of IFN production, enhancement of Th1 immune responses, activation of T cells and NK cells to generate IFN-γ, and cooperation with IL-12 to support Th1 differentiation, all of which can contribute to tumor immunity. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:645-652]

The same cytokine can also support cancer progression. According to the review, IL-18 can promote angiogenesis, metastasis, and tumor growth, and it can contribute to immune evasion. When an immunosuppressive microenvironment is established, IL-18 further boosts tumor growth. The source cites evidence that IL-18 markedly enhances monocytic MDSCs through differentiation of `CD11b(-)` bone marrow progenitors, suppressing in vitro T-cell expansion and IFN production. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:652-660]

The review also describes an antitumor mechanism for inflammasome-linked IL-18 in innate immunity. NLRP3-inflammasome-mediated IL-18 increases NK-cell maturation, raises surface expression of the death ligand FasL, and enhances the ability of NK cells to eliminate FasL-sensitive malignancies. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:660-663]

## HMGB1

HMGB1 is generated during pyroptosis and is described as a nuclear protein involved in DNA recombination repair, transcription, and chromatin remodeling. Once extracellular, HMGB1 can activate the [[NLRP3 inflammasome]] after engaging multiple sensors and can initiate NF-κB signaling. HMGB1 signaling through TLR2 and TLR4 also induces cytokines such as IL-6, TNF, and IL-8, which the review states are necessary for CD8+ T-cell activation. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:664-672]

The source explicitly states that HMGB1 has both tumor-promoting and tumor-suppressive properties in malignancies. On the antitumor side, HMGB1 released from dying tumor cells can interact with TLR4 to initiate tumor-antigen processing by mature dendritic cells and promote anticancer immune responses; the review also notes that HMGB1 is associated with increased cancer immunity. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:673-679]

On the tumor-promoting side, HMGB1 is linked to neo-angiogenesis and, within an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment, to improved survival and recruitment of MDSCs, which supports tumor-cell persistence. Elsewhere in the review, HMGB1 released from GSDME-mediated pyroptotic epithelial cells is described as participating in colitis-associated colorectal cancer tumorigenesis by stimulating tumor-cell proliferation and PCNA expression through the ERK1/2 pathway. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:673-682, pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:1220-1224]

## Role in pyroptotic tumor microenvironments

The overall picture in pyroptotic tumor microenvironments is that these mediators do not act in a single direction. The review frames pyroptosis-associated cytokines and danger signals as drivers of either procancer or anticancer immune-cell responses depending on timing, intensity, and microenvironmental context. Chronic inflammatory signaling tends to sustain tumor-favoring conditions, while acute, broad pyroptotic activation can recruit immune cells and reinforce antitumor immunity. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:547-565, pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:612-621, pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:1661-1670]

This context dependence is one reason the review treats pyroptosis, inflammasomes, and their downstream mediators as both promising therapeutic targets and difficult variables in cancer therapy. The source notes that more research is needed to clarify how pyroptosis-associated inflammatory factors can be used therapeutically and how their procancer and anticancer effects might be exploited or controlled. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:619-621, pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:1661-1678]
