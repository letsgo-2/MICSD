# Canonical and noncanonical inflammasome pathways in pyroptosis

[[Pyroptosis]] is a pro-inflammatory form of programmed cell death characterized by plasma membrane pore formation, release of inflammatory contents, cell swelling, and lysis. Its execution depends on inflammasome signaling and the gasdermin family protein [[Gasdermin D|GSDMD]], which forms membrane pores after proteolytic activation. The resulting membrane damage allows mediators such as IL-1b and IL-18 to exit the cell and amplify local and systemic inflammation.^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:43-46, a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:88-104]

## Overview

Pyroptosis can proceed through both canonical and noncanonical inflammasome pathways. In the canonical pathway, inflammasome assembly activates caspase-1. In the noncanonical pathway, inflammatory caspases caspase-4 and caspase-5 in humans, or caspase-11 in mice, drive pyroptosis. Both routes converge on GSDMD cleavage and pore formation, linking inflammasome activation to inflammatory cell death.^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:88-95, a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:646-672, a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:674-692]

## Inflammasomes as signaling platforms

Inflammasomes are polyprotein complexes important in innate immunity. They are composed of pattern recognition receptors (PRRs), the adaptor protein [[ASC]], and an effector pro-caspase. PRRs detect pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs) from microbes or host-derived damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) such as HMGB1, heat shock proteins, S100 proteins, ATP, and uric acid crystals, leading to recruitment and activation of inflammatory caspases.^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:592-603]

Extensively studied pyroptosis-associated inflammasomes include [[NLRP1 inflammasome|NLRP1]], [[NLRP3 inflammasome|NLRP3]], [[NLRC4 inflammasome|NLRC4]], and [[AIM2 inflammasome|AIM2]]. Among them, NLRP3 is described as the most concretely studied and relatively well understood. The source also notes growing attention to how other inflammasomes, including NLRP1 and NLRC4, relate to pyroptosis.^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:603-608, a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:643-645]

## Canonical inflammasome pathway

The canonical pathway is the classic route of pyroptosis and is mediated mainly by caspase-1. In this pathway, inflammasome assembly promotes hydrolysis of pro-caspase-1 into active caspase-1. Caspase-1 then cleaves pro-IL-1b and pro-IL-18 into their mature inflammatory cytokine forms and also directly cleaves GSDMD, triggering pore formation in the plasma membrane and pyroptotic cell death.^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:646-659]

For the [[NLRP3 inflammasome]], the receptor contains leucine-rich repeat (LRR), NACHT, and pyrin (PYD) domains. After sensing stimuli, NLRP3 changes conformation, oligomerizes, and recruits ASC through PYD-PYD interactions. ASC then recruits pro-caspase-1 through CARD-mediated interactions, completing inflammasome assembly. The assembled NLRP3 inflammasome self-activates, generating active caspase-1 and promoting maturation of IL-1b and IL-18.^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:609-628]

Not all canonical inflammasomes require ASC. The source states that NLRP1 and NLRC4 can function without the adaptor because their receptors themselves contain CARD domains that can connect directly to pro-caspase-1. The AIM2 inflammasome, by contrast, consists of AIM2, ASC, and caspase-1; after assembly, it likewise promotes caspase-1 activation and maturation of IL-1b and IL-18.^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:629-637]

## Noncanonical inflammasome pathway

The noncanonical pathway is mediated by human caspase-4 and caspase-5, or mouse caspase-11. It is associated especially with Gram-negative bacterial infection. In this route, cytoplasmic lipopolysaccharide (LPS) acts as the activating PAMP by directly binding and activating caspase-11 independently of TLR4. Human caspase-4 and caspase-5 are described as homologs with the same activation mode and function as caspase-11.^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:648-672]

The source further states that caspase-11 can cleave GSDMD and also trigger activation of the classical pathway, promoting maturation of pro-IL-1b through pyroptosis and leading to death of infected cells. This positions the noncanonical pathway as both a direct route to membrane pore formation and a contributor to downstream inflammatory cytokine activation.^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:663-668, a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:674-692]

## Shared execution through GSDMD

A central unifying step in canonical and noncanonical pyroptosis is cleavage of GSDMD by inflammatory caspases. GSDMD was identified as a common substrate of caspase-1 and caspase-4/5/11 and as a key effector of inflammatory cell death. At rest, GSDMD is autoinhibited in the cytoplasm. Upon cleavage, it yields an N-terminal fragment that binds the plasma membrane lipid bilayer and forms pores approximately 10-15 nm in diameter, disrupting membrane integrity.^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:518-531, a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:674-695]

These GSDMD pores allow active secretion of IL-1b and IL-18, followed by water influx, cell swelling, membrane rupture, and pyroptosis. The C-terminal fragment has an inhibitory role, restraining the cytotoxic activity of the N-terminal fragment and contributing to structural autoinhibition.^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:686-695]

## Functional significance

Pyroptosis contributes to innate immune defense by helping eliminate infected cells and supporting inflammasome-dependent cytokine secretion. At the same time, excessive or inappropriate activation can amplify inflammation and damage tissues, and the source links pyroptosis to infectious, neurological, and cardiovascular diseases among others. Within the research landscape, pathway mechanisms involving inflammasomes, caspases, GSDMD, and especially NLRP3 have been major hotspots.^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:105-133, a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:377-383, a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:584-591, a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:830-846]
