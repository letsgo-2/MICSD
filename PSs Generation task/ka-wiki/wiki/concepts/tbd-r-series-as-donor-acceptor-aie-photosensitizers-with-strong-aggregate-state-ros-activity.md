# TBD-R series as donor-acceptor AIE photosensitizers with strong aggregate-state ROS activity

The **TBD-R series** comprises three membrane-anchoring [[photosensitizers]] named `TBD-1C`, `TBD-2C`, and `TBD-3C`. In the source, they are described as donor-acceptor-type [[aggregation-induced emission|AIE]] photosensitizers built from the same aromatic core and differentiated by the number of attached cationic chains. The series was developed to combine aggregate-state fluorescence with efficient photodynamic ROS generation and membrane-targeting behavior. ^[activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:125-149, activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:155-162]

Structurally, the source classifies TBD-R molecules as `D-A'-p-A` systems. The `TBD` core consists of methoxy-substituted tetraphenylethylene as the donor (`D`), benzothiadiazole as an acceptor (`A'`), phenyl as the linker (`p`), and dicyanovinyl as the terminal acceptor (`A`). This core was further decorated with one, two, or three cationic groups to produce `TBD-1C`, `TBD-2C`, and `TBD-3C`, respectively. ^[activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:156-167]

## Photophysical properties

All three TBD-R molecules showed broad absorption from `300 to 500 nm` and weak red emission centered at `650 nm`. They also displayed typical AIE behavior: weak emission in molecularly dissolved states and strong red emission in aggregate states. The source presents this aggregate-state brightening as a key advantage over conventional photosensitizers affected by aggregation-caused quenching. ^[activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:84-90, activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:168-174]

## ROS generation

The ROS-generating ability of the TBD-R series was evaluated using `ABDA` as an indicator and `Rose Bengal` as a reference. Under white-light irradiation, ABDA absorbance decreased rapidly in the presence of `TBD-1C`, `TBD-2C`, and `TBD-3C`, indicating efficient ROS production. Their reported ABDA decomposition rates were `13.5`, `14.3`, and `15.1 nmol/min`, respectively, all higher than `Rose Bengal` at `10.8 nmol/min` under the same photosensitizer concentration. ^[activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:174-186]

Although the three compounds differ in cationic-chain number, the source states that their ROS generation abilities were similar, with only insignificant differences attributable to charge number. In other words, the strong ROS activity is presented as a shared property of the donor-acceptor TBD-R scaffold rather than a feature unique to one member of the series. ^[activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:180-188]

## Relationship to membrane anchoring

The cationic chains were introduced to improve aqueous solubility and modulate lipid-membrane anchoring capability. As the number of cationic groups increased from one to three, membrane anchoring became stronger. In HeLa cells, the Pearson correlation coefficients between probe signal and membrane tracker were reported as `0.75` for `TBD-1C`, `0.88` for `TBD-2C`, and `0.95` for `TBD-3C`, with `TBD-1C` also showing visible cytoplasmic fluorescence. ^[activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:129-143, activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:189-219]

This distinction is important because the source links photodynamic performance in cells not to major differences in intrinsic ROS generation among the three compounds, but to how effectively each photosensitizer localizes ROS production at the plasma membrane. The stronger membrane anchoring of `TBD-3C` was therefore associated with more direct membrane damage and greater cancer-cell ablation under irradiation. ^[activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:185-188, activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:239-257, activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:455-457]

## Biological significance in the study

Within the reported [[photodynamic therapy]] system, strong in situ ROS production at the cell membrane led to membrane damage, LDH release, and pyroptosis-associated changes. The study reports that pyroptosis became more dominant as membrane anchoring increased across the series, with `TBD-3C` showing the strongest pyroptosis-associated responses under light irradiation. ^[activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:23-29, activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:320-345, activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:380-398, activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:428-440]

The source therefore presents the TBD-R series as a set of donor-acceptor AIE photosensitizers that retain strong ROS activity in the aggregate state while enabling membrane-localized photodamage. In this framework, aggregate-state photosensitization and membrane anchoring work together: the former supports efficient ROS production, and the latter determines where that ROS acts and how strongly pyroptotic cell death is promoted. ^[activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:84-90, activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:125-149, activation-of-pyroptosis-by-membraneanchoring-aie-photosensitizer-design-new-prospect-for-photodynamic-cancer-cell-ablation.md:442-463]
