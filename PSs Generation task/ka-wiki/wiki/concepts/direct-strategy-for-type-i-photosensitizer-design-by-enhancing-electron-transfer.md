# Direct strategy for type-I photosensitizer design by enhancing electron transfer

The direct strategy for designing [[type-I photosensitizer|type-I photosensitizers]] focuses on increasing the efficiency of the [[electron transfer]] (ET) process so that excited photosensitizers generate superoxide radicals rather than relying mainly on [[excitation energy transfer]] to produce [[singlet oxygen]]. In the reviewed framework, this is one of the two major routes to type-I behavior, alongside the indirect strategy of suppressing the type-II pathway by lowering the `T1` energy. ^[the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:20-30, the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:174-181]

## Why the direct strategy matters

Type-I photodynamic processes are presented as attractive because they are less dependent on oxygen availability than type-II processes. In the type-I pathway, the excited triplet state of the photosensitizer undergoes ET to produce `O2·−`, which can then participate in further reactions to form `H2O2` and `OH·`, with oxygen being recycled during the process. The review also notes added benefits of higher cytotoxicity through hydroxyl-radical formation and potential immunological effects through macrophage repolarization. ^[the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:123-141, the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:157-166]

The need for a direct strategy arises because the ET reaction of `3PS*` with nearby substrates is often difficult to make competitive with the more favorable type-II [[excitation energy transfer]] process between `3PS*` and `O2`. The review therefore frames enhancement of ET as a central design problem for obtaining effective type-I photosensitizers. ^[the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:169-181, the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:388-415]

## Core mechanistic idea

The review describes two main ET routes for generating `O2·−`. In one route, the excited triplet photosensitizer first abstracts an electron from the surrounding environment and then transfers that electron to oxygen. In the other route, the excited photosensitizer transfers electrons to an electron-deficient substance, which then serves as an intermediary that relays electrons to oxygen. ^[the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:390-409]

Based on these routes, the review organizes the direct strategy into three construction methods: forming an electron-rich microenvironment, providing an electron-deficient intermediate transmitter, and introducing an enhanced electron-transfer-capacity primitive. Quinone and related structures are highlighted as especially promising examples of the third category. ^[the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:409-423, the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:749-755]

## 1. Forming an electron-rich microenvironment

An electron-rich microenvironment is used to promote ET between the photosensitizer and surrounding molecules, thereby improving type-I ROS generation and photodynamic performance. The review states that such an environment can be created either by supplying an external “electron library” or by embedding electron-rich heteroatoms or heterocyclic structures into the system. ^[the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:424-430]

One approach uses external electron-rich partners. The review cites supramolecular systems built from a conventional type-II BODIPY photosensitizer and electron-rich `BP5A`, where `BP5A` donates electrons to the excited sensitizer and thereby enables `O2·−` production. It also cites a `PS/BSA` system in which bovine serum albumin acts as an “electron reservoir”; electrochemical changes after adding BSA were interpreted as evidence of complex formation supporting this reservoir-like role. ^[the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:431-459]

The review notes practical limitations for these assembled systems: their effectiveness depends on careful matching of energy levels, intermolecular distance, and interaction strength. Because of those constraints, increasing attention has shifted to covalently introducing electron-rich heteroatoms or heterocyclic motifs, whose lone-pair electrons can enhance [[intersystem crossing]], facilitate ET, and increase type-I ROS production. ^[the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:460-479]

Examples in this category include heteroatom-containing and heterocycle-containing photosensitizers. For `COi6-4Cl`, electron-rich `O` and `S` atoms increased donor ability and, together with a strong acceptor segment, produced strong intramolecular charge transfer and a small `ΔES1-T2` that favored ISC. In another series (`TPP`, `TPS`, `TPO`), electron-rich heterocyclic rings increased both total ROS yield and the proportion of `O2·−`, while cyclic voltammetry showed reduction behavior consistent with stronger ET ability. A related `D-π-A` series with thiophene-containing bridges also showed enhanced free-radical production, including more pronounced `OH·` generation for the most electron-transfer-favored system. ^[the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:480-512, the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:513-541]

## 2. Providing an electron-deficient intermediate transmitter

The second direct-design route introduces electron-deficient structures that receive electrons from the excited photosensitizer and then pass them onward to oxygen, increasing formation of `O2·−` and `OH·`. The review divides this route into two sub-strategies: using electron acceptors as intermediate transmitters and using molecular cationization to create electron-deficient character and improve electron separation. ^[the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:542-559]

In the electron-acceptor approach, acceptor molecules are placed close enough to the photosensitizer to shorten donor-acceptor distance and improve ET efficiency. The review describes supramolecular systems in which conventional type-II sensitizers were co-assembled with electron acceptors (`A1`, `A2`, `A3`), producing `O2·−` and showing thermodynamically feasible intermolecular ET by cyclic voltammetry and Rehm-Weller analysis. It also describes a BODIPY/peralkyne diimide assembly in which photoinduced ET generated radical ion pairs and could ultimately support `OH·` production, including under anaerobic conditions. ^[the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:549-555, the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:560-602]

Another example is an acceptor-triggered system based on co-assembly of `PDI` and `4F`. In that case, introducing the electron-deficient `PDI` increased type-I ROS production substantially, with the review reporting `3.5`-fold and `2.5`-fold increases in `OH·` and `O2·−`, respectively. Mechanistically, `PDI` was described as facilitating ET around excited `4F`, while the resulting radical chemistry enabled oxidation of surrounding water and release of `OH·`. ^[the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:615-635]

The review presents molecular cationization as a more internally encoded way to provide electron-deficient gaps and improve electron transport. Cationization can strengthen the electron-accepting capacity of the cationic segment, promote ISC, and increase charge separation and ET. In the examples discussed, cationized derivatives such as `TBZPyI`, `CTBZPyI`, `DTPAPyPF6`, and `DTPANPF6` showed higher overall ROS generation and larger free-radical contributions than their non-cationized analogues. ^[the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:636-651, the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:672-719]

Mechanistically, the review attributes these gains to better donor-acceptor character and improved excited-state properties. For example, cationization sharply reduced `ΔES-T` values in one series and converted the pyridine segment in `DTPAPyPF6` into an electron-accepting cationic site, concentrating the `LUMO` there and producing more distinct `HOMO-LUMO` separation. The review also cites anion-`π+` AIEgens in which iodide and a strongly reducing co-donor created electron-rich aggregate microenvironments that further supported radical ROS generation. ^[the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:647-671, the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:714-748]

## 3. Introducing an enhanced electron-transfer-capacity primitive

The third route adds a structural unit with inherently strong ET ability and suitable redox properties. The review identifies quinone and its derivatives as the most promising examples because of their redox cycling behavior and electron-transfer capacity. ^[the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:749-755]

One example combines classic type-II photosensitizers with carvacrol-derived chemistry. In that system, carvacrol is converted in situ into `TQ`, which then acts as an ET mediator between the photosensitizer and oxygen. The review reports that combining `CA` with `PS1` markedly increased fluorescence responses from ROS probes relative to `PS1` alone, that `TQ` quenched the excited state of `PS1` with a large Stern-Volmer quenching constant, and that the combined `TQ/PS1` system showed stronger photocurrent than either component alone, all consistent with enhanced charge transfer and type-I ROS generation. ^[the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:756-778]

A second example is a series of quinone-fused cyclopenta[2,1-b]indoles developed as pure type-I photosensitizers. The review states that all tested compounds generated substantial ROS, and that representative compound `3af` showed EPR signals assigned to both `O2·−` and `OH·`. Its calculated `ΔES1-T2` supported ISC, while cyclic voltammetry showed quinone-like electrochemical behavior and a ground-state reduction potential well below the `O2/O2·−` couple, which the review interprets as evidence of excellent ET ability. Additional polymeric systems using anthraquinone as the acceptor motif also increased `DHR123` signal and generated `O2·−`. ^[the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:779-830]

The review also describes a combined design that integrates all three direct-strategy elements at once: a cationized quinone derivative self-assembled with `BSA`. In that system, cationization provided the electron-deficient transmitter function, the quinone derivative provided enhanced ET capacity, and `BSA` supplied the electron-rich microenvironment, together yielding a system with a large proportion of type-I ROS and especially strong `OH·` generation. ^[the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:831-841]

## Advantages and limitations

According to the review, the main advantage of the direct strategy is that it offers a clearer mechanistic basis than indirect control of triplet-state energetics. It directly targets the ET step through environmental engineering, intermediate electron relays, or built-in redox-active primitives, and the summary section presents these three categories as the main current framework for constructing type-I photosensitizers. ^[the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:842-845, the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:879-888, the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:909-918]

The review also emphasizes unresolved tradeoffs. Strengthening electron donation to build an electron-rich environment can lower the oxidation potential of the photosensitizer and weaken its photooxidation ability toward biomolecules. Electron-deficient intermediate transmitters may also increase unwanted reactivity with non-target biomolecules, creating side effects or reducing treatment precision. More broadly, type-I and type-II ROS often coexist, pure type-I behavior remains difficult to realize, and electron-transfer-capacity primitives remain scarce, with quinones being the main class explored so far. ^[the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:842-870, the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:909-918]

## Outlook

The review concludes that direct ET-enhancing strategies have clarified important design principles for type-I photosensitizers, but broader molecular platforms are still needed. It specifically calls for development of new intermediates with strong ET ability, better methods for determining `OH·` and `O2·−`, and photosensitizers with longer-wavelength optical properties and compatibility with combination therapies such as chemotherapy or immunotherapy. ^[the-current-advances-in-design-strategy-indirect-strategy-and-direct-strategy-for-typei-photosensitizers.md:919-950]
