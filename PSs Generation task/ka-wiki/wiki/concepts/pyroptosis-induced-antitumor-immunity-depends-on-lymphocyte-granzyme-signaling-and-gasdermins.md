# Pyroptosis-induced antitumor immunity depends on lymphocyte granzyme signaling and gasdermins

Pyroptosis is a regulated form of programmed cell death that depends on the [[gasdermin family]] and is associated with inflammatory responses. In the cancer context, the reviewed source states that pyroptosis in tumor cells can induce antitumor immunity, making it relevant not only as a tumor-cell killing mechanism but also as a way to reshape immune responses against tumors. ^[pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:17-24, pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:602-604, pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:751-754]

## Core idea

The source describes antitumor immunity after tumor-cell pyroptosis as a gasdermin-dependent process that is amplified by cytotoxic lymphocytes. In [[GSDME]]-expressing tumor cells, damage-associated molecular patterns generated during pyroptosis recruit immune cells into the tumor microenvironment, increase the number of [[tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes|TILs]], and enhance macrophage phagocytic capacity. ^[pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:603-609]

A key point in the source is that this immune effect depends on lymphocyte-derived granzymes acting through specific gasdermins. [[Granzyme B]] released by [[natural killer cell|NK cells]] has the same cleavage site as caspase-3 and can cleave GSDME to induce pyroptosis, which in turn promotes antitumor immunity and inhibits tumor growth. ^[pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:610-616]

The review also reports that the tumor-suppressive effect of GSDME disappeared in immunodeficient mice and in mice lacking NK cells and CD8+ killer T cells. This finding indicates that the antitumor effect attributed to GSDME-mediated pyroptosis depends on these cytotoxic immune cells rather than on tumor-cell death alone. ^[pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:613-616]

## Lymphocyte granzyme signaling

The source identifies cytotoxic lymphocytes, including [[cytotoxic T lymphocyte|cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CTLs)]] and NK cells, as major effector cells that kill target cells after recognition by releasing perforin and other mediators. While lymphocyte killing has generally been viewed as apoptosis-inducing, the review highlights evidence that perforin also enables granzymes to enter tumor cells and trigger pyroptosis through gasdermins. ^[pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:617-623]

Specifically, the review states that [[Granzyme A]] can enter tumor cells via [[perforin]] and induce [[GSDMB]]-mediated pyroptosis. It further notes that upregulating GSDMB expression can promote this pyroptotic response. ^[pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:622-630]

The source adds that GSDMB is highly expressed in tumor cells derived from epithelial cells of the digestive system, and that GSDMB-mediated pyroptosis can enhance antitumor immunity. It presents this as part of the explanation for how cytotoxic lymphocytes kill some tumor targets. ^[pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:631-635]

Figure-based summary text in the review states that GzmA and GzmB released by CTLs and NK cells, with the help of perforin, enter cancer cells and induce GSDMB-mediated and GSDME-mediated pyroptosis, respectively. The same figure links these events to suppression of tumor growth and promotion of antitumor immunity. ^[pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:773-776]

## Gasdermin dependence

The broader mechanistic background in the source is that pyroptosis depends on pore formation by gasdermin family proteins. Gasdermins contain inhibitory C-terminal and pore-forming N-terminal regions; once cleavage releases the N-terminal fragment, it oligomerizes in membranes and induces pyroptosis. ^[pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:117-124]

Within this framework, different gasdermins connect distinct lymphocyte proteases to pyroptosis. The review states that GZMB can cleave GSDME, and GzmA can cleave GSDMB; a figure in the paper also summarizes gasdermin cleavage relationships by showing GzmA cleaving GSDMB and caspase-3/GzmB cleaving GSDME. ^[pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:228-230, pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:259-261, pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:390-391]

Because the immune effects described in the source are tied to GSDME- and GSDMB-mediated pyroptosis, gasdermin expression status functions as an important determinant of whether lymphocyte granzyme delivery results in pyroptotic, immunostimulatory tumor-cell death. The review therefore presents gasdermin family proteins as potential biomarkers for tumor immunotherapy and as possible targets for antitumor drug development. ^[pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:641-644]

## Strength of the immune response

The source highlights that even limited pyroptosis within a tumor may be sufficient to alter immunity. It cites a bioorthogonal-system study showing that a small number of tumor cells undergoing pyroptosis was enough to regulate the tumor immune microenvironment effectively and activate a strong T cell-mediated antitumor immune response. ^[pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:636-640]

This observation supports the article’s broader conclusion that pyroptosis is not only a direct cell-death mechanism but also an immune-modulating event with potential relevance for [[tumor immunotherapy]]. The review explicitly states that exploiting tumor pyroptosis to stimulate stronger immune function could become another major advance in tumor treatment. ^[pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:640-644, pyroptosis-a-new-breakthrough-in-cancer-treatment.md:751-758]
