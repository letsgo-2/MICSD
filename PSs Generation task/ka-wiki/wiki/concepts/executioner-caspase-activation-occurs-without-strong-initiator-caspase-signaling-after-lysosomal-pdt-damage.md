# Executioner caspase activation occurs without strong initiator-caspase signaling after lysosomal PDT damage

This phrase describes a photodynamic treatment outcome in HeLa cells where lysosomal damage preceded broader cellular injury, but caspase measurements did not show a strong upstream initiator-caspase surge before executioner-caspase activation. The source reports that after photodynamic treatment with `1Zn-Et` or `2Zn-Et`, initiator caspases `8` and `9` were mostly lower than control, while executioner caspases `3` and/or `7` were comparable to or higher than control and rose significantly about 4 hours after irradiation. ^[jm5014852-114.md:574-609]

The study further links this pattern to lysosomal rupture. The authors note that photodynamic treatment first damaged PS-containing lysosomes, after which the photosensitizer redistributed through the cell and secondary damage followed, and they conclude that the absence of prior initiator-caspase activation suggests another mechanism for executioner-caspase activation, with released lysosomal cathepsins and/or other proteases presented as the most plausible explanation. ^[jm5014852-114.md:462-480, jm5014852-114.md:516-520, jm5014852-114.md:600-609]

This observation sits within a broader cell-death profile that was not purely apoptotic. Flow-cytometry and morphology data showed early necrosis-like damage, with annexin V-FITC/PI-positive populations appearing later and debris increasing, while the authors state that classical apoptosis played a less significant role than necrosis-like cell death after sensitization with these compounds. They also report no significant autophagosome signal 24 hours after irradiation. ^[jm5014852-114.md:536-573, jm5014852-114.md:610-626]
