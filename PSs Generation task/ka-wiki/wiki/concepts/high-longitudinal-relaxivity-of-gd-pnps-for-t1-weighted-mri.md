# High longitudinal relaxivity of Gd-PNPs for T1-weighted MRI

High longitudinal relaxivity of [[Gd-PNPs]] for T1-weighted MRI refers to the strong T1 contrast performance reported for gadolinium-porphyrin-based supramolecular nanoparticles assembled from Gd-TCPP, PEI, and a PGA-g-mPEG outer layer. In the study, Gd-PNPs showed concentration-dependent positive contrast enhancement in T1-weighted MR images and a calculated longitudinal relaxivity (`r1`) of `16.157 mM^-1 s^-1`, supporting their use as a T1 MRI contrast agent within a fluorescence/MR imaging-guided photodynamic therapy platform. ^[d1nr04489c-110.md:19-29, d1nr04489c-110.md:362-370, d1nr04489c-110.md:590-605]

## Basis for MRI contrast

The MRI capability of Gd-PNPs was attributed to the presence of `Gd3+` associated with the nanoparticle surface. XPS analysis showed characteristic gadolinium peaks, and the authors state that the coexistence of Gd 4d and Gd 3d peaks confirmed the presence of `Gd3+` on the surface of Gd-PNPs, thereby verifying their capability as a T1-MR contrast agent. ^[d1nr04489c-110.md:321-327, d1nr04489c-110.md:336-342]

In the MR characterization experiments, gray-scale T1-weighted images of Gd-PNP dispersions showed obvious concentration-dependent positive contrast enhancement. From the linear regression between longitudinal relaxivity and equivalent gadolinium concentration, the `r1` value was calculated as `16.157 mM^-1 s^-1`. The ratio `r2/r1` was `1.16`, which the authors interpret as consistent with classification of Gd-PNPs as T1 contrast agents. ^[d1nr04489c-110.md:362-370]

## Comparison with clinical contrast agents

The reported `r1` of Gd-PNPs was higher than the values listed in the paper for several commercially available T1-weighted MR contrast agents, including Gadobutrol (`2.5 mM^-1 s^-1`), Gadovist (`4.34 mM^-1 s^-1`), Magnevist (`3.2 mM^-1 s^-1`), Omniscan (`3.3 mM^-1 s^-1`), and ProHance (`4.3 mM^-1 s^-1`). The paper states that a higher `r1` value implies that less contrast agent dosage may be needed, with the potential for fewer side effects. ^[d1nr04489c-110.md:368-376]

## In vivo T1-weighted MRI performance

In CT 26 tumor-bearing mice, Gd-PNPs were evaluated for in vivo MR imaging after intravenous injection at `0.05 mmol Gd3+ kg^-1`, with Gadobutrol used as a comparator at the same gadolinium dose. MR images were acquired at `1`, `3`, `6`, `9`, and `12 h` after injection. ^[d1nr04489c-110.md:224-229]

The in vivo MR data showed time-dependent positive contrast enhancement at tumor sites for both Gd-PNP-treated and Gadobutrol-treated mice. For Gd-PNP-treated mice, the strongest MR signal with a sharp border outlining the tumor was observed at `6 h` after intravenous injection, whereas Gadobutrol-treated mice showed their strongest MR signal at `1 h`. The study also reports that Gd-PNPs produced up to `3.5-fold` MR signal enhancement at the tumor site, compared with a maximum of `2.5-fold` for Gadobutrol. ^[d1nr04489c-110.md:467-475, d1nr04489c-110.md:488-505]

The authors interpret these imaging results as evidence that Gd-PNPs had more efficient tumor accumulation and longer retention time at tumor sites than free [[Gd-TCPP]] or Gadobutrol, which they ascribe to the [[enhanced permeability and retention effect|EPR effect]]. In the context of the study, the high `r1` value and tumor-localized MR enhancement supported the use of Gd-PNPs as part of an [[FL/MR dual-modal imaging]] system for imaging-guided therapy. ^[d1nr04489c-110.md:464-510, d1nr04489c-110.md:602-605]

## Relationship to the broader nanotheranostic design

The high longitudinal relaxivity of Gd-PNPs was one component of a broader multifunctional design. The same nanoplatform was reported to provide fluorescence imaging capability, singlet oxygen generation for [[photodynamic therapy]], good colloidal stability, and biocompatibility, allowing a single Gd-TCPP-based formulation to function as an FL probe, MR contrast agent, and photosensitizer. ^[d1nr04489c-110.md:23-29, d1nr04489c-110.md:344-361, d1nr04489c-110.md:377-425, d1nr04489c-110.md:590-607]
