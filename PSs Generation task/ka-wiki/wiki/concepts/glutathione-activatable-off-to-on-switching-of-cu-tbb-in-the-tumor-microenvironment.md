# Glutathione-activatable off-to-on switching of Cu-TBB in the tumor microenvironment

Glutathione-activatable off-to-on switching of [[Cu-TBB]] refers to the tumor-selective activation mechanism of a copper-bacteriochlorin nanosheet composed of `Cu2+` and `5,10,15,20-tetra(p-benzoato) bacteriochlorin` (TBB). In the source, Cu-TBB is described as remaining in an inactivated "off" state under normal conditions, but switching to an activated "on" state in the [[tumor microenvironment]] when exposed to elevated [[glutathione|GSH]], leading to release of `Cu+` and TBB. ^[copperbacteriochlorin-nanosheet-as-a-specific-pyroptosis-inducer-for-robust-tumor-immunotherapy.md:140-152]

## Mechanistic basis

The proposed basis for this switching behavior is coordination between `Cu2+` and the photosensitizer TBB within a stable nanoassembly. In this assembled state, Forster resonance energy transfer between `Cu2+` and the photosensitizer quenches TBB fluorescence and suppresses light-triggered ROS production, leaving Cu-TBB functionally "off." ^[copperbacteriochlorin-nanosheet-as-a-specific-pyroptosis-inducer-for-robust-tumor-immunotherapy.md:125-130, copperbacteriochlorin-nanosheet-as-a-specific-pyroptosis-inducer-for-robust-tumor-immunotherapy.md:216-220]

The source proposes that this inactive assembly becomes selectively destabilized in tumors because the tumor microenvironment contains overexpressed GSH at `1-10 mm`. Reaction of GSH with Cu-TBB causes disassembly of the nanosheet and formation of `Cu+`, TBB, and `GSSG`, thereby converting the system from the quenched off state to an active on state inside tumors rather than in normal tissues. ^[copperbacteriochlorin-nanosheet-as-a-specific-pyroptosis-inducer-for-robust-tumor-immunotherapy.md:131-139, copperbacteriochlorin-nanosheet-as-a-specific-pyroptosis-inducer-for-robust-tumor-immunotherapy.md:228-249]

## Evidence for switching

Several measurements in solution supported GSH-responsive activation. After exposure to GSH, Cu-TBB showed a redshift in UV-vis absorption attributed to `S-Cu2+` charge transfer, appearance of a characteristic `GSSG` absorption signal, and ESR evidence of `O2−•` generation after incubation with GSH. Together, these findings were interpreted as evidence that GSH reacts with Cu-TBB to produce `Cu+`, which then reacts with oxygen to generate superoxide. ^[copperbacteriochlorin-nanosheet-as-a-specific-pyroptosis-inducer-for-robust-tumor-immunotherapy.md:228-249]

GSH treatment also restored the fluorescence of previously quenched TBB, with signal intensity increasing as GSH concentration rose from `0` to `10 mm`. Under `750 nm` laser irradiation, ROS generation likewise increased after GSH treatment, and TEM analysis showed gradual structural destruction and disassembly of Cu-TBB in response to GSH. The paper therefore frames the off-to-on switch as a coupled process of fluorescence recovery, nanosheet disassembly, and restored ROS-generating activity. ^[copperbacteriochlorin-nanosheet-as-a-specific-pyroptosis-inducer-for-robust-tumor-immunotherapy.md:252-282]

## Functional consequences of the on state

Once activated, released `Cu+` was reported to catalyze conversion of endogenous oxygen to `O2−•`. The study further describes downstream `SOD` disproportionation, [[Haber-Weiss reaction]], and Fenton-like chemistry that generate additional oxygen and highly toxic `·OH`, establishing a Cu-driven intracellular ROS cascade. ^[copperbacteriochlorin-nanosheet-as-a-specific-pyroptosis-inducer-for-robust-tumor-immunotherapy.md:147-154, copperbacteriochlorin-nanosheet-as-a-specific-pyroptosis-inducer-for-robust-tumor-immunotherapy.md:330-370]

The released TBB also regains photodynamic function after switching on. Upon `750 nm` irradiation, GSH-activated Cu-TBB generated both `O2−•` and `1O2`, so the active state combined copper-driven ROS chemistry with [[photodynamic therapy]] activity. The paper presents these two ROS-producing routes as acting together to amplify oxidative stress in cancer cells. ^[copperbacteriochlorin-nanosheet-as-a-specific-pyroptosis-inducer-for-robust-tumor-immunotherapy.md:151-154, copperbacteriochlorin-nanosheet-as-a-specific-pyroptosis-inducer-for-robust-tumor-immunotherapy.md:372-387]

## Tumor selectivity in vivo

In tumor-bearing mice, fluorescence imaging after intravenous injection showed detectable and increasing tumor fluorescence over time, with a peak at `48 h` post-injection. The source attributes the weak blood fluorescence to the much lower GSH concentration in blood and reports negligible fluorescence in several normal organs, while tumor organ-to-blood ratios increased over time because of elevated tumor GSH and the enhanced permeability and retention effect. ^[copperbacteriochlorin-nanosheet-as-a-specific-pyroptosis-inducer-for-robust-tumor-immunotherapy.md:489-525]

Based on these observations, the study concludes that TBB fluorescence can be switched from off to on in response to overexpressed GSH within the tumor microenvironment, enabling selective activation of Cu-TBB in tumors while maintaining low toxicity in normal tissues and organs. ^[copperbacteriochlorin-nanosheet-as-a-specific-pyroptosis-inducer-for-robust-tumor-immunotherapy.md:521-525, copperbacteriochlorin-nanosheet-as-a-specific-pyroptosis-inducer-for-robust-tumor-immunotherapy.md:666-672]

## Biological significance

The paper presents this GSH-triggered switching as the upstream event that enables Cu-TBB to induce [[GSDMD-mediated pyroptosis]], increase release of `IL-1beta` and `IL-18`, enhance [[dendritic cell maturation]], and promote [[T cell priming]] and infiltration. In the study's in vivo tumor models, these effects were associated with elimination of primary tumors and inhibition of distant tumor growth and metastasis. ^[copperbacteriochlorin-nanosheet-as-a-specific-pyroptosis-inducer-for-robust-tumor-immunotherapy.md:153-179, copperbacteriochlorin-nanosheet-as-a-specific-pyroptosis-inducer-for-robust-tumor-immunotherapy.md:604-665]
