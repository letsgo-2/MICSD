# Bioorthogonal nanoparticle release of GSDMA3 links pyroptosis to antitumor immunity and checkpoint blockade

This study described a nanobioorthogonal chemical system, `NP-GSDMA3`, built to examine how [[pyroptosis]] relates to antitumor immunity. In this system, [[Gasdermin A3|GSDMA3]] was linked to nanoparticles through a triethylsilyl (TES) ether linker so that GSDMA3 could be released in a controlled and efficient manner in tumors. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:709-720]

The review states that the relationship between the proinflammatory cell death program of pyroptosis and antitumor immunity had been unknown, motivating the use of a bioorthogonal system to probe that question in living systems. Because `NP-GSDMA3` was nanoscale, it showed high tumor accumulation through the enhanced permeability and retention effect. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:709-723]

## Mechanism

A tumor-imaging probe, phenylalanine trifluoroborate (`Phe-BF3`), selectively entered cells and cleaved the silyl ether bond in `NP-GSDMA3`, releasing GSDMA3 protein. The released GSDMA3 then formed membrane pores, followed by pyroptosis. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:723-727]

The source further states that pyroptosis caused release of inflammatory cytokines, which activated antitumor immune responses in vivo. In the review’s summary table for this platform, the reported immune-associated readouts included `CD3+ T`, `CD4+ T`, `CD8+ T`, `CD4+FOXP3+ Treg`, `NK` cells, `M1` and `M2` macrophages, as well as `IL-1b`, `IL-18`, and `HMGB1`, in a [[4T1]] model. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:410-412, cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:727-730]

## Cellular evidence

When HeLa, EMT6, [[4T1]], and `BMDM` cells were treated with `NP-GSDMA3` plus `Phe-BF3`, the N domains of gasdermin were observed to enrich at membranes, and the cells showed pyroptotic morphology. These findings were presented as evidence that the desilylation-triggered release system could activate gasdermin pore formation and pyroptosis across multiple cell types. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:731-734]

## In vivo antitumor effects

In the 4T1 tumor model, intratumoral and intravenous administration produced similar tumor regression, which the review interprets as evidence that tumor inhibition resulted from activation of GSDMA3 at the tumor site. Notably, `NP-GSDMA3` plus `Phe-BF3` generated only about `20%` propidium iodide-positive pyroptotic cells in the tumor, yet the whole tumor tissue could be eradicated, implying that the immune system contributed substantially to the antitumor effect. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:735-741]

Immune profiling in treated tumors showed increased populations of `CD4+ T` cells, `CD8+ T` cells, and natural killer cells, while neutrophils, monocytes, and myeloid-derived suppressor cells did not increase. The review describes this pattern as confirming a potent tumor immunosuppressive effect of the `NP-GSDMA3` and `Phe-BF3` treatment. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:741-758]

## Cooperation with checkpoint blockade

On its own, `NP-GSDMA3` plus `Phe-BF3` did not inhibit 4T1 tumor growth in the reported experiment. However, when that treatment was followed by [[PD-1 blockade|anti-PD1 therapy]], tumor growth was clearly inhibited. The review concludes from this result that pyroptosis-driven inflammation within the tumor microenvironment can cooperate with [[immune checkpoint blockade]] to enhance antitumor immunotherapy. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:759-765]

The authors summarize the `Phe-BF3` desilylation platform as a smart in vivo tool and suggest that gasdermin activation can reveal an anticancer immune function of pyroptosis. They further propose that gasdermin agonists may help improve the efficacy of cancer immunotherapies. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:766-771]
