# Extracellular bactericidal activity of GSDMD-N against *Staphylococcus aureus*

Extracellular bactericidal activity of [[Gasdermin D|GSDMD-N]] against [[Staphylococcus aureus]] refers to the reported ability of the cleaved N-terminal fragment of GSDMD to kill *S. aureus* from outside the host cell during infection. In the reviewed mechanism, inflammatory caspases activated at the onset of *S. aureus* infection cleave GSDMD, releasing GSDMD-N from its autoinhibitory C-terminal domain, and the activated GSDMD-N then kills the pathogen without harming bystander host cells. ^[gasdermin-d-kills-bacteria.md:366-374]

## Context

The source describes *S. aureus* as a major healthcare-associated pathogen with substantial antibiotic resistance and disease severity ranging from mild infection to severe sepsis. It can persist as a commensal organism in humans but also become pathogenic, and its ability to survive within macrophages contributes to a complex host-pathogen relationship and resistance to clearance. ^[gasdermin-d-kills-bacteria.md:330-365]

Because *S. aureus* can evade complete clearance and use host macrophages as a shield, host innate immune mechanisms are important in limiting infection. Within this review, GSDMD is presented as one component of that defense in staphylococcal infection. ^[gasdermin-d-kills-bacteria.md:357-365, gasdermin-d-kills-bacteria.md:416-440]

## Mechanism

At the onset of *S. aureus* infection, inflammatory caspases 1, 4, 5, and 11 are activated in the host. These caspases cleave human GSDMD after Asp276 and mouse GSDMD after Asp275, releasing GSDMD-N from GSDMD-C, which normally exerts an autoinhibitory effect. ^[gasdermin-d-kills-bacteria.md:366-371]

The source states that activated GSDMD-N kills *S. aureus* extracellularly, meaning the bactericidal step occurs from outside the cell rather than by direct intracellular attack. It also states that this killing does not harm bystander host cells. ^[gasdermin-d-kills-bacteria.md:372-374]

An in vitro study summarized in the review found that GSDMD-N first oligomerizes in the plasma membrane before exerting bactericidal activity against *S. aureus*. Intermolecular and intramolecular disulfide bonds were reported to contribute to this oligomerization, after which GSDMD-N binds membrane lipids. ^[gasdermin-d-kills-bacteria.md:375-380]

The review further summarizes membrane lipid specificity studies showing that GSDMD-N binds multiple lipids, with strongest binding to cardiolipin and phosphatidylinositol phosphates, especially `PtdIns(4)P` and `PtdIns(4,5)P2`. It binds weakly to phosphatidic acid and phosphatidylserine, and shows no appreciable binding to phosphatidylcholine or phosphatidylethanolamine. The source adds that because GSDMD-N binds inner-leaflet phospholipids, the outside of the plasma membrane is unaffected. ^[gasdermin-d-kills-bacteria.md:381-400]

Upon binding membrane lipids, GSDMD-N forms pores, and the resulting pyroptotic process releases factors into the phagosome and extracellular space. According to the source, release of GSDMD-N and other cytosolic contents into the extracellular space serves to limit bacterial spread and inhibit release of active bacteria from pyroptotic cells. ^[gasdermin-d-kills-bacteria.md:401-409]

## Infection-related consequences

The review cites a mouse model of *S. aureus* skin infection in which GSDMD deficiency led to severe abscess formation, extensive skin damage, bacterial spread, and cellular inflammation. These effects were linked to overproduction of the [[CXCL1-CXCR2 axis|Cxcl1-Cxcr2]] pathway rather than simply to reduced [[IL-1beta|IL-1β]]. ^[gasdermin-d-kills-bacteria.md:410-425]

Although IL-1β was reduced in GSDMD-deficient conditions, the source states that this cytokine was not responsible for disease severity in that setting. Instead, increased Cxcl1 associated with GSDMD deficiency exacerbated *S. aureus* infection, and inhibition of both Cxcl1 and Cxcr2 reduced bacterial burden, supporting a protective role for GSDMD in host defense against staphylococcal infection. ^[gasdermin-d-kills-bacteria.md:426-440]

## Interpretation in the source

Within this review, extracellular killing of *S. aureus* by GSDMD-N is presented as a bactericidal extension of [[pyroptosis]]-linked innate immunity. The core claim is that caspase cleavage activates GSDMD-N, which then oligomerizes, binds selected membrane lipids, and contributes to extracellular control of *S. aureus* while helping limit bacterial dissemination during infection. ^[gasdermin-d-kills-bacteria.md:366-409]
