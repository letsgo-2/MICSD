# Chloride sensing by WNK1 restrains NLRP3 through cation-chloride cotransporters

[[WNK1]] is identified in this study as a negative regulator of the [[NLRP3 inflammasome]] in macrophages. The authors report that loss of WNK1 protein or kinase activity increases [[caspase-1]] activation, [[IL-1beta]] production, ASC oligomerization, and [[pyroptosis]], while myeloid WNK1 knockout in mice increases IL-1beta production in response to NLRP3 stimulation in vivo. ^[chloride-sensing-by-wnk1-regulates-nlrp3-inflammasome-activation-and-pyroptosis.md:17-25, chloride-sensing-by-wnk1-regulates-nlrp3-inflammasome-activation-and-pyroptosis.md:572-595]

## Core mechanism

The paper proposes that WNK1 restrains NLRP3 by sensing low intracellular chloride and then activating the canonical [[OXSR1]]/[[STK39]] signaling pathway and downstream [[cation-chloride cotransporters]]. These cotransporters promote recovery of intracellular ion balance, particularly Cl− and K+, during NLRP3 stimulation, which dampens further inflammasome activation and pyroptosis. ^[chloride-sensing-by-wnk1-regulates-nlrp3-inflammasome-activation-and-pyroptosis.md:392-403, chloride-sensing-by-wnk1-regulates-nlrp3-inflammasome-activation-and-pyroptosis.md:449-451, chloride-sensing-by-wnk1-regulates-nlrp3-inflammasome-activation-and-pyroptosis.md:815-819]

More specifically, WNK1 is described as a chloride-sensitive kinase that phosphorylates OXSR1 and STK39, which then activate SLC12-family Na+/K+/Cl− cotransport processes to maintain intracellular Na+, K+, and Cl− concentrations. In the context of NLRP3 activation, this response offsets stimulus-induced Cl− and K+ loss from macrophages. ^[chloride-sensing-by-wnk1-regulates-nlrp3-inflammasome-activation-and-pyroptosis.md:96-105, chloride-sensing-by-wnk1-regulates-nlrp3-inflammasome-activation-and-pyroptosis.md:392-403, chloride-sensing-by-wnk1-regulates-nlrp3-inflammasome-activation-and-pyroptosis.md:420-450]

## Evidence in macrophages

Pharmacologic inhibition of WNK kinases with WNK463 increased NLRP3 activation in LPS-primed bone marrow-derived macrophages stimulated with ATP, nigericin, or monosodium urate. The reported readouts included increased caspase-1 activation, IL-1beta release, ASC oligomerization, propidium iodide uptake, and LDH release, indicating enhanced inflammasome activation and pyroptotic death. A selective WNK1 inhibitor, WNK-IN-11, produced similar effects and also increased NLRP3 speck formation. ^[chloride-sensing-by-wnk1-regulates-nlrp3-inflammasome-activation-and-pyroptosis.md:135-160, chloride-sensing-by-wnk1-regulates-nlrp3-inflammasome-activation-and-pyroptosis.md:170-208]

Genetic evidence was consistent with the inhibitor studies. Conditional deletion of WNK1 in immortalized and primary macrophages increased NLRP3 activation after ATP, nigericin, or MSU stimulation and increased LDH release and propidium iodide uptake, indicating more pyroptosis. The study further states that WNK1 deletion augmented the activation step rather than the priming step, because enhanced responses were observed regardless of whether priming was induced by Pam3CSK4, poly(I:C), or LPS. ^[chloride-sensing-by-wnk1-regulates-nlrp3-inflammasome-activation-and-pyroptosis.md:230-260, chloride-sensing-by-wnk1-regulates-nlrp3-inflammasome-activation-and-pyroptosis.md:262-283]

The effect was largely selective for NLRP3 among the inflammasomes tested. According to the source, WNK1 inhibition or deletion did not alter NLRC4, NLRP1, or non-canonical caspase-11 inflammasome activity, although it did increase AIM2 activity by a mechanism that the authors state was not regulated by Cl− or K+. ^[chloride-sensing-by-wnk1-regulates-nlrp3-inflammasome-activation-and-pyroptosis.md:291-304]

## Role of OXSR1/STK39 and cotransporters

The study tested whether canonical downstream WNK1 signaling mediates this restraint. Inhibiting OXSR1 and STK39 with closantel or rafoxanide increased propidium iodide uptake, IL-1beta release, LDH release, and caspase-1 activation in response to ATP or imiquimod, supporting a requirement for the WNK1/OXSR1/STK39 pathway in controlling NLRP3 activity. ^[chloride-sensing-by-wnk1-regulates-nlrp3-inflammasome-activation-and-pyroptosis.md:305-338, chloride-sensing-by-wnk1-regulates-nlrp3-inflammasome-activation-and-pyroptosis.md:404-414]

The authors connect this pathway to [[SLC12 family|SLC12-family]] cotransporters, whose activation promotes cation and chloride uptake. They also report that hydrochlorothiazide, a thiazide diuretic that inhibits the SLC12A3 cotransporter, enhanced ATP- and MSU-induced NLRP3 activation and pyroptosis in macrophages, consistent with the idea that blocking downstream cation-chloride cotransport can remove a brake on inflammasome signaling. ^[chloride-sensing-by-wnk1-regulates-nlrp3-inflammasome-activation-and-pyroptosis.md:339-345, chloride-sensing-by-wnk1-regulates-nlrp3-inflammasome-activation-and-pyroptosis.md:380-390]

## Ion flux findings

A central result is that WNK1 limits the loss of intracellular chloride during NLRP3 stimulation. Using MQAE-based measurements, the authors found that wild-type macrophages lost less intracellular Cl− than WNK1-deficient macrophages after nigericin, ATP, or imiquimod treatment, whereas resting intracellular Cl− levels were similar between genotypes. Wild-type cells lost about 25 to 30% of intracellular Cl− over the measured intervals, while WNK1-deficient cells or WNK463-treated wild-type cells lost about 50%. ^[chloride-sensing-by-wnk1-regulates-nlrp3-inflammasome-activation-and-pyroptosis.md:452-467]

Because WNK1-regulated cotransporter flux couples K+ movement to Cl− movement, WNK1 deficiency also led to greater intracellular K+ loss during NLRP3 stimulation. The source interprets this as the reason WNK1-deficient macrophages cannot offset ion loss and therefore undergo stronger NLRP3 activation and pyroptosis. ^[chloride-sensing-by-wnk1-regulates-nlrp3-inflammasome-activation-and-pyroptosis.md:484-493]

Chloride-replacement experiments supported the cotransporter model. In wild-type macrophages, stimulation in chloride-free medium increased IL-1beta release, LDH release, and caspase-1 activation compared with normal medium. In WNK1-deficient macrophages, chloride-free medium did not further increase these responses above the already elevated level seen in normal medium, and intracellular K+ in wild-type cells was lower in chloride-free medium, whereas WNK1-deficient cells showed similar K+ levels in normal and chloride-free conditions. The authors conclude that inhibiting cotransporter activity by removing extracellular chloride phenocopies WNK1 loss. ^[chloride-sensing-by-wnk1-regulates-nlrp3-inflammasome-activation-and-pyroptosis.md:420-450]

The paper further argues that combined loss of intracellular Cl− and K+ is what drives robust NLRP3 activation. In chloride-free medium, wild-type cells showed a drop in Cl− without a comparable early drop in K+ and did not activate NLRP3, whereas WNK1-deficient cells eventually lost both ions and activated NLRP3. In potassium-free medium, both wild-type and WNK1-deficient cells lost Cl− and K+ and activated NLRP3, with a stronger response in WNK1-deficient cells. Excess extracellular Cl− or K+, but not sodium, blocked the drop in intracellular K+ and inhibited NLRP3 activation in response to ATP or imiquimod. ^[chloride-sensing-by-wnk1-regulates-nlrp3-inflammasome-activation-and-pyroptosis.md:494-544]

## In vivo relevance

To test physiological relevance, the study used an MSU-induced peritonitis model. Myeloid WNK1 conditional knockout mice showed higher IL-1beta levels in peritoneal exudates than control littermates after both 6-hour and 16-hour MSU challenge, along with increased neutrophil infiltration and a higher neutrophil proportion in the exudate. TNFalpha did not differ significantly. ^[chloride-sensing-by-wnk1-regulates-nlrp3-inflammasome-activation-and-pyroptosis.md:572-595, chloride-sensing-by-wnk1-regulates-nlrp3-inflammasome-activation-and-pyroptosis.md:699-705]

## Specificity within the WNK family

Although both WNK1 and [[WNK4]] are described as chloride-sensitive kinases, the study found that WNK4 knockout did not increase NLRP3 activation. A WNK1/WNK4 double knockout also did not show greater in vitro or in vivo effects than WNK1 loss alone, indicating that WNK1, but not WNK4, is the critical family member for this regulatory function in the tested macrophage settings. ^[chloride-sensing-by-wnk1-regulates-nlrp3-inflammasome-activation-and-pyroptosis.md:596-618]

## Clinical implications

The authors suggest that inhibiting the WNK1 pathway may have inflammatory consequences. Because WNK inhibitors, OXSR1/STK39 inhibitors, and thiazide-mediated cotransporter inhibition all increased NLRP3 activation in their experiments, they propose a link between antihypertensive strategies targeting this pathway and increased risk or severity of NLRP3-mediated inflammatory conditions such as [[gout]]. They specifically discuss thiazide and loop diuretics as interventions that may potentiate urate crystal-driven inflammasome activation, in part through cotransporter inhibition and associated hypokalemia. ^[chloride-sensing-by-wnk1-regulates-nlrp3-inflammasome-activation-and-pyroptosis.md:380-390, chloride-sensing-by-wnk1-regulates-nlrp3-inflammasome-activation-and-pyroptosis.md:657-693, chloride-sensing-by-wnk1-regulates-nlrp3-inflammasome-activation-and-pyroptosis.md:738-749]
