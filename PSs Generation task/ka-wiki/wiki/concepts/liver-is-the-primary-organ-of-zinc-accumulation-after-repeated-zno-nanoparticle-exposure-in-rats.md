# Liver is the primary organ of zinc accumulation after repeated ZnO nanoparticle exposure in rats

Repeated exposure to [[zinc oxide nanoparticles|ZnO nanoparticles]] in rats led to zinc accumulation across multiple organs, with the [[liver]] showing the highest zinc burden among the tissues examined. In the study, Sprague-Dawley rats received intraperitoneal ZnO nanoparticle suspensions at `12.5`, `25`, or `50 mg/kg` body weight for `7` consecutive days, and tissue zinc content was then measured. ^[oxidative-stress-related-canonical-pyroptosis-pathway-as-a-target-of-liver-toxicity-triggered-by-zinc-oxide-nanoparticles.md:251-271, oxidative-stress-related-canonical-pyroptosis-pathway-as-a-target-of-liver-toxicity-triggered-by-zinc-oxide-nanoparticles.md:495-503]

## Key finding

After `7` days of administration, zinc concentrations in the high-dose group were elevated relative to control across the heart, liver, spleen, kidney, lung, and brain, indicating disrupted zinc homeostasis in rat tissues. The liver had the greatest zinc distribution of all measured organs, which the authors describe as evidence that the liver was the primary target organ of zinc penetration induced by ZnO nanoparticles. ^[oxidative-stress-related-canonical-pyroptosis-pathway-as-a-target-of-liver-toxicity-triggered-by-zinc-oxide-nanoparticles.md:495-503]

The reported order of tissue zinc concentration in the high-dose group was `liver > spleen > kidney > lung > heart > brain`, with corresponding values of `104.96`, `67.21`, `60.14`, `45.73`, `32.37`, and `18.52`. These measurements support the specific claim that the liver was the dominant site of zinc accumulation after repeated exposure in this rat model. ^[oxidative-stress-related-canonical-pyroptosis-pathway-as-a-target-of-liver-toxicity-triggered-by-zinc-oxide-nanoparticles.md:502-509]

## Experimental context

The tissue-distribution result was obtained using flame atomic absorption spectroscopy after repeated intraperitoneal administration. The study specifically states that accumulated zinc element content was measured in cells and in different rat tissues using this assay, and that organ collection after the exposure period included the heart, liver, spleen, kidneys, lung, and brain. ^[oxidative-stress-related-canonical-pyroptosis-pathway-as-a-target-of-liver-toxicity-triggered-by-zinc-oxide-nanoparticles.md:242-250, oxidative-stress-related-canonical-pyroptosis-pathway-as-a-target-of-liver-toxicity-triggered-by-zinc-oxide-nanoparticles.md:251-271]

In the discussion, the authors place this finding in the context of earlier distribution studies and state that their results were consistent with prior reports identifying the liver as a major accumulation site for ZnO nanoparticles. They further note that continuous abdominal exposure in their experiment caused zinc accumulation in all examined organs, with the maximum in the liver and the minimum in the brain. ^[oxidative-stress-related-canonical-pyroptosis-pathway-as-a-target-of-liver-toxicity-triggered-by-zinc-oxide-nanoparticles.md:919-925]

## Relevance to hepatotoxicity

The study frames hepatic accumulation as relevant to [[hepatotoxicity]] from ZnO nanoparticles. Alongside the high liver zinc burden, the authors report inflammatory liver injury, elevated serum liver enzymes, oxidative stress, and activation of [[pyroptosis]]-related signaling in rat liver, reinforcing the liver's role as a major toxic target in this exposure model. ^[oxidative-stress-related-canonical-pyroptosis-pathway-as-a-target-of-liver-toxicity-triggered-by-zinc-oxide-nanoparticles.md:510-538, oxidative-stress-related-canonical-pyroptosis-pathway-as-a-target-of-liver-toxicity-triggered-by-zinc-oxide-nanoparticles.md:539-557]
