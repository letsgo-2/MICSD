# Biomimetic PLGA/cell-membrane nanoparticles combining decitabine and indocyanine green for photoactivated pyroptotic immunotherapy

Biomimetic PLGA/cell-membrane nanoparticles are a nanoformulation designed to combine [[photodynamic therapy]], chemotherapy, and [[pyroptosis]]-linked [[cancer immunotherapy]]. In the reviewed study, the platform was described as a biomimetic nanoparticle (BNP) built to regulate tumor-cell pyroptosis and thereby strengthen anti-tumor immune responses. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:367-370]

## Composition and design

The BNP consisted of a [[PLGA]] polymeric core with a cell-membrane shell. [[Decitabine]] (DAC) and [[indocyanine green]] (ICG) were co-loaded into the hydrophobic core. The cell-membrane shell was reported to provide low immunogenicity and tumor-targeting capability, while ICG enabled conversion of low-dose photon input into local hyperthermia at the tumor site. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:371-377]

The study presented this design as a way to address limits of conventional chemotherapeutic induction of pyroptosis, including innate drug resistance and severe toxicity, while leveraging pyroptosis as a proinflammatory form of cell death that can support tumor immunotherapy. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:355-367]

## Proposed mechanism

According to the review, BNP accumulated effectively in tumors because of the tumor-targeting, low-immunogenic cell-membrane shell. Under low-dose near-infrared irradiation, ICG within the particles was reported to pierce the tumor-cell membrane and induce a sudden rise in cytoplasmic Ca2+ concentration. This promoted cytochrome c release and activation of [[caspase-3]]. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:378-383]

In parallel, DAC was described as increasing [[GSDME]] expression through its demethylating activity. The elevated GSDME then enhanced caspase-3-catalyzed GSDME cleavage, which ultimately caused pyroptosis after photoactivation. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:383-387]

The review further notes that after incubation with BNP, GSDME expression was greatly increased, whereas after photoactivation total GSDME expression decreased while GSDME N-terminal fragment levels and caspase-3 cleavage increased markedly. These changes were interpreted as evidence that the pyroptosis pathway had been activated by the photothermal treatment. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:386-388, cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:422-425]

## Immunostimulatory effects

Photoactivated pyroptosis induced by BNP was associated with activation of immune cells. The review reports increased secretion of [[IL-6]] and [[TNF-alpha]] by bone-marrow-derived dendritic cells and increased surface expression of CD86 and CD11c on those cells, which was presented as evidence that photoactivated pyroptosis could activate the immune system for cancer immunotherapy. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:426-430]

In the summary table, the platform is listed as producing immunostimulatory effects that included IL-6, TNF-alpha, IFN-gamma, and increases in CD11c+, CD4+, and CD8+ cell populations in a [[4T1]] model. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:393-397]

## In vivo findings

In vivo, both primary and distant tumor growth were reported to be efficiently suppressed after BNP plus photoactivation. The review also states that no obvious tissue injury or weight loss was observed, supporting the conclusion that the formulation was highly biocompatible. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:431-434]

The same treatment was associated with high percentages of CD4+ and CD8+ T cells in spleens and distant tumors. In addition, isolated splenocytes caused significant cell death and secreted high levels of [[IFN-gamma]], suggesting activation of systemic T-cell responses against solid tumors. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:434-440]

## Significance in the review

The review presents this BNP system as a pyroptosis-associated strategy with anti-tumor and anti-metastatic potential, systemic T-cell activation, good biocompatibility, and broad possible clinical applicability. It is discussed as an example of how nanomedicine can be used to harness pyroptosis for immunotherapy by combining a demethylating drug with photoactivation. ^[cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:440-444, cell-death-mediated-by-the-pyroptosis-pathway-with-the-aid-of-nanotechnology-prospects-for-cancer-therapy.md:1094-1110]
