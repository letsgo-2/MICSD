# Visible-light antimicrobial photodynamic therapy for rapidly growing mycobacterial infections

[[Antimicrobial photodynamic therapy]] (aPDT) is presented in the source as a non-invasive, selective approach in which a [[photosensitizer]] is activated by light in the presence of molecular oxygen to generate reactive oxygen species (ROS) that kill microorganisms. The paper frames this approach as a potentially useful adjunct for localized infections caused by rapidly growing mycobacteria (RGM), especially because these infections can be difficult to diagnose early, may require prolonged treatment, and can involve organisms with natural resistance to standard antitubercular drugs. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:25-27, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:40-57, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:98-120]

In this study, visible-light aPDT was tested against four standard RGM strains: [[Mycobacteroides abscessus subsp. abscessus]], [[Mycobacteroides abscessus subsp. massiliense]], [[Mycolicibacterium fortuitum]], and [[Mycolicibacterium smegmatis]]. The investigators evaluated two tetra-cationic palladium(II) porphyrins, `4-PdTPyP` and `3-PdTPyP`, under white LED irradiation spanning `400-800 nm` at `50 mW/cm^2` with a total dose of `270 J/cm^2` over `90 min`. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:27-38, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:123-141, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:143-148]

## Rationale

The source identifies RGM as opportunistic pathogens that can cause skin, soft-tissue, respiratory, endocardial, meningeal, osseous, and disseminated infections. It also notes that cutaneous mycobacteriosis may be diagnosed late because clinical presentations are broad and suspicion is often low, while treatment can be ineffective because of natural drug resistance and the need for long treatment courses. These features are presented as reasons to investigate alternative therapies with strong antibacterial activity and different mechanisms of action. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:98-120]

The paper also places this work in the broader porphyrin aPDT literature. It states that porphyrins have favorable photophysical properties and low cytotoxic effects in human cells, and that related porphyrin derivatives have shown antimicrobial activity against Gram-positive bacteria, Gram-negative bacteria, and members of the [[Mycobacteriaceae]]. Prior studies cited in the paper also reported mycobacterial photoinactivation and, in one murine localized infection model, photoinactivation of [[Mycobacterium bovis BCG]] after irradiation. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:58-66, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:84-97]

## Photosensitizers and visible-light conditions

The tested compounds were meso-tetra-cationic palladium(II) porphyrins bearing `[Pd(bpy)Cl]+` complexes, arranged as either the para isomer `4-PdTPyP` or the meta isomer `3-PdTPyP`. The study used broth microdilution assays with photosensitizer concentrations ranging from `93.75` to `0.04 ug/mL` (`39` to `0.017 uM`) and compared illuminated conditions with matched dark and light-only controls. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:123-141, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:149-168]

Under dark conditions, both porphyrins showed the same minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) of `93.75 ug/mL` (`39 uM`) against all four RGM strains. Under visible white LED irradiation, `4-PdTPyP` improved only modestly to `46.87 ug/mL` (`19.5 uM`) for all strains, whereas `3-PdTPyP` showed substantially stronger activity, with MICs of `2.92 ug/mL` (`1.21 uM`) for [[Mycolicibacterium fortuitum]] and [[Mycobacteroides abscessus subsp. abscessus]], and `5.85 ug/mL` (`2.43 uM`) for [[Mycobacteroides abscessus subsp. massiliense]] and [[Mycolicibacterium smegmatis]]. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:223-231, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:267-276]

## Antimycobacterial activity under repeated irradiation

Because `3-PdTPyP` gave the best MIC results, the study followed it in time-kill experiments. At `MIC` and `2xMIC`, most strains had growth inhibited after two white-light irradiation sessions applied at `0` and `24 h`. [[Mycolicibacterium smegmatis]] required a third irradiation at `48 h` to show bactericidal action at the MIC, while `1/2 MIC` still reduced growth to some extent relative to the positive control. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:169-178, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:232-242]

In the discussion, the authors emphasize that eradication of mycobacterial growth after two visible-light sessions is notable because treatment of RGM infections often requires multiple drugs and extended duration. The paper states that skin-associated mycobacteriosis may last `4-6 months`, while pulmonary disease may require up to `24 months`, and it suggests that localized aPDT could therefore be relevant as a complementary approach. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:549-570]

## Reactive oxygen species

ROS-scavenger experiments were used to infer which ROS contributed to `3-PdTPyP` activity. The source reports that susceptibility was reduced mainly in the presence of ascorbic acid, used here as a singlet-oxygen quencher, indicating that [[singlet oxygen]] (`1O2`) was the main ROS associated with mycobacterial photoinactivation by `3-PdTPyP`. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:179-191, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:243-253]

The reported MIC shifts support that interpretation. In the presence of the singlet-oxygen quencher, `3-PdTPyP` MICs increased from `2.92` to `23.43 ug/mL` for [[Mycolicibacterium fortuitum]], from `2.92` to `11.71 ug/mL` for [[Mycobacteroides abscessus subsp. abscessus]], from `5.85` to `23.43 ug/mL` for [[Mycobacteroides abscessus subsp. massiliense]], and from `5.85` to `11.71 ug/mL` for [[Mycolicibacterium smegmatis]]. The discussion states that loss of singlet oxygen in solution reduced photosensitizer activity and increased MIC. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:378-413, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:458-467]

The authors further describe singlet oxygen as a major mediator of photodynamic damage in microorganisms and note that its high reactivity allows it to attack membrane lipids and proteins, cross the membrane barrier, and react with intracellular targets including DNA bases. They connect this chemistry to irreversible photodamage in mycobacteria. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:499-515]

## Cell-surface damage observed by AFM

The study used [[atomic force microscopy]] (AFM) to assess the effect of `3-PdTPyP` on [[Mycolicibacterium smegmatis]]. The AFM images indicated that bacteria largely preserved their morphology in dark conditions, while porphyrin plus white light significantly altered morphology, producing wrinkling and thinning of bacilli. The authors interpret these findings as evidence that both the porphyrin and visible-light exposure contribute to the cell-death pathway. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:255-266, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:283-287]

AFM nanomechanical measurements showed increased adhesion force after treatment. Untreated bacteria in the dark had an adhesion force of `0.14 +/- 0.03 uN`, compared with `0.17 +/- 0.03 uN` under light alone, `0.25 +/- 0.05 uN` in the dark plus `3-PdTPyP`, and `0.4 +/- 0.16 uN` under light plus `3-PdTPyP`. Treated bacilli also became narrower and more elongated, with the light-plus-porphyrin condition showing a width of `0.43 +/- 0.05 um` and length of `3.60 +/- 0.33 um`. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:288-298, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:475-492]

Electrostatic force microscopy showed that the mycobacterial surface had lower electrostatic forces over the cells and stronger positive forces around the bacterial perimeter, but this electrostatic component decreased after porphyrin treatment. After light exposure with `3-PdTPyP`, dark spots spread across the bacterial surface, which the authors associate with electrochemical degradation of the cell wall. In the discussion, they further describe cavities in the membrane and loss of charges around the bacteria-substrate interface. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:299-314, photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:516-548]

## Why the meta isomer performed better

The paper identifies `3-PdTPyP` as the more efficient photosensitizer than `4-PdTPyP` against RGM. In the discussion, the authors attribute this difference partly to better aqueous behavior and lower hydrophobicity of the meta isomer, noting that `4-PdTPyP` had a greater tendency to aggregate and a higher reported `log POW` value. They argue that higher solubility and reduced aggregation should improve interaction with bacteria and preserve ROS generation efficiency. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:420-457]

The same discussion also proposes that the cationic character of these porphyrins favors interaction with negatively charged microbial structures. For mycobacteria specifically, the authors suggest binding to anionic groups, probably including mycolic-acid-associated membrane components, with resulting changes in membrane electrostatic potential that make cells more susceptible to ROS attack. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:429-442]

## Therapeutic relevance

The conclusion states that this was the first demonstration of antimycobacterial effects for tetra-cationic porphyrins bearing peripheral palladium(II) complexes `[Pd(bpy)Cl]+`, with `3-PdTPyP` emerging as the most promising photosensitizer. The authors conclude that its photochemical and photobiological properties promoted local ROS production, especially singlet oxygen, and caused cell-wall damage together with altered nanomechanical and electrostatic adhesion properties. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:571-580]

The paper specifically highlights skin and soft-tissue infections as common RGM diseases and therefore considers localized photoinactivation an interesting application for these compounds. It also notes that combining aPDT with conventional antimycobacterial therapy could be an attractive strategy to shorten treatment time and improve adherence. ^[photo-damage-promoted-by-tetra-cationic-palladiumii-porphyrins-in-rapidly-growing-mycobacteria.md:580-593]
