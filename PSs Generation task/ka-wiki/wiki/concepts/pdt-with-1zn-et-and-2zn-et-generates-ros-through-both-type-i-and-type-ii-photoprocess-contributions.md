# PDT with 1Zn-Et and 2Zn-Et generates ROS through both type I and type II photoprocess contributions

[[Photodynamic therapy|PDT]] with `1Zn-Et` and `2Zn-Et` was reported to involve contributions from both [[Type I photoprocess|type I]] and [[Type II photoprocess|type II]] photoprocesses. In the study, the zinc phthalocyanines were highly phototoxic, and their activity tracked with singlet oxygen production, while separate cellular ROS measurements also detected formation of non-singlet-oxygen reactive species during irradiation. ^[jm5014852-114.md:290-314, jm5014852-114.md:379-400, jm5014852-114.md:636-643]

## Type II contribution

The source describes singlet oxygen as the most aggressive reactive species in PDT and identifies it as the product of photoprocess type II. For the zinc complexes studied here, phototoxicity followed the order `2Zn-Et > 1Zn-Et > 3Zn-Et`, which the authors state was proportional to singlet oxygen production. `2Zn-Et` was especially notable for a high singlet oxygen quantum yield (`PhiDelta = 0.91`), while `1Zn-Et` also showed a high value (`PhiDelta = 0.68`). ^[jm5014852-114.md:39-46, jm5014852-114.md:198-206, jm5014852-114.md:225-233, jm5014852-114.md:308-312, jm5014852-114.md:638-640]

## Type I contribution

The study also assessed broader intracellular [[reactive oxygen species|ROS]] generation using `H2DCFDA`, which the source describes as detecting free-radical species such as `HO•`, `RO•`, and `NO2•` and as being insensitive to singlet oxygen. In HeLa cells preincubated with `1Zn-Et` or `2Zn-Et`, increased `DCF` fluorescence was observed during irradiation only in the presence of the photosensitizers, with no significant secondary ROS production after irradiation ended. ROS production was stronger for `2Zn-Et` than for `1Zn-Et`. The authors interpret these findings as evidence that photoprocess type I contributes to photodynamic action. ^[jm5014852-114.md:379-397, jm5014852-114.md:1149-1169]

## Relative importance of the two pathways

Although the ROS assay supported a type I contribution, the paper states that this contribution may be less significant than that of type II, because the compounds retained high `PhiDelta + PhiF` values, leaving relatively little room for other excited-state deactivation pathways. In the conclusion, the authors summarize the mechanism by stating that both photoprocess types I and II may contribute to the lethal effect, since ROS production was observed during irradiation and phototoxicity remained proportional to singlet oxygen generation. ^[jm5014852-114.md:395-400, jm5014852-114.md:638-643]

## Experimental context

These ROS measurements were performed in HeLa cells after 12 hours of incubation with `1Zn-Et` or `2Zn-Et` at concentrations corresponding to their `IC85` values, followed by irradiation under the same conditions used for photodynamic treatment. Fluorescence from oxidized `DCF` was monitored before, during, and after irradiation, alongside dark and light controls to exclude nonspecific fluorescence changes. ^[jm5014852-114.md:386-393, jm5014852-114.md:1149-1169]
