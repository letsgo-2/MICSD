# Oxylipins in pyroptotic secretome support wound healing

Pyroptotic cells can release a secretome with pro-repair activity. In this study, supernatants from macrophages undergoing pyroptosis without IL-1beta or IL-1alpha release ("Pyro-1") induced gene programs associated with migration, proliferation, and wound healing in recipient macrophages, increased fibroblast migration, and accelerated wound closure in vitro and tissue repair in vivo. The authors conclude that the pyroptotic secretome contains oxylipins and metabolites with tissue-repair properties that may be therapeutically useful. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:43-59, oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:120-129]

A key oxylipin identified in the Pyro-1 secretome was prostaglandin E2 (PGE2). Lipidomics found enrichment of oxylipins, including PGE2, in Pyro-1 supernatants, and comparison with a PGE2-treated macrophage dataset showed substantial overlap with the Pyro-1-induced gene signature. The source reports that PGE2 synthesis is induced de novo during pyroptosis, downstream of caspase-1 activation and cyclooxygenase-2 activity, and that its release depends on gasdermin D pores. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:50-59, oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:213-230, oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:993-1028]

The repair effect was demonstrated in multiple assay systems. Pyro-1 supernatants accelerated closure of scratch wounds in primary mouse fibroblast monolayers, increased migration of fibroblasts and macrophages in Transwell assays, and promoted repair in a mouse colonic punch-biopsy model. In a full-thickness skin wound model, Pyro-1 supernatants improved wound closure, increased proliferation in the wound bed, and increased revascularization and fibroblast repopulation. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:231-254, oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1029-1047, oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1048-1078]

The study also found that metabolites in the pyroptotic secretome contribute to repair. Untargeted metabolomics identified unique and shared metabolites in Pyro-1 supernatants, including metabolites previously linked to tissue repair and anti-inflammatory signaling. In wounded skin, Pyro-1 supernatants increased CD45+ cells and promoted CD301+ macrophage polarization, with subsequent IL-27 expression; blocking IL-27 reduced healing. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1087-1118, oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1119-1188, oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1495-1537]

The authors further show that oxylipin effects are only part of the response. Aspirin-treated Pyro-1 supernatants, which had reduced prostaglandin production, still retained partial wound-healing activity, while the untreated Pyro-1 secretome was more effective. This supports a model in which both oxylipins such as PGE2 and additional metabolites jointly drive the pro-repair activity of the pyroptotic secretome. ^[oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1072-1078, oxylipins-and-metabolites-from-pyroptotic-cells-act-as-promoters-of-tissue-repair.md:1555-1602]
