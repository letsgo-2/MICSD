# Nanodelivery systems as a strategy to induce tumor-selective pyroptosis

[[Nanodelivery systems]] are presented in the source as a way to improve pyroptosis-based cancer therapy by helping induce cancer cell death while protecting healthy cells. The review frames this as part of a broader therapeutic goal in cancer treatment: selectively eradicating tumor cells while sparing normal tissue, especially when apoptosis-based strategies are limited by resistance. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:86-95, pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:149-151, pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:1623-1631]

## Rationale

The source argues that pyroptosis is a promising alternative programmed cell death pathway for cancer therapy because it can eliminate cancer cells and stimulate inflammation and immune responses. At the same time, pyroptosis is inflammatory and can damage surrounding normal tissues, so the review identifies the need for delivery strategies that improve selectivity and reduce off-target effects. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:118-125, pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:1566-1572, pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:1618-1635]

According to the review, many pyroptosis-inducing substances have substantial systemic adverse effects because they are insoluble, nonspecific, and degrade rapidly in vivo. Nanodelivery systems are proposed as a response to these limitations because they can improve agent stability and specificity, enable regulated delivery, and support continuous, targeted distribution to cancer cells. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:1636-1646]

## How nanodelivery supports tumor selectivity

The review explicitly states that one approach to reducing the risk of severe inflammatory toxicity, including cytokine release syndrome, is to create targeted delivery systems that specifically induce pyroptosis in cancer cells while sparing healthy tissues. In this framework, nanodelivery is not only a carrier technology but a strategy to make pyroptosis induction more tumor-selective and clinically tolerable. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:1623-1635]

A schematic summary in the review describes molecular drugs and chemotherapeutic drugs loaded into nanodelivery systems to target cancer cells and activate pyroptosis, leading to immune-cell responses, elevated immune cytokines, and tumor cell death. The review therefore links nanodelivery-mediated targeting with both direct tumor killing and immune activation. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:1529-1530]

## Reported nanomedicine approaches

In [[lung cancer]], the review cites inhaled poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid) porous microspheres loaded with decitabine and doxorubicin (`CO-MPs`). These were reported to induce pyroptotic death in lung cancer cells with fewer systemic side effects by reversing [[GSDME]] silencing and upregulating [[caspase-3|caspase 3]], which suppressed lung tumor growth and promoted immunological memory. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:827-840]

In [[breast cancer]], the review describes a biomimetic nanoparticle containing indocyanine green and decitabine. In this system, indocyanine green activated caspase 3 and decitabine increased GSDME expression by inhibiting DNA methylation; under low-dose photo-activation, the nanodrug induced pyroptosis and generated systemic antitumor immunity for tumor inhibition. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:931-938]

The same section also notes the use of oncolytic viruses combined with inhibitor nano-prodrugs (`MPNPs`) to promote GSDME-mediated pyroptosis, reverse an immunosuppressive [[tumor microenvironment|TME]], and enhance the effectiveness of anti-[[PD-1]] treatment in breast cancer models. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:939-942, pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:1059-1066]

In [[gastric cancer]], the review reports a pH-responsive liposome containing apatinib and cinobufagin and coated with a hybrid membrane (`LP-R/C@AC`). This nanocomplex was described as improving drug effects, reducing side effects, and providing superior solubility and targeting ability, while inhibiting tumor growth through the `VEGFR2/STAT3` pathway and stimulation of pyroptosis. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:1158-1167]

Also in gastric cancer, the review describes `ZIF-8@TPZ/Ce6`, a nanodrug built from zeolitic imidazole framework-8, chlorin e6, and tirapazamine, modified with gastric cancer cell membrane for targeting. With ultrasound irradiation, this complex generated [[reactive oxygen species|ROS]], induced pyroptosis, and exerted antitumor effects in a sonodynamic chemotherapy setting. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:1167-1177]

In [[colorectal cancer]], the review identifies two nanoparticle-based strategies. One involved `T22-PE24-H6` and `T22-DITOX-H6`, which targeted colorectal cancer cells, inhibited tumor growth, and blocked lymphatic and hematogenous metastasis by triggering pyroptotic activation. Another combined decitabine with chemotherapeutic nanodrugs to predemethylate and upregulate GSDME, followed by cisplatin-loaded nanoliposomes (`LipoDDP`) to activate the caspase 3 pathway and induce pyroptosis for strong tumor suppression. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:1247-1257]

## Mechanistic themes

Across these examples, the review repeatedly links nanodelivery-enabled pyroptosis to known pyroptotic execution routes, especially the [[caspase-3]]/[[Gasdermin E|GSDME]] axis and, in some settings, inflammasome-related pathways. The source also emphasizes that restoring or increasing GSDME expression, including through decitabine-mediated demethylation, can improve the ability of tumor cells to undergo pyroptosis and strengthen antitumor treatment effects. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:1597-1617]

The review further connects pyroptosis to immune remodeling of the TME. It notes that pyroptosis-related mediators and proteins can influence immune-cell infiltration and antitumor immunity, and that GSDME-mediated pyroptosis can help convert "cold" tumors into "hot" tumors by producing inflammatory mediators and improving NK-cell and CD8+ T-cell infiltration and function. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:693-704, pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:1464-1471]

## Benefits and limitations

The review describes several expected advantages of nanodelivery systems in pyroptosis-based cancer therapy: improved solubility, better stability, more specific delivery, regulated release, fewer systemic side effects, and decreased toxicity to healthy cells. These features are presented as reasons nanomedicine may help translate pyroptosis-inducing agents into more selective antitumor strategies. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:1636-1650]

At the same time, the source stresses that most pyroptosis-related nanomedicines have not entered clinical practice. It attributes this gap to difficulties in scaling up production, maintaining consistent quality control, and the high cost of development and manufacturing, and concludes that more research and clinical trials are needed to establish efficacy and safety. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:1650-1660]

## Summary

In this review, nanodelivery systems are presented as a practical strategy for making pyroptosis induction more tumor-selective. By stabilizing pyroptosis-inducing agents, improving targeting, and reducing exposure of normal tissues, they aim to preserve the anticancer and immune-stimulating benefits of pyroptosis while limiting systemic toxicity. The evidence summarized in the source spans multiple tumor types and includes microspheres, biomimetic nanoparticles, liposomes, membrane-coated nanodrugs, and nanoliposomes, but the field remains largely preclinical. ^[pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:1529-1530, pyroptosis-and-inflammasomes-in-cancer-and-inflammation.md:1636-1660]
