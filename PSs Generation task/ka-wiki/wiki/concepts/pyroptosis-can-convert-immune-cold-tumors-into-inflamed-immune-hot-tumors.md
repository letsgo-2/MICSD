# Pyroptosis can convert immune-cold tumors into inflamed immune-hot tumors

[[Pyroptosis]] is an inflammatory form of programmed cell death that has been proposed as a way to overcome two major barriers in cancer therapy: tumor resistance to apoptosis and an immunosuppressive [[tumor microenvironment]]. The reviewed evidence indicates that inducing pyroptosis in tumor cells can provoke robust inflammatory responses, promote tumor regression, and support tumor-specific immunity. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:13-25, pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:49-58]

In the source’s framing, “cold” tumors are immune-tolerant or immune-silent states in which tumor cells avoid immune detection and killing by recruiting immunosuppressive cells, increasing immune checkpoint proteins, impeding antigen presentation, and releasing immune-inhibitory factors. Pyroptosis is described as a way to “heat” such tumors by shifting them toward inflammatory states that support immune activation. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:845-850]

## Why pyroptosis can heat tumors

Pyroptosis is mediated by pore-forming [[gasdermin]] proteins and is characterized by membrane pore formation, cell swelling, rupture, and release of intracellular contents. This process enables release of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as [[IL-1beta]] and [[IL-18]], along with other immunogenic material and [[damage-associated molecular patterns|DAMPs]], which can initiate or perpetuate inflammatory responses. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:185-197, pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:235-239, pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:264-267]

The review links this inflammatory output to [[immunogenic cell death]]. It states that, unlike apoptosis, pyroptosis has the molecular machinery to elicit a robust inflammatory response and is considered a form of immunogenic cell death in some contexts. Tumor-cell pyroptosis is described as promoting tumor clearance by amplifying immune activation and immune effector function. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:526-545]

Figure 2 in the source presents this transition as a progression from “cold tumor” to “warming tumor,” then to “warm tumor,” and finally to “hot tumor.” In that model, pyroptotic tumor cells release pro-inflammatory cytokines and immunogenic material that prompt immune-cell activation and recruitment; the resulting infiltrated immune cells then recognize and kill tumor cells, potentially creating a positive feedback loop that enhances tumor-specific immunity. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:845-850]

## Immune changes associated with pyroptosis

The source summarizes experimental evidence that pyroptosis can reshape the immune landscape inside tumors. In one cited Gsdma3 delivery study, inducing pyroptosis in a minority of tumor cells was sufficient to eliminate whole 4T1 mammary tumor grafts in immunocompetent mice, but not in T-cell-deficient mice, indicating that the antitumor effect depended at least partly on the immune system. Treated tumors showed increased [[CD3]]+ T-cell infiltration and decreased [[regulatory T cells|T regulatory cells]]. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:557-580]

That same study also reported broader remodeling of the tumor immune microenvironment. Compared with controls, pyroptosis-inducing treatment increased [[CD4]]+ and [[CD8]]+ T cells, [[natural killer cells|NK cells]], and M1 macrophages, while decreasing monocytes, neutrophils, [[myeloid-derived suppressor cells]], and M2 macrophages. Serum and tumor levels of IL-1beta, IL-18, and [[HMGB1]] also increased, while immunostimulatory and antitumor effector genes were upregulated and immunosuppressive or protumor genes were downregulated. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:585-597]

Additional studies cited in the review support similar immune-heating effects through other gasdermins. GSDME expression in murine breast tumors significantly inhibited tumor growth and increased infiltration of NK cells and tumor-associated macrophages, while also increasing cytotoxic and cytokine-associated features in CD8+ tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes. A separate melanoma study is described as linking treatment-induced GSDME-mediated pyroptosis and HMGB1 release to infiltration of tumor-associated T cells and activated dendritic cells, suggesting a route by which pyroptosis-associated DAMPs may support T-cell priming and maturation. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:757-797, pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:829-837]

## Therapeutic implications

The review describes multiple therapeutic strategies aimed at using pyroptosis to engage antitumor immunity, including nanoparticle delivery of gasdermins, chemotherapy- or targeted-therapy-associated apoptosis-to-pyroptosis switching, microparticle drug delivery, and engineered immune-cell approaches. Across these examples, pyroptosis is presented as a feasible mechanism for increasing immune infiltration or activation and improving tumor control. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:838-945]

Most directly, the review concludes that reports of pyroptosis induction synergizing with [[PD-1]] inhibitors suggest its potential to turn “cold” tumors “hot.” It further argues that pyroptosis may be most effective when combined with other anticancer modalities, particularly in the setting of immunosuppressive tumor microenvironments that otherwise limit immunotherapy. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:930-949, pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:950-973]

## Caveats

The same source emphasizes that pyroptosis is context-dependent and can also support tumor progression under some conditions. Aberrant or prolonged pyroptosis-related signaling may contribute to immunosuppressive cells, epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition, extracellular matrix remodeling, chronic tumor necrosis, and other protumor features. As a result, whether pyroptosis heats or supports a tumor depends on tumor type, genetics, cell context, and duration or mode of induction. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:332-361]
