# Pyroptosis as a strategy to overcome apoptosis resistance and stimulate anticancer immunity

[[Pyroptosis]] is a lytic, inflammatory form of programmed cell death that has attracted interest in cancer research because it may address two major barriers to effective treatment: tumor resistance to [[apoptosis]] and the immunosuppressive [[tumor microenvironment]]. Unlike apoptosis, pyroptosis is mediated by pore-forming [[gasdermin]] proteins and is associated with release of pro-inflammatory cytokines and immunogenic cellular contents after membrane rupture, features that can promote immune activation and tumor regression. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:13-25, pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:49-58]

## Why pyroptosis is relevant in cancer therapy

The review states that apoptosis resistance is highly prevalent in tumors and has helped drive interest in alternative programmed cell death pathways. In that context, pyroptosis is presented as a potentially harnessable way to bypass apoptosis resistance while also activating tumor-specific immunity or improving the effectiveness of existing anticancer therapies. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:28-35, pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:49-58]

Pyroptosis differs from apoptosis in both mechanism and immune consequence. It is characterized by pore formation, cell swelling, and osmotic lysis, and is typically executed when caspases activate gasdermin proteins that form membrane pores. These pores enable release of inflammatory mediators such as [[IL-1beta]], [[IL-18]], [[HMGB1]], ATP, and other [[damage-associated molecular patterns|DAMPs]], which can initiate or sustain inflammatory responses. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:183-197, pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:224-239]

## Mechanistic basis for overcoming apoptosis resistance

A key therapeutic idea in the review is that some anticancer treatments can redirect cell death from apoptosis toward pyroptosis. In particular, under certain contexts such as chemotherapy or targeted therapy, caspase-3 can cleave [[Gasdermin E|GSDME]] and trigger pyroptosis rather than conventional apoptotic execution. The source notes that when GSDME levels are high, caspase-3 activation rapidly promotes pyroptosis, whereas low GSDME levels favor apoptosis instead. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:287-298]

The review also notes that many proteases involved in pyroptosis can mediate apoptosis when their corresponding gasdermin is absent, suggesting that gasdermin expression strongly influences whether tumor cells undergo apoptotic or pyroptotic death. This helps explain why strategies that restore, deliver, or exploit functional gasdermins may allow therapies to convert otherwise limited apoptotic responses into inflammatory tumor cell death. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:316-330, pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:907-929]

## Link to immunogenic cell death and anticancer immunity

The review frames the immune significance of pyroptosis through [[immunogenic cell death]]. It defines immunogenic death in terms of antigenic and adjuvant features and states that, unlike apoptosis, which is fundamentally immune tolerant, pyroptosis has the machinery to elicit a robust inflammatory response and is considered a form of immunogenic cell death in some settings. Pyroptosis-mediated tumor clearance is described as occurring through amplification of immune activation and immune-cell function. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:526-548]

More specifically, pyroptotic tumor cells release cytokines and immunogenic material that can recruit and activate immune cells. The review’s therapeutic model describes pyroptosis as a way to convert immune-silent or “cold” tumors into more inflamed states, culminating in greater immune-cell infiltration and tumor-cell killing; it further suggests that this process may enter a positive feedback loop in which infiltrating immune cells drive additional tumor cell killing and stronger tumor-specific immunity. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:845-850, pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:945-949]

## Evidence that pyroptosis can stimulate antitumor immune responses

In one study highlighted in the review, selective delivery of a mouse [[GSDMA]] isoform into tumor cells caused pyroptosis in a fraction of cancer cells yet produced marked tumor shrinkage in immunocompetent mice. The antitumor effect was lost in T-cell-deficient mice, and depletion of CD4-positive or CD8-positive cells prevented tumor regression, indicating that immune cells were required. Treated tumors also showed increased CD3-positive T-cell infiltration, fewer regulatory T cells, increased NK cells and M1 macrophages, decreased myeloid-derived suppressor cells and M2 macrophages, elevated IL-1beta, IL-18, and HMGB1, and upregulation of immunostimulatory effector genes. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:549-597]

The review also summarizes evidence that cytotoxic lymphocytes can directly induce tumor-cell pyroptosis. In different contexts, [[cytotoxic T lymphocytes|CTLs]], [[natural killer cell|NK cells]], or [[CAR-T]] cells deliver granzymes that cleave specific gasdermins in target cells, including [[GSDMB]] via granzyme A and GSDME via granzyme B. These studies support the idea that pyroptosis is not only a therapy-induced endpoint but also part of active antitumor immune killing. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:660-699, pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:752-828]

For [[GSDME]], the review describes experiments in which ectopic expression in murine breast tumor cells inhibited tumor growth in immunocompetent mice and increased infiltration of NK cells and tumor-associated macrophages, while also enhancing cytotoxic features of CD8-positive tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes. The antitumor effect depended on lymphocytes and perforin, and vaccine/challenge experiments strongly suggested that pyroptosis acted as immunogenic cell death in this setting. Additional studies linked GSDME-mediated pyroptosis to HMGB1 release, dendritic-cell activation, and increased tumor-associated T cells. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:753-797, pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:829-837]

## Therapeutic strategies discussed in the review

The review describes several ways pyroptosis has been explored therapeutically. These include tumor-cell-derived microparticles delivering methotrexate to induce GSDME-mediated pyroptosis, combinations of BRAF and MEK inhibitors that trigger pyroptosis in melanoma, metformin-induced [[caspase-3gsdme-mediated-pyroptosis|Caspase-3/GSDME-mediated pyroptosis]], and molecularly targeted therapies for KRAS-, EGFR-, or ALK-mutant lung cancers that induce concurrent apoptosis and GSDME-dependent pyroptosis. It also highlights RIG-I agonism and compounds such as `13d` and `L61H10`, which were reported to promote an apoptosis-to-pyroptosis switch together with antitumor effects in vivo. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:859-906]

The review further discusses strategies aimed at restoring the tumor’s ability to undergo pyroptosis. Because many cancers downregulate gasdermins or carry non-functional variants, one proposed solution is epigenetic targeting. The example given is decitabine-mediated demethylation of GSDME combined with nanoliposomal chemotherapy that activates caspase-3, which restored pyroptosis and was reported to suppress tumor growth, metastasis, and recurrence while stimulating immune responses through cytokine release. Direct nanotechnology-based delivery of functional gasdermin proteins is presented as another possible way to circumvent defective endogenous gasdermin function. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:907-929]

## Combination with immunotherapy

The review emphasizes that pyroptosis may be especially useful in combination with other immunotherapies that must operate in an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment. One example is engineered NK92 cells with a receptor that converts inhibitory PD-1 signaling into activation; these cells killed lung cancer cells through GSDME-mediated pyroptosis in vitro and inhibited tumor growth in vivo. The review also points to evidence that pyroptosis induction can synergize with [[PD-1]] blockade to turn “cold” tumors “hot,” highlighting combination therapy as a major area of promise. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:930-949]

## Limits and cautions

The same inflammatory properties that make pyroptosis attractive can also support tumor progression in some settings. The review notes that prolonged or aberrant activity of gasdermins, inflammasomes, or pro-inflammatory cytokines can promote immunosuppressive cells, epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition, and extracellular matrix remodeling, and that pyroptosis has been linked to tumor-supportive effects in some cancers. Its role is therefore described as context-dependent rather than uniformly beneficial. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:332-361]

The review also identifies practical limits to therapeutic use. Gasdermin expression and function vary widely across and within cancers, and some interventions that trigger pyroptosis can be associated with adverse inflammatory effects such as [[cytokine release syndrome]]. For that reason, the authors suggest pyroptosis will likely be most effective when tailored to specific tumors and patients and used in combination with other anticancer modalities rather than as a universal standalone strategy. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:950-973]

## Summary

In this review, pyroptosis is presented as a promising strategy to overcome apoptosis resistance because it can redirect cell death toward gasdermin-mediated membrane rupture and inflammatory tumor-cell destruction. It is also presented as a way to stimulate anticancer immunity by releasing DAMPs and cytokines, increasing immune-cell infiltration and activation, and potentially converting immune-cold tumors into immune-hot tumors. At the same time, the review stresses that the value of pyroptosis is highly context dependent and that successful therapeutic use will depend on tumor biology, gasdermin status, delivery strategy, and rational combination with other treatments. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:13-25, pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:49-58, pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:950-973]
