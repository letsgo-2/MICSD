# Pyroptosis-driven tumour clearance depends on adaptive T cell immunity

Pyroptosis-driven tumour clearance in this study refers to the finding that inducing pyroptosis in only a minority of tumour cells was enough to eliminate whole tumours, but only when host adaptive immunity, especially T cells, was intact. Using a bioorthogonal Phe-BF3 desilylation system to release active [[Gasdermin A3|GSDMA3]] in tumour cells, the authors showed that less than 15% of tumour cells undergoing pyroptosis could clear 4T1 mammary tumours and that this effect correlated with stronger antitumour immune responses. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:33-41, a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:834-846]

## Core finding

The study reports that pyroptosis of less than 15% of tumour cells was sufficient to clear an entire 4T1 mammary tumour graft in mice. In vivo, three rounds of treatment with nanoparticle-conjugated GSDMA3 plus Phe-BF3 caused major tumour shrinkage and reduced tumour burden to a negligible level by day 25, whereas NP-GSDMA3 alone, Phe-BF3 alone, or pore-forming-deficient mutant GSDMA3 with Phe-BF3 did not produce tumour regression. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:33-41, a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:502-510]

The same antitumour effect was observed after either intravenous or intratumoural treatment, which the authors interpret as evidence that tumour regression resulted from GSDMA3 activation at the tumour site. Tumours from treated mice contained more propidium-iodide-positive pyroptotic cells, but these cells represented only a small fraction of the total tumour-cell population, consistent with the roughly 20% pyroptosis measured in cultured 4T1 cells. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:634-643]

A complementary mixed-cell tumour model strengthened this point. When normal 4T1 cells were mixed with 10% or 30% doxycycline-inducible 4T1-GSDMA3 dox-on cells, intratumoural doxycycline completely stopped tumour growth, despite only a fraction of inoculated cells being capable of inducible pyroptosis. The authors interpret this as evidence that elimination of the whole tumour could not be explained only by direct killing of pyroptotic cells. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:643-652, a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:803-810]

## Dependence on adaptive T cells

The key mechanistic result is that tumour clearance depended on host T cells. In athymic Nu/Nu mice, which lack mature T cells, treatment with NP-GSDMA3 plus Phe-BF3 did not cause tumour regression. In immunocompetent BALB/c mice, however, the same treatment increased tumour-infiltrating `CD3+` T cells, increased both `CD4+` and `CD8+` populations, and reduced the proportion of `FOXP3+ CD4+` regulatory T cells. These changes were not seen with the pore-forming-deficient GSDMA3 mutant control. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:834-846]

Antibody depletion experiments showed that both major T-cell compartments were required. Depletion of either `CD4+` or `CD8+` cells blocked tumour regression induced by NP-GSDMA3 plus Phe-BF3, leading the authors to conclude that pyroptosis-induced tumour regression requires both cytotoxic T cells and `CD4+` T helper cells. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:843-846, a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:1403-1414]

## Immune microenvironment changes

Single-cell RNA sequencing of tumour `CD45+` leukocytes showed that pyroptosis-inducing treatment reshaped the tumour immune landscape. Compared with PBS-treated tumours, treated tumours contained more `CD4+` T cells, `CD8+` T cells, and natural killer cells, and fewer monocytes, neutrophils, and myeloid-derived suppressor cells. The M1 macrophage population increased, while the fraction of cells expressing the M2 marker `Arg1` decreased. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:847-856]

The same analysis found increased expression of chemotactic cytokine genes `Ccl5`, `Cxcl9`, and `Cxcl10`, as well as activation-related genes such as `Cd69` and `Klrk1`. Antitumour effector genes including `Ifng`, `Gzma`, `Gzmb`, `Gzmk`, and `Fasl` were upregulated, while protumoural or immunosuppressive genes such as `Csf1`, `Vegfa`, `Arg1`, `Cd274` (`PD-L1`), and `Pdcd1lg2` (`PD-L2`) were downregulated. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:856-866]

## Cytokine contribution

The study links this adaptive immune response to inflammatory mediators associated with pyroptosis. Serum and tumour levels of `IL-1β` and `IL-18` increased after NP-GSDMA3 plus Phe-BF3 treatment, and `HMGB1` levels also increased. Neutralization experiments showed that blocking `IL-1β` severely inhibited tumour regression, whereas blocking `IL-18` had a partial effect and blocking `HMGB1` had no detectable effect. The authors therefore conclude that `IL-1β`, probably derived from activated macrophages, is required for the antitumour immunity induced by tumour-cell pyroptosis. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:867-877, a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:1720-1734]

## Relation to checkpoint blockade

The source further places this finding in the context of [[immune checkpoint blockade]]. The 4T1 tumour model is described as a "cold" tumour that did not respond to anti-PD1 alone, and one reduced, single round of NP-GSDMA3 plus Phe-BF3 also did not prevent tumour growth by itself. However, when that ineffective pyroptosis-inducing dose was followed by anti-PD1 treatment, tumour volume and tumour weight were markedly reduced, indicating that pyroptosis-induced inflammation can sensitize tumours to checkpoint blockade. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:35-41, a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:877-892]

## Significance within the study

Within this paper, the dependence of tumour clearance on adaptive T cell immunity is central to the interpretation of pyroptosis as more than a direct cytolytic event. The authors present pyroptosis-induced inflammation as a trigger for robust antitumour immunity, with both helper and cytotoxic T-cell responses required for full tumour regression and with potential utility for improving cancer [[immunotherapy]]. ^[a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:40-41, a-bioorthogonal-system-reveals-antitumour-immune-function-of-pyroptosis.md:893-904]
