# Non-canonical LPS-caspase-4/5/11-GSDMD pyroptosis pathway

The non-canonical [[pyroptosis]] pathway is a principal [[Gasdermin D|GSDMD]]-dependent route to pyroptotic cell death that is distinct from the canonical inflammasome pathway because it does not rely on [[caspase-1]] for initiation. In this pathway, the relevant inflammatory caspases are [[caspase-4]] and [[caspase-5]] in humans and [[caspase-11]] in mice. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:213-221, pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:268-272]

Activation occurs when [[lipopolysaccharide|LPS]] directly binds the corresponding pro-caspases in the cytoplasm, bypassing the need for upstream inflammasome sensor proteins. The source states that cytoplasmic LPS originates from gram-negative bacteria and may be delivered by infection or by membrane vesicles. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:273-277]

Once activated, caspase-4/5 or caspase-11 cleaves the autoinhibited linker region of GSDMD, releasing the pore-forming N-terminal fragment from the repressive C-terminal domain. The liberated GSDMD N-terminus then translocates to cellular membranes, oligomerizes, and forms beta-barrel transmembrane pores that disrupt osmotic balance, promote cell lysis, and enable release of inflammatory intracellular contents. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:224-239, pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:299-304]

Unlike the canonical pathway, the non-canonical pathway does not directly process [[IL-1beta]] or [[IL-18]]. Instead, GSDMD-driven pyroptosis causes potassium efflux, which activates the [[NLRP3 inflammasome]] and upregulates caspase-1 activity; through caspase-1, IL-1beta and IL-18 can then be matured and released through GSDMD pores. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:278-286, pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:299-304]

More broadly, this pathway fits the general morphology of pyroptosis described in the source: pore formation, cell swelling, plasma-membrane rupture, and osmotic lysis together with release of pro-inflammatory mediators and damage-associated material. Pyroptosis is presented as a lytic, inflammatory form of programmed cell death mediated by gasdermin family proteins and typically associated with antimicrobial defense. ^[pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:33-45, pyroptosis-at-the-forefront-of-anticancer-immunity.md:175-197]
