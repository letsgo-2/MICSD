# Pyroptosis as an Inflammatory Programmed Cell Death

Pyroptosis is a pro-inflammatory form of [[programmed cell death]] first named in 2001 after its identification as a caspase-1-dependent death process in Salmonella-infected macrophages. It is characterized by plasma membrane pore formation, release of inflammatory intracellular contents, cell swelling, and eventual lysis. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:43-46, a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:88-104]

## Overview

Pyroptosis is driven by [[inflammasome]] assembly and activation. Current understanding described in the source distinguishes a canonical pathway mediated by caspase-1 and a noncanonical pathway mediated by caspase-4, caspase-5, and caspase-11. In both settings, inflammatory caspases activate downstream mechanisms that culminate in pore formation in the plasma membrane and inflammatory cell death. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:88-95, a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:585-591, a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:646-672]

As membrane integrity breaks down, pro-inflammatory mediators including IL-1b and IL-18 are secreted through membrane pores. Water influx then increases intracellular osmotic pressure, causing swelling and lysis, and the released mediators and cellular contents initiate inflammatory cascades that can amplify local and systemic inflammation. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:96-104, a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:687-692]

## Core Molecular Mechanism

Inflammasomes are polyprotein complexes involved in innate immunity. According to the source, they are composed of pattern recognition receptors (PRRs), the adaptor ASC, and pro-caspase effectors. PRRs detect pathogen-associated molecular patterns and host-derived damage-associated signals, which promotes recruitment and activation of inflammatory caspases. Extensively studied inflammasomes linked to pyroptosis include [[NLRP3 inflammasome|NLRP3]], NLRP1, NLRC4, and AIM2, with NLRP3 described as the most concretely studied. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:592-608]

In the NLRP3 pathway, stimulus recognition leads to NLRP3 oligomerization and recruitment of ASC, which then recruits pro-caspase-1 to complete inflammasome assembly. The assembled complex self-activates, generating active caspase-1 and promoting maturation of IL-1b and IL-18. Some inflammasomes such as NLRP1 and NLRC4 may connect directly to pro-caspase-1 without ASC, while AIM2 also forms a caspase-1-activating inflammasome complex. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:609-637]

## Canonical and Noncanonical Pathways

The canonical pathway of pyroptosis is mainly mediated by caspase-1. Activated caspase-1 cleaves pro-IL-1b and pro-IL-18 into their active forms and also cleaves GSDMD, linking cytokine maturation to pore formation and cell death. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:646-658]

The noncanonical pathway is mediated by human caspase-4 and caspase-5 or mouse caspase-11. In this pathway, cytoplasmic lipopolysaccharide can directly bind and activate caspase-11 independently of TLR4, and the human homologs are described as functioning similarly. The source notes that noncanonical pyroptosis has become an important research direction. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:659-672]

## Gasdermin D and Membrane Pore Formation

[[Gasdermin D]] (GSDMD), identified in 2015 as a central mediator of pyroptosis, is described in the source as a common substrate of caspase-1 and caspase-4/5/11. In resting cells, GSDMD is auto-inhibited in the cytoplasm. Cleavage by inflammatory caspases generates an N-terminal fragment that binds the lipid bilayer and forms membrane pores approximately 10-15 nm in diameter, while the C-terminal fragment contributes to self-inhibition. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:674-695]

These GSDMD pores disrupt membrane integrity, enable secretion of IL-1b and IL-18, and drive water influx, swelling, and lysis. The source also notes later structural work showing that GSDMD pores contain 31 to 34 subunits and providing mechanistic insight into pore assembly and interleukin release. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:687-704, a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:719-724]

## Relationship to Other Cell Death Pathways

The source places pyroptosis among several forms of programmed cell death, alongside [[apoptosis]], [[autophagy]], [[necroptosis]], and [[ferroptosis]], and emphasizes that these pathways are interconnected rather than fully separate. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:762-769]

Several examples of crosstalk are described. In the absence of GSDMD, active caspase-1 can induce apoptosis through caspase-3/7. Caspase-3/7 can also cleave GSDMD at different sites and inactivate it, blocking pyroptosis. Conversely, caspase-8 can activate GSDMD under some infectious or signaling conditions, shifting cell death toward pyroptosis. The source further identifies caspase-8 activity as an important switch among apoptosis, necroptosis, and pyroptosis. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:770-809]

The source also describes a gasdermin-mediated link between apoptosis and pyroptosis through GSDME. Caspase-3 can cleave GSDME to generate a pore-forming N-terminal fragment that induces pyroptosis. In the context described, normal tissues expressing GSDME may undergo pyroptosis after chemotherapy-induced caspase-3 activation, whereas cancer cells lacking GSDME expression tend to die by apoptosis. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:810-829]

## Biological and Disease Relevance

Pyroptosis contributes to innate immune defense by controlling inflammasome-dependent cytokine secretion and helping remove infected cells. At the same time, excessive or inappropriate pyroptosis can be harmful because large-scale cell death and inflammatory cascades can damage tissues and organs and may contribute to organ failure. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:105-117, a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:830-846]

The source states that pyroptosis is involved in the occurrence and progression of infectious diseases, metabolic diseases, tumors, nervous system diseases, and cardiovascular diseases. Specific examples mentioned include coronavirus disease 2019, Parkinson’s disease, epilepsy, ischemic stroke, atherosclerosis, ischemia-reperfusion injury, and myocardial infarction. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:113-130, a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:431-436, a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:840-846]

Pyroptosis is described as a double-edged sword in cancer. The source notes growing interest in targeted therapies directed at molecules such as NLRP3, caspase-1, and GSDMD, and it reports examples of GSDMD pore inhibitors and NLRP3 or caspase-1 inhibitors. However, it also states that large-scale clinical data are still lacking to establish safe dosing and clinical efficacy for pyroptosis inhibitors. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:847-888]

## Research Context

Research on pyroptosis has increased substantially over time. In the bibliometric analysis, publication output was low from 2001 to 2012, grew steadily from 2012 to 2017, and then expanded rapidly from 2017 to 2020. The source identifies major research focuses as pathway mechanisms, crosstalk with other forms of programmed cell death, and roles in disease. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:206-215, a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:377-397, a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:905-922]

Frequently occurring keywords in the field include NLRP3 inflammasome, apoptosis, cell death, inflammasome, GSDMD, caspase-1, and inflammation. The source characterizes these as major hotspots in pyroptosis research. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:377-397, a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:456-467]
