# Short C-terminal peptide autoinhibition in bacterial gasdermins

In bacterial gasdermins ([[bacterial gasdermins|bGSDMs]]), autoinhibition is mediated not by the large alpha-helical C-terminal domain seen in mammalian gasdermins, but by a short C-terminal peptide. Structural analysis of bGSDMs from *Bradyrhizobium tropiciagri* and *Vitiosangium* sp. showed that these proteins retain an inactive conformation similar to inactive mammalian gasdermins despite lacking the canonical large inhibitory domain. ^[bacterial-gasdermins-reveal-an-ancient-mechanism-of-cell-death.md:83-94]

The short C-terminal peptide wraps around the twisted beta-sheet core of the bGSDM and terminates across the strand equivalent to mammalian beta2, where it helps stabilize the inactive state. In the *Bradyrhizobium* structure, the peptide also helps cap a hydrophobic tunnel that contains a buried palmitoyl modification attached to the N-terminal cysteine C3. ^[bacterial-gasdermins-reveal-an-ancient-mechanism-of-cell-death.md:93-106]

Detailed structural analysis of the *Bradyrhizobium* bGSDM explains how this peptide restrains the pore-forming core. Residues F245 and F247 lie along the surface between positions equivalent to the mammalian alpha1 helix and beta9 strand, with additional support from contacts involving N21 and the peptide backbone. F253 latches over the palmitoyl group, and the peptide terminates below the strand equivalent to beta2, where hydrogen bonds from R27 to the L256 backbone and from N29 to E258 further support the inhibited conformation. ^[bacterial-gasdermins-reveal-an-ancient-mechanism-of-cell-death.md:172-206]

Experimental truncation of the C-terminal peptide in *Runella*, *Bradyrhizobium*, and *Vitiosangium* bGSDM constructs caused arrested cell growth, indicating that the peptide is required to maintain autoinhibition. ^[bacterial-gasdermins-reveal-an-ancient-mechanism-of-cell-death.md:199-206]

Activation occurs through proteolytic cleavage by adjacent caspase-like proteases. In the *Runella* system, the cleavage site lies in a loop immediately preceding the C-terminal peptide, and structural packing suggested that the peptide can dissociate from the bGSDM surface after cleavage. The study therefore links protease cleavage to removal of this short inhibitory segment and release of bGSDM activity. ^[bacterial-gasdermins-reveal-an-ancient-mechanism-of-cell-death.md:157-171, bacterial-gasdermins-reveal-an-ancient-mechanism-of-cell-death.md:604-629]

More broadly, the authors propose that in both mammalian gasdermins and bGSDMs, proteolytic cleavage releases inhibition of the lipophilic N-terminal pore-forming region. They further note that the existence of a notably short inhibitory C-terminal peptide in bGSDMs raises the possibility that short-form eukaryotic gasdermins such as [[pejvakin]] may be activated through a similar mechanism. ^[bacterial-gasdermins-reveal-an-ancient-mechanism-of-cell-death.md:270-279]
