# Mitochondrial GSDMD-ROS axis restricts *Burkholderia cenocepacia*

The mitochondrial GSDMD-ROS axis refers to a host-defense mechanism in which activated [[Gasdermin D|GSDMD]] promotes mitochondrial reactive oxygen species generation to limit *[[Burkholderia cenocepacia]]* infection. In the reviewed source, this mechanism is described as a way GSDMD restricts bacterial survival in phagosomes rather than as a bystander effect of generalized cell damage. ^[gasdermin-d-kills-bacteria.md:459-461, gasdermin-d-kills-bacteria.md:489-492]

## Overview

*Burkholderia cenocepacia* is described as an opportunistic human pathogen, notable for infecting the respiratory system of people with [[cystic fibrosis]] and for causing severe disease in immunocompromised individuals. The source notes that some strains are resistant to virtually all known antibiotics, and that in cystic fibrosis this infection can progress to Cepacia syndrome, marked by worsening respiratory failure, bacteremia, and necrotizing lung inflammation. ^[gasdermin-d-kills-bacteria.md:442-459]

Within this context, the review states that GSDMD-N can disturb the mitochondrial membrane and promote reactive oxygen species (ROS) generation, which contributes to clearance of *B. cenocepacia* in phagosomes. The paper presents this as a specific antibacterial role for GSDMD during *B. cenocepacia* infection. ^[gasdermin-d-kills-bacteria.md:459-461, gasdermin-d-kills-bacteria.md:489-492]

## Upstream activation

The source places this response within inflammasome signaling. It states that inflammatory stimuli activate pattern-recognition receptors such as ALRs, Pyrin, and NLRs, and that the [[NLRP3 inflammasome]] consists of NLRP3, ASC, and caspase-1. Activation of this inflammasome leads to caspase-1-dependent GSDMD pyroptosis and cytokine release. ^[gasdermin-d-kills-bacteria.md:463-469]

For *B. cenocepacia* reduction specifically, the review states that the non-canonical pathway is initiated by intracellular LPS, which activates caspase-11. Caspase-11 then cleaves GSDMD to generate the active N-terminal fragment, GSDMD-N. ^[gasdermin-d-kills-bacteria.md:492-497]

## Mitochondrial mechanism

According to the source, GSDMD-N inserts into the mitochondrial membrane because of its preference for lipids such as cardiolipins. This insertion produces pores, disrupts mitochondrial membrane potential, and increases mitochondrial ROS production. ^[gasdermin-d-kills-bacteria.md:497-512]

The review further states that GSDMD is involved in mitochondrial production of ROS species including hydroxyl radicals and superoxide anions during *B. cenocepacia* infection. It also notes more broadly that mitochondrial dysfunction can disrupt mitochondrial membrane potential and promote ROS generation, although the precise relationship between membrane-potential disruption and increased ROS in pyroptosis is described as not fully clear. ^[gasdermin-d-kills-bacteria.md:469-476, gasdermin-d-kills-bacteria.md:479-484]

## Antibacterial outcome

In this model, the increased mitochondrial ROS serves an antimicrobial function. The source states that ROS generated downstream of mitochondrial GSDMD activity inhibits *B. cenocepacia* growth in phagocytes and clears the bacterium through autophagy activation. Figure 3 in the review summarizes this mechanism as GSDMD-N insertion into the mitochondrial membrane, disruption of mitochondrial membrane potential, elevation of ROS, and clearance of *B. cenocepacia* in the phagosome. ^[gasdermin-d-kills-bacteria.md:489-512]

## Relation to pyroptosis

The review situates this pathway within the broader biology of [[pyroptosis]]. It describes GSDMD as a pore-forming effector activated by caspase-1 or caspase-11 through canonical or non-canonical inflammasome pathways, respectively. In the *B. cenocepacia* section, however, the emphasized antibacterial effect is mitochondrial ROS-dependent restriction of bacterial growth and clearance through autophagy, rather than direct bacteriolysis by GSDMD at the bacterial membrane. ^[gasdermin-d-kills-bacteria.md:485-497, gasdermin-d-kills-bacteria.md:508-512]
