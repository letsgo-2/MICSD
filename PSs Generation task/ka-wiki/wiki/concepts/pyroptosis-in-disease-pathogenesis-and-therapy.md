# Pyroptosis in disease pathogenesis and therapy

Pyroptosis is a pro-inflammatory form of [[programmed cell death]] first named in 2001. It is characterized by plasma membrane pore formation, cell swelling and lysis, and release of inflammatory contents. Canonical activation through caspase-1 and noncanonical activation through caspase-4, caspase-5, or caspase-11 can both drive this process, with [[Gasdermin D|GSDMD]] acting as a key pore-forming executor. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:43-46, a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:88-104]

Pyroptosis has a dual role in disease. As part of innate immunity, it helps remove infected cells and supports inflammasome-dependent cytokine secretion and antimicrobial defense. However, excessive or inappropriate pyroptosis can amplify local and systemic inflammation, damage tissues and organs, and contribute to organ failure, linking it to the pathogenesis of many diseases. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:105-117, a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:830-846]

## Core pathogenic mechanisms

Pyroptosis is mainly mediated by [[inflammasome]] activation, which activates inflammatory caspases including caspase-1. These caspases cleave GSDMD, leading to pore formation in the plasma membrane and release of pro-inflammatory mediators such as IL-1b and IL-18, which trigger inflammatory cascades. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:584-591, a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:646-658]

Inflammasomes are polyprotein complexes composed of pattern recognition receptors, the adaptor ASC, and pro-caspases. Extensively studied pyroptosis-related inflammasomes include [[NLRP3 inflammasome|NLRP3]], NLRP1, NLRC4, and AIM2, with NLRP3 described here as the most concretely studied and relatively well understood. These complexes sense microbial structures and host-derived danger signals, assemble, and promote caspase-1 activation as well as IL-1b and IL-18 maturation. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:592-608, a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:620-637]

The nonclassical pathway is mediated by human caspase-4 and caspase-5 or mouse caspase-11, especially in response to Gram-negative bacterial infection. Cytoplasmic lipopolysaccharide can directly bind and activate caspase-11 independently of TLR4, and these pathways have become a major research focus. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:648-672]

GSDMD is a central downstream mediator of pyroptosis. After cleavage by inflammatory caspases, its N-terminal fragment binds membrane lipids and forms pores about 10-15 nm in diameter, disrupting membrane integrity, enabling IL-1b and IL-18 release, and driving cell swelling and lysis. The source also notes emerging work on pore structure, membrane repair through ESCRT-III, and additional gasdermin family members such as GSDME. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:674-745, a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:746-754]

## Role in disease pathogenesis

The source describes pyroptosis as widely involved in the occurrence and progression of [[infectious disease]]s, metabolic diseases, tumors, nervous system diseases, and [[cardiovascular disease]]s. Disease involvement is attributed to pathogen- or danger signal-induced cell lysis and cytokine release, which can be beneficial in moderation but harmful when excessive. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:830-846]

In infectious disease, pyroptosis contributes to host defense, but excessive inflammasome activation can worsen disease. The source cites coronavirus disease 2019 as an example in which inflammasome activation exacerbates immune dysregulation and can contribute to severe disease; inflammasomes and pyroptosis are therefore considered therapeutic targets in COVID-19. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:110-117]

In nervous system disease, pyroptosis is described as being involved in conditions including Parkinson’s disease, epilepsy, and ischemic stroke. The cited bibliometric discussion also identifies inflammasome/pyroptosis research in the brain as a current and future hotspot, with attention to mitochondrial mechanisms and crosstalk with other regulated cell death pathways in glial cells. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:118-126]

In cardiovascular disease, pyroptosis mediated by inflammasome activation has been linked to atherosclerosis, ischemia-reperfusion injury, and myocardial infarction. The paper also notes that cardiovascular disease is a chronic inflammatory condition, which helps explain why pyroptotic signaling has pathogenic relevance in this setting. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:126-133]

The source also presents disease-specific examples in which pyroptosis appears to promote pathology or serve as a treatment target. Examples include alcoholic liver disease, diabetic nephropathy, food-borne gastroenteritis, and infectious or inflammatory disease models, as well as atherosclerosis and ischemic heart disease models in which suppressing pyroptosis-related signaling showed protective effects. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:927-993]

## Crosstalk with other cell death pathways

Pyroptosis is discussed alongside [[apoptosis]], [[autophagy]], [[necroptosis]], and [[ferroptosis]] as part of the broader landscape of programmed cell death. The source emphasizes that these pathways are interconnected rather than isolated. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:760-769]

Several examples illustrate this crosstalk. In the absence of GSDMD, active caspase-1 can induce apoptosis through caspase-3/7, while caspase-3/7 can cleave and inactivate GSDMD, blocking pyroptosis. Caspase-8 can also activate GSDMD in some pathogen-triggered settings, and has been described as a switch affecting apoptosis, necroptosis, and pyroptosis cell fate. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:770-809]

This crosstalk has therapeutic implications. The source describes GSDME cleavage by caspase-3 as a mechanism by which chemotherapy can induce pyroptosis in normal tissues, contributing to toxic side effects, whereas cancer cells lacking GSDME tend to undergo apoptosis instead. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:810-829]

## Therapeutic strategies

The paper states that regulation of pyroptosis by drugs or other treatments may be protective in various diseases. Current targeted strategies focus on molecules in the pyroptosis pathway, especially the NLRP3 inflammasome, caspase-1, and GSDMD. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:843-858]

GSDMD inhibition is presented as one therapeutic approach. Necrosulfonamide was reported to inhibit inflammatory cell death and sepsis in a model system by targeting a key cysteine residue in GSDMD and disrupting pore formation. Disulfiram, an FDA-approved drug for alcohol addiction, was also reported to inhibit pyroptosis by covalently modifying the same GSDMD cysteine site and blocking membrane pore formation. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:859-869, a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:1333-1339]

Upstream inhibition is another strategy. The source lists several NLRP3 inflammasome inhibitors, including colchicine, OLT1177, and MCC950, which inhibit ATPase activity and prevent NLRP3 assembly and activation. It also notes caspase-1 inhibitors such as VX-765, which have been used in cardiovascular and infectious disease contexts. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:876-881]

The therapeutic picture is mixed. Although VX-765 is described as well tolerated and effective at reducing inflammatory reactions, the source says its clinical effect has been less than expected. More broadly, while many pyroptosis inhibitors have shown effectiveness in animal experiments, the paper states that large-scale clinical data are still lacking for safe dosing and clinical efficacy. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:881-888]

Pyroptosis may also be therapeutically harnessed rather than only suppressed. The source describes cancer as a context in which pyroptosis is a “double-edged sword,” and notes findings that gasdermin agonists could improve the efficacy of cancer immunotherapy and that pyroptosis-related anti-tumor immune effects have become a major topic of interest. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:532-538, a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:847-858]

## Research trends

According to this bibliometric analysis, the main research hotspots in pyroptosis include pathway mechanisms, crosstalk with other forms of programmed cell death, and roles in disease. Frequently occurring keywords included NLRP3 inflammasome, apoptosis, cell death, inflammasome, GSDMD, caspase-1, and inflammation, indicating the main directions of the field. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:33-40, a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:377-397, a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:905-922]

The analysis also indicates that pyroptosis research expanded rapidly after 2017, with disease-focused and gasdermin-focused work becoming particularly prominent. The authors conclude that research on pyroptosis in disease has broad application prospects, while mechanisms, disease roles, and interactions with other programmed cell death pathways are likely to remain central topics. ^[a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:213-215, a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:561-570, a-bibliometric-analysis-of-pyroptosis-from-2001-to-2021.md:919-922]
