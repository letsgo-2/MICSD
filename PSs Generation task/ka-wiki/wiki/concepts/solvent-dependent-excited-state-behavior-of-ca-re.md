# Solvent-dependent excited-state behavior of CA-Re

CA-Re is a rhenium(I) complex identified as `[Re(CO)3(IP-TT)L]PF6`, where the supporting information reports solvent-dependent absorption, emission, transient absorption, and computed excited-state data relevant to its photophysics. The reported measurements compare CA-Re in PBS, dichloromethane (`CH2Cl2`/DCM), and acetonitrile (`CH3CN`), with additional discussion of the ligand [[IP-TT]] and excited-state assignments from theory. ^[2022-su-caix-anchored-rhenium-photosensitizer-evokes-pyroptosis-si.md:156-173, 2022-su-caix-anchored-rhenium-photosensitizer-evokes-pyroptosis-si.md:410-416, 2022-su-caix-anchored-rhenium-photosensitizer-evokes-pyroptosis-si.md:491-507]

## Overview

The source describes a clear solvent dependence in the emissive behavior of CA-Re. In PBS and `CH2Cl2`, CA-Re shows a relatively strong emission near 685-691 nm, while in `CH3CN` that long-wavelength emission disappears and a shorter-wavelength band in the 450-550 nm region becomes more prominent. The authors interpret the long-wavelength, relatively featureless band as arising from a `^3MLCT` state, and the 450-550 nm band as emission from the organic chromophore [[IP-TT]], with the dominant emissive channel determined by solvation effects. ^[2022-su-caix-anchored-rhenium-photosensitizer-evokes-pyroptosis-si.md:410-416, 2022-su-caix-anchored-rhenium-photosensitizer-evokes-pyroptosis-si.md:491-497]

## Spectral behavior by solvent

Reported photophysical data in deaerated solvents show modest shifts in the absorption maximum but much larger changes in emission. CA-Re absorbs at 422 nm in PBS, 410 nm in `CH2Cl2`, and 408 nm in `CH3CN`. Its emission maximum is 685 nm in PBS, 691 nm in `CH2Cl2`, and 493 nm in `CH3CN`, with low reported emission quantum yields in all three media: 0.04% in PBS, 0.03% in `CH2Cl2`, and 0.01% in `CH3CN`. ^[2022-su-caix-anchored-rhenium-photosensitizer-evokes-pyroptosis-si.md:491-497]

This pattern indicates that the solvent has a stronger effect on the nature of the emissive excited state than on the ground-state absorption profile. The source explicitly notes that CA-Re retains both the long-wavelength emission and a weak 450-550 nm band in PBS and `CH2Cl2`, whereas in `CH3CN` the 685 nm band is absent and the shorter-wavelength band becomes dominant. ^[2022-su-caix-anchored-rhenium-photosensitizer-evokes-pyroptosis-si.md:410-416]

## Assignment of the emitting states

The supporting information compares CA-Re with the bidentate ligand [[IP-TT]]. Because IP-TT itself shows a strong featured emission band at 450-550 nm, the authors assign that region in CA-Re to the organic chromophore. They further assign the relatively strong, featureless emission around 685 nm to a `^3MLCT` state of CA-Re. ^[2022-su-caix-anchored-rhenium-photosensitizer-evokes-pyroptosis-si.md:410-416]

The calculated excited-state transitions support a mixed intraligand charge-transfer and metal-to-ligand charge-transfer manifold. The listed singlet absorptions are assigned as `^1ILCT` for `S0→S1` (483.50 nm), `S0→S2` (444.42 nm), and `S0→S3` (396.57 nm), and as `^1MLCT` for `S0→S4` (370.01 nm) and `S0→S5` (365.42 nm). For emission-related states, the table lists `T2→S0` at 650.70 nm as `^3ILCT` and `T7→S0` at 418.04 nm as `^3MLCT`. ^[2022-su-caix-anchored-rhenium-photosensitizer-evokes-pyroptosis-si.md:498-507]

## Transient absorption and modeling context

Transient absorption spectra of CA-Re were measured in deaerated `CH2Cl2` at room temperature following 355 nm pulsed excitation, with differential excited-state absorption monitored from 380 to 700 nm. The supporting information also includes a decay trace monitored at 618 nm, indicating that the excited-state dynamics were experimentally probed in DCM specifically. ^[2022-su-caix-anchored-rhenium-photosensitizer-evokes-pyroptosis-si.md:189-195, 2022-su-caix-anchored-rhenium-photosensitizer-evokes-pyroptosis-si.md:422-424]

The theoretical excited-state calculations were performed with DFT and TDDFT using the PBE0 functional, `6-31G(d)` basis sets for lighter atoms, and an SDD effective core potential for Re. The solvent effect of dichloromethane was included through the SMD solvation model, so the computed orbital and state picture presented in the source is explicitly anchored to the DCM environment. ^[2022-su-caix-anchored-rhenium-photosensitizer-evokes-pyroptosis-si.md:184-188, 424]

## Relation to photosensitizing behavior

The source also evaluates reactive oxygen species generation in different solvents, including singlet oxygen measurements in aerated `CH3CN` and PBS using ABDA as a probe. This places the solvent-dependent excited-state behavior in direct relation to CA-Re’s photosensitizing function, although the provided excerpt does not give numerical singlet oxygen yields in the text body. ^[2022-su-caix-anchored-rhenium-photosensitizer-evokes-pyroptosis-si.md:196-202, 2022-su-caix-anchored-rhenium-photosensitizer-evokes-pyroptosis-si.md:430-433]

## Interpretation

Taken together, the source presents CA-Re as a photosensitizer whose emissive excited-state output changes with solvent: PBS and `CH2Cl2` favor long-wavelength emission attributed by the authors to `^3MLCT`, while `CH3CN` favors a shorter-wavelength band associated with the [[IP-TT]] chromophore. The authors summarize this behavior as a solvation-controlled change in which emissive component dominates. ^[2022-su-caix-anchored-rhenium-photosensitizer-evokes-pyroptosis-si.md:410-416, 2022-su-caix-anchored-rhenium-photosensitizer-evokes-pyroptosis-si.md:491-497]
