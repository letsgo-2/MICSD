# LiPOPs optogenetic toolkit for non-apoptotic cell death control

`LiPOPs` are a set of optogenetic tools introduced to enable photoswitchable control of the non-apoptotic cell death programs [[necroptosis]] and [[pyroptosis]] in live cells with varying kinetics. The study presents them as tools for reconstructing key molecular steps in these pathways with light and also demonstrates their use, when coupled to [[upconversion nanoparticles]] or bioluminescence, for wireless optogenetic or chemo-optogenetic killing of cancer cells in multiple mouse tumor models. ^[optogenetic-control-of-nonapoptotic-cell-death.md:13-23]

## Overview

The rationale for LiPOPs is that apoptosis, necroptosis, and pyroptosis share upstream effectors and exhibit signaling cross-talk, which makes it difficult to specifically commit cells to one death mode using physiological stimuli alone. The authors therefore argue that noninvasive tools with precise specificity are needed for research and therapeutic intervention, and they position optogenetics as a way to achieve spatial and temporal control over these pathways in live cells and tissues. ^[optogenetic-control-of-nonapoptotic-cell-death.md:114-149]

LiPOPs were generated by inserting genetically encoded photosensory modules derived from [[CRY2]] or [[LOV2]] into key proteins involved in necroptosis and pyroptosis. The study uses the term “LiPOPtosis” for this light-driven approach and describes the toolkit as a means to dissect essential steps in non-apoptotic cell death using light rather than conventional receptor-mediated or inflammatory triggers. ^[optogenetic-control-of-nonapoptotic-cell-death.md:137-149]

## Necroptosis control

To reconstruct upstream necroptotic signaling, the authors fused RIPK1 or RIPK3 to the photolyase-homologous region of CRY2, which oligomerizes under blue light. In HeLa cells, light-induced clustering of CRY2-RIPK1 recruited RIPK3 and increased RIPK3 phosphorylation, while light-induced clustering of CRY2-RIPK3 recruited MLKL and increased MLKL phosphorylation, supporting the sequence `RIPK1 -> RIPK3 -> MLKL` as reconstructed optogenetically. ^[optogenetic-control-of-nonapoptotic-cell-death.md:152-206]

The first optimized effector tool, `LiPOP1`, was built from an engineered N-terminal fragment of [[MLKL]] fused to CRY2. After testing multiple truncations and charge-neutralizing mutations to reduce dark toxicity, the authors identified the `H15A/K16A/R17A` triple-mutant variant as having the lowest basal activity while retaining light-triggered plasma membrane perforation; this construct was named LiPOP1. ^[optogenetic-control-of-nonapoptotic-cell-death.md:207-246, optogenetic-control-of-nonapoptotic-cell-death.md:305-307]

In HeLa cells, LiPOP1 translocated from the cytosol to the plasma membrane within seconds after blue light stimulation, was followed by Annexin V staining consistent with phosphatidylserine exposure, and killed nearly all cells within 30 minutes of pulsed blue light illumination. The study also reports pronounced `Ca2+` influx after photostimulation and shows that LiPOP1 bound plasma membrane-enriched phosphoinositides under blue light, consistent with retention of MLKL-like membrane-engaging behavior. ^[optogenetic-control-of-nonapoptotic-cell-death.md:247-278, optogenetic-control-of-nonapoptotic-cell-death.md:309-320]

## Pyroptosis control

For pyroptosis, the authors designed two optogenetic strategies centered on [[GSDMD]]. One strategy, `LiPOP2a`, used the LOVTRAP system so that the pore-forming GSDMD N-terminus and inhibitory GSDMD C-terminus remained associated in the dark and dissociated upon light stimulation. The second strategy, `LiPOP2b`, fused an engineered GSDMD N-terminal fragment to circularly permuted LOV2 so that light-dependent conformational change uncaged pore-forming activity. ^[optogenetic-control-of-nonapoptotic-cell-death.md:473-503, optogenetic-control-of-nonapoptotic-cell-death.md:534-580, optogenetic-control-of-nonapoptotic-cell-death.md:698-705]

In HeLa cells, LiPOP2a showed minimal cell death in the dark and increased double-positive Annexin V and SYTOX Blue staining after illumination, with time-lapse imaging showing pyroptotic death within 8 hours and LDH release increasing over time. For LiPOP2b, the authors identified a `R138A/K146A/R152A/R154A` mutant with low dark activity but restored cytotoxicity in the lit state, and they validated light-inducible pyroptosis by imaging and LDH release assays. ^[optogenetic-control-of-nonapoptotic-cell-death.md:514-533, optogenetic-control-of-nonapoptotic-cell-death.md:555-580]

A lipid-binding assay further showed light-dependent interaction of LiPOP2b with plasma membrane-enriched phosphoinositides, with additional interaction reported for `PI3P` and `PI4P` under blue light. The authors interpret these constructs as recapitulating key signaling steps of pyroptosis through either inducible release from autoinhibition or photo-inducible uncaging of the pore-forming gasdermin domain. ^[optogenetic-control-of-nonapoptotic-cell-death.md:567-580]

## Wireless activation strategies

Because blue light has limited tissue penetration, the study extends LiPOPs with two wireless activation approaches. In one approach, LiPOP1 was combined with [[upconversion nanoparticles]] that convert `980 nm` near-infrared light into blue emission, allowing indirect activation in vivo. In a HeLa xenograft model, intratumoral UCNP injection followed by pulsed NIR illumination produced blue emission at the tumor site and reduced tumor size in the LiPOP1 group relative to control. ^[optogenetic-control-of-nonapoptotic-cell-death.md:279-360, optogenetic-control-of-nonapoptotic-cell-death.md:326-359]

In the second approach, the authors created a `NanoLOGS` platform by fusing NanoLuc to LiPOP1 so that luciferase-generated bioluminescence could activate CRY2. In cultured tumor cells, furimazine induced LiPOP1-NanoLuc membrane translocation and necroptosis, and the improved substrate fluorofurimazine (`FFz`) provided longer-lasting and brighter luminescence with reported good biocompatibility in mice. In a 786-O xenograft model, repeated intratumoral or systemic FFz-triggered activation of LiPOP1-NanoLuc was associated with reduced tumor size compared with control. ^[optogenetic-control-of-nonapoptotic-cell-death.md:361-472, optogenetic-control-of-nonapoptotic-cell-death.md:585-607, optogenetic-control-of-nonapoptotic-cell-death.md:988-1019]

## Demonstrated applications

Beyond tumor-cell killing, the study tested LiPOP2b as a genetically encoded bactericide. In `BL21(DE3)` *E. coli*, IPTG-induced expression of LiPOP2b reduced colony-forming units and blocked growth in liquid culture under blue light, whereas the control showed no light-dependent effect. ^[optogenetic-control-of-nonapoptotic-cell-death.md:581-635, optogenetic-control-of-nonapoptotic-cell-death.md:826-829, optogenetic-control-of-nonapoptotic-cell-death.md:973-987]

The authors also evaluated `LiPOP2a` and `LiPOP2b` as light-inducible suicide genes in Jurkat [[CAR T-cell]] models. Blue light induced pyroptotic morphology, LDH release, and about fourfold to fivefold higher cell death within 6 hours in LiPOP2-expressing cells, while controls showed no significant light-inducible effect. ^[optogenetic-control-of-nonapoptotic-cell-death.md:636-671, optogenetic-control-of-nonapoptotic-cell-death.md:830-836]

## Significance and proposed uses

In the discussion, the authors state that LiPOPs reconstruct necroptotic and pyroptotic signaling without co-activating apoptotic signaling that may accompany pathophysiological stimuli. They further argue that, compared with chemically inducible suicide systems such as inducible caspase-9 and HSV-TK, LiPOPs offer higher temporal and spatial resolution together with rapid activation kinetics. ^[optogenetic-control-of-nonapoptotic-cell-death.md:672-683]

The paper proposes that LiPOPs could be useful for mechanistic studies of how necroptosis and pyroptosis reshape the tumor microenvironment and anti-tumor immunity, for selective elimination of therapeutic cells after adoptive transfer, and for light-triggered bacterial killing. It also notes two strategies for extending use to deeper tissues: NIR-activated UCNP coupling and bioluminescence-based NanoLOGS, with the latter trading some spatial resolution for broader tissue reach. ^[optogenetic-control-of-nonapoptotic-cell-death.md:729-763]
