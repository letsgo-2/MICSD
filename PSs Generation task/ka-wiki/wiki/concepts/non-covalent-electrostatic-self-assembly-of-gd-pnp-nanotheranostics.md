# Non-covalent electrostatic self-assembly of Gd-PNP nanotheranostics

Non-covalent electrostatic self-assembly of Gd-PNP nanotheranostics is the preparation strategy used to build [[Gd-PNPs]] as supramolecular [[nanotheranostics]] for [[fluorescence imaging]], [[magnetic resonance imaging]], and [[photodynamic therapy]]. In the source study, this approach was developed to encapsulate gadolinium porphyrin (`Gd-TCPP`) into homogeneous nanoparticles through electrostatic interactions rather than covalent synthesis, with the goal of improving theranostic performance while keeping the preparation route simple. ^[d1nr04489c-110.md:19-30, d1nr04489c-110.md:117-126]

## Overview

The study positions this assembly strategy within the broader need for theranostic platforms that combine treatment and imaging guidance for more precise cancer management. It specifically notes that [[photodynamic therapy]] benefits from imaging guidance, and that [[FL/MR dual-modal imaging]] is attractive because fluorescence and magnetic resonance provide complementary information for tumor diagnosis and boundary detection. ^[d1nr04489c-110.md:52-77]

The authors further describe [[metalloporphyrin]] molecules as useful theranostic building blocks because they can function as photosensitizers and fluorescence probes, while the central metal can tune their properties. In this context, `Gd3+`-porphyrin is highlighted for strong proton relaxation efficiency in MR imaging, but practical use of metalloporphyrins is limited by poor water solubility and low molecular weight, motivating their integration into nanoparticle systems. ^[d1nr04489c-110.md:100-117]

## Assembly design

The assembly process began with synthesis of `Gd-TCPP` and `mPEG-PGA`. `Gd-TCPP` was prepared by reacting `TCPP` with excess anhydrous `GdCl3` in imidazole at `220 °C` for `2 h`, while `mPEG-PGA` was prepared from `gamma-PGA` and `mPEG5k-NH2` using `EDC/NHS` chemistry in PBS followed by dialysis and freeze-drying. ^[d1nr04489c-110.md:138-166]

To form the nanotheranostics, the negatively charged `Gd-TCPP` was first mixed with positively charged [[PEI|polyethyleneimine (PEI)]], producing supramolecular particles through non-covalent electrostatic interaction-induced self-assembly. After optimization of the `PEI/TCPP` mass ratio and reaction time, negatively charged `mPEG-PGA` was adsorbed onto the particle surface to give the final `Gd-PNPs`. ^[d1nr04489c-110.md:166-173, d1nr04489c-110.md:304-317]

The source states that the outer `mPEG-PGA` layer was added to reduce possible biotoxicity associated with surface charge. After this coating step, the surface potential dropped from `+50.3 mV` to `-0.1 mV`, and the optimized feed mass ratio for `Gd:TCPP:PEI:mPEG-PGA` was `8:4:2:1.6`. ^[d1nr04489c-110.md:309-317]

## Structural and physicochemical features

TEM showed that the resulting `Gd-PNPs` were well-dispersed spherical particles with an average diameter of about `200 nm`, consistent with DLS measurements. XPS detected characteristic gadolinium peaks, confirming the presence of `Gd3+` in the nanoparticles, while elemental analysis indicated a composition including gadolinium, carbon, oxygen, nitrogen, sodium, chlorine, and sulfur. ^[d1nr04489c-110.md:318-338]

According to the XPS and FTIR analyses, the particles had abundant surface amine and carboxyl groups. The paper attributes to these surface groups the observed excellent colloidal stability and good cell-membrane permeability. ^[d1nr04489c-110.md:339-342]

The authors summarize the final nanotheranostic as a layer-by-layer assembly of `Gd-TCPP`, `PEI`, and a `PGA-g-mPEG` shell. They also state that this non-covalent electrostatic strategy simplified synthesis while improving colloidal stability and dispersity. ^[d1nr04489c-110.md:590-600]

## Functional role in theranostics

Within `Gd-PNPs`, `Gd-TCPP` serves three roles: a fluorescence imaging probe, a `T1` MR contrast agent, and a photosensitizer for PDT. The study reports that encapsulation preserved fluorescence behavior without obvious quenching and provided MR contrast with a longitudinal relaxivity (`r1`) of `16.157 mM^-1 s^-1`, which was higher than several commercial `T1` MR agents listed in the paper. ^[d1nr04489c-110.md:344-376, d1nr04489c-110.md:594-597]

The nanotheranostics also retained photodynamic function. The paper reports concentration-dependent singlet oxygen generation, plateauing after `10 min` of laser irradiation, and states that neither gadolinium chelation nor nanoparticle encapsulation obviously reduced PDT efficiency. ^[d1nr04489c-110.md:511-537]

## Reported advantages of the self-assembly strategy

The abstract identifies three main advantages of `Gd-PNPs`: excellent fluorescence imaging, high longitudinal relaxivity, and good singlet oxygen production; excellent long-term colloidal stability, dispersity, and biocompatibility; and improved in vivo FL/MR imaging-guided tumor growth inhibition in `CT 26` tumor-bearing mice. These points summarize the intended outcome of using the non-covalent electrostatic self-assembly route. ^[d1nr04489c-110.md:19-30]

Experimentally, the study reports negligible free `Gd3+` leakage over `48 h`, high cell viability in HeLa and `CT 26` cells even at `400 ug mL^-1`, low hemolysis, stable particle size in FBS for up to `48 h`, no obvious lesions or teratogenesis in zebrafish, and no observable organ damage or inflammation in major organs of treated mice. ^[d1nr04489c-110.md:377-426]

In vivo, `Gd-PNPs` showed time-dependent tumor accumulation, stronger and more persistent fluorescence in tumors than free `Gd-TCPP`, and stronger MR signal enhancement at tumor sites than `Gadobutrol` at the reported peak comparisons. The paper attributes the improved tumor accumulation and longer retention mainly to the [[enhanced permeability and retention effect|EPR effect]]. ^[d1nr04489c-110.md:440-510]

Under `635 nm` laser irradiation, only the `Gd-PNP + laser` treatment group showed marked tumor inhibition in `CT 26` tumor-bearing mice, with reduced tumor volume and weight and extensive apoptosis and necrosis on H&E and TUNEL staining, while body weight remained stable across groups. ^[d1nr04489c-110.md:538-572]

## Significance in the study

The paper presents this non-covalent electrostatic self-assembly method as a simple and robust route for producing multifunctional metalloporphyrin-based nanotheranostics with enhanced theranostic properties. In the authors' conclusion, the resulting platform combined biocompatibility, efficient tumor accumulation, long retention, and support for FL/MR imaging-guided PDT, and the strategy may help inspire design of other multifunctional cancer nanoplatforms. ^[d1nr04489c-110.md:590-609]
