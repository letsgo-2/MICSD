Theoretical calculation
All optimized ground geometries for photodrugs C-TOP, N-TOP, E-TOP, and M-TOP were conducted at
the PBE0/6-31G(d,p) level with the Gaussian 16 Rev. A.03 program using a water continuum model.
Molecular counterions were not considered because the frontier molecular distribution is almost
independent of the anions. Excited-state calculations were performed with the time-dependent DFT using
the same functional and basis set as for the ground-state geometry optimization. Spin-orbit coupling matrix
elements were calculated based on the optimized ground state geometry using the ORCA 5.0 program at
the PBE0 /def2-TZVP level with a water continuum model, using the RIJCOSX approximation with def2/J
auxiliary basis set and “defgrid2” DFT integration with “TightSCF” convergence criteria.

Cell lines
Human glioblastoma cell line (U251-MG), human renal epithelial cell line (HEK293), human uterine
adenocarcinoma cell line (HeLa), human hepatocellular carcinoma cell line (HepG2), human breast
adenocarcinoma cell line (MCF7), mouse clonal carcinoma cell line (CT26), mouse skin melanoma cell
line (B16-F10), and mouse hepatoma cell line (Hepa1-6) were purchased from the American Type Culture
Collection (ATCC). All cell lines were examined for mycoplasma contamination before use. U251-MG,
HEK293, HeLa, HepG2, MCF7, and Hepa1-6 cells were cultured in complete DMEM medium with 10%
fetal bovine serum and 1% penicillin-streptomycin. B16-F10 and CT26 cells were cultured in complete
RPMI-1640 medium with 10% fetal bovine serum and 1% penicillin-streptomycin. According to manual’s
instruction, an additional 2 mM glutamine, 1% non-essential amino acids, and 1 mM sodium pyruvate were
added into DMEM medium to culture the U251-MG cell line.
Cellular uptake
U251-MG cells were seeded at a density of 1×105 cells per well in 24-well plates and incubated overnight
at 37 oC. The culture medium was removed and replenished with fresh medium containing different types
of photodrugs at a fixed concentration of 0.5 μM. Various co-culture times (0-60 min) were kept for time-
dependent cellular uptake studies. Cells were washed three times using DPBS buffer, detached by adding
trypsin-EDTA, and finally harvested by centrifugation at 1000 rpm. Cellular uptake was measured by flow
cytometry (BD LSR FORTESSA).
Intracellular distribution
The intracellular distribution of photodrugs was recorded by CLSM (LSM800, Zeiss, Germany). Briefly,
U251-MG cells were cultured in 35 mm confocal dishes at a density of 1×105 cells per dish and then
incubated with photodrugs at a fixed concentration of 0.5 μM at 37 oC 37 under 5% CO2 atmosphere for
30 min. The cells were then washed three times with DPBS buffer and stained with Hoechst 33342 (2
mg/mL) and Lysotracker Green dye DND 26 (100 nM) for 20 min to obtain colocalization of endo-
/lysosomal internalization. The other cells were stained with Hoechst 33342 (2 mg/mL) and Mitotracker
Green FM dye (100 nM) for 20 min to confirm the distribution of mitochondria. The medium was removed
and washed three more times with DPBS. Finally, cells were rinsed with colorless DMEM (FluoroBrite™

Hoechst), green channel (excitation: 488 nm, Lysotracker or Mitotracker), and far-red channel (excitation:

ROS identification
The ROS indicators DCFH-DA, SOSG, and DHE were selected for general ROS, 1O2, and O2- detection,
respectively. The general ROS production can be confirmed by DCFH-DA with CLSM (excitation: 488
nm, emission: 525 nm) because of the formation of highly fluorescent 2’,7’-dichlorofluorescein from its
non-fluorescent form in the cells. SOSG initially exhibits weak blue fluorescence while in the presence of
1O2, which is insensitive to radicals, it emits green (excitation: 488 nm, emission: 525 nm).
Dihydroethidium (DHE) was used to evaluate cellular superoxide anion radical generation, which can be
converted to 2-hydroxyethidium (2-OH-E+) in the presence of O2- (excitation: 488 nm, emission: 590-620
nm) (Ref. PNAS, 2005, 102, 5727–5732).
Cells cultured in 35 mm confocal dishes were treated with 0.5 μM of photodrugs for 30 min. The
dishes were washed three times with DPBS to remove excess drug. Fresh DMEM medium containing 10%
FBS was added with 10 μM DCFH-DA, 1 μM SOSG, or 10 μM DHE dyes for another 30 min incubation.
The dishes were washed three times with DPBS and finally rinsed in FluoroBrite™ DMEM containing 5%
FBS. CLSM images were captured immediately after light irradiation (1 min exposure, 640 nm LED, 15
mW/cm2).
Flow cytometry assay for identification of apoptosis and pyroptosis
U251-MG cells were seeded at a density of 1×105 cells per well in a 12-well plate and incubated overnight
at 37 oC. The culture medium was then replaced with fresh medium containing M-TOP. After photodrug
treatment, cells were stained with Annexin V-FITC for 20 min and PI for another 10 min according to the
manual’s instructions. Morphological changes were recorded in both phase-contrast and
photoluminescence imaging modes using an inverted fluorescent microscope (EVOS M7000). The
apoptotic and pyroptotic indices were determined by flow cytometry. M-TOP dose dependent cell death (0-
1 μM) was measured with a fixed irradiation duration of 640 nm LED at 15 mW/cm2 for 5 min and flow
cytometry assays were conducted after an additional 3-h incubation following phototreatment. Irradiation
dependent cell death (0-10 min, 15 mW/cm2) was performed with a fixed dosage of M-TOP at 0.25 μM

Western blot and analysis of inflammatory factors
The changes in the expression of caspase-3, cleaved PARP, GSDME, and GAPDH proteins in U251-MG
cells upon photodrug treatment were determined by Western blot. For the assays in Figure 1i, U251-MG
cells were seeded at a density of 5×105 cells per well in a 6-well plate and incubated at 37 oC for 24 h. Cells
with different photodrug treatment (1 μM C-TOP, N-TOP, E-TOP and M-TOP) were washed three times
with DPBS to remove excess drugs, followed by the addition of fresh culture medium and irradiation with
15 mW/cm2 for 5 min. Cells were cultured for another 3 h and supernatant was removed and collected from
the wells for subsequent analysis of inflammatory factors (IL-1β and LDH). The cells were then lysed by
the addition of 100 μL ice-cold RIPA solutions (5X) at 4 oC for 30 min, followed by collection and high-
speed centrifugation (15k rpm) for 30 min. Pierce™ BCA Protein Assay Kits were used to determine the
protein concentration of each cell lysate. Simultaneously, the remaining supernatants were mixed with 1:1
vol% loading buffer and then boiled for 6 min at 95 oC. The lysates were stocked under -20 oC and subjected
to 12% sodium dodecyl sulfate SDS-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) and transferred to
polyvinylidene difluoride (PVDF) membrane (Merck Millipore). The PVDF membrane was blocked with
5 wt% BSA in TBST and shaken at room temperature for one hour. It was then immunoblotted with anti-
caspase-3 antibody (1:2000, Cell Signaling Technology) and shaken overnight at 4 oC. Washed three times
with TBST, horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated secondary antibodies (1:5000, Thermo Fisher) were
incubated with the membrane for one hour at room temperature. Immediately after the 1 min incubation
with ECL substrate, the membrane was placed in an ECL detection system (Bio-Rad, USA) to visualize the
protein bands. Antibodies bound to the membrane were then detached with stripping buffer for 30 min and
re-blocked with BSA solutions. For visualization of other proteins such as C-PARP, GSDME, and GADPH,
Similar procedures were conducted to the membrane. ImageJ software was used for statistical analysis of
individual protein bands.

MTT assays
Cell cytotoxicity was measured using the MTT viability assay. Taking the measurement of U251-MG cells
for example, the cells were seeded in 96-well plates at 1*104 per well and cultured overnight at 37 oC and
5% CO2. The medium was removed and 100 μL of fresh culture medium containing different
concentrations of photodrugs was added. The cells were kept in the dark or irradiated by 640 nm LED (5
min exposure, 15 mW/cm2), followed by incubation for another 2 or 24 h. Subsequently, 10 μL of MTT
reagent (5 mg/mL) was added to each well and the plate was kept in the incubator for another 4 h. After
removing the supernatant, 80 μL of DMSO (biological grade, Sigma-Aldrich) was added to each well. The
absorbance at 570 nm was recorded using a microplate reader. The procedures are the same for cytotoxicity
tests with other types of cells.
Effects of M-TOP on dendritic cell maturation
Briefly, immature dendritic cells (DC2.4 cells) were seeded in 24-well plates and co-cultured for 24 h with
the supernatants after photodrug treatments. The DC cells were then collected and incubated with anti-
mouse CD40 and anti-mouse CD86 on ice for 30 min. The expression level of CD40 and CD86 were
determined by a Flow cytometer. The release of TNF-α and IL-1β cytokines in the DC supernatants was
measured with ELISA kits according to the manual’s instruction.
Animals
All animal experiments were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Treatment Committee of Fudan
University, Shanghai, China (Protocol No.: #202203007S). Female C57BL6 mice aged 6 weeks were kept
in a specific pathogen-free (SPF) animal room and fed with sterile water and food. The room was well
ventilated and had a specific humidity at 25 oC. All mice for each group were randomly divided.
Antitumor effect against subcutaneous xenograft carcinoma
For the subcutaneous carcinoma model, 6-week-old female C57BL6 mice were used. To assess the
photodynamic therapeutic effect against subcutaneous carcinoma, 1×106 Hepa1-6 cells in 50 μL of PBS

proceeded after 7 days of tumor growth when the tumor volume reached 100 mm3. The photodrug M-TOP

expression of specific proteins such as HMGB1 and CRT was detected using a fluorescence microscope.
Antitumor effect against subcutaneous xenograft distal carcinoma
To establish the subcutaneous xenograft model for distal carcinoma, 1×106 Hepa1-6 cells in 50 μL of PBS
were injected into the left or right flank of mice, respectively. The mice were randomly divided into four
groups (n=8 for each group). Photodynamic therapy was performed after 7 days of tumor growth when the
tumor volume reached 100 mm3 in both flanks. Intratumoral injection of photodrugs and laser irradiation
(same dosage and exposure as above) were performed only on the tumor transplanted in left flank, while
the right tumor remained untreated. The mice were euthanized at the end of treatment (60 days) by
inhalation of carbon dioxide. The volume of tumors on both flanks was monitored every 6 days during
therapy. The weight and photos of the tumor on both sides were collected and recorded after each treatment

Antitumor effect against subcutaneous xenograft recurrent carcinoma
The subcutaneous xenograft recurrent carcinoma model was created by subcutaneously injecting 1 ×106
Hepa1-6 cells into the left flank of C57BL6 mice. The tumor was surgically removed 90% when it reached
400 mm3 after 15 days of growth (n = 6 for each group). Then photodynamic therapy was performed three
times every 6 days. Tumor growth and survival of mice were monitored regularly till the end of therapy
(54 days). The tumors of the mice with recurrent xenograft carcinoma were evaluated for the
immunological memory effect by flow cytometry. The release of cytokines IL-6, TNF-α, and IFN-γ in the
serum was determined using an ELISA assay.
Statistical analysis
Results were expressed as mean ± s.d. and data analyses were performed using Prism software (version 8.0,
GraphPad Software). Flow cytometry data were analyzed using the CytExpert and Flow Jo software.
Statistical analysis was performed via a two-tailed Student’s t-test for two-group comparisons and one-way
ANOVA with Tukey’s tests. The p value was denoted with * for p < 0.05, ** for p < 0.01, *** for p < 0.001.
