A B S T R A C T
Background: Aesculin (AES), an effective component of Cortex fraxini, is a hydroxycoumarin glucoside that has
diverse biological properties. The nucleotide-binding domain leucine-rich repeat-containing receptor, pyrin
domain-containing 3 (NLRP3) inflammasome has been heavily interwoven with the development of myocardial
ischemia/reperfusion injury (MIRI). Nevertheless, it remains unclear whether AES makes a difference to the
changes of the NLRP3 inflammasome in MIRI.
Purpose: We used rats that were subjected to MIRI and neonatal rat cardiomyocytes (NRCMs) that underwent
oxygen-glucose deprivation/restoration (OGD/R) process to investigate what impacts AES exerts on MIRI and the
NLRP3 inflammasome activation.
Methods: The establishment of MIRI model in rats was conducted using the left anterior descending coronary
artery ligation for 0.5 h ischemia and then untying the knot for 4 h of reperfusion. After reperfusion, AES were
administered intraperitoneally using 10 and 30 mg/kg doses. We evaluated the development of reperfusion
ventricular arrhythmias, hemodynamic changes, infarct size, and the biomarkers in myocardial injury. The in-
flammatory mediators and pyroptosis were also assessed. AES at the concentrations of 1, 3, and 10 μM were
imposed on the NRCMs immediately before the restoration process. We also determined the cell viability and cell
death in the NRCMs exposed to OGD/R insult. Furthermore, we also analyzed the levels of proteins that affect the
NLRP3 inflammasome activation, pyroptosis, and the AKT serine/threonine kinase (Akt)/glycogen synthase
kinase 3 beta (GSK3β)/nuclear factor-kappa B (NF-κB) signaling pathway via western blotting.
Results: We found that AES notably attenuated reperfusion arrhythmias and myocardia damage, improved the
hemodynamic function, and ameliorated the inflammatory response and pyroptosis of cardiomyocytes in rats and
NRCMs. Additionally, AES reduced the NLRP3 inflammasome activation in rats and NRCMs. AES also enhanced
the phosphorylation of Akt and GSK3β, while suppressing the phosphorylation of NF-κB. Moreover, the allosteric
Akt inhibitor, MK-2206, abolished the AES-mediated cardioprotection and the NLRP3 inflammasome
suppression.

Introduction
Although great advances have been made in the strategies for the
control of ischemic heart disease, myocardial ischemia/reperfusion
injury (MIRI) continues to be a serious medical condition that is
concomitant with blood reperfusion and reoxygenation following an
ischemic episode (Chen et al., 2020; Frank et al., 2012). MIRI can in-
crease the myocardial infarct size and exacerbate the cardiac dysfunc-
tion, thereby reducing the beneficial effects of reperfusion therapies,
including surgeries and use of potent drugs. The detailed pathophysi-
ology of MIRI has not yet been understood, and there is no effective
therapy to successfully control and prevent MIRI. However, it has been
widely reported that MIRI is mainly characterized by the death .of car-
diomyocytes, oxidative damage, mitochondrial dysfunction, and aber-
rant immunoinflammatory responses (Neri et al., 2017).
The nucleotide-binding domain, leucine-rich repeat-containing re-
ceptor (NLR) proteins, including NLR family, pyrin domain-containing
(NLRP) 1 (NLRP1), NLRP3, and NLR family, CARD-containing (NLRC)
4 (NLRC4), are a family of intracellular pathogen and danger signal
sensors and considered to be a part of the effector molecules of innate
immunity (Toldo et al., 2018). They can recruit the apoptosis-associated
speck-like protein containing CARD and the effector procaspase-1 to
assemble a group of multimeric protein complexes called inflamma-
somes. The activated inflammasome can further induce the active

caspase-1, leading to pyroptosis, which represents an inflammatory form
of programmed cell death, featuring with pore formation in the plasma
membrane, discharge of interleukin (IL)-1β and IL-18, and cell lysis (Lu
et al., 2020; Tsuchiya, 2020; Wang et al., 2019). There is emerging ev-
idence that the NLRP3 inflammasome-mediated pyroptosis are widely
implicated in the pathophysiology of MIRI (Qiu et al., 2017; Yao et al.,
2020). Anti-inflammation, especially via the inactivation of the NLRP3
inflammasome, has gained attention as a promising therapeutic strategy
to ameliorate MIRI (Davidson et al., 2019).
Aesculin (AES, Fig. 1A) is a hydroxycoumarin glucoside that is found
in many plants, particularly Fraxinus rhynchophylla Hance, whose dried
bark, known as Cortex fraxini, is extensively prescribed to treat diarrhea,
arthritis, leucorrhea, and gout in traditional Chinese medicine (Kutsu-
zawa, 1955; Tian et al., 2019; Zheng et al., 2015). AES has been iden-
tified as the main effective constituent of C. fraxini and is closely linked
with multiple pharmacological activities, including bacteriostasis and
anti-inflammatory, antitumor, antioxidative, antithrombotic, and anti-
apoptotic activities (Ahmad et al., 2018; Biljali et al., 2012; Mok-
dad-Bzeouich et al., 2016; Wang et al., 2015; Zheng et al., 2015).
Whereas, there is very little information available regarding what po-
tential impacts AES exerts on MIRI. Moreover, it remains ill-defined
whether AES has a profound impact on the NLRP3 inflammasome acti-
vation in MIRI.
In this study, we carried out in vivo and in vitro experiments to judge
the potential therapeutic use of AES using the MIRI-injured rats and the
oxygen-glucose deprivation/restoration (OGD/R)-injured neonatal rat
cardiomyocytes (NRCMs), and if so, to determine whether suppressing
the NLRP3 inflammasome-mediated pyroptosis was associated with the
AES-induced cardioprotection.
Materials and methods
Materials
The test compound AES (purity > 97%), the calcium channel blocker
nifedipine, and the allosteric Akt inhibitor MK-2206 were supplied by
MedChemExpress (Beijing, China). They were dissolved in dimethyl
sulfoxide (DMSO, Sigma-Aldrich, MO, USA) and freshly diluted with 5%
Tween-80 (Sigma-Aldrich) in saline to the appropriate concentrations.
The final concentration of DMSO was maintained at no more than 0.1%
in both the rats and NRCMs.
Animals, grouping, and treatment
A total of 60 male Sprague-Dawley rats (240–280 g body weight)
were provided by Beijing HuaFuKang Biotechnology (Beijing, China).
They were kept under controlled environmental conditions and fed with
commercial food along with tap water. The research protocol was
reviewed and authorized by the Laboratory Animal Management and
Welfare Ethics Committee, and all the animals were handled following
Guidance on the Care of Laboratory Animals.
Rats were divided in the sham-operated rats (sham) and the MIRI-
injured rats. The MIRI model in rats was built by tying and then unty-
ing the left anterior descending coronary artery (LAD), as described in
our prior report (Wang et al., 2020). Rats who underwent successful
MIRI surgeries were randomly allocated to four groups: rats with MIRI
receiving the vehicle (MIRI), rats with MIRI receiving 10 mg/kg AES
(MIRI + AES10), rats with MIRI receiving 30 mg/kg AES (MIRI +
AES30), and rats with MIRI receiving 10 mg/kg nifedipine (MIRI +
NIF10). When reperfusion was implemented by untying the knot
following 0.5 h of ischemia, AES or nifedipine was intraperitoneally
injected, respectively.
Assessment of the development of reperfusion arrhythmias
The traces of lead II electrocardiogram (ECG) were continuously
monitored for 30 min following reperfusion using the BL-420S data
collection and processing system (Chengdu TME Technology, Chengdu,
China). Moreover, the reperfusion ventricular arrhythmias during the
first 30 min of reperfusion were analyzed and scored in the light of the
following grading system (Curtis and Walker, 1988; Sun et al., 2014):
0 (ventricular premature beats (VPBs) < 10); 1 (VPBs ≥ 10); 2 (ven-
tricular tachycardia (VT) < 5); 3 (VT > 5 or ventricular fibrillation (VF)
= 1); 4 (VF = 2–5); 5 (VF > 5); 6 (length of time of VT and VF ≤ 300 s);
and 7 (length of time of VT and VF > 300 s).
Determination of hemodynamic changes
To evaluate the hemodynamic changes, an SPR-838 Millar pressure-
volume catheter (Millar Instruments, TX, USA) interfaced with a Pow-
erLab 8/35 data acquisition system (ADInstruments, Shanghai, China)
was retrogradely introduced into the left ventricle to continuously re-
cord the left ventricular pressure (LVP), based on which the maximum
rate of increase in LVP (+dp/dtmax) as well as the maximum rate of
decrease in LVP (-dp/dtmax) was calculated. The LV systolic pressure
(LVSP) and LV end-diastolic pressure (LVEDP) were also compared.
Assessment of infarct size
A suture kept on the LAD was tied again, and 1% Evans blue solution
(Sigma-Aldrich) was retrogradely perfused via the aortic cannulation
before harvesting the heart. Following freezing for 20 min, the heart was
transversely sliced into sections (1-2 mm in thickness) that were sub-
sequently immersed in the phosphate-buffered 1% 2,3,5-triphenyltetra-
zolium chloride (TTC, Sigma-Aldrich) solution for 15 min at 37 ◦C. After
photographing, a proficient experimental operator measured the area of
the left ventricular wall (ALV), area at risk (AAR), and infarcted area
(INF) to calculate the percentages of AAR/ALV and INF/AAR.
Myocardial injury biomarkers assay
The sera were isolated from the blood samples, and the levels of
lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) and aspartate transaminase (AST) were
detected via commercial kits (Biosino Bio-Technology and Science,
Beijing, China) based on the spectrophotometric assays for biological
activities. Moreover, we determined the level of cardiac troponin T
(cTnT) via a commercial enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)
kit (Cusabio Technology, Wuhan, China).
Measurement of myocardial inflammatory mediators
The heart tissue was homogenized in the phosphate-buffered saline,
and the supernatant was isolated via centrifugation for 5 minutes at
5000 g, 2–8 ◦C. Commercial ELISA kits (Cusabio Technology) were used
to examine the IL-1β, IL-18, and tumor necrosis factor alpha (TNFα)
levels.
Caspase-1 activity assay
A fluorometric kit (Abcam, Shanghai, China) was applied to assay
caspase-1 activities in the heart homogenates. Upon cleavage of the
fluorogenic substrate by caspase-1, a free fluorescent product 7-amino-
4-trifluoromethyl coumarin (AFC) with a maximum emission wave-
length of 505 nm was released. The activity of caspase-1 was determined
by comparing the fold increase in fluorescence with that of the control.
Histopathological analysis
To evaluate the histological changes, the paraffin-embedded sections
(5 μm) were sliced from the fixed heart apex tissues, and successively
stained with 0.1% hematoxylin and 0.5% eosin. A qualified investigator,
who was blinded to the criteria used for group allocation, checked the

Primary culture of NRCMs

OGD/R treatment

induce oxygen-glucose deprivation (OGD) for 120 min in NRCMs using
an airtight container filled with a mixture of 5% carbon dioxide (CO2)
and 95% nitrogen (N2). Thereafter, the NRCMs were incubated for 4 h in
a complete culture medium under 5% CO2 and 95% air. Then, AES (at
final concentrations of 1, 3, and 10 μM) and MK-2206 (at a concentra-
tion of 3 μM) were administered when the restoration process started
(Fig. 5 A).

Quantitative data are expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD).

quartile ranges and compared using the Kruskal-Wallis test. Statistical
significance was set at p < 0.05.
Results
AES mitigated reperfusion ventricular arrhythmias and improved the
hemodynamic function in the rats with MIRI
ECG analysis showed that, during the early 30 min period of reper-
fusion, the rats with MIRI exhibited severe reperfusion ventricular ar-
rhythmias, in marked contrast to the sham-operated rats. (Fig. 1C). In
contrast to the MIRI group, the MIRI + AES30 and MIRI + NIF10 groups
showed noticeable improvement in ventricular arrhythmia (p < 0.05).
Hemodynamic dysfunction occurred widely in the rats with MIRI, as
indicated by the significant drop in LVSP, +dp/dtmax, and -dp/dtmax
along with increased LVEDP (Fig. 1D-G). However, there were signifi-
cant improvement for the +dp/dtmax, and -dp/dtmax, and LVEDP values
in the MIRI + AES30 and MIRI + NIF10 groups. In comparison with the
rats with MIRI, the rats treated with 10 mg/kg AES also revealed a
notable rise in the -dp/dtmax values and a significant decline in the
LVEDP (p < 0.05).

Similar to the increased leukocyte infiltration shown in Fig. 2C,
ELISA results of the heart homogenates from the rats with MIRI sug-
gested a dramatic rise in the levels of the inflammation mediators, such
as IL-1β, IL-18, and TNFα (Fig. 3A), indicating the occurrence of
inflammation in the myocardium following the MIRI insult. However,
the rats from the MIRI + AES30 group exhibited lower levels of

also showed a notable decrease in IL-18 level (p < 0.05). Moreover, it
was revealed using western blotting assay that the MIRI-injured rats had
markedly higher myocardial NLRP3 level along with cleaved caspase-1
and cleaved IL-1β, as the downstream effectors of the active NLRP3
inflammasome, compared to the sham-operated rats, indicating a heavy
involvement by the activated NLRP3 inflammasome in the development
of MIRI (Fig. 3B). Conversely, the rats from the MIRI + AES30 group
presented a steep decline in myocardial NLRP3, cleaved caspase-1, and
cleaved IL-1β (p < 0.01). Additionally, there was a notable decrease in
the cleaved caspase-1 level in the rats from the MIRI + AES10 group (p <
0.05).

to the NRCMs exposed to OGD/R, the cell injuries resulting from the
OGD/R insult were expressively improved in the NRCMs treated with 3
μM and 10 μM AES (p < 0.01).
AES modulated the Akt/GSK3β/NF-κB pathway to attenuate the NLRP3
inflammasome activation in OGD/R-insulted NRCMs
As previously observed in the rats with MIRI, the protein levels of
NLRP3, together with the active fragments of caspase-1, IL-1β, and
GSDMD, elevated notably in the OGD/R-treated NRCMs in comparison
with those of the normal NRCMs, implying that the NLRP3 inflamma-
some was activated during the OGD/R insult (Fig. 6A). Conversely, AES
at concentrations of 3 and 10 μM distinctly reduced their levels in the
OGD/R-treated NRCMs (p < 0.05, p < 0.01).
Subsequently, we also checked whether the Akt/GSK3β/NF-κB
pathway played an important part in the NLRP3 inflammasome inhibi-
tion induced by AES. Western blotting analysis revealed that the OGD/
R-insulted NRCMs exhibited a noticeable rise in the phospho-NF-κB p65
(Ser536) level, suggesting the upregulated activity of NF-κB p65, which
diminished following 3 or 10 μM AES administration (Fig. 6D). How-
ever, OGD/R exposure decreased the level of phospho-GSK3β (Ser9)
(Fig. 6C), which represents an inactive form of GSK3β that prevents NF-
κB p65 Ser536 phosphorylation as well as its activation and trans-

Discussion
Emerging evidence has demonstrated that the mortality rate of acute
myocardial infarction has been rapidly increasing worldwide, especially
in China (Yan et al., 2017). MIRI has been shown to affect the clinical
efficacy and long-term prognosis in acute myocardial infarction. To
date, vast efforts have been made to resolve the urgent need for effica-
cious strategies to curb MIRI. Chinese herbal medicines have been
clinically verified as alternative therapies against MIRI. As one of main
effective ingredients of C. fraxini, AES has been described to improve the
endotoxin shock (Li et al., 2016), modulate the bone metabolism (Zhao
et al., 2017), reduce tumor cells proliferation (Mo et al., 2020; Mok-
dad-Bzeouich et al., 2016), and protect against diabetic nephropathy
(Serralha et al., 2020), colitis (Tian et al., 2019; Yang et al., 2017), acute
liver injury (Liu et al., 2018), and gastric lesions (Li et al., 2017). Our
study is the first to demonstrate that AES potently attenuated MIRI in the
rats and cultured NRCMs. Further analysis showed that AES inhibited
the NLRP3 inflammasome and cardiomyocyte pyroptosis in the rats and
NRCMs with MIRI. An in vitro investigation revealed that AES promoted
Akt phosphorylation and suppressed the GSK3β/NF-κB pathway.
Consequently, our study addressed that AES can be a potential drug
candidate to treat MIRI.
In the experimental study on the rats with MIRI, nifedipine, a cal-
cium channel blocker used to treat coronary artery diseases, was chosen
as a positive drug to verify the AES-mediated effects. In conformity to
the previous studies (Chen et al., 2016; Huang et al., 2009; Xu et al.,
2020), our results demonstrated that nifedipine alleviated the reperfu-
sion arrhythmias and myocardial damage, while improving the cardiac
diastolic function. We also compared the protective effects of AES and
nifedipine in MIRI. It was observed that the improvement of some as-
pects, including the arrhythmia score and hemodynamic parameters, in
the rats administered with 10 mg/kg AES was decreased compared to
those in the rats administered with 10 mg/kg nifedipine. However, the
group that underwent 30 mg/kg AES treatment showed no remarkable
difference in the reperfusion arrhythmias, myocardia damage, and he-
modynamic function from the nifedipine-treated group. These results
confirmed the potent cardioprotective effects of AES in the rats exposed
to MIRI.
Many studies have reported that myocardial damage is closely
related to the activated NLRP3 inflammasome, and the inflammation
and pyroptosis contribute to the development of MIRI (Xu et al., 2020;
Xu et al., 2018). Pyroptosis is characterized by the cleavage of caspases
and gasdermins, membrane pore formation and membrane rupture, and
proinflammatory cytokines discharge, in which the NLRP3 inflamma-
some acts a pivotal part (Xu et al., 2018). During pyroptosis, inflam-
matory caspases, including caspase-1, are responsible for cleaving and
activating the effector protein GSDMD, which is responsible for forming

studies in various models of inflammatory diseases have manifested that
AES as a potent modulator of inflammation has attracted intense
attention (Niu et al., 2015; Tian et al., 2019; Tianzhu and Shumin, 2015;

study has illustrated that AES treatment suppressed the NLRP3 inflam-
masome and restricted the inflammatory response, as evidenced by the
finding that levels of active caspase-1, truncated GSDMD, IL-1β, and
IL-18 fell sharply. By contrast, nifedipine exerted some unstable in-

caspase-1 and GSDMD, suggesting that the modulation of inflamma-
tion may not be responsible for the nifedipine-mediated car-
dioprotection in rats.
It has been revealed in a few studies that AES could boost the
phosphorylation of Akt and GSK3β (Kang et al., 2014; Mo et al., 2019).
The activated protein kinase Akt then phosphorylates GSK3β at Ser9 and
deactivates it (Zhai et al., 2017). This inactive form of GSK3β (phos-
phorylated at Ser9) later prevents the NF-κB p65 Ser536 phosphoryla-
tion, which stimulates NF-κB p65 to its active stage. Active NF-κB p65
translocates to cell nucleus showing the upregulated transcriptional
activity 	and 	prompts 	the 	expression 	of 	the 	NLRP3
inflammasome-associated modules, such as NLRP3, pro-IL-1β, and
pro-IL-18. Moreover, it has been reported that the protective effects of
AES against inflammation-related diseases might result from its control
of the NF-κB pathway (Tianzhu and Shumin, 2015; Zhao et al., 2017).
Our study on cultured NRCMs showed that AES remarkably promoted
the phosphorylation of Akt and GSK3β at Ser473 and Ser9, respectively,
while suppressing the phosphorylation of NF-κB p65 at Ser536, thereby
alleviating the NLRP3 inflammasome activation. Moreover, MK-2206,
an allosteric Akt inhibitor, abolished the AES-mediated protective ef-
fects on the OGD/R-treated NRCMs and NLRP3 inflammasome sup-
pression. Therefore, the present study has illustrated that the
AES-mediated cardioprotection and NLRP3 inflammasome suppression
might be heavily reliant upon the hyperactivation of Akt and the sub-
sequent disruption of the GSK3β/NF-κB pathway (Fig. 7).
Conclusion
Taken together, our findings manifested that AES alleviated MIRI
and suppressed the NLRP3 inflammasome-mediated pyroptosis. In
addition, the AES-mediated cardioprotective effects were found to be
attributable to the upregulated Akt activation and the restraint of the
GSK3β/NF-κB pathway.
