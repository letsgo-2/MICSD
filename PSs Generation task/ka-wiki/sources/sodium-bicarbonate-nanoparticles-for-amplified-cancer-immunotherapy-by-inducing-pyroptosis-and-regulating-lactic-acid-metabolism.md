Abstract: Although immunotherapy has a broad clinical application
prospect, it is still hindered by low immune responses and
immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment. Herein, a simple and
drug-free inorganic nanomaterial, alkalescent sodium bicarbonate
nanoparticles (NaHCO3 NPs) are prepared via a fast microemulsion
method for amplified cancer immunotherapy. The obtained
alkalescent NaHCO3 regulates lactic acid metabolism through acid-
base neutralization, so as to reverse the immunosuppressive tumor
mildly acidic environment. Additionally, it can further release high
amounts of Na+ ions inside tumor cells and induce a surge in
intracellular osmolarity, and thus to activate the pyroptosis pathway
and immunogenic cell death (ICD), release a mass of damage-
associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) and inflammatory factors,
and improve immune responses. Collectively, NaHCO3 NPs
observably inhibit primary/distal tumor growth and tumor metastasis
through acid neutralization remitted immunosuppression and
pyroptosis induced immune activation, showing an enhanced
antitumor immunity efficiency. This work provides a new paradigm
for lactic acid metabolism and pyroptosis mediated tumor treatment,
which has a great potential to be applied in clinical tumor
immunotherapy.
Introduction
Immunotherapy, represented by immune checkpoint block (ICB)
therapy, is the fourth major clinical cancer therapy strategies
after surgery, chemotherapy and radiotherapy, and has a broad
clinical application prospect, but it is still hindered by low
immune responses and immunosuppressive tumor
microenvironment (TME).[1] Metabolic reprogramming is an
important means of immune escape of tumor cells, such as the
famous "Warburg effect", that is, tumor cells metabolize a large
amount of glucose to produce lactic acid through glycolysis even
when oxygen is sufficient.[2] Lactic acid accumulated in TME not
only promotes tumor progression, but also is metabolized as
energy source to induce tumor angiogenesis, invasion and
metastasis.[3] In addition, an increasing number of studies
demonstrated that lactic acid in TME was able to inhibit the
immune response of body through various ways, greatly limiting
the efficacy of tumor immunotherapy.[4] For example, lactic acid
can promote the M2-type polarization of tumor-related
macrophages to promote tumor growth through inducing the
expression of vascular endothelial growth factor and arginase;[5]
it inhibits the antigen presentation process and mature
differentiation of dendritic cells (DCs);[6] it also restrains the
excretion of lactic acid from cytotoxic T cells, thus inhibiting their
cytotoxicity.[7] What’s more, the recent report indicated that
tumor cells supply regulatory T cells (Treg) with lactic acid to
counteract the killing effect of effector T cells.[8] In short, lactic
acid, as a glycolytic metabolite, plays an important role in
maintaining immunosuppressive TME and targeting lactic acid
metabolism may become a new and effective cancer therapy
strategy.[9]
Tumor immunotherapy is severely hindered by not only the
immunosuppressed TME, but also the low immune response in
traditional apoptosis mediated tumor therapy, which is generally
considered to be nonimmunogenic due to the slow or even no
release of damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs).[10]
Unlike apoptosis, pyroptosis is a programmed inflammatory cell
death that is activated by caspase and relies on gasdermins
protein family to form pores in cytomembrane, which is often
accompanied by the release of plenty of DAMPs and
antigens.[11] It has been demonstrated that pyroptosis with strong
immunogenicity is able to activate body's immune responses
and initiate tumor immunotherapy effectively.[12] Hence, it is
highly desirable to develop a new drug formulation to induce
pyroptosis and relieve immunosuppressive TME simultaneously
for improving the efficacy of immunotherapy.
In view of two above-mentioned main challenges about
immunotherapy, herein, a simple and drug-free inorganic
nanomaterial, alkalescent sodium bicarbonate nanoparticles
(NaHCO3 NPs) were prepared via a fast microemulsion method
for amplified cancer immunotherapy (Scheme 1). The obtained
alkalescent NaHCO3 regulates lactic acid metabolism through
acid-base neutralization, so as to reverse the
immunosuppressive tumor mildly acidic environment.

Results and Discussion
The Synthesis and Characterization of NaHCO3 NPs. First,
NaHCO3 NPs were prepared via a fast microemulsion method
(10 min) at room temperature using sodium oleate and
ammonium hydrogen carbonate as reagents (Figure 1a). It can
be observed in Figure S1 that the clear solution turned to typical
turbidity after NH4HCO3 aqueous solution added, suggesting the
occurrence of reaction. The reaction solution was further
centrifuged and a white product could be obtained. Transmission
electron microscopy (TEM) images show that the product
possesses spherical morphology with particle sizes of ∼70 nm
(Figure 1b and 1c). The high-resolution TEM (HRTEM) image
indicates a lattice distance of 0.24 nm corresponds to (211)
lattice planes of nahcolite (NaHCO3) (Figure 1d). In addition,
from the X-ray diffraction (XRD) pattern (Figure 1e), all the
diffraction peaks can be well indexed to the pure NaHCO3
(JCPDS no. 15-0700). X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS)
spectra further demonstrate the existence of Na, O and C
elements (Figure 1f and S2) and all these data prove the
successful preparation of NaHCO3 NPs.
Next, to improve the dispersibility and biocompatibility, the
obtained NaHCO3 was modified with DSPE-PEG, verified by
Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectra (Figure 1g). The
obtained NaHCO3 NPs aqueous solution is weakly alkaline, with
the pH values being increased from 7.83 to 8.76 at 12.5-400
μg/mL (Figure 1h), which is similar with free sodium bicarbonate
(SoB) with the pH values being increased from 7.71 to 8.56 at
12.5-400 μg/mL. The character of weakly alkaline from NaHCO3
NPs is conducive to acid-base neutralization with lactic acid
accumulated in TME. Then to study the ion release ability, the
prepared NaHCO3 NPs were dispersed into Tris-HCl buffer
solution (without Na+ ions) with different pH values (7.4 and 6.5).
As presented in Figure 1i, the Na+ ion release rates in both
groups are fast and similar, up to 85% within 24 h. Such a result
suggests that NaHCO3 NPs can serve as an ion reservoir to
release high amounts of ions.

In Vitro Therapy and Lactic Acid Metabolism Regulation.
The characters of ion reservoirs and weakly alkaline of NaHCO3
NPs motivate us to study in vitro therapy effect and acid-base
neutralization ability. In consideration of the high glycolytic
metabolism activity of mouse breast cancer cell line (4T1 cells),
we chose 4T1 cells for the subsequent research.[13] Firstly, to
evaluate in vitro cytotoxicity of NaHCO3 NPs, a standard methyl
thiazolyl tetrazolium (MTT) assay was performed on mouse
fibroblast cell lines (L929 cells) and 4T1 cells. Compared with
free SoB with negligible cell killing effect on both 4T1 and L929
cells (Figure 2a and S3a), a concentration dependent
cytotoxicity was observed in NaHCO3 NPs treatment. As
displayed in Figure 2b, when NaHCO3 NPs concentration is up
to 400 μg/mL, the 4T1 cell viability drops down to under 10%
(IC50: 168 μg/mL). Interestingly, although NaHCO3 NPs show a
degree of cytotoxicity on L929 cells (Figure S3b), the toxic
effect is significantly lower than that from 4T1 cells (IC50 is over
400 μg/mL), which may be caused by the high intracellular ion
concentrations of cancer cells, making them more
impressionable to osmolarity imbalance.[14] This remarkable
difference in toxicity between normal and cancer cells means
NaHCO3 NPs possess a certain degree of selectivity for tumor
therapy. Meanwhile, it is worth noting that NaHCO3 NPs also
show a good biocompatibility on 4T1 cells at low concentrations,
proving a low concentration Na+ ions show negligible toxicity
(when the concentration is 100 μg/mL, 4T1 cell viability is close
to 90%). With the increase of the concentration up to 200 μg/mL,
a sudden drop of cell viability can be found (4T1 cell viability is
down to 36%), which may be attributed to an overdose of Na+
ion induced intracellular osmolarity imbalance
instantaneously.[15] This phenomenon is further confirmed by a
flow cytometry apoptosis assay using a typical Annexin V-FITC
and propidium iodide (PI) co-staining protocol. Figure 2c and S4
show that the percentage of apoptosis after 100 μg/mL of
NaHCO3 NPs treatment is only 15.14%, while it is up to 94.6%
when the concentration goes up to 400 μg/mL, which is also
significantly greater than the control (7.36%) and SoB (8.19%)
groups.
Then we further studied the internal mechanism of favourable
therapeutic effect in vitro. At first, intracellular Na+ ions were
examined by enhanced NaTrium green-2 (ENG-2) as a Na+ ion
probe. As expected, compared with control and SoB groups,
NaHCO3 group shows a significantly improved fluorescence
signal, suggesting NaHCO3 NPs can serve as a Na+ ion
reservoir to achieve intracellular Na+ ion release and osmolarity
rising (Figure S5). A surge in intracellular osmolarity and
homeostasis imbalance further resulted in reactive oxygen
species (ROS) enhancement. As can be seen in Figure 2d, an
obvious green fluorescence from ROS probe 2′,7′-
dichlorofluorescin diacetate (DCFH-DA) was observed in
NaHCO3 NPs, indicating ion overload induced oxidative stress. It
has been reported that effective ion overload is able to active
pyroptosis, a proinflammatory mode of programmed cell death
characterized by cellular swelling with large bubbles.[12a]
Therefore, to study the ability of NaHCO3 NPs to induce
pyroptosis, cell morphology observation was performed.

change, NaHCO3 NPs treated cells show an obvious cellular

Immunogenic cell death (ICD) is a programmed cell death that
causes an immune response via damage associated molecular
patterns (DAMPs) release, such as calreticulin (CRT),
adenosine triphosphatase (ATP) and so on.[17] Except for
pyroptosis, we also found that NaHCO3 NPs could dramatically
induce ICD. As supported by Figure 2i, in the NaHCO3 NPs
group, the relative ATP amount is about 5.9 and 5.5 folds than
that of the control and SoB groups, respectively. Additionally, a
remarkable exposure of CRT was detected in this group (Figure
S12). These DAMPs can further trigger antigen presentation,
and thus play a crucial part in antitumor immunity responses. All
these data suggest that NaHCO3 NPs can not only initiate
pyroptosis to kill tumor cells and promote inflammatory molecule
release, but also lead to ICD and DAMPs secretion.
Next the ability of acid-base neutralization of the obtained
NaHCO3 NPs in vitro was further verified. Intracellular pH after
different treatments was performed firstly by an intracellular pH
indicator (pHrodo™ Green AM), which shows strong green
fluorescence under low pH values. Figure 2j clearly testified that
an attenuated fluorescence was found in NaHCO3 NPs treated
group in comparison with the other two groups, indicating the
intracellular pH increased due to the adding of NaHCO3 NPs. It
is worth mentioning that no obvious fluorescence change was
observed in free SoB group, which may be put down to the low
efficiency of endocytosis. After that, we looked at extracellular
pH changes with time. In view of the influence of external
environment (thermostatic CO2 incubator, 5% CO2 at 37 °C) on
pH, a blank group with culture medium but without 4T1 cells was
set simultaneously. As displayed in Figure 2k, after 4 h
incubation, the pH value of blank group dropped from 7.73 to
7.33, which may cause by the dissolution of CO2 into substrate.
Compared with the blank group, the pH value of control group
(pH4 h=7.29) reduced more because of tumor cell metabolism-
generated acid secretion. On the contrary, higher pH values
were observed in free SoB (pH4 h=7.43) and NaHCO3 NPs (pH4
h=7.69) groups, certifying that the two groups both effectively
inhibited the acidification of tumor cell culture medium within 4 h.
However, after 24 h of cultivation, the pH value of free SoB
dropped down to 6.77, which is close to the control group (pH24
h=6.74) and far below the blank (pH24 h=7.02) and NaHCO3 NPs
(pH24 h=7.00) groups (Figure S13). This is because tumor cells
rapid proliferation and metabolism can generate abundant lactic
acid. Thanks to the high-efficiency tumor growth inhibition and
acid-base neutralization abilities, NaHCO3 NPs effectively
maintained the stabilization of pH values within a longer period
of time. Subsequently, we further measured the lactic acid
contents in culture medium after various treatments (Figure 2l).
Similarly, it can be clearly found that lactic acid was dramatically
reduced in NaHCO3 NPs group, suggesting a great potential in
regulating lactic acid metabolism (Figure S14).

In Vivo Immune Performances. It has been reported that lactic
acid as a glycolytic metabolite, plays an important role in
maintaining immunosuppressive TME and can inhibit the
immune response of body through various ways, greatly limiting
the efficacy of tumor immunotherapy.[18] Besides, plenty of
inflammatory factors and cellular contents release in pyroptosis
and DAMPs secretion in ICD both observably activate immune
responses for tumor immunotherapy.[19] Inspired by NaHCO3
NPs sensitized pyroptosis, ICD induction and lactic acid
consumption (Figure 3a), a model of female BALB/c mice
bearing 4T1 tumors (tumor sizes are about 30-50 mm3) was
established to explore the potential immune performances and
antitumor therapeutic effect in vivo. After triple treatments, the
single-cell suspensions of tumor-draining lymph node (TDLN)
and tumor were obtained on scheduled time and a cell sorting
process about different immune cells was provided in Figure
S15 and S16. The maturation of dendritic cells (DCs) in TDLN
was quantified firstly, which has an important role in antigen
presentation and inducing adaptive antitumor immunity. Figure
3b and S17a show that free SoB activated moderate DCs
maturation (CD11c+CD80+CD86+), which may owe to acid
neutralization remitted immunosuppression.[20] Thanks to the
combined effect of pyroptosis/ICD and lactic acid metabolism
regulation, the highest percentage of DCs maturation was found
in NaHCO3 NPs group. Then tumor infiltrating CD4+ T cells
(CD3+CD4+) and cytotoxic CD8+ T cells (CD3+CD8+) were
emphatically evaluated because of their crucial functions in
cellular immunity.[21] As can be seen in Figure 3c, 3d, S17b and
S17c, CD4+ and CD8+ T lymphocytes in nanomaterial group is
up to 38.5% and 1.3%, which are over 1.6 and 2.3 folds than
those in control group, respectively. All these data provide
strong evidence that the prepared NaHCO3 NPs can recruit
immune cells into tumor to prominently trigger adaptive immune
responses.
As well known, the effect of tumor immunotherapy is also greatly
hindered by immunosuppressive TME. Hence, to further study
the immunological enhancement after NaHCO3 NPs treatments,
some representative immunosuppressive cells were examined
through flow cytometry, such as tumor-associated macrophage
(TAM), myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs) and
regulatory T cells (Treg). As provided in Figure 3e-3h and
S17d-S17g, an obvious macrophage polarization from anti-
inflammatory M2 TAM (from 26.0% to 15.5%) to pro-
inflammatory M1 TAM (from 11.2% to 24.7%) was observed
firstly in experimental group. In addition, compared with the
other two groups, the frequencies of MDSCs and Treg
descended simultaneously with drug-free NaHCO3 NPs
administration. These results clearly suggest NaHCO3 NPs can
not only activate antitumor immunity via the recruitment of
immune cells, but also remit immunosuppressive TME by
reducing multifarious immunosuppressive cells, and thus having
favorable antitumor potentiality.

In Vivo Antitumor Therapy. In the process described above,
the tumor sizes were measured and body weights were
recorded every 2 days at the same time. The control and free
SoB groups both present rapid tumor growth, indicating poor
and negligible antitumor effect (Figure 4a). In contrast, after
NPs injection, a significant inhibition of tumor growth appeared
due to the effective pyroptosis induction and immune activation.
Additionally, after NPs treatment, no obvious body weight
change (Figure 4b), main organs damage (Figure S18) and
hemolytic reaction (Figure S19) were found, demonstrating low
toxicity and good biosecurity. To further investigate the potential
antitumor mechanism, tumor sections stained by hematoxylin
and eosin (H&E) and CD8 antibody were performed. Figure 4c
proves NaHCO3 NPs induce the most severe apoptotic cell
death and the highest CD8+ T cell infiltration. These data
testified the prepared NaHCO3 NPs have a good potential in
pyroptosis and lactic acid metabolism enhanced tumor
immunotherapy.
The satisfactory tumor inhibition effect and immune activation in
vivo encouraged us to explore in vivo antitumor efficiency in a
distal 4T1 tumor model. In brief, after triple drug administrations
of 4T1 tumor-bearing BALB/c female mice (at the left back, and
tumor sizes are about 40 mm3), BALB/c mice were inoculated
with 4T1 cells on the right back again to simulate the distal
tumor nidus. As presented in Figure 4d-4f, compared with the
other two groups, the distal tumor growth in NaHCO3 NPs group
was significantly inhibited in spite of no nanomaterial treatments
directly, indicating an initiation of effective immunotherapy. It is
well known that metastasis and recurrence of tumor are one of
the key reasons why tumor is difficult to cure, which is also the
most dominant cause of death. Hence, an antimetastatic therapy
study was further carried out to examine NaHCO3 NPs mediated
pyroptosis and immunotherapy. In short, after triple drug
administrations of 4T1 tumor-bearing BALB/c female mice, 100
μL of PBS containing 3×105 4T1 cells were injected into the
veins of the tails for lung metastasis inductions. Then the
spleens were surgical removed and collected for H&E staining at
the scheduled time. As expected, the fewest metastasis in NPs
group was observed (Figure 4g and S20). All these results
strongly demonstrated that the obtained NaHCO3 NPs can
effectively inhibit not only primary/distal tumor growth but also
pulmonary metastasis thanks to the drug-free nanomaterial
induced double actions of pyroptosis and lactic acid
consumption.
Conclusion
In conclusion, a simple and drug-free inorganic nanomaterial,
alkalescent NaHCO3 NPs as pyroptosis and metabolism
immunoadjuvants were obtained via a fast microemulsion
method for amplified cancer immunotherapy through acid
neutralization remitted immunosuppression and pyroptosis/ICD
induced immune activation. After drug administrations, the best
synergistic immunopotentiation action was observed in NaHCO3
NPs group, verified by the highest frequency of CD8 T cells,
mature DCs and M1 TAM, as well as the lowest percentages of
immunosuppressive M2 TAM, MDSCs and Treg. Collectively,
NaHCO3 NPs dramatically inhibited primary/distal tumor growth
and tumor metastasis. This work provides a new paradigm for
lactic acid metabolism and pyroptosis mediated tumor treatment,
which is instructive for the design and development of pyroptosis
agents.
