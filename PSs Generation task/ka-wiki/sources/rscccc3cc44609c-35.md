Water-soluble non-aggregating zinc phthalocyanine
and in vitro studies for photodynamic therapy†
Saad Makhseed,*a Miloslav Machacek,b Waleed Alfadly,a Ahmad Tuhl,a
Mickey Vinodh,a Tomas Simunek,b Veronika Novakova,c Pavel Kubat,d Emil Rudolfe
and Petr Zimcik*f
Newly synthesized zinc phthalocyanine bearing sixteen quaternized
imidazolyl moieties on the periphery displays high water-solubility,
lack of aggregation and high singlet oxygen quantum yield in water
(UD > 0.33). The in vitro tests indicated excellent anticancer photo-
dynamic activity (EC50 = 36.7 nM) and low dark toxicity to non-
cancerous cells (TC50 = 395 lM).
Photodynamic therapy (PDT) is a clinically approved method of
cancer treatment in which the cancer cells die due to the cytotoxic
effect of singlet oxygen.1 This highly reactive species is formed after
energy transfer from an excited photosensitizer (PS) that absorbs
light of an appropriate wavelength. Strong absorption at wave-
lengths close to 700 nm and high singlet oxygen quantum yields
(FD) in water medium represent important characteristics of a good
PS. Zinc phthalocyanines (Pcs) meet most of the photophysical
criteria including very high FD. On the other hand, they often suffer
from poor water solubility and strong aggregation in this polar
medium, which renders the Pc molecules photodynamically
inactive. Several synthetic attempts to increase the water solubility
by suitable peripheral substitution have been recently reviewed.2
Aggregation of zinc Pcs in water without addition of surfactants
or organic solvents, however, has not been satisfactorily solved
so far. This communication therefore focuses on rational
design, synthesis, determination of photophysical properties
and in vitro photodynamic activity of water-soluble, highly
efficient, non-aggregating zinc Pc.
A previously reported approach involved the use of bulky sub-
stituents to inhibit aggregation of Pcs in organic solvents.3 However,
this strategy that may work even in polar solvents for axially
substituted SiPcs4,5 is unfortunately not applicable in water medium
for peripherally substituted zinc Pcs that do not allow axial sub-
stitution on the central cation.6 For this reason, a different strategy
has to be applied. Charged substituents introduced to the periphery
of the macrocycle may support disaggregation based on electrostatic
repulsion forces. However, inhibition of aggregation of zinc Pcs in
water media using even the latter strategy is usually not complete
since the charged substituents are mostly freely pending that may
still allow p–p stacking of the hydrophobic cores.2,5,7 For this reason,
the ‘‘rigid bulky’’ and the ‘‘ionic’’ approaches were combined
leading to a unique zinc Pc derivative that does not aggregate in
water based media and exerts high photodynamic activity.
As depicted in Scheme 1, the target hexadeca-cationic zinc
complex 5, peripherally substituted with imidazole functionalized
phenoxy moieties, was successfully prepared by the metal-ion
mediated cyclotetramerisation reaction of the corresponding
phthalonitrile precursor 2 followed by a quaternization reaction
with CH3I (experimental details are provided in the ESI†). The
quaternized phthalonitrile 3 was also prepared using the same
procedure for comparison to support identification of the target
complex 5. The polycationic complex 5 was fully characterized by
elemental analysis, IR, 1
H NMR and UV/vis spectroscopy which
confirmed its structural identity.
Single crystal X-ray diffraction analyses of precursors 2 and 3
supported the anticipated spatial arrangement. The non-coplanar
conformation adapted by the phenoxy substituents almost perpen-
dicular to the plane of the phthalonitrile is enforced by mutual
steric interaction exerted by two imidazolyl functional moieties per
substituent (Fig. 1, for further discussion on X-ray see ESI†).

Although good quality single crystals could not be developed from
the complexes 4 and 5, we can presume that these complexes adapt
the same orthogonal orientation as found in the precursors 2 and 3.
The non-pending arrangement of quaternized imidazolyls over and
below the macrocycle plane therefore leads to a rigid position of
aggregation-inhibiting charged substituents.
Compound 5 was well soluble in water and polar organic
solvents (DMF, ethanol, and methanol) with partition coefficient
P B 0 (see ESI†). Absorption spectra had a typical shape for
monomeric Pcs (Fig. 2) with a high energy B-band at 362 nm and
a low energy Q-band over 680 nm with an extinction coefficient over
200 000 M1 cm1 both in water and organic solvents (Table 1).
Absorption spectra were measured in water at a wide range of
concentrations in order to assess the tendency to aggregate. The
data followed the Beer–Lambert law even at high concentrations
(10–500 mM) with a constant extinction coefficient (e) (Fig. 2, left
inset). The normalized spectra at all concentrations could be easily
superimposed without any change in the shape of the Q-band
(Fig. 2, right inset). This indicates that no aggregates are present in
water at any of the tested concentrations. Changes in pH, presence
of ions (e.g. in buffers) or culture media with serum that are often
problematic from the aggregation point of view8 did not induce any
spectral changes as well (see ESI†).
The aggregation behavior of 5 was investigated using
1 H NMR spectroscopy in D 2 O at even higher concentrations
ranging from 3–20 mM (ESI†). Complex 5 gave a well-resolved
spectrum with sharp peaks with no broadening in both aromatic
and aliphatic regions implying that the aggregation behavior is
totally absent in water. In addition, the 1
H NMR spectra of
precursor 3 were perfectly identical with the same outstanding
quality apart from the chemical shifts resulting from the ring
current effect of the aromatic macrocycle. To the best of our
knowledge, such low tendency of zinc Pcs to aggregate in water is
unprecedented in the literature and makes them a good candidate
for high photodynamic efficiency.
Fluorescence emission spectra of complex 5 in water and DMF
mirrored the absorption spectra with the maxima only slightly red-
shifted (Stokes shift o 10 nm). A perfect match of fluorescence
excitation and absorption spectra further confirmed the lack of
aggregates (Fig. 2). The fluorescence quantum yield (FF) and
fluorescence lifetime (tF) were fully comparable in both solvents
(Table 1). A value of FF = 0.16 in water is sufficient for any
detection purposes in in vitro biological tests. On the other hand,

FD values obtained by time-resolved near-infrared luminescence of
singlet oxygen O2(1Dg) at 1270 nm in DMF and in D2O were
different (Table 1) despite no aggregation, as unequivocally con-
firmed above. The data in D2O were confirmed by measurements
with two different reference compounds and at two different
excitation wavelengths.
Differences in triplet quenching by oxygen, both in the
fractions of triplets trapped by oxygen and in the quenching rate
constants (Table 1 and Table S3 in ESI†), cannot account for the
ca. 40% decrease in FD in D2O relative to DMF. Other solvent-
induced processes must therefore influence FD. Some literature
data show a similar dramatic decrease of FD in D2O in comparison
with organic solvents (more than one order of magnitude) for
dendrimers with a ruthenium phthalocyanine core.9
Excellent results from aggregation and photophysical studies
prompted us to introduce compound 5 to in vitro tests on cells
for toxicity (TC50 value) and photodynamic activity (EC50 value).
It is noteworthy that extremely high water-solubility (without
addition of any solubilizers or organic solvents) allowed deter-
mination of the TC50 value that defines the inherent toxicity of
the compound in the dark. This value, important for further
development of the compound as a potential drug, cannot be
usually obtained for Pcs due to their low solubility. A TC50 value
of 395 mM (Fig. 3) was determined for 3T3 cells (non-malignant
mouse fibroblast cell line) indicating very low toxicity to normal
cells. Similar results (TC50 = 628 mM) were also obtained for HeLa
cells (human cervix carcinoma cell line) without irradiation.
On the other hand, irradiation of the HeLa cells with red light
(l > 570 nm, 12.4 mW cm2
, 11.2 J cm2
) induced a strong
photodynamic effect with EC50 = 36.7 nM (Fig. 3).‡ Very high
ratios TC50/EC50 (‘‘therapeutic ratios’’) that reached values over
10 000 (Fig. 3) are an excellent prerequisite for a successful drug.
For comparison, phototoxicity and dark toxicity data of some
studied or established PS are mentioned in the ESI.† Fluores-
cence microscopy on HeLa cells showed punctate distribution of
compound 5 in the cytoplasm where it colocalized mainly with
lysosomes. No significant colocalization with mitochondria or
any other cellular compartment was evident. Furthermore, time-
lapse microscopy experiments revealed conspicuous changes in
cellular morphology that started by the completion of light
exposure and proceeded afterwards. Retraction of filopodia
and discontinuation of the cellular monolayer were followed by
cellular shrinkage and eventually formation of cellular debris
(see Supplementary video, ESI†).
In conclusion, a new promising PS for PDT from the group
of zinc Pcs was developed. The compound bears two ‘‘rings’’ of
positive charges – one over and one below the macrocycle core.
This unique spatial arrangement that covers the hydrophobic
Pc macrocycle completely precludes aggregation and leads to
high singlet oxygen production (FD > 0.33) in water medium.
The high photodynamic activity that was anticipated on the basis
of photophysical data was confirmed on prototypic malignant
HeLa cells. At the same time, the compound displayed very low
inherent toxicity in the dark. The promising photophysical and
in vitro results rationalize deeper investigations on the mechanism
of photodynamic action of 5 on a subcellular level that will follow.
