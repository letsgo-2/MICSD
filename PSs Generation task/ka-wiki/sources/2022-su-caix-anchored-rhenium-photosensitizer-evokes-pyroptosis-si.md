HPLC investigation
Prepared 50 μM of Re(I) solutions that containing 1% DMSO in PBS/acetonitrile (CH3CN) (V/V, 3:7). Analytical HPLC was used
for analysis with an injection volume of 20 μL. The HPLC mobile phase consisted of a linear gradient of the two mixtures of
0.1% formic acid in water, and 0.1% formic acid in CH3CN. The absorbance wavelength was set to 280 nm. To measure the
photostability process, 50 μM Re(I) solutions were under irradiation (425 nm for 10 min or 525 nm for 1 h) and then incubated
together at 37 ºC in the dark for 24 h and 1 week. To measure the reduction process, 50 μM Re(I) solutions were mixed with
20 mM ascorbic acid in PBS/CH3CN (V/V, 3/7) and then incubated at 37 ºC in the dark for 24 h after irradiation (425 nm for 10
min or 525 nm for 1 h). Each sample was analyzed by HPLC (Thermo Fisher) with a C18-H HPLC column (4.6
× 250 mm, 5 μm, 120 Å) at a flow rate of 1.0 ml/min and column temperature of 25 °C. The retention times were listed as
follows: L is 3.508 min, CA-Re is 15.867 min.
Theoretical calculations of the excited states in CA-Re
The computations were carried out by the Gaussian 16 program[4]. The DFT and TDDFT theoretical methods were used for ground
state and excited state optimizations and computations. The selected functional was the PBE0 hybrid functional[5]. The 6-31G(d) basis
set was used for the C, H, O, N, S atoms, and the Stuttgart−Dresden double-zeta (SDD) basis set with an effective core potential
(ECP)[6] was used for the Re atom. The solvent effect of dichloromethane was considered by SMD solvation model[7].
Transient absorption spectroscopy
Tansient absorption (TA) spectra of CA-Re (typically 20 μM) in deaerated CH2Cl2 were measured on an EOS sub-nanosecond
transient absorption spectrometer (Ultrafast Systems, LLC) at room temperature. The excitation pulse at 355 nm (width ≈ 5 ns) was
generated by the second harmonic generation of an 800 nm pulse (100 fs, 1 kHz repetition rate, Astrella-Tunable-V-F-1k, Coherent)
from an optical parametric amplifier (OPerA Solo, Coherent). A probe light (350–950 nm) was generated in a sapphire crystal by a
built-in photonic crystal fiber super-continuum laser source. The differenial excited state abosption sepctra were measured in the
visible region from 380 to 700 nm.
Singlet oxygen detection
The singlet oxygen capture agent (ABDA) was used to measure the singlet oxygen generated by Re complexes. Firstly, the
absorbance of CA-Re at 425 nm was adjusted to about 1.5 in PBS or acetronitrile. Secondly, 100 M ABDA (the final concerntration)
was added in the CA-Re solution to one quratz cuvette. Thirdly, the cuvette was irradiated with 425 nm laser (20 mW/cm2) for different
time (0, 40, 80, 120, 160 and 200 second). And the UV-Vis absorption spectra were recorded immediately. The quantum yields for 1O2
production (ΦΔ) of CA-Re under irradiation in aerated solution were calculated using [Ru(bpy)3]Cl2 as the standard (ΦΔ = 0.18 in
aerated H2O / ΦΔ = 0.57 in aerated acetronitrile)[8]。
Hydroxyl radical (·OH) detection
Hydroxyphenyl fluorescein (HPF) is used as the hydroxyl radical (·OH) indicator, which can be changed from colorless to green
fluorescence in the presence of ·OH. The complex CA-Re and HPF were prepared as 20 M and 10 M in 1 × PBS solution. Then the
cuvette was exposed to 425 nm laser (20 mW/cm2) for different time (0, 20, 40, 60, 80, 100, and 120 second), and the fluorescence
spectra were recorded immediately. The excitation wavelength was 490 nm, and the emission spectra was collected from 500 to 640
nm.

ESR Experiment
Sample solutions (30 μL) were put in glass capillary tubes with internal diameters of 0.5 mm and sealed. The tubes were inserted into
the ESR cavity and measured at selected times. ESR parameter settings were as follows: modulation amplitude 1 G, scan range 100
G, microwave power 20 mW and time constant 163.84 ms for detection of spin adducts using spin traps DMPO. Tubes containing 100
mM DMPO and 100 μM CA-Re in CH3OH were exposed to 425 nm laser (20 mW/cm2, 5 min) and immediately characterized.
Molecule docking study
All the docking calculations were performed with AutoDock 4.2 using Lamarckian genetic algorithm method. We used the PDB
structure of the cocrystallized CAIX-AAZ complex (PDB code: 3IAI) as the starting point. The search space was defined as following:
center x = 67.419, center y = 55.704, center z = 14.134; size x = 72, size y = 74, size z = 72; with the spacing set to 0.375 Å and the
exhaustiveness set to 25 iterations. The structure of CA-Re was optimized using Gaussian 09 at B3LYP/genecp (LanL2DZ basis for
Re and 6-31G* for other atoms) computation level[4].
In vitro kit test to study the inhibitory effect on the activity of CAs
The CAs inhibition activity of compounds was evaluated as previously reported[9]. 4-Nitrophenylacetate (4-NPA) is hydrolyzed by
carbonic anhydrase to form 4-nitrophenol. 4-Nitrophenol which has absorption at 405 nm, could be measured by a microplate reader
to measure enzyme activity.
Cell cultures
All used cell lines were obtained from the animal experimental Centre of Sun Yat-Sen University (Guangzhou, China). MDA-MB-231
(human triple negative breast cancer cells), MCF-10A (human breast epithelial cells), and 4T1 (mouse breast cancer cells) were
maintained in DMEM media supplemented with 10% FBS, 100 μg/mL streptomycin, and 100 U/mL penicillin (complete DMEM) in a
humidified atmosphere with 5% CO2 at 37 °C. Aerobic culture: Cells were maintained in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% of
CO2 at 37 °C. Hypoxic culture: Cells was maintained at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere containing 1% oxygen, 5% carbon dioxide
and 94% nitrogen. Simulated hypoxic culture: 100 μM CoCl2 solution was to simulate the hypoxia conditions according to reference [10].
Cell viability assay and In vitro cytotoxicity
MTT assay was used to determined the in vitro cytotoxicities of complexes CA-Re and cisplatin. Firstly, the cells were seeded into
96-well microtiter plates, and incubated overnight for the adhere cells. Stepwise diluted concentrations of the complexes were added
to cell plates. The cells were incubated with CA-Re at the indicated concentrations for 48 h in dark, and for the light cytotoxicity they
were irradiated with a 425 nm laser (20 mW/cm2, 10 min, 12 J/cm2) or a 525 nm laser (15 mW/cm2, 60 min, 54 J/cm2) after 24 hours’
incubation. MTT solution (20 μL, 5 mg/mL in 1× PBS) was added to each well. After 4h’s incubation, the cultures were removed and
150 μL of DMSO solution was added into each well. The optical density of each well was measured on a microplate spectrophotometer
at a wavelength of 595 nm. The percentage of cell viability was calculated using the equation: (mean OD of treated cells/mean OD of
control cells) × 100%. Cells treated with vehicle control (1% DMSO) were used as controls. Data were reported as the mean ±
standard deviation (n = 3).
Intracellular uptake and localization measured by Confocal microscopy
MDA-MB-231 cells were seeded into 35 mm confocal dishes at a density of about 1.5 × 105 cells per well and incubated overnight at
37 °C in a 5% CO2 incubator. For time-dependent uptake and colocalization studies, cells were incubated with CA-Re (10.0 μM) for
different time (3 h, 6 h, 12 h and 24 h). For colocalization studies, cells were incubated with CA-Re (10 μM) for 6 h, and then stained
with Dil (10 μM) for another 15 min. Cells were then washed for three time with PBS, and fresh medium was replenished before
visualized by confocal microscopy. CA-Re: λex = 405 nm, λem = 680 ± 30 nm. Dil: λex = 561 nm, λem = 580 ± 20 nm.
ICP-MS assay
MDA-MB-231 cells were seeded onto a 12 well plate and were grown in complete DMEM media in the cell incubator with 5% CO2
atmosphere at 37 °C overnight. After the cells were treated with CA-Re (10 μΜ) for 3 and 6 h, cells were washed for three times with
sterile PBS containing with 1% DMSO. Subsequently, the cells were counted and digested in 400 μL of 60 % HNO3 at 60 °C for 1 h.
The solution was then diluted to a final volume of 10 mL with Milli-Q water. The concentration of Re was measured using the XSERIES
2 ICP-MS. The standards for Re calibration were freshly prepared by diluting a rhenium standard solution with 2 % HNO3 in Milli-Q
water.
Determination of Intracellular ROS levels in normoxic MDA-MB-231 cells
The intracellular ROS was determined by the DCFH-DA assay. Briefly, MDA-MB-231 cells were seeded into 35 mm confocal dishes at
a density of about 1.5 × 105 cells per well and incubated overnight at 37 °C in a 5% CO2 incubator. Upon treatment with CA-Re
complexes for 24 h, these cells were irradiated with/without a 425 nm laser (20 mW/cm2, 10 min). Then the cells were carefully rinsed
twice with PBS and incubated with 10 μM DCFH-DA in FBS-free DMEM for 15 min in the dark. The cells were further rinsed twice with
PBS and determined with confocal microscopy. ROS: λex = 488 nm, λem = 520 ± 20 nm.

Intracellular superoxide anion (·O2-) detection in normoxic MDA-MB-231 cells
MDA-MB-231 cells were treated with CA-Re (100 nM) for 24 h incubation followed by 30 min treatment with 10 M DHE. After that,
cells were washed with PBS and then irradiated with/without a 425 nm laser (20 mW/cm2, 10 min). The cells were determined with

Intracellular lipid peroxidation detection in normoxic MDA-MB-231 cells
According to the manufacturer's directions, The intracellular lipid peroxidation levels were measured by Lipid Peroxidation MDA Assay
Kit, MDA-MB-231 cells were seeded onto a 6 well plate and were grown in complete DMEM in a 5% CO2 incubator with atmosphere at

In vivo anti-tumor efficacy and abscopal effect
All of the animal experiments were approved by the university animal care and use committee of Sun Yat-Sen University. SPF Female
BALB/c mice, 4˗5 weeks of age, were purchased from Guangdong Provincial Medical Laboratory Animal Center and bred in the
Experimental Animal Center of Sun Yat-Sen University. 4T1 tumor cells were inoculated subcutaneously on the right flanks of BALB/c
female mice, and then on the left flanks (containing 2 × 106 cells in 100 μL 1 × PBS) after 7 days (Scheme S2). The tumor on the right
flanks was used as a primary tumor for the PDT effect of CA-Re, and the left tumor was used as a distant tumor to test the anti-tumor
immunity. The tumor-bearing mice were randomly divided into three groups: (a) control group, (b) Re group, (c) Re+light group. The
complex was dissolved in 2 % DMSO and 4 % PEG 400, 4 % tween 80 and 92 % PBS successively. Mice were injected intratumorally
with 5 mg/kg CA-Re (50 μL) on days 0, 4 and 8 day, and then irradiated with a 525 nm laser (15 mW/cm2, 60 min) after1 hour’s
incubation. The body weight and tumor volume of mice were recorded every two days. The tumor volume (V) was calculated as V = W2
× L/2, where W is the width and L is the length of the tumor. The mice were sacrificed on day 16 for further assays. Tumor and major
organs (including heart, liver, spleen, lung and kidney) of each group were further studied by hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining
assay.
Note: In vivo anti-tumor efficacy are generally evaluated using BALB/c nude mice bearing MDA-MB-231 tumors. However, nude
mice are immunodeficient and are not suitable for anti-tumor immune studies. Therefore, immunocompetent BALB/c mice was used in
this work, and homogenotypic mouse 4T1 breast tumor model rather than human MDA-MB-231 tumor model was employed so as to
stimulate systemic immune responses in mice.
In vivo anti-tumor immunity
To systematically investigate the in vivo anti-tumor immunity, a series of immune cells were analyzed by flow cytometry after staining
with antibodies. The distant tumors and tumor draining lymph nodes were obtained from the mices (n = 3 mice in each group) and
were made into a single cell suspension with 1 mg/mL collagenase IV and 0.1 mg/mL DNase I for 1 h at 37 °C. After being filered
through a 70 μm nylon cell strainer, the blood samples were removed by red blood cell lysis buffer. And then the obtained dendritic
cells and T lymphocyte cells were stained with anti-CD80-mouse-APC, anti-CD86-mouse-PE, anti-CD11c-mouse-FITC,
anti-CD8a-mouse-APC, anti-CD4-mouse-PerCP or anti-CD3-mouse-FITC, and then evaluated by flow cytometry. Serum samples
were isolated from mice after above treatments and stored at liquid nitrogen for analysis. And the secretion of proinflammatory
cytokines (IL-12p70, IL-6, and TNF-α) and antiinflammatory cytokine (IL-10), interleukin IL-18 and IL-1β of serum was measured by
ELISA Kit according to the manufacturer’s protocols.
Statistical analysis
All the experiments were replicated and the specific number of replicates done was either annotated in the figure legends or the
specific section. Unless otherwise specified, the statistical significance in this study is determined by the unpaired, two-tailed Student’s
t-test at *p < 0.05 and **p <0.01. Data were presented as means ± standard deviations (SD).

Figure S12. Inhibitory effect of CA-Re on the activity of CA isoforms measured by in vitro kit test.
Discussion: An ideal CAIX inhibitor should be selective to CAIX rather than other CAs to reduce off-targets and side effects. We compared the inhibitory effect of
CA-Re on the activity of CAIX and other three CA isoforms (CAII, CAVB, and CAXII). Among them, CAIX and CAXII were trans-membrane proteins and
well-established anticancer drug targets, while CAII and VB were relative to other disease and localized in cytoplasm and mitochondria, respectively. It was found
that CA-Re could effectively inhibit the activity of these four CA isofoms with similar inhibitory IC50 value (ca. 3.7 M), meaning that CA-Re had no selectivity to
CAIX in vitro. This is reasonable because these CAs have similar catalytic active centers which is a large conical cavity with zinc ion center, while CA-Re uses the
sulfonamide moiety to coordinate zinc ion to inhibit the activity of these CAs. Nevertheless, most CA-Re have none opportunity to interact with CAII or other
intracellular CA isoforms because it would meet and interact with trans-membrane CAIX first. As shown in Figure 1A, CA-Re was always anchoring on cell
membrane rather than entering in cytoplasm even after 24 h incubation, thus avoiding its interaction with other CAs and making sure its selectivity to
trans-membrane CAIX/CAXII in living cells. It was worth mentioning that CAXII was less investigated in literature but basically it was also an anticancer target and
had not interfere with our goals.
