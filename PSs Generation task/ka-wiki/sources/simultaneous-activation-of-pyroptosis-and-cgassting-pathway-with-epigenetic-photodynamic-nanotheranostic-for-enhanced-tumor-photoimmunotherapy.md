1. Introduction
Immunotherapy, exemplified by immune checkpoint blockade therapy and chimeric antigen
receptor T-cell therapy, has revitalized cancer treatments and revolutionized the field of oncology.[1]
However, the clinical benefits offered by this advanced cancer immunotherapy in patients with solid
tumors are often restricted by the immunosuppressive microenvironment[2] and insufficient tumor
immunogenicity.[3] In this context, enhancing innate immunity through pharmacologically stimulating
immunogenic cell death[4] or activating the cyclic GMP-AMP synthase-stimulator of interferon gene
(cGAS-STING) signaling pathway[5] represents a promising opportunity to bolster the potency of the
adaptive antitumor immunity for improved responsiveness to immunotherapy.

Pyroptosis represents a distinctive type of immunogenic cell death characterized by the
activation of caspases to cleave the gasdermin (GSDM) protein, generating cytomembrane-
penetrating GSDM-N fragments that lead to cell swelling and the release of immunostimulatory
cellular content.[6] While mounting chemotherapeutic agents[7] and ions[8] have been reported to be
effective in provoking caspase-3-mediated pyroptosis regarding the cellular expression of GSDME,
the therapeutic effectiveness of current pyroptosis inducers may be significantly hindered by their
poor bioavailability, indiscriminate cytotoxicity, and insufficient intracellular GSDME expression
ascribing to the promotor hypermethylation.
The cGAS-STING signaling pathway plays a crucial role as a conserved pathogenic DNA-sensing
machinery to initiate innate immunity involving a robust type I interferon response against microbial
infections.[9] Notably, the cGAS-STING pathway can also be activated by damage-associated double-
strand DNA (dsDNA) fragments released from dysfunctional mitochondrials[10] or the disintegrating
chromatin within the nucleus,[11] making it promising in enhancing antitumor immunity. Briefly,
cytosolic dsDNA fragments could be recognized and further catalyzed by cGAS to form cyclic GMP-
AMP (cGAMP) from ATP and GTP. Subsequently, cGAMP activates STING and leads to the cascade
phosphorylation of TANK binding kinase 1 (TBK1) as well as interferon regulatory factor 3 (IRF3) to
promote the expression of type I interferons (IFNs) and other immune-stimulated genes (ISGs).[12] In
recent years, mounting genotoxic agents[13] and DNA damage response inhibitors[14] have been
reported to efficiently trigger the cGAS-STING pathway, and diverse synthetic or natural STING
agonists[15] have also been developed to synergize with immunotherapy. Despite preliminary
antitumor efficacy, the intrinsic instability or uncontrollable systemic side effects of the
aforementioned agents represent major challenges to be addressed.
Recently, the rapid development of material science has provided unique strategies for the
prevention, diagnosis, and therapy of diseases.[16] Herein, we developed a sophisticated
nanotheranostic system comprised of an oxidation-responsive nanoparticle (NP1) containing
photosensitizer TBE, and a liposome (NP2) loaded with DNA methyltransferase inhibitor (DNMTi)

2. Results and Discussion
Developing photosensitizers with high biocompatibility, biodegradability, absorption in the NIR
window, and photoenergy-conversion efficiency is essential for the photodynamic therapy of deep
tumors.*17+ To obtain a photosensitizer TBE with an excellent near-infrared (NIR) absorption
coefficient suitable for dual fluorescence imaging and photodynamic therapy, a degradable pseudo-
semiconducting polymer was synthesized via Stille polycondensation of monomers 2,5-bis(2-
ethylhexyl)-3,6-bis(5-(trimethylstannyl)thiophen-2-yl)-2,5-dihydropyrrolo-*3,4-c+pyrrole-1,4-dione
(M1), 1,2-bis(4-bromophenyl)-1,2-diphenylethene (M2), 3,7-bis(E-4-bromostyryl)-5,5-difluoro-1,9-
dimethyl-10-(trifluoromethyl)-5H-4λ4,5λ4-dipyrrolo*1,2-c:2’,1’ -f+*1,3,2+diazaborinine (M3) and
disulfanediylbis(ethane-2,1-diyl)bis(5-bromothiophene-2-carboxylate) (M4) (Figure 1A). Of which,
M1 with strong electron-withdrawing properties was chosen to narrow the bandgap, thereby fine-
tuning the absorption spectra of TBE. M2 was one of the most common fluorescent monomers, the
introduction of which was expected to endow TBE with potent fluorescence for NIR fluorescence
imaging. The photoactive monomer M3 could enable efficient singlet oxygen (1O2) generation for
PDT, which has been proved in our previous studies.*18+ And the oxidative-sensitive monomer M4
could render optimal biodegradability in response to ROS-enriched TME.*19+ The successful synthesis
of TBE was confirmed by 1H-NMR (Figure S1).

3. Conclusions
In summary, this study presented a smart nanotheranostic system that integrates PDT, NIR
fluorescence bioimaging, and TME modulation through concurrent activation of pyroptosis and the
cGAS-STING pathway for cancer therapy. The nanotheranostic system comprises a ROS-sensitive
nanoparticle NP1 loaded with photosensitizers TBE and a liposome loaded with DNMTi decitabine
(NP2). Upon administration, decitabine released from NP2 could effectively reverse promotor
methylation and restore intracellular expression of STING and GSDME, while TBE released from NP1
in response to the oxidative environment could exhibit magnificent light-to-1O2 efficiency under 808
nm laser exposure. Excessive ROS generated by the NP1-mediated PDT could then induce potent
mitochondrial dysfunction, enabling the cytosolic release of mtDNA to stimulate the cGAS-STING
pathway, and the activation of caspase-3 to cleave the upregulated GSDME to generate
cytomembrane-penetrating GSDME-N terminal fragments for pyroptosis. Proinflammatory cytokines
released by concurrent pyroptosis ignition and the cGAS-STING pathway activation could elicit an
intense and multifaceted antitumor immunity by activating the maturation of DCs and NK cells to
